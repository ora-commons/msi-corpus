## Nearly 200 countries fail to agree on moving away from coal and gas

ANTALYA, Turkey — International climate change talks here broke down in overtime hours over the main cause of planetary warming: fossil fuels. On Sunday, nearly 200 countries failed to reach an agreement on whether they should move away from energy sources like coal and natural gas.

The talks ended in a dramatic fashion, with European Union negotiators saying they could not agree to a final agreement without mentioning the transition away from fossil fuels — the primary driver of climate change. The EU bloc then walked out of the room.

After the EU walked out, so did other countries pushing for a speedy transition away from fossil fuels.

The European climate chief, Wopke Hoekstra, said that when the world is heading for an overshoot of 1.5 degrees, the EU could not tolerate an agreement that does not mention fossil fuels.

"That is not acceptable," Hoekstra told reporters after walking out of the talks. "We do that with a heavy heart with many other countries, but we simply cannot accept this."

Burning fossil fuels like oil, gas and coal make up roughly 73% of global emissions.

This is the first U.N. climate summit where there has been scientific consensus that the world will surpass a key global warming threshold. Countries agreed in 2015 to limit climate change to 1.5 degrees Celsius (2.7 degrees Fahrenheit), but the planet has essentially reached that limit already.

Passing that threshold means more severe climate impacts, like more intense hurricanes and heat waves. The United Nations found that a best-case scenario of 1.7 or 1.8 degrees Celsius would require cutting emissions immediately, reducing them by 45% by 2035. That, scientists say, would require dramatically cutting fossil fuel use.

Mohamed Adow, of Power Shift Africa, a nonprofit focused on climate change in Africa, said the outcome was better than the alternative on the table.

"It's good that that decision has been rejected," Adow said. "It doesn't have enough to help protect the people who are most vulnerable to climate change, who have contributed the least and are now suffering firsthand the worst."


The walkout ends a summit that had already run past its scheduled close: as [MSI reported on Nov. 21](/articles/2026-11-21-cop31-talks-run-into-overtime-as-fossil-fuels-stay-out-of-draft-deal/), negotiators went into an added day of talks after a draft final agreement drew strong condemnation for failing to mention fossil fuels, with no sign at the time that the extra session would produce a deal. Sunday's collapse resolved that standoff by closing the talks with no agreement on the fossil fuel language the EU and allied countries had demanded.
