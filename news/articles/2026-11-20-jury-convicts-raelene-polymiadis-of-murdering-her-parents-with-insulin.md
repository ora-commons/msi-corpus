## Unanimous verdicts followed a four-month trial delayed beyond its initial estimate

A jury in the South Australian Supreme Court in Adelaide convicted Raelene Polymiadis, 66, of murdering her parents, Brenda and Lynton Anderson, with insulin on Friday. The unanimous verdicts followed a four-month trial that began on June 15 and ran beyond its 12-week estimate because of illnesses and other delays. The jury needed less than five hours to return the verdicts on Friday morning.

Polymiadis clutched the dock as the first verdict was read out. After the second guilty verdict was announced, she dabbed at her eyes with a tissue, repeatedly shook her head and blinked rapidly. The caption accompanying a photograph by Abe Maddison/AAP said Polymiadis left the South Australian Supreme Court in Adelaide on Wednesday.

Justice Sandi McDonald thanked the jury for its service during the “long and difficult trial.” The court revoked Polymiadis's bail and remanded her in custody ahead of sentence. She faces two mandatory life sentences and will reappear in court on December 16 for a directions hearing.

Polymiadis's son accompanied his mother to court throughout the trial. Neither he nor her husband, Michael, commented as they left the courthouse.

## Both parents faced declining health and opposed being moved into aged care

The jury accepted that Polymiadis, who has type 1 diabetes, injected Brenda and Lynton Anderson with fatal doses of insulin in separate incidents in March 2022 and April 2023. Each parent was 94 when they died. Both had faced declining health, and both were strongly opposed to being moved into aged care. Prosecutors raised one potential motive: a misplaced idea that killing her parents would spare them from entering a nursing home.

Brenda had a fall in February 2022 and was admitted to Noarlunga hospital with a fractured vertebra; pneumonia and another fall complicated her recovery. She was recovering and had a discharge plan in place when Polymiadis visited her on March 12. Nursing staff later found her “largely unresponsive.” Prosecutors alleged that Polymiadis administered insulin to Brenda on two occasions, on March 12 and between March 13 and March 15.

After Brenda's death, Lynton continued to live at home but became more frail, and his four children began staying there at night on a shift basis. Polymiadis stayed with her father over the weekend and left the house on April 30, 2023. Two hours later, a carer found Lynton unconscious. He died on May 1 as a result of insulin toxicity.

## Key evidence and witness testimony

Polymiadis's former boyfriend, Ian Edward Willcourt, told the court that she had said, “insulin was the perfect murder weapon because it’s natural in the body and it can’t be detected”.

Key evidence included a police listening device that captured Polymiadis alone in her car, allegedly confessing to God that she had killed her parents.

Police also seized notes written by Polymiadis. One read: “What they will do is wait for a few weeks, months, year to see if I ‘think I got away with it’ and I get cocky and slip up.” Another read: “Hang on buddy, it could be a long ride, otherwise the suspect would be arrested now.”
