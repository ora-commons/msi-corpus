## Qiu calls Chen case fourth current-cycle fall among sitting Politburo members

China’s Central Commission for Discipline Inspection said on November 25, 2026, that it was investigating Chen Jining, the Shanghai party secretary and a Politburo member, for suspected violations of party discipline. The anti-corruption watchdog’s brief announcement provided no further details.

Chen has served as Shanghai party secretary since 2022. He previously held senior positions in Beijing’s municipal government and headed the Ministry of Environmental Protection.

The Shanghai party secretary position has traditionally been viewed as a stepping stone to China’s top leadership. The country’s former President Jiang Zemin, its premier, Li Qiang, and Xi Jinping previously held the position.

The investigation follows sweeping purges under President Xi Jinping, who has emphasized greater party discipline. Xi has pursued an anti-corruption campaign for more than a decade, carrying out purges on a scale not seen since Mao Zedong’s rule. Chen is the latest senior official targeted in the campaign, which has shown no signs of letting up in scope or depth.

A statement released by the Shanghai city government said the Standing Committee of the Shanghai Committee of the CCP “expressed firm support” for the decision to investigate Chen. The statement said the decision sent “a clear and powerful signal that the fight against corruption would continue without pause or retreat,” with “a firm determination to win.”

“The takedown again shows that Xi Jinping’s anti-corruption campaign has no limits,” Mingda Qiu, a senior analyst at Eurasia Group, said in an emailed statement. “This is the fourth Politburo member to fall, and the fourth sitting member brought down in the current cycle. The pattern is unprecedented.”

Other Politburo members purged include Gen. Zhang Youxia and He Weidong, both senior members of the Central Military Commission, which oversees the armed forces, and Ma Xingrui, a provincial party secretary.

Joseph Torigian, a professor at American University based in Washington, said the recent Politburo purges highlight how much Chinese politics depend on shifting circumstances and how even officials with qualities Xi apparently valued have been vulnerable to removal. “On paper,” they appear to be “individuals Xi would appreciate,” Torigian said, but afterward, “we could easily come up with hypotheses for why he got rid of them.”

Chen is under scrutiny as the latest senior target in Xi’s anti-corruption campaign. Beyond the watchdog’s brief announcement and Shanghai’s statement of support, no further details about the investigation have been provided.
