## August inquiry followed allegations of inappropriate sexual contact against Gomez

A House Ethics Committee report released Monday said U.S. Rep. Jimmy Gomez made "unwelcome sexual advances" toward a House staff member while "disregarding the power imbalance inherent" between them. BBC News, citing CBS News as its U.S. media partner, described the investigation's result as credible evidence that Gomez engaged in sexual misconduct.

The committee interviewed a woman who said Gomez made unwanted advances toward her on two occasions while she was a House staff member. She said one encounter occurred in public and the other in his congressional office.

The bipartisan committee announced its investigation into Gomez in August after lawyers for the House staffer accused him of inappropriate sexual contact.

Gomez disputed the report. In a Monday statement, he said he had "strong disagreements" with the committee's findings but saw "no other alternative than to resign." CBS News quoted the statement.

He also said: "I recognize the seriousness of the Ethics Committee's report and as I previously stated I take full responsibility for the personal mistakes I committed outside of my marriage." The acknowledgment came as Gomez continued to state his disagreement with the findings.

Gomez, 51, is a California Democrat who represented part of Los Angeles. He lost his seat in the midterm elections earlier this month and was serving in the House only until the end of the year.

Gomez said he would resign on Dec. 1. Because he had already lost his seat and was due to leave at the end of the year, the Dec. 1 departure would not extend his House tenure.

BBC News said it had contacted Gomez's office for comment.
