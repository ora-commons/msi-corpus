## Prosecutors said pandemic aid funded payments for about two dozen mercenaries

Christian Emmanuel Sanon, a Florida pastor with dual Haitian-American citizenship, was convicted Wednesday in federal court in Miami of U.S. charges arising from the 2021 assassination of Haitian President Jovenel Moïse. Sanon had faced eight charges, including conspiracy to kidnap or kill a person outside the United States, after a nearly eight-week trial. He faces up to life in prison at sentencing.

Federal prosecutors said South Florida was a hub for planning and financing the plot to oust Moïse and install a president of the conspirators’ choosing. The government identified Sanon as the conspirators’ initial choice to replace Moïse. Prosecutors said Sanon helped raise money for the conspiracy and arranged travel to Haiti and lodging for himself and others.

Sanon acknowledged in trial testimony that he was in communication at one point with people trying to replace Moïse. He said the group had moved to another candidate long before the assassination and denied any knowledge of plans to kill Moïse.

Haiti has not had a president since authorities said a squad of mostly retired Colombian soldiers killed Moïse and wounded his wife in a nighttime raid at their home on July 7, 2021. Haiti is the poorest country in the Americas and has a history of dictatorship, political upheaval and foreign intervention that has long hindered democratic rule. The election of a new president is scheduled for next year.

Prosecutors said, citing court records, that the scheme to kill Moïse included paying about two dozen foreign mercenaries with money obtained from U.S. government programs intended to help businesses during the COVID-19 pandemic. U.S. prosecutors have charged more than 40 people with crimes related to the assassination, and a dozen have been convicted. Five of those convicted people are serving life sentences.

According to an indictment, the plot to oust Moïse took its final shape in April 2021, when Sanon met in South Florida with a former Haitian senator and several U.S. businesspeople. The group agreed that, once in power, Sanon would award contracts to two Florida-based companies, Counter Terrorist Unit Federal Academy and Counter Terrorist Unit Security, collectively known as CTU. The proposed contracts covered infrastructure projects, security forces and military equipment, according to investigators.

CTU initially hired about 18 Colombian nationals with military training to provide security for Sanon, according to prosecutors. Officials said conspirators also spent months obtaining weapons and body armor and attempting to build ties with Haitian gangs.

Prosecutors said the conspirators had determined by June 2021 that Sanon lacked both the constitutional qualifications and sufficient popular support to become president. The group shifted its support to Wendelle Coq Thélot, a former Haitian Supreme Court judge who died in January 2025 while a fugitive.

Sanon was born in Haiti and moved to the United States as a teenager. He testified that he worked with churches in Haitian immigrant communities in Florida and the Midwest. Sanon was previously scheduled to be tried with four other men, who were convicted earlier this year, but his case was separated from theirs and delayed for health reasons.
