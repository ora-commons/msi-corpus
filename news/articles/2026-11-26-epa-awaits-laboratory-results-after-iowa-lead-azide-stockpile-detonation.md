## EPA sampled air, water, soil and dust before the blast

The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency said Thursday that laboratory results from environmental sampling after the U.S. Army detonated the nation’s entire stockpile of lead azide in Iowa could begin arriving “as early as tomorrow.” The controlled detonation occurred Wednesday.

EPA personnel and contractors took over 1,500 samples before the blast. The pre-detonation collection covered air, water, soil and dust. Lead azide is a chemical used to make explosives, and the operation disposed of the nation’s entire stockpile of the material.

After the detonation, the EPA took hundreds of additional samples, including from homes, parks and drinking water. More samples were expected to be collected Thursday. The agency said the samples had been sent to laboratories for testing.

The EPA said “zero” airborne lead had been detected in the region since Wednesday’s controlled detonation. That finding covered airborne lead while the broader laboratory analysis continued.

The agency was also testing for other compounds that could have been released during the explosion. “The agency is testing for everything we were anticipating could potentially be released as a result of the controlled detonation,” the EPA said.

The EPA said it would remain on the ground as long as needed to ensure proper environmental monitoring following the controlled detonation. Its presence included additional sample collection and laboratory work, with results still pending.

The Army said detonating the stockpile was the safest option because the lead azide was degrading and could have exploded uncontrollably as the weather grew colder. That reasoning concerned the risk posed by leaving the material in place, while the EPA’s continuing work focused on what the controlled detonation released.

The update left parts of the environmental assessment unfinished. No airborne lead had been detected in the region so far, but additional samples were being collected and laboratory results had not yet arrived.
