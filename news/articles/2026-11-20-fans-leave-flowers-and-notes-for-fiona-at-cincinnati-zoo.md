## Fiona died from complications caused by swallowing a knit hat

CINCINNATI — Fans of Fiona, the Cincinnati Zoo's hippo, draped a hippo statue with flowers and notes written in crayon on Friday, a day after she died. A little girl held up a drawing of Fiona while a woman left behind a watermelon, one of Fiona's favorite treats. One of the notes said, “She brought so much love in her lifetime.”

## Friday tributes and public response

The Associated Press wrote: “Born prematurely almost a decade ago and given slim odds of survival, Fiona became not only a source of hometown pride but also a worldwide symbol of hope and inspiration for many — from teachers to parents to children.”

The tributes reached well beyond Cincinnati. The Associated Press reported that Fiona is mourned by many worldwide and wrote, “Fiona captured the hearts of millions in her first months and final days, uniting people across all boundaries and political spectrums.”

Thayne Maynard, the zoo board's executive chair, said in a statement, “I have never known an animal so beloved in all my life. From the day she was born, she was like lightning in a bottle.” He added, “And, she will be remembered and beloved many years from now.”

Zoo director Dave Jenike said the Cincinnati Zoo's animal-care team worked to save Fiona. “Our animal care team was heroic in their efforts to save her. They did everything possible, but her body couldn’t fight anymore,” he said.

Usha Vance, the wife of Vice President JD Vance, posted a photo showing one of their children face-to-face with Fiona through the glass wall of her enclosure. Former first daughter Chelsea Clinton posted photos of her behind-the-scenes encounter with Fiona, writing that she would treasure “the smiles she gave me.”

Speaking outside the zoo Thursday, Amy Haacke, a teacher from Dayton, said she was a “huge fan” of Fiona because she likes underdogs and uses the Fiona book series in her classroom. “It kind of means a lot to be here,” she said.

Zoo officials have yet to release the specific medical cause of Fiona's death or details surrounding her final hours. Fiona was 9 years old.

## The hat incident and the zoo's response

The Associated Press reported that Fiona died from complications caused by swallowing a knit hat that was accidentally flung into her enclosure. She swallowed the hat 11 days before her death. A zoo visitor tossed the hat, which a ceiling fan caught and flung into the hippo habitat, and Fiona initially showed no signs of distress.

A few days later, her appetite started to decline, zoo officials said. By Monday, the zoo announced that Fiona was undergoing veterinary care.

The zoo's animal-care team gave her fluids through an IV, conducted a colonoscopy and tried to shake the obstruction loose with a vibration table borrowed from a horse farm. The zoo worked around the clock to save Fiona, and the team was cautiously optimistic she could pass the hat.

“Despite multiple attempts to move the hat out of her system, it was ultimately too much to overcome,” the zoo said. Zoo director Dave Jenike said Fiona's death came as a shock. “We did not expect this outcome. She fought until she couldn’t anymore,” he said.

## Background: a premature birth and a hand-raised hippo

Fiona was not expected to survive her first weeks. Born six weeks premature, she weighed 29 pounds (13.2 kilograms), far below the lowest recorded birth weight for her species at the time.

Zoo staff raised her by hand. She couldn't nurse, so a zoo staffer hand-milked her mother to feed her.

The Associated Press reported that Fiona “became a celebrity during her nine years” and noted that hippos typically live 40 to 50 years in zoos.

The Associated Press wrote that “Her story marked an uplifting turn for the Cincinnati Zoo after months of backlash over the 2016 shooting of Harambe.” Harambe, the gorilla, became a pop culture phenomenon after he was killed because a child got into his enclosure.
