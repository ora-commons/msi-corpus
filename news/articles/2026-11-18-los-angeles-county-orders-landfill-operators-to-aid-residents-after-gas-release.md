## Drilling crews encountered a gas pocket about 150 feet below Chiquita Canyon

A noxious, dark geyser of decomposing material and smoke began to rise from the Chiquita Canyon landfill in Santa Clarita Valley on Monday afternoon, prompting advisories for nearby communities. The Los Angeles County Department of Public Health later ordered the companies operating the landfill, including Texas-based Waste Connections, to immediately provide temporary hotel stays, filtered-air refuge centers, transportation assistance and air purifiers to affected residents.

The city of Santa Clarita said residents in affected areas “should be prepared to evacuate if instructed by authorities. This is a warning, not an evacuation order.” The warning followed advisories for nearby communities and preceded the county health department’s assistance order.

According to Aaron Katon, a captain with the Los Angeles County Fire Department, Waste Connections said in a statement that its crews were drilling a new well when they encountered a gas pocket about 150 feet below ground. Katon said the erupting cloud was not an explosion but the release of naturally occurring gases produced by rotting material. The gas pocket carried small pieces of decomposing garbage into the air, he said.

“There was no fire, it was an escape of gas, and after that initial debris got blown off, the plume diminished significantly,” Katon said.

Katon also said the plume posed no hazard to anyone in the community. A fire department spokesman told the Los Angeles Times that six landfill workers were taken to a hospital for decontamination.

The Guardian reported that the 639-acre dump had for years wrestled to control a chain reaction within the landfill. The landfill’s owners and operators are facing a state lawsuit alleging that they had mismanaged toxic waste.

Los Angeles County Supervisor Kathryn Barger said the “families of Castaic and the surrounding communities have lived with the burden of this landfill for far too long, and they should not have to wonder whether they are safe. I want answers, and I will not accept vague explanations. What happened at the site today, why it happened, and what safeguards failed are questions the operator and every responsible agency must answer fully and promptly.”

She continued: “Those responsible will be held accountable. My office will stay in close contact with our first responders and regulators, and we will share verified information with the community as soon as we have it. Residents should rely on official County sources and follow guidance from public safety officials.”
