## Detainee is accused of ordering attack near U.S. Consulate that killed officer

Mexican federal authorities have confirmed the arrest of Axel Alfredo Cárdenas Rodríguez, known as "El Titán" or "Axel," a senior figure in Los Ciclones, one of the Gulf Cartel's main factions, and the arrest has triggered roadblocks, armed clashes and civilian deaths in the northern border state of Tamaulipas.

Security and Citizen Protection Secretary Omar García Harfuch announced the operation in a post on October 29. He said Mexican Army special forces and National Guard personnel, acting on military intelligence work by the Defense Ministry, detained Cárdenas Rodríguez and another suspect in Matamoros. Officers seized weapons, ammunition, drugs and a vehicle during the operation, he said. Both suspects were flown to Mexico City aboard an air force aircraft and placed in the custody of the Specialized Prosecutor's Office for Organized Crime.

García Harfuch described Cárdenas Rodríguez as a high-priority target linked to homicides, kidnappings, extortion and the trafficking of drugs, weapons and people into the United States. His post identified the detainee as the second in command of the Los Ciclones criminal cell.

The security secretary also said Cárdenas Rodríguez is believed to have ordered a May 17 armed attack against federal officers guarding the outer perimeter of the U.S. Consulate in Matamoros. One Mexican officer was killed in that attack and several others were wounded, according to García Harfuch.

Cárdenas Rodríguez belongs to a criminal family based in Matamoros. He is the son of José Alfredo Cárdenas Martínez, known as "El Contador," who faces U.S. charges involving cocaine, methamphetamine and fentanyl trafficking and remains subject to extradition proceedings.

According to Infobae, investigations indicate that Los Ciclones operated a cross-border trafficking network that moved weapons and cash from the United States into Mexico while smuggling migrants, fentanyl and other drugs in the opposite direction. Los Ciclones and Los Escorpiones, another Gulf Cartel faction linked to the Cárdenas family, also have been implicated in the March 2023 kidnapping of four U.S. citizens in Matamoros, a case investigated by the FBI and other U.S. federal agencies.

The arrest prompted an immediate violent response from local criminal groups. Throughout Thursday, suspected cartel members erected multiple roadblocks using burning vehicles along major highways connecting Matamoros with Río Bravo and Ciudad Victoria, the state capital, Mexico Daily News reported.

The U.S. Consulate issued a security alert for its personnel and urged U.S. citizens to avoid the affected area. The unrest disrupted commercial activity and prompted precautionary school closures across the border region.

Transportation and civil protection authorities reported deadly incidents linked to the roadblocks, including a crash that killed four people after a vehicle struck a burning cargo truck obstructing a highway. Separately, a 7-year-old U.S. citizen was killed after being caught in crossfire during the armed clashes.

García Harfuch wrote that the memory of the officer killed in the May 17 attack and the commitment of personnel who risk their lives to protect citizens oblige investigators to continue their work until everyone responsible is detained. "No permitiremos que esta agresión quede impune," he wrote in Spanish, saying the attack would not go unpunished.
