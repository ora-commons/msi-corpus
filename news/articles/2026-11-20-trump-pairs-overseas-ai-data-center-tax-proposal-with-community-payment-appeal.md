## Tech industry ramps financial incentives; Google and Meta seek transparency

WASHINGTON — President Donald Trump said Friday that U.S. technology companies will face “a tremendous tax” if they build artificial intelligence data centers in other countries. The Republican president did not describe the tax’s terms and said he had not yet told his own officials about the plan. The proposal came after Democrats took control of Congress in midterm elections in which opposition to data centers became a potent issue.

Trump paired the proposal with an appeal aimed at communities that host the facilities. Trump said: “You ought to take advantage of the data centers. Make them pay you, make them pay your community, but don’t shut them off.” The appeal repeated a point he made before the midterms, when he urged Americans to welcome data-center construction and extract concessions from developers.

The political environment shifted after Trump’s earlier appeal. Anti-data-center sentiment was a potent issue in the midterm elections, which ended with Democrats taking control of Congress. The elections also brought a wave of Democratic candidates promising tighter restrictions on data centers with high energy demands.

The Associated Press reported that a tougher regulatory environment for AI giants and data-center developers is expected even if Trump stalls federal regulatory restrictions. The wire service described Trump as “perhaps the industry’s biggest champion” and reported that, despite Trump’s backing, the data centers powering the artificial intelligence boom had become “politically toxic.”

Host communities across the United States have opposed large data centers. In response, America’s technology giants are promising to take fewer taxpayer handouts, keep fewer secrets or provide more money to communities willing to permit the facilities. The Associated Press described the companies behind the new round of promises as the world’s richest companies.

The tech industry says it has gotten the message. In addition to ramping up financial incentives, officials with Google and Meta said in interviews with The Associated Press that their companies are trying to address mistrust by being more transparent with communities where they would like to build.

America’s technology giants are also pledging to reduce their impact on the environment, offering assurances that data centers will boost local property values and maintaining that artificial intelligence is a positive force for the American economy despite its skeptics.

The Trump administration’s actions on the industry extend beyond where the buildings are located. On Monday, the administration issued new rules restricting remote access to advanced U.S. computing chips and servers. The Associated Press described the rules as the latest step to limit China’s ability to use U.S. chips to train its best artificial intelligence models.

Trump’s proposal would make overseas construction costly for U.S. technology companies, while his message to domestic host communities urged those communities to seek payments from developers. He did not describe the tax, and he had not yet told his own officials about the plan. America’s technology giants’ pledges came amid continued skepticism about data-center projects.

His latest proposal adds an unspecified tax for U.S. technology companies that build AI data centers in other countries.
