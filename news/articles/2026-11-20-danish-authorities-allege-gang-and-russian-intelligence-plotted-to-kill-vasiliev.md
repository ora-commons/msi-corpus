## Russian embassy rejection follows arrests of two men near Copenhagen venue

Danish security services accused Russian intelligence and Loyal to Familia of plotting to kill Albert Vasiliev, known under his stage name Kyivstoner, on Danish soil. The security services said Loyal to Familia, a Danish street gang banned since 2018, orchestrated the foiled attempt. The Russian Embassy in Copenhagen called the Danish allegations baseless and said the notion that Russia would assassinate “a musician with limited political influence and questionable fame” was “absurd.”

Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen said: “We know the Russian intelligence services are behind this. We are absolutely certain.” He said: “This is an outrageous and completely unacceptable violation of our borders, and it demands a firm response.” Rasmussen said Denmark would not expel Russia’s ambassador, a step intended to preserve some diplomatic contact. He instead summoned the envoy and ordered the defense attaché expelled within seven days. Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova said Moscow would give an appropriate response to Denmark’s decision without elaborating, according to state news outlet TASS.

Danish authorities said two men arrested in late September were in a car carrying weapons near the venue where Vasiliev was scheduled to perform, a century-old inn known for hosting weddings and conferences in a northern suburb of Copenhagen. According to Danish public broadcaster DR, the judge who ordered the accused men to remain in custody said one had purchased tickets to Vasiliev’s concert three weeks before the performance was due to take place in late September. The plotters appeared to know at least who their target was.

The authorities did not provide details about how the alleged collaboration between Russian intelligence and the gang’s criminal members occurred. European security services said such schemes often involve a foreign state using gangs as middlemen to hire perpetrators for a fee.

National Commissioner of the Danish Police Lasse Boje said at a press conference that the would-be assassins were likely directed by some gang leaders based abroad, including in countries such as Turkey or Morocco. He warned that Danish authorities would take stronger action against criminal gangs that assist foreign intelligence services. “When someone offers their services to foreign intelligence services, they cross a red line,” Boje said.

Justice Minister Nikolaj Wammen said the Danish government would propose harsher punishments for crimes committed on behalf of a foreign state.

Rasmussen also said Denmark would tighten visa rules for Russians. He proposed to his European colleagues that they stop issuing Schengen visas, which allow free travel throughout the European Union, to Russian citizens, noting that Denmark issued only 173 visas to Russians last year. “But when we look across Europe, a huge number of tourist visas are still being issued. And the challenge is that once someone has received a Schengen visa, they can travel freely throughout most of Europe, including to Denmark, and that is of course totally unsustainable,” Rasmussen said.

Vasiliev is a 34-year-old rapper, comedian and streamer of modest fame. His most popular songs have garnered 1.2 million streams on Spotify and 31 million views on YouTube. Russia had previously accused him of terrorism.

In July, Russia’s Federal Security Service accused Vasiliev of plotting an attack against a defense enterprise in the Moscow region under the direction of Ukraine’s security services, using drones smuggled from Slovakia. The FSB also accused him of trafficking cocaine. Vasiliev denied the accusations.

Vasiliev appears to have been the target of at least one other recently averted plot. In August, Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk announced the arrest of a Russian man accused of planning to kill a U.S.-Ukrainian dual citizen in Warsaw. Polish news outlet RMF24 identified the arrested man as Vasiliev. Danish police said they were aware of Vasiliev’s safety as soon as he entered Denmark.

Vasiliev was Ukrainian-born, attended college in the U.S. and received American dual citizenship. He later cited that citizenship as his reason for not joining Ukraine’s armed forces after online compatriots criticized him for avoiding the fight against Russia’s invasion. He also spent at least some time in the United States after Russia’s 2022 invasion.

The Wall Street Journal reported that online recruitment of criminals to attack enemies or conduct sabotage such as arson had become an increasingly popular tactic, particularly for Russia and Iran. Europol created a task force last year to address what the agency described as the growing trend of “outsourcing violent acts to criminal service providers.” Crime analysts said such attacks often involve young perpetrators working for a fee without necessarily knowing the identity of their target or employer.

The Wall Street Journal characterized the plot as the latest example of a government accusing Russia of engaging proxies to commit a crime on European soil. Western officials said Russia had increased attacks involving arson, sabotage and drone assaults. Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen said: “It is a clear illustration of the grave security situation Denmark and Europe find ourselves in.” She added: “We are facing a Russia under pressure which is acting more aggressively and is more willing to take risks.”
