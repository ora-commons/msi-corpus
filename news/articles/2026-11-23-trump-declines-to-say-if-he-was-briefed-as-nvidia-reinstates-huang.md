## Nvidia cites government input, says Huang's return poses no national security risk

President Donald Trump declined on Monday to say whether he had been briefed on U.S. intelligence reports that the woman who had an affair with Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang was in contact with a top official at China's top spy agency. The phone call with PBS News came shortly after 8 a.m. EST, and less than an hour later Nvidia said it had reinstated Huang as president and CEO and a member of its board.

Trump did not deny that he had been briefed. Liz Landers of PBS News asked whether he had been briefed on a New York Times story about the woman's contact with an official of China's top spy agency. Trump said, "I am not going to comment on anything having to do with that. You'll have to figure that out for yourself."

Asked a second time about reporting that he had been briefed on the matter on at least two occasions, Trump said, "I don't want to comment on it now, I'm sorry. I won't comment on anything having to do with the story."

The call ended as Landers attempted to ask about Democratic oversight.

The New York Times reported Sunday that Alice Huang, a Chinese woman who had a secret affair with Jensen Huang, had been in contact with a top official from the China Ministry of State Security from spring 2025 until the fall of this year. PBS News characterized her as the alleged Chinese spy, and the reporting raised questions about national security risk at Nvidia.

Trump nonetheless signaled that Huang would return. He said Huang was "back where he should be, and we see he did absolutely nothing wrong from that standpoint," and added that Huang had "certainly learned a lesson." Trump said he hoped the board would reinstate Huang, calling him "a very talented guy" and saying he hoped Huang would get back in and continue running the company.

Less than an hour after Trump's call, Nvidia announced that Huang had been reinstated as president and CEO, as well as a member of the board. In a statement, the company said: "In the process of reaching its decision, the Board received input from senior levels of the United States government, and based on that input, the Board is confident that Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk."

Trump later wrote on his Truth Social platform: "Congratulations to NVIDIA on having made, what I consider to be, an absolutely GREAT decision!"

Later in the morning, Trump told CNBC that he had spoken with Huang directly on Sunday. The board asked for assurance about threats to national security, Trump said. "And we gave them assurances at least with everything we know, and we know a lot," he said.

PBS News reported that Huang had admitted to multiple affairs with female employees. Nvidia, the chip designer, said its board's conclusion rested in part on input from senior levels of the United States government.

Trump's refusal to answer left the question of what he had been briefed on, and on how many occasions, unanswered. Nvidia's announcement said that its board received input from senior levels of the U.S. government and was confident that Huang's reinstatement posed no national security risk.
