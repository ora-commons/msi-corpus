## CJNG succession avoids the factional fighting seen in Sinaloa

Mexican authorities have arrested Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as “El Chapo Isidro,” over the weekend with U.S. intelligence support, adding to a string of actions against the Sinaloa Cartel and the Jalisco New Generation Cartel as both groups reorganize after losing senior figures. Mexican and U.S. officials hailed the capture as a security win that may shift the balance within Sinaloa, but violence continues among rival criminal groups across Mexico. The two organizations continue to dominate Mexico’s criminal landscape. Experts say they still retain strong power in Mexico and abroad despite U.S. designations of both as foreign terrorist organizations.

Meza Flores led his own criminal group, which was heavily involved in drug trafficking and maintained a tight grip on northern Sinaloa while also having a presence in southern Mexico. Authorities said the group first allied with “Los Chapitos” and later with Zambada’s followers. Sinaloa journalist Ismael Bojórquez said Meza Flores was known for his ability to corrupt local authorities, meaning his arrest could provide clues about politicians’ links to the cartel.

The latest arrest landed amid a long internal struggle in Sinaloa. In late July 2024, two months before President Claudia Sheinbaum took office, one of the Sinaloa Cartel’s founders, Ismael “El Mayo” Zambada, was arrested in the United States after an airplane kidnapping orchestrated by “Los Chapitos,” the sons of his former partner, Joaquín “El Chapo” Guzmán. The Sinaloa Cartel, a confederation of criminal groups, soon split, and fighting between “Los Mayos” and “Los Chapitos” sent violence soaring across the state and placed intense pressure on Sheinbaum.

Sheinbaum’s government reinforced its military presence in Sinaloa several times. About 23% of its troops are now deployed in the state, and security forces have arrested hundreds of people from both factions. Miguel Calderón of the State Public Security Council, a Sinaloa citizens’ group, said the criminal groups offset those arrests by recruiting increasingly younger people.

Washington increased its pressure after accusing ruling-party officials in Sinaloa of ties to drug trafficking and calling for their arrests. Sheinbaum refused. Zambada is serving a life sentence in the United States, and two of his sons are cooperating with U.S. authorities. Mexico accused Washington of interference in the Zambada case, while U.S. officials denied it.

The Jalisco New Generation Cartel followed a different course. A year and a half after Zambada’s arrest, the Mexican army killed Nemesio Oseguera Cervantes, “El Mencho,” leader of the Jalisco New Generation Cartel. His death set off a violent response across 20 Mexican states that left more than 70 people dead, though the fighting lasted only a few days. The United States celebrated both operations.

The Jalisco New Generation Cartel, which has a pyramidal command structure, underwent a leadership transition without the internal fighting seen in Sinaloa, allowing it to benefit from its rival’s weakness and continue its business despite pressure from authorities, security analyst David Saucedo said. For a time, there were fears that two potential successors would clash. Two months after Oseguera Cervantes was killed, Mexico arrested one of them, Audias Flores Silva, leaving the cartel boss’ Mexican-American stepson, Juan Carlos González Valencia, as leader. The United States is offering a $25 million reward for González Valencia.

Mexico continued arresting regional leaders, including members of a cell responsible for extensive violence in Michoacán; Saucedo said those arrests indirectly strengthened González Valencia. Among the figures now most wanted by both countries are Julio Alberto Castillo Rodríguez, “El Mencho’s” son-in-law; Hugo Gonzalo Mendoza Gaytán, who was linked to a ranch that exposed the cartel’s horrors; and Ricardo Ruiz Velasco, considered a member of the Jalisco New Generation Cartel’s special forces and responsible for propaganda.

The United States also is offering rewards of up to $10 million each for Iván Archivaldo and Jesús Alfredo Guzmán Salazar, two top Sinaloa Cartel figures. These “Chapitos” are described as members of a new generation of drug traffickers experimenting to produce the world’s most potent fentanyl.

Sheinbaum’s strategy marked a shift from the “hugs, not bullets” approach of her political mentor and predecessor, Andrés Manuel López Obrador, which avoided direct confrontations with cartels. Brookings Institution researcher Vanda Felbab-Brown said it was too early to know whether the strategy would succeed. The approach helped Mexico defuse U.S. President Donald Trump’s threats of military intervention, while allowing Washington to claim successes and receive nearly 100 criminals for prosecution.

Felbab-Brown welcomed U.S. efforts, working with Mexico, to begin targeting the middle operational ranks of criminal groups, particularly along the border. Meza Flores’ capture fits that approach, she said. “That's a far more effective and far more comprehensive strategy than just going after the capos,” she said.

Violence across Mexico has not come under control, Felbab-Brown said. Homicides have declined, but disappearances and extortion have not. Authorities also have failed to regain territory seized by the cartels, break the groups’ grip on businesses or politicians, or cut off the northward flow of drugs. The arrests are reshaping leadership and operational pressure while the cartels’ territorial and commercial reach remains unresolved.
