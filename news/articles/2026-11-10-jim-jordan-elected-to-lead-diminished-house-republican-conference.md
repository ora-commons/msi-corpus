## Come January, House oversight will be wielded against Jordan's party

House Republicans elected Jim Jordan on Tuesday to lead the House Republican conference, placing the Ohio congressman at the center of a party adjusting to life out of power. The conference he will lead has shrunk to one of its smallest sizes in decades. Jordan's new role comes three years after he was pushed out of the House speakership race, when his Republican colleagues repeatedly voted against his bid for the gavel after Kevin McCarthy's ouster.

The selection reverses the direction of congressional oversight. For two years, Jordan used the House Judiciary Committee to investigate the Biden administration. Come January, the other side will wield oversight, leaving Jordan and his party on the receiving end. He played a key role in Donald Trump's bid to overturn the 2020 election results and continues to defend the Trump administration. The Guardian says he is known for his far-right politics and hardball legislative tactics.

Jordan's route to the leadership post began in Ohio. He was born in Troy and raised in rural Champaign County. He won four state wrestling titles in high school and later coached wrestling at Ohio State University. He served in both chambers of the Ohio legislature before winning his congressional seat in 2006.

In Washington, Jordan co-founded the House Freedom Caucus and served as its first chairman. The Guardian describes the group as a hard-right bloc that regularly defied its own leaders. As chairman of the House Judiciary Committee from January 2023, Jordan led investigations of the Biden administration and headed a select subcommittee on the “weaponization” of the federal government.

Republicans said the panel exposed government pressure on social media companies, while Democrats dismissed it as a vehicle for conspiracy theories. Those competing accounts framed the oversight work Jordan is leaving behind as he takes command of the conference.

Jordan has also drawn criticism over his refusal of a 2022 subpoena from the committee investigating the 6 January attack on the US Capitol, whose legitimacy he challenged. The committee said Jordan spoke with Trump by phone at least twice that day and referred him to the House ethics committee. Democrats have raised the episode whenever Jordan has demanded that others comply with subpoenas.

Separately, former Ohio State wrestlers and staff members have repeatedly alleged that Jordan knew about and ignored sexual abuse by the team's doctor, Richard Strauss, while Jordan served as an assistant coach. Jordan has consistently denied the allegations, saying he never heard of any abuse and calling them politically motivated. Several former coaches and athletes have defended him.

Jordan has sponsored few bills but co-sponsored well over a thousand, and The Guardian says he is more known for his combative presence from the bench than for his legislative skill. He supports tax cuts, abortion restrictions and tougher border enforcement. The profile also describes him as a long-time defender of Trump and says oversight has been his specialty.

Jordan's leadership assignment brings the earlier disputes into a new institutional setting. The conference is one of the smallest in decades, the party is adjusting to life out of power, and the Republican investigator of the Biden administration will now face congressional scrutiny from the other side. His positions on tax cuts, abortion and border enforcement, his refusal of the 2022 committee subpoena and his denial of the Ohio State allegations form the public backdrop to the post.
