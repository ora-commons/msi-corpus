## CPR News reported at least 18 affected people in Colorado

US District Judge Charlotte Sweeney found government officials in contempt after ICE instantly re-arrested a man whom she had ordered released, according to court records in the US District Court for the District of Colorado.

In a Wednesday ruling, Sweeney wrote that “ICE’s new ‘policy’ of immediately re-detaining petitioners following release made a mockery of court orders mandating petitioners’ immediate release.” The contempt finding concerned the man whom the court had ordered released and whom officials immediately re-arrested.

Sweeney declined to take any further action after an official swore in court that officials had ceased the practice at issue. She said the officials had “cured the contempt by ceasing the practice at issue.”

CPR News, the news division of Colorado Public Radio, reported that at least 18 people in Colorado had been held in detention longer than they should have been or re-arrested within minutes of a judge’s order releasing them during the previous six weeks. CPR News said immigration attorneys estimated that the number may be higher because some immigrants represented themselves.

The practice of releasing detainees and then quickly re-detaining them has been noticeable in recent months, and legal advocates said the trend is on the rise. Some people are re-arrested days later, while others are re-arrested without leaving the detention center. According to advocates, the re-detentions often violate a federal court order.

ICE, which is part of the Department of Homeland Security, did not immediately respond to a request for comment on what the judge said on Wednesday. The Trump administration maintained that its actions aimed to improve domestic security and were in compliance with the law. Rights groups said the Trump administration’s immigration enforcement and deportation actions violated free speech and due process rights. Human rights groups also said those actions created an unsafe environment, particularly for ethnic minorities, who raised concerns about racial profiling.

The Guardian reported that Democrats, who won majorities in the House and Senate in the midterm elections, now aim to hold the Trump administration accountable for its immigration policies.
