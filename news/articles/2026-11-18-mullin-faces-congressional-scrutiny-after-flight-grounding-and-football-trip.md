## Jay Clayton's late addition could ease pressure at Wednesday hearing

Markwayne Mullin returns to Capitol Hill on Wednesday to testify with Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton about threats facing the United States. The hearing comes after Mullin tried at the White House on Friday to explain why thousands of flights were suddenly grounded and after the homeland security secretary flew to Texas the next day for a prime-time college-football game.

On Friday, while at the White House, Mullin addressed reporters about the disruption. “My understanding is that we can still communicate, it’s just at different levels and the clarity we can get to, but there’s redundancy in most communications so as you can see, there’s still planes landing,” Mullin said.

The next day, while thousands of U.S. travelers were still dealing with the effects of Friday's disruption, Mullin flew to Texas for a prime-time college-football game. The New York Post reported that he met a DHS contractor in a luxury box. Asked about the trip on CNBC on Tuesday morning, Mullin deflected and said Rep. Steve Scalise (R., La.) had arranged the ticket.

The Wall Street Journal said the past week's string of glitches had prompted people to ask what was happening and who was in charge, while conspiracy theories about the causes circulated on social media. President Trump and top members of his cabinet were in Asia during that period.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the root cause of the flight problem was still being investigated. The Wall Street Journal described the administration's posture as shifting over the span of a couple of hours from “nothing to see here” to “we’re looking into it.”

Clayton was a late addition to Wednesday's schedule and could take some pressure off Mullin by reducing his need to explain everything alone, The Wall Street Journal said. Because Mullin is a former senator, his testimony would normally take place before a friendly audience, the newspaper said, adding: “But then again, it might not be.”

The report also said the White House had not given DHS a large degree of independence and that DHS secretaries did not tend to last too long in Trump's orbit. Mullin's appearance before Congress gives him an opportunity to address the disruptions and the security threats that are part of the hearing.

The same Politics newsletter also summarized other developments in Washington. House Democrats would hold leadership elections to nominate a House speaker candidate and select a majority leader, whip, conference chair and other junior positions. Vice President JD Vance was scheduled to speak at the New York Times DealBook summit in New York. President Trump was to be in Shenzhen, China, on Wednesday after wrapping up a visit to the Philippines.

Progressive grassroots anger was driving Democrats' Senate leadership fight, the newsletter said. Activists demanded new leadership after the midterm elections and backed Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland for majority leader. Supporters of Sen. Chuck Schumer of New York saw him as the architect of the midterm wins, although some questioned his leadership style.

Democrat Mary Peltola unseated Alaska GOP Sen. Dan Sullivan, according to the Associated Press. With 99% of the vote counted, Peltola — the first Alaska Native elected to the U.S. Senate — held 50.1% to Sullivan's 45.8%. Her victory will bring Democrats' Senate majority to 56 seats when they take control of Congress next year.

President Trump's pick to lead the Navy faced a rocky reception from Republicans and Democrats during his confirmation hearing. Hung Cao revealed that the U.S. Navy had been using expired Tomahawk missiles in the Middle East because of a shortage of critical munitions. He also responded to lawmakers' questions about the living conditions and mental health of sailors serving extended deployments.

Wednesday's testimony will place Mullin before Congress alongside Clayton as Duffy continues to investigate the grounding. Questions about the government's communications and the timing of the Texas trip are likely to remain central.
