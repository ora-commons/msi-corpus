## ANZ points to vessel attacks and increased missile and drone activity

Front-month WTI crude oil futures rose 0.2% to $80.31 a barrel in early Asian trading on Nov. 24, 2026. The gain followed an overnight settlement at the contracts’ lowest level since August, and the market report described the move as a likely technical recovery.

ANZ Research analysts said oil futures might also be supported by signs of increasing hostilities in the Middle East that could lead to supply disruptions.

ANZ said Iran continued to attack vessels in the Strait of Hormuz. The analysts also relayed President Trump’s announcement that Iran had launched more drones and missiles in the previous week than at any time over the year.

For oil futures, the relevant distinction was between a potential supply risk and an actual interruption. ANZ’s note did not report a supply loss; it said the increase in hostilities could lead to disruptions. The market report attributed the 0.2% rise to a likely technical recovery, with ANZ identifying signs of increasing hostilities as possible additional support for futures.

In a separate sovereign-credit assessment, S&P Global Ratings said Saudi Arabia’s ability to redirect oil exports through the Red Sea and its growing non-oil economy were helping protect the country’s creditworthiness despite the Middle East conflict. Lead sovereign analyst Ravi Bhatia said the kingdom’s East-West pipeline, which carries crude to the Red Sea port of Yanbu, had helped it avoid some disruptions around the Strait of Hormuz.

Higher oil prices had also supported Saudi government revenue, Bhatia said. Speaking on S&P’s Middle East Credit Gateway video series, Bhatia said the agency expected regional tensions to persist into 2027 but remain largely contained. S&P affirmed Saudi Arabia’s A+ rating with a stable outlook in September.

Taken together, the market notes showed both a small technical recovery in futures and an ongoing threat to shipping. Saudi export infrastructure offered a partial buffer, but ANZ’s assessment still centered on continued vessel attacks and the possibility of supply disruption.


MSI [previously reported on Aug. 18](/articles/2026-08-18-oil-settles-higher-as-us-and-iran-both-claim-hormuz-control/) that the United States and Iran had both claimed control of the Strait of Hormuz while shipping was limited and oil prices had risen. By Nov. 24, ANZ analysts were again citing attacks on vessels in the waterway, showing that the shipping risk from the earlier confrontation remained unresolved as crude futures recovered from their lowest overnight settlement since August.
