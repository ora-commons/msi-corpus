## UK energy price cap expected to rise 26% in January

European wholesale gas prices rose on Monday as a forecast cold snap for the coming weeks across north-west Europe fed concern about depleted supplies and expensive household bills this winter. The Dutch month-ahead contract, the European benchmark for gas prices over the next four weeks, climbed as much as 8.6% to €96.08 before settling 4% higher at €92.50, The Guardian reported. Much of north-west Europe is bracing for a tough winter, when energy demand would be at its highest.

European gas storage stood at about 69% full against a typical level of 80%, well below the seasonal average. The Guardian linked the price move to fears that a cold snap would arrive while Europe's inventories were already depleted.

In the UK, energy system operators said the country will have enough gas and electricity to meet demand during the coldest months. Part of the reason, they said, is that Britain has been paying higher market prices than its neighbours to secure imports. The Guardian reported that this will leave British households probably paying some of the highest energy bills in Europe this winter.

The energy regulator Ofgem is expected to announce a 26% rise in its quarterly price cap from January this week, adding £443 to the average annual bill for a dual-fuel household. It could be one of the biggest quarterly price rises on record.

The Guardian reported that Europe's gas supplies have been hit by a slowdown in exports from Qatar due to the war in the Middle East. The outlet also cited an increase in consumption during the summer to power refrigeration and cooling during the prolonged heatwave.

The charity Citizens Advice has said it expects a wave of more people seeking emergency help. Its chief executive, Clare Moriarty, said every day its “advisers see people whose budgets have been completely wiped out by years of compounding crises.”

“Millions of households are entering the coldest months of the year with nothing left,” Moriarty said. Last month, Chancellor John Healey used his first budget to announce a £100 giveaway to 20m households in council tax bands A to D to help with energy bills.

Moriarty said the top-up was welcome but “simply won’t stretch far enough for those who need the most support.” She warned that “Many households will quickly burn through it, leaving them at risk of deeper debt or being unable to top up their prepayment meter just as winter bites.”

Moriarty called on the government to act before the cold weather takes hold. She asked for a targeted top-up to the warm home discount and an emergency support credit for prepayment customers, describing the measures as “vital, practical lifelines” that would “stop households being left in the cold and dark this winter.”

Chris O’Shea, the boss of British Gas owner Centrica, has also said he is “hugely concerned” about the forecast jump in energy bills. He said the number of people unable or unwilling to pay their bills would rise in the new year.

Speaking to the BBC on Sunday, O’Shea said there is a “massive problem” with people not paying their bills in the UK, with some without the means to do so and an “increasing number of people that are choosing not to pay their bills.”
