## Intel forecast a prolonged PC slowdown alongside disappointing Q3 results

Intel shares tumbled 28.6% on Oct. 30, wiping out about $140 billion of market value, the day after the chip maker reported disappointing third-quarter results and forecast a prolonged slowdown in PC sales. The weak results were compounded by production challenges in its server chip business and weaker-than-expected fourth-quarter revenue and earnings guidance.

An Intel spokeswoman declined to comment. Intel shares were quoted down 6.07% in the report.

Chief Executive Lip-Bu Tan said on a call with analysts following the results that he was disappointed by manufacturing challenges, supply shortages, an "unprecedented memory crunch," and resulting softness in PC demand. "We need to address these challenges with urgency," he said at the time.

Intel's stock had more than doubled in the year leading up to its third-quarter results, due in part to the next wave of artificial-intelligence technology—AI agents completing tasks for users—which relies on the central processing units, or CPUs, that are the company's specialty.

However, huge demand for AI servers in data centers is causing a shortage in memory chips, made by other companies, that are crucial components inside PCs. This has sent memory-chip prices higher and forced PC makers to raise prices, reducing sales to consumers.

Shareholders are now suing Intel, claiming the U.S. chip maker hid the scale of a slump in personal-computer sales that contributed to shares plunging nearly 29% in a single day last month.

A proposed class action filed on Friday by Robbins Geller Rudman & Dowd claims Intel concealed the impact of a slowdown in PC sales and inventory glut on its revenue and profitability. The lawsuit also says that Intel failed to disclose that supply constraints limited production to meet data-center demand and that any positive impact from artificial intelligence and data-center demand was more than offset by the PC slowdown and inventory buildup.

"As a result of the above, defendants' statements regarding Intel's business, operations, financial results, and ability to capitalize on the boom for AI-related infrastructure spending were materially false and misleading when made," the law firm said in a statement released in conjunction with the filing.

Friday's lawsuit seeks to represent shareholders who owned Intel common stock between July 23 and Oct. 29 this year.
