## U.S. output reached 43% of global supply; Qatar and Russia face export limits

## Domestic and foreign helium supply

The U.S. Energy Department designated helium as a critical material on Friday, the 20th critical material identified by DOE. The department cited helium's indispensable role in making semiconductors essential to energy generation and said global supply faced a risk of disruption. The designation makes more federal financing available to helium producers and brings helium under closer government monitoring.

The United States is the world's largest producer of both natural gas and helium, and helium is primarily produced as a byproduct of natural gas processing. The U.S. produced 81 million cubic meters of helium in 2025, or 43% of global supply, and exported 38 million cubic meters of that output. U.S. production has run at roughly 80 million cubic meters a year since 2021.

Qatar and Russia are the other top helium producers. Exports of Qatar's helium supply have been severely curtailed by Iran's closure of the Strait of Hormuz as a result of its war with Israel and the United States. Iranian strikes on Qatar's infrastructure have also affected supply, while Russia imposed temporary export controls on helium in April to protect its domestic supply.

Large helium reserves sit in Wyoming, Kansas, Texas, Oklahoma, Colorado and Utah. Though the United States has abundant resources, helium extraction plants are at their limits, and it takes years to permit and build new ones, which continues to constrain U.S. production, according to DOE. Federal data from 2023 showed the plants extracting the most helium were in Kansas, Texas, Colorado, New Mexico, Arizona and Oklahoma.

## Financing, monitoring and list criteria

Helium is the only gas on the critical materials list besides fluorine. The last material added before helium was metallurgical coal, used for steelmaking, in 2025. To make the list, a non-fuel mineral element or substance must serve an essential function in one or more energy technologies and face a high risk of disruption to the supply chain.

The designation does not put more helium on the market by itself. It does open federal financing and closer monitoring for a material whose domestic output is capped by plant capacity and permitting timelines, at a moment when the two other top producers, Qatar and Russia, both face export limits.

## Semiconductor uses and industry response

Energy Secretary Chris Wright said the Trump administration is freeing America from reliance on foreign sources of critical materials, strengthening domestic manufacturing, supporting American jobs and securing materials essential to U.S. economic and national security. In a statement, Wright said, "President Trump is bringing back America’s manufacturing industry, and that means making sure we have the materials needed to build things in America again."

Assistant Secretary of Energy Audrey Robertson, who oversees the Office of Critical Minerals and Energy Innovation, said helium's unique properties make it irreplaceable. The gas is non-combustible and has the lowest boiling point of any gas, Robertson said, and the nation needs an ample supply for semiconductors, quantum and super computing, and superconducting magnets. "We need to make sure that we can build those here. And helium is one of those components that could be a single point of failure, where we cannot let that happen. That’s why we’re designating it within our statutory authority, but I think why it’s important is not necessarily for our birthday parties," Robertson said in an interview. "It’s really towards the future of American competitiveness and energy security, which we believe is national security."

The Energy Department said helium makes balloons rise, but that is not why DOE is interested in it. The gas plays an important role in wind, solar and nuclear energy production, space exploration, medical imaging and research.

David Isaacs, vice president of the Semiconductor Industry Association trade group, said helium plays an essential role in the chip industry's cooling, photolithography, leak detection and more. "We look forward to continuing to work with government and industry leaders in the U.S. and across the world to strengthen semiconductor supply chain resilience," Isaacs said.

Some in the helium industry have urged the U.S. government to formally designate helium as a critical resource. The North American gas exploration company Avanti Helium Corp. said formal designation was urgent and that an increase in North American supply was needed to avoid becoming dependent on foreign sources. "We are very pleased to have helium recognized as a critical material," Avanti Helium CEO Chris Bakker said. "The U.S. has the potential to remain self-sufficient in helium. However, this will require investment in exploration for new reserves and inclusion on the critical materials list will bring significant support to these efforts."

## Federal history and environmental context

The United States designates both critical minerals and critical materials, with some crossover between the two. The U.S. Geological Survey included helium on its 2018 list of critical minerals, but later lists omitted it because the domestic supply of helium was considered secure.

The federal helium stockpile was privatized. There used to be a strategic national stockpile in Texas for helium, much like America's Strategic Petroleum Reserve. Congress mandated in 2013 that it be privatized, and the Bureau of Land Management completed the sale of the federal helium system to the industrial gas company Messer in 2024.

Recovering helium from natural gas carries environmental concerns, but Greeshma Gadikota, director of the Lenfast Center for Sustainable Energy at Columbia University, said there are also opportunities. "If we burn the natural gas, there are a lot of CO2 emissions associated with that," Gadikota said. "But if you just extract the helium and use it, the uses of it don’t contribute necessarily to greenhouse gas emissions."

"So from a climate standpoint, if we are going to continue down the line of extracting natural gas and recovering helium," she added, "I would prefer to see natural gas being used in a form where we directly produce hydrogen and we produce solid carbon." Hydrogen is a clean-burning fuel, and solid carbon can provide permanent carbon sequestration and can be used widely in industrial applications, including rubber and pigments.
