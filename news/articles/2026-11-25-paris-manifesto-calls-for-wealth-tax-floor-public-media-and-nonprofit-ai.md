## Signatories frame extreme wealth and AI deployment as democratic risks

An open letter published by The Guardian on Nov. 25, 2026, calls on people around the world to join a transnational coalition against what its authors describe as an international oligarchy. Initiated by Gabriel Zucman and titled the Paris manifesto, the letter says the group should defend democracy by changing how the ultra-rich are taxed, how information and media are controlled and how AI is developed.

The published signatory list includes Daron Acemoglu, Esther Duflo, Nancy Fraser, Thomas Piketty and Joseph Stiglitz. The manifesto states that Gabriel Zucman initiated the effort.

The document opens its case with figures from Forbes rankings that began in 1987. It says the wealth of the richest 0.0001% of the planet's population — just 3,000 households — equaled 3% of global GDP then and has since reached 17%. The authors describe that concentration as the defining social fact of their time.

The manifesto says this concentration leaves choices affecting humanity to a few billionaires at the helm of a handful of corporations. It also says economic power is being used to consolidate political power, while a controlling shareholder's decision can wipe away jobs, deplete resources or determine the course of war. Across borders, the authors say, wealthy interests can disrupt work and home lives, inject ideas into public discourse, bend policy, siphon revenue from states and damage the earth.

The first of the manifesto's three principles is that the ultra-rich should pay taxes in proportion to their resources. The document says tax loopholes allow the wealthiest to pay little or no income tax and proposes an annual floor equal to 2% of personal wealth for fortunes above $100 million.

That idea was first developed in a report commissioned by the G20 in 2024 during Brazil's presidency, according to the manifesto. The report proposed the 2% floor for fortunes above $100 million. The signatories say the measure would ensure the ultra-rich pay at least as large a share of income in tax as the middle class, calling that the bare minimum required by tax fairness.

The manifesto pairs the annual floor with a one-time “catch-up” tax on the wealth of the super-rich to settle outstanding tax inequalities. It says the levy should account for taxes paid during the covered period, meaning only ultra-rich individuals who had avoided meeting their fiscal obligations would be affected.

Information is the manifesto's second concern. It says information is not just another commodity and that oligarchic control over information must be curtailed. The purchase of media outlets and social platforms gives the ultra-rich control over what is said, heard, learned and read, according to the letter, which calls for stronger public media and anti-concentration policies on an international scale.

The manifesto also argues that knowledge, artistic creation and works of imagination assembled over centuries are being captured, processed and repackaged, often without permission, by a handful of corporations and resold as AI to the humanity that produced them. Calling the process a hijacking, the letter says there is still time to refuse it.

On deployment, the manifesto lists the possibility of cyberattacks with lethal consequences, rapid and profound transformations of the labor market, new forms of manipulation and disinformation, and even human extinction. It says leaving decisions about AI deployment to a few Silicon Valley billionaires would be folly.

The letter points to Wikipedia, Signal and Firefox as proof that nonprofit organizations can build widely used applications and websites. It says companies working in AI should consider adopting the nonprofit foundation model, with deployment decisions piloted by workers. The manifesto says workers have repeatedly stepped down from their posts to warn of looming AI risks.

At the close, the authors call for a transnational coalition of interconnected citizens to defend democracy. They write: “It is possible for democracy to reverse the dynamic of oligarchy.” The final appeal says: “Now is the time to reorient our trajectory. We hope you will join us.”
