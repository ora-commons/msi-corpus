## New Start's expiration leaves no binding limits on U.S. and Russian arsenals

Donald Trump, Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping met in southern China last week, where Trump said they would “talk about nuclear weapons and denuclearization,” Sen. Edward J Markey wrote in an opinion article in The Guardian. Markey said the United States should rebuild the arms-control architecture it constructed with Russia over the last half century, restore limits after the New Start Treaty expired and include China in the process. His proposal would immediately freeze U.S. and Russian nuclear arsenals while Washington and Moscow negotiated a successor agreement.

Markey described the gathering as the first time the leaders of the world’s three largest nuclear powers had come together to discuss the future of nuclear weapons. He called the discussion overdue but characterized the meeting as “all pomp and little pop.” Markey said Trump’s talk about “denuclearization” was not a strategy: “talk is not a strategy.” Markey represents Massachusetts in the US Senate and is co-chair of the bicameral congressional nuclear weapons and arms control working group.

Markey argued that nuclear weapons remain the greatest immediate threat to the security of the United States, Russia, China and the entire world. He said the danger had not diminished as risks from artificial intelligence, the climate emergency and geopolitical instability grew. Markey linked the move toward a dangerous new arms race to eroded arms-control agreements and the modernization and expansion of nuclear arsenals.

He called for rebuilding the arms-control architecture that the United States and Russia spent half a century constructing. Markey said treaties negotiated during and after the Cold War helped prevent nuclear confrontation and reduced the two countries’ nuclear arsenals by roughly 88%. He said those agreements showed that adversaries could find common ground when the stakes were existential.

The New Start Treaty was the last remaining agreement limiting the strategic nuclear arsenals of the United States and Russia, and it expired earlier this year without a replacement, Markey said. He said no legally binding limits now exist on the size of U.S. and Russian nuclear arsenals, while China has never been subject to comparable restrictions. Markey described the result as a growing risk of a three-way nuclear arms race that would leave the three countries and the world less safe.

Markey said China is expanding its nuclear arsenal, which has roughly 600 weapons, compared with approximately 4,000 in the arsenals of both the United States and Russia. He called demands that Beijing make reductions on the same terms as Washington and Moscow unrealistic. During Trump’s first administration, Trump tried to bring China into bilateral nuclear arms-control negotiations, but the effort failed after Beijing pointed out that its arsenal was much smaller than those of the other two countries.

Markey also rejected waiting for Putin to move first. He said Putin has increasingly relied on nuclear threats to discourage other countries from supporting Ukraine and advance broader strategic objectives. Even if Putin resists reductions, Markey said, that would not mean Russia wants to spend the next decade locked in another costly nuclear arms race.

Shortly before New Start expired, Moscow proposed that the United States and Russia voluntarily maintain their existing limits on strategic nuclear weapons while negotiating a successor agreement. Markey said Trump rejected the proposal, calling it a missed opportunity. He said Trump should put the proposal back on the table and immediately agree with Putin to freeze U.S. and Russian nuclear arsenals while negotiating a new arms-control agreement to replace New Start.

Markey went further and proposed that a new agreement reduce American and Russian nuclear arsenals to 1,000 weapons each, counting strategic long-range and tactical shorter-range weapons. He said the reductions would lower the risk of nuclear war, rebuild a measure of trust between Washington and Moscow and save taxpayers billions of dollars otherwise spent building and maintaining “weapons we do not need.”

Markey said a serious American-Russian agreement would create the conditions for China to join the process. He said Beijing should commit to refraining from further significant increases in its nuclear arsenal as long as Washington and Moscow make real progress toward substantial reductions. His stated objective was to bring American and Russian arsenals down toward China’s level, not drive China to build up toward the American and Russian level.

Markey rejected arguments that the United States must maintain nuclear superiority over China and should never reduce its arsenal unless Beijing agrees to comparable reductions. He said that approach mistakes nuclear superiority for nuclear security. Markey said history demonstrates that arms control works when countries negotiate from a position of mutual security and seek stability rather than endlessly pursuing an advantage over one another.

Markey said a new nuclear arms race among the United States, Russia and China would serve nobody’s interests. He said it would waste enormous sums of public money, accelerate environmental damage, undermine international security and increase the possibility that a crisis or miscalculation could end in nuclear catastrophe. He called for leadership, diplomacy and a clear commitment to a safer future. “If Trump is serious about denuclearization, moving forward, he now has his chance to prove it,” Markey wrote.
