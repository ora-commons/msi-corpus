## National Security Act allows detention without charge for up to 14 days

The US Army soldier arrested on suspicion of spying for Russia was based at Imjin Barracks in Innsworth, Gloucestershire, the BBC understands. The barracks serve as the headquarters of NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps, a strategic operational base for the alliance, according to the BBC.

Counter Terrorism Policing London said the man had been arrested near Gloucester on Tuesday under section one and section three of the National Security Act 2023. Section one of the Act relates to obtaining or disclosing protected information, while section three relates to assisting a foreign intelligence service. The unnamed soldier is currently in custody at a London police station. The Act allows police to hold him without charge for up to 14 days.

Counter Terrorism Policing said the arrest was not linked to any other ongoing investigations. As part of the investigation, officers are carrying out searches at two locations in Gloucestershire.

FBI director Kash Patel said in a statement: "This important arrest involves allegations of espionage, which is of the utmost concern to the United States and our allies."

"While this investigation is continuing, I want to send a clear message – this FBI has no tolerance for anyone who breaks their oath to the United States and whose actions endanger our national security and that of our partners," Patel added.

Deputy Assistant Commissioner Vicki Evans, from Counter Terrorism Policing London, described the arrest as "significant". "The allegations are serious and our investigation is very much ongoing," she said.

Brig Gen Sean Stinchon, commanding general of US Army Counterintelligence Command, said: "The alleged conduct of a senior soldier, entrusted with sensitive information, is a sobering reminder that insider threats are real and persistent."

Stinchon also said: "I encourage anyone who thinks they've witnessed something suspicious to report it to their local counterintelligence office."

Additional reporting was provided by Daniel Sandford, the BBC's UK correspondent.
