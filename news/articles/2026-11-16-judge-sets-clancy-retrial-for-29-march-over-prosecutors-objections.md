## Prosecutors sought May date; judge rejected dismissal bid a week earlier

A Massachusetts judge on Monday, 16 November 2026, scheduled Lindsay Clancy's second trial in the killings of her three young children for late March, over the objections of prosecutors. Clancy was charged with three counts of first-degree murder.

Prosecutors had sought a May trial date, citing scheduling conflicts, but superior court judge William Sullivan set the retrial for 29 March in Plymouth superior court.

Clancy's first trial on charges of first-degree murder ended in a mistrial after jurors were unable to reach a unanimous verdict on whether she was criminally responsible for the killings. Eleven jurors favored acquittal on the grounds of insanity, while one disagreed.

The decision to schedule Clancy's second trial came a week after Sullivan rejected a defense request to dismiss the charges. He ruled that a retrial would not violate Clancy's constitutional protection against double jeopardy. Clancy's lawyers had argued that the judge should have investigated or removed the lone juror who opposed acquittal before declaring a mistrial.

A former labor and delivery nurse, Clancy has never disputed that she killed her three children at the family's Duxbury home in January 2023. Her attorneys said she was suffering from severe mental illness at the time. Prosecutors argued Clancy planned the killings and understood what she was doing.

Attorneys are scheduled to return to court at 9am on 30 December to discuss trial logistics, security and media arrangements. A pretrial conference is set for 18 February.
