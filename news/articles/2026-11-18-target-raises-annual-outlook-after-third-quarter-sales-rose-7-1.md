## Target raises annual outlook after third-quarter sales rose 7.1%

**Subtype:** fact

- Target raised its full-year sales and earnings outlook after third-quarter net sales increased 7.1%.
- Target reported quarterly net sales of $27.07 billion, above FactSet's $26.5 billion expectation.
- Target said digital sales from channels operating for at least 12 months increased 9.8%.
- Target reported comparable sales at established stores rose 4.6% for the quarter ended Oct. 31.
- Target said November net sales so far were in line with its full-year outlook.

## Digital sales rose 9.8% as established-store comparable sales gained 4.6%

Target reported sales growth across its digital and established-store operations in the third quarter. Sales made through digital channels operating for at least 12 months increased 9.8% from a year earlier, while comparable sales at established stores rose 4.6%. Target also posted comparable-sales growth for a third straight quarter; the measure includes sales from online and physical stores. The three-month period ended Oct. 31 overlapped with the back-to-school shopping season.

Target raised its annual sales and earnings guidance again on Wednesday after net sales increased 7.1%, the company's strongest net-sales performance since the fourth quarter of 2021. The company said November net sales so far were in line with its full-year outlook.

Net sales rose 7.1% to $27.07 billion, compared with $25.27 billion in the year-ago period. Target said it earned $1.21 billion, or $2.65 per share, including a 50-cent benefit from a government tariff refund. In the third quarter of 2025, the company reported $689 million, or $1.51 per share. Analysts expected $2.08 per share on net sales of $26.5 billion, according to FactSet. Shares were essentially flat before the opening bell.

Together, comparable-store and digital sales in August, September and October increased 5.6%, compared with a 2.7% drop in the year-ago period. Target also reported a 5.6% gain in the first quarter and a 3.8% uptick in the second quarter of this year.

Target, the Minneapolis retailer, reported growth in a majority of its six core merchandise categories, led by electronics, gaming equipment, sporting goods, toys, trading cards and collectibles. The company referred to trading cards and collectibles as “Fun 101,” and it also cited higher sales of beauty products, food and beverages.

CEO Michael Fiddelke, a 20-year company veteran who became chief executive in February, told reporters this week that sales momentum was not limited to certain categories or based on any one initiative. He said Target had seen business pick up across its markets among shoppers of all incomes. “It’s the cumulative impact of changes across our business that is driving this guest preference for Target,” Fiddelke said.

Target's strong quarterly gains were fueled by a revitalized product lineup, expanded same-day deliveries and price cuts that brought in shoppers pressured by inflation. In March, Fiddelke unveiled a multibillion-dollar investment plan aimed at reversing a post-pandemic sales slump. The overhaul included stocking products and brands intended to help restore Target's reputation as a place to go for affordable yet stylish items.

Target launched a new Target Beauty Studio concept in more than 600 locations in September. The new area, which features upscale beauty products and staff trained to offer advice, partly replaced Ulta shops that had operated in some stores before Target and Ulta Beauty ended their partnership in August. Target executives said work to improve the retailer's clothing and home-goods offerings was ongoing.

With U.S. consumers expressing concerns about the economy and their own finances, Target promoted price reductions this year, including cuts on nearly 2,000 additional items during the third quarter. The company said most of the new items in its holiday shopping lineup cost $20 or less, including thousands priced at $5 and under.

Target was one of the first big retailers to report third-quarter earnings. The results could give industry analysts and economists another read on how U.S. consumers responded to ongoing inflation pressure before the end-of-year holidays.

Target now expects full-year net sales to rise roughly 6%, one percentage point above the guidance it gave in August. It projects full-year earnings per share of $10.65 to $11.40, including a $2.14 tariff-refund benefit; in August, the company expected earnings of $9.90 to $10.90 per share. Analysts projected profits of $10.25 per share on sales of $110.27 billion.
