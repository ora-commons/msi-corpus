## Reported DOJ probe followed DHS inspector general referral

The Washington Sun reported this week that some open-records requests from DHS were denied during Corey Lewandowski’s tenure because of an ongoing criminal probe. The Associated Press report that included the Washington Sun account also covered a Justice Department inquiry into allegations that Lewandowski profited from his past role as a DHS adviser.

The Wall Street Journal reported that the probe was launched after a referral from the DHS inspector general. The referral included details about Lewandowski’s time at the agency. Associated Press reporting said the details of the allegations against Lewandowski were not yet clear.

Lewandowski worked at DHS as a special government employee during Kristi Noem’s tenure as Homeland Security secretary and was a longtime ally of Trump. He was Trump’s first campaign manager in 2016 and Noem’s lead adviser while at the department. His position was supposed to include a limit on the days he worked, but The Wall Street Journal said he appeared to have played an outsized role at DHS.

Adam Trigg, Lewandowski’s attorney, wrote in a texted comment to The Associated Press: “Mr. Lewandowski has not been contacted by the Justice Department regarding any investigation, and he denies any wrongdoing when he was proudly serving his country in a voluntary capacity.”

President Donald Trump told reporters in Washington on Thursday, after media reports of the investigation had surfaced, that he had “heard something about” the Justice Department investigating allegations that Lewandowski profited from his past DHS advisory role. “I don’t know exactly what it is,” Trump told reporters. “All I know is, I want my people to be honest. And if they’re not going to be honest, they have to be prosecuted.” He added, “I’ll have my DOJ go and do whatever they have to do.”

The Associated Press characterized Trump’s response as one in which he notably did not defend Lewandowski.

The Associated Press said an investigation into a longtime Trump adviser would be a departure for the Justice Department, which has focused considerable efforts on investigating and attempting to prosecute longtime Trump political critics.

The Justice Department and Department of Homeland Security did not immediately respond to an Associated Press request for comment.
