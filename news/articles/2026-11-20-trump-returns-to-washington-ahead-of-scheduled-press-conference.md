## Trump, Xi and Putin discussed nuclear proliferation, Iran and global relations

President Trump is scheduled to hold a White House press conference at noon ET on November 20 after returning to Washington from a four-day trip to Asia. The trip included a rare three-way meeting in Shenzhen, China, with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin. Trump said he would discuss the historic meetings in China and trade agreements reached earlier in the Philippines, where he arrived for the tail end of a summit with South Asian leaders.

Trump did not formally take questions while in China at the end of his trip, though such opportunities are typically expected on presidential foreign trips. Putin held a press conference before leaving the country.

Trump, Xi and Putin, who command the world's largest nuclear arsenals, gathered at a lakeside resort in Shenzhen, China, and sat around a large round wooden table. They discussed nuclear proliferation, Iran and global relations.

In his opening remarks alongside Xi and Putin, Trump said the three leaders were making history and had a responsibility to the rest of the world to maintain open and respectful dialogue. “Too often, past leaders avoided talking to nations with whom they had disagreements. And we feel the opposite,” Trump said. “No problems were ever solved under the old school, refusing to talk about it. No peace was ever forged by declining opportunities for engagement and speech.”

NPR's analysis said displays of power were prominent, but the talks offered few signs that either Xi or Putin was prepared to make significant concessions to Trump. NPR said kind words and pats on the back were prevalent while the lack of deliverables was also noteworthy.

Suzanne Maloney, vice president and director of the foreign policy program at the Brookings Institution, said the trip offered Trump a particular kind of political presentation after an election at home. “The trip enabled the president to bask in the glow of great power amity after a really bruising election outcome here at home,” Maloney said. “He wanted the optics of this direct engagement with Presidents Xi and Putin as a sort of validation of his own standing as a historical figure. And I think the photo op provided that.”

NPR's analysis gave each leader a distinct objective. It said Xi could point to China's global leadership by bringing together the world's greatest nuclear powers to discuss global challenges. The outlet said the meeting gave Putin a chance to reestablish Russia as a “great geopolitical power” on par with China and the U.S. It said the trip offered Trump a chance to “turn the page and demonstrate his ongoing power” at a time when he had been weakened domestically after the “devastating midterm elections.”

NPR's analysis said Trump “has spent much of the last few years trying to replace the postwar rules-based order, ripping up traditional alliances in favor of a more personal approach to diplomacy.” The outlet said he prefers to work out deals personally with the world's superpowers. NPR said the visit raised questions about Trump's approach to foreign policy and may demonstrate that access does not always produce results.

NPR assessed that Xi and Putin's strategic objectives were not going to change because they were friendly with Trump. As Maloney put it, Xi and Putin also had to calculate whether agreements reached with Trump could survive domestic scrutiny or a change of administration in the coming years. “I think both Putin and Xi recognized that President Trump's time is beginning to wane,” Maloney said, “that his command of U.S. politics is also beginning to wane, and they're going to be looking to who and what may be coming next.”

NPR characterized foreign trips by heads of state as often largely about the “pomp and circumstance” involved in meetings. Trump's arrival in China included a military flyover and young dancers. NPR said the events were extensively choreographed, with aides working for months in advance to lock down agreements and commitments the leaders could cite as evidence of a successful trip. The outlet said that did not appear to be the visit's main objective.
