## Authorities advise people to stay away from Stinson Beach as waves continue

The evacuation warning for residents of beachfront properties in Stinson Beach was lifted later Wednesday. Marin County officials had issued it because large waves expected through the afternoon could send water and debris into roads and homes. Authorities advised people to stay away from the beach as large waves continued to pound the area.

King tides and powerful waves destroyed part of a pier in Pacifica, Northern California, on Wednesday. A video showed a massive wave tearing down a portion of the pier and slamming it into the ocean.

The L-shaped pier is 1,140 feet (350 meters) long. Officials had closed it in the summer after finding cracks in the structure.

Beachfront homes in Pacifica, a city south of San Francisco, began flooding this week as king tides, the highest tides of the year, arrived in the area. Officials warned that more beachfront homes were at risk of flooding.

High tides had already flooded several communities that week along California's coast.

The National Weather Service issued a coastal flood warning for the Pacific Coast and bay shorelines along San Francisco Bay and San Pablo Bay through 4 p.m. Wednesday. It also issued a high surf warning in the Bay Area for Wednesday.

Breaking waves of 20 to 30 feet (6 to 9 meters), about two to three stories high, could overpower swimmers and sweep people into the ocean.

"Rising tides will bring water and waves farther up the beach than normal, restricting escape from beaches and coastal area," the weather service said.

King tides occur when the gravitational pull of the moon and sun aligns to raise water levels. Their effects this year are being amplified by the ongoing super El Niño, which has warmed the ocean, leading to an increased volume of seawater.

Some homes and streets in the coastal Marin County cities of Mill Valley, Larkspur, Sausalito and Corte Madera flooded.

Farther north, in Humboldt County near the Oregon border, the sheriff's office ordered evacuations in a small coastal community where most of the buildings flooded.
