## Justice Department historically avoided public probes within 60 days of voting

Department of Homeland Security agents conducted the court-authorized search of Rep. Ilhan Omar's Washington home less than a month before the 3 November midterm elections, according to The Guardian. The operation occurred on 12 October in the nation's capital.

Omar won her re-election bid in those midterms. Donald Trump's Republican Party lost control of Congress for the second half of Trump's second presidency. The Guardian also noted that the Justice Department has historically avoided public investigative actions involving a candidate within 60 days of an election; the 12 October search occurred within that period.

The timeline adds electoral context to the legal proceedings. The heavily redacted filings do not appear to shed new light on the reason or outcome of the search, and they disclose no new details about the immigration-fraud investigation.

Before authorizing the search, U.S. Magistrate Judge Matthew Sharbaugh raised concerns about the implications of applying the constitutional "speech or debate clause" to a member of Congress's private residence. The Guardian said the clause is designed to protect legislators from executive branch interference or intimidation and includes a privilege against compelled disclosures.

Sharbaugh disagreed with the government's argument that the privilege extended only to official congressional offices and certain electronic devices. "That logic does not hold," the magistrate wrote. He concluded that the government's law-enforcement interests needed to be balanced against Omar's constitutional rights as a member of Congress, and the government ultimately agreed to a protocol allowing Omar to invoke the privilege over any speech-or-debate material before agents entered.

The heavily redacted documents were released in response to a lawsuit filed by media organizations. Washington DC federal court chief judge James Boasberg ordered the partial unsealing of filings related to the 12 October search after more than a dozen news organizations, including the Associated Press, argued that the public had a right of access to the records under the US Constitution's First Amendment free-press protections.

Only part of the record was opened. Boasberg agreed with the government that it was premature to unseal the 67-page search warrant affidavit because it contained sensitive details of the investigation. In an October court filing, the government argued that unsealing the court records before a charging decision would jeopardize the investigation.

The Guardian said the documents disclose no new details about an immigration-fraud investigation launched by the Trump administration, which Omar claims is politically motivated. Federal authorities are investigating potential fraud in Omar's family's immigration paperwork and have issued grand jury subpoenas to some relatives, the Associated Press reported in October. That report cited a person briefed on the investigation who spoke on condition of anonymity because the person was not authorized to discuss the active investigation.

Omar is a naturalized US citizen born in Somalia. The Guardian reported that she has been a target of anti-immigrant rhetoric by Donald Trump and many of the president's Republican allies. She has not been charged with any crimes and has denied wrongdoing. An attorney for Omar did not immediately respond to an email seeking comment Monday on the unsealed documents.

Omar said DHS agents seized 17 items, including phones, notebooks, a Somali-language children's book and a vintage camera that Omar's husband had received as a gift.

A photograph by Annabelle Gordon for Reuters showed DHS agents at the entrance to Omar's Washington residence on 12 October. A photograph by Tim Evans for Reuters showed Omar speaking at a campaign rally for Peggy Flanagan in Minneapolis, Minnesota, on 20 July.

Omar remains uncharged, and the unsealed filings do not appear to shed new light on the reason for or outcome of the search. The newly reported election timeline adds context without resolving those questions.
