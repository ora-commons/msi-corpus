## Lawmakers may question CISA leadership, cyber risks and immigration enforcement

The House Committee on Homeland Security was scheduled to hold a hearing at 10 a.m. EST Wednesday, with Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin expected to answer questions about threats facing the country. The annual hearing is a standard opportunity for members of Congress to question top administration officials, and it was the first such opportunity for Democrats after the midterm elections.

Warnings involving Iran were among the issues linked to the hearing, alongside the flight grounding, technology failures and immigration enforcement questions outlined for the session. The United States has faced a series of cyber and technology problems that have left Americans feeling vulnerable, including failures across government agencies and private companies. FBI Deputy Director Christopher Raia and the Trump administration's national intelligence director, Jay Clayton, were also expected to face questioning. Mullin, a former senator from Oklahoma, is key to Trump's mass deportation agenda; his department includes Customs and Border Protection and Immigration and Customs Enforcement.

Mullin was at the center of the Republican administration's public response to last week's nationwide grounding of all aircraft. At a Friday White House news conference with Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy, Mullin pushed for more funding for the aging air traffic control system while saying the government was not seeing signs of a deliberate cyberattack.

The Trump administration said newly installed routers caused the Federal Aviation Administration's telecommunications system to be flooded with data, forcing the agency to ground all flights while it addressed the problem. Thousands of flights were canceled, and travelers across the United States scrambled for alternative travel options after the ground stop.

Mullin also faced scrutiny over his decision to fly to Louisiana on Saturday to watch the LSU-Texas game. His flight occurred after the stoppage was lifted and aircraft were flying again, while thousands of canceled flights left travelers scrambling. Mullin said on CNBC that the airways had been open for 21 hours by the time his flight took off.

"We would have never taken airspace if airspace wasn't open. And we still gotta live our lives at the same time," Mullin said.

The hearing came amid a wider run of technology failures across government agencies and private companies. Microsoft said a technical issue arising during an upgrade caused an outage on Nov. 12 that lasted for hours and severely disrupted Outlook, Teams and the artificial intelligence assistant Copilot. The banking giant Chase and airline carrier United also experienced technology outages. An internal National Security Agency network used to communicate classified information went offline Tuesday after what the agency described as routine maintenance.

On Fox News Channel on Monday, Mullin said he could say with "98%" certainty that the string of cyber and technology problems was not the result of a cyberattack.

Mullin could face questions about the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, which is supposed to play a central role in the federal response to cyber incidents. CISA works with industry to protect infrastructure, including water and electric generation, election systems, chemical facilities and cyber networks.

CISA has not had a Senate-confirmed leader since Trump took office for a second term in early 2025. Trump's original pick to lead the agency withdrew his nomination earlier this year after it languished for months in the Senate. CISA also lost staff last year during cuts aimed at reducing the size of the federal government.

Immigration enforcement was the other major line of questioning. Democrats vowed rigorous oversight of everything from immigration detention contracts to the use of force by ICE officers. Trump has indicated he could work with Democrats on immigration legislation, but he has also elevated Stephen Miller, described by the AP as the architect of his immigration crackdown, to be chief of staff. The AP said the move raised questions about how easily compromise could be reached on the issue.

Mullin took over as DHS secretary in March from ousted Secretary Kristi Noem after immigration enforcement officers killed two American citizen protesters in Minneapolis, forcing the administration to adjust its mass deportation strategy. Immigration arrests initially fell but then rebounded as ICE, flush with billions of dollars in new congressional funding, hired new officers and maximized cooperation from local and state law enforcement agencies across the country.

The scheduled hearing was expected to bring the flight response, technology disruptions, CISA's leadership gap and immigration enforcement before Mullin, Raia and Clayton.
