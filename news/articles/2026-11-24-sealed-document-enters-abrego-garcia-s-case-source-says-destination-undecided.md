## Lawyers say Trump administration threatened punishment and pressure to plead guilty

A person familiar with the matter said Kilmar Abrego Garcia may not stay in Spain long term and has not decided on a final destination. U.S. District Judge Paula Xinis has presided over two civil cases brought by Abrego Garcia and his family in Greenbelt, Md., and a sealed document was entered in the second case last week. Abrego Garcia spent more than eight months in detention in the U.S. and abroad, including in at least six American states and in prison in El Salvador.

His departure from the U.S. for Spain comes after a federal judge allowed him to arrange his own exit, according to people familiar with the matter. Abrego Garcia, 31, is a sheet-metal laborer who entered the country illegally as a teenager. The outcome fell close to what Abrego Garcia's lawyers had envisioned but well short of what Trump administration officials had wanted.

A federal judge in Tennessee dismissed human-smuggling charges against Abrego Garcia, saying prosecutors had improperly charged him for political reasons. After that dismissal, the matter fell to Xinis to resolve.

Xinis, an Obama appointee, has presided over both Greenbelt civil cases. The second challenged the administration's attempts to deport Abrego Garcia to countries such as Uganda, Eswatini, Ghana or Liberia, where he had no connections and risked being sent back to El Salvador. A sealed document was entered into the record for that case last week.

Abrego Garcia's lawyers repeatedly said he was willing to go to Costa Rica because the country had offered him residence and assured him that he would not be forcibly repatriated. Xinis pressed government lawyers to explain why the Trump administration was unwilling to let him go to Costa Rica and criticized them for failing to engage with that option.

His lawyers also said the Trump administration was making threats to punish him and pressure him to plead guilty to the human-smuggling charges. Abrego Garcia denied the charges.

An immigration judge in 2019 said Abrego Garcia could not be sent to El Salvador, where a gang had targeted his family's pupusa business. Federal agents picked him up in Baltimore in March 2025 during a push to sweep up alleged gang members. His deportation to a mega-prison in El Salvador violated a court order barring his return to his native country.

Federal agents held him in Louisiana and Texas before flying him to El Salvador, where he was imprisoned at Cecot alongside hundreds of other men whom U.S. officials accused of gang membership. U.S. officials later said he had been sent to the mega-prison in error but that they were powerless to bring him back. In all, Abrego Garcia spent more than eight months in detention in the U.S. and abroad, including in at least six American states and in El Salvador.

Abrego Garcia returned to the U.S. almost three months later to face human-smuggling charges. The U.S. Marshals held him in a Tennessee jail in Cookeville.

In August, the U.S. judge overseeing his criminal case ordered Abrego Garcia released on bail. He spent less than three days outside detention before going to the Immigration and Customs Enforcement office in Baltimore for what had been billed as an interview and was immediately taken into custody.

He was held first in immigration detention in Farmville, Virginia, and then transferred to a facility in Philipsburg, Pennsylvania. In December, Xinis released him into home confinement after ruling that the government could not continue to hold him indefinitely.

Abrego Garcia's case drew nationwide attention in the early months of the second Trump administration and placed him at the center of a debate over the administration's deportation policies. Administration officials had described him as a violent gang member and wanted him deported to a foreign country to which he had no ties.
