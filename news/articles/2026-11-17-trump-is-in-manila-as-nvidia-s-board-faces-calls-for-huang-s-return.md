The morning's briefing stretched across air travel, summit diplomacy, technology leadership and the courts. Trump is in Manila for the ASEAN summit, where he is to meet allies, and is scheduled to travel to Shenzhen, China, for a joint summit with Chinese leader Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin. The gathering, on the sidelines of the APEC summit that Xi is hosting in Shenzhen, would be the first joint summit of the three leaders.

Trump said as he left the White House that the leaders would discuss his "favorite subject": Ukraine. The White House had previously announced that Ukraine would not be on the trilateral meeting's agenda. NPR reporter Deepa Shivaram said Europe and Ukraine are concerned about how Ukraine may be discussed at the meeting and what might come out of it. Shivaram said Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy will not be at APEC, and that Trump has recently been more lenient toward Putin and Russia.

Nvidia's chief executive, Jensen Huang, resigned, and the departure has shaken Silicon Valley, drawing calls from some technology leaders for the board to reinstate him. Trump said he agreed with those calls. Nvidia is the world's dominant producer of AI chips, and NPR reporter Shannon Bond said many prominent artificial intelligence and venture capital figures have recognized Huang as a visionary whose company played a significant role in the AI boom.

An online petition supporting Huang's reinstatement has garnered more than 160,000 signatures, though Bond said some of them may not be real. Elon Musk said he signed it. Brad Gerstner from Altimeter Capital, a key Nvidia investor, urged the board to thoroughly investigate and keep all options open. The interim chief executive and chief financial officer will address the public for the first time following today's earnings report.

The U.S. government also introduced a rule to block remote access to advanced AI chips and servers, closing a loophole that could allow adversarial governments to access American AI technology through overseas data centers.

Air travel was still absorbing the effects of a nationwide ground stop when a technology issue with United Airlines delayed departing flights on Monday evening. The Federal Aviation Administration ordered the ground stop Friday, while L3Harris workers were converting copper wires to a fiber optic system and installing a new router in El Dorado, Arkansas.

Trump said he hopes the episode will prompt Congress to allocate more funds to upgrade the outdated air traffic control system. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the FAA will reevaluate future maintenance procedures, according to NPR reporter Joe Hernandez. Mike McCormick, a former FAA senior executive and air traffic manager, told Hernandez that he thinks another nationwide ground stop is unlikely. McCormick said the FAA sometimes imposes a maintenance moratorium, temporarily halting such work.

Prosecutors are moving to retry Lindsay Clancy for the deaths of her three children, with the new trial set for March 29, 2027. Judge William Sullivan, who presided over the first trial, will oversee the new proceedings. No changes were made to the charges during Monday's court hearing, despite speculation that they could be reduced to manslaughter or second-degree murder after the previous trial ended in a mistrial. Sullivan denied Clancy's defense team's request to dismiss the charges based on double jeopardy.

Clancy's defense argues that she should not be held criminally responsible for the deaths of her children because she was suffering from severe postpartum psychosis.

One year ago, New Mexico became the first state to offer free childcare to all families regardless of income, as long as adults are working, seeking work, in school or unable to work. Many parents have found the initiative life-changing, but capacity is limited: only 50% of the some 67,000 children age 5 and under are enrolled in the universal program. Some of the remaining families participate in other programs, such as PreK and Head Start, and state officials say the program must expand to meet demand.

The issues observed in New Mexico are mirrored across the nation, prompting policymakers and childcare advocates to monitor the state's initiative closely. Almost half of U.S. children age 5 and under live in licensed childcare deserts, where demand exceeds availability, according to the Center for American Progress, which NPR describes as a left-leaning think tank.

The program has an operating budget of nearly $600 million, and some lawmakers are concerned it could be $50 million over budget this year. State Sen. Steve Lanier told NPR the steep cost of the program is why he supports universal childcare only for some people, saying someone earning around $450,000 or $600,000 might not need assistance. Lanier has previously sued to end the initiative, and the lawsuit was unsuccessful.

Since the enactment of free, universal childcare in New Mexico, lower-income mothers have experienced significant gains, with their full-time employment rising 23% according to a new paper from Arizona State University.

Millions of shorebirds embark on migrations to Alaska each year to breed, traveling thousands of miles from places such as New Zealand, Asia and South America. Researchers have found that nearly half of the 41 shorebird species that breed in Alaska are in decline. The birds time their journeys to coincide with peak insect availability, and climate change is throwing off their schedules. Warmer springs lead to earlier insect emergence, creating a mismatch that could leave the birds without enough food. Scientists are racing to understand whether the migratory birds can adapt to the changing Arctic landscape.
