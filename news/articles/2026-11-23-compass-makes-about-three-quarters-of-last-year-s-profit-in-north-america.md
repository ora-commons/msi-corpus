## Compass uses dollars for reporting and London-listed share trading

Compass makes about three-quarters of last year's $3bn (£2.3bn) profit in North America, a region that includes New York. It also uses dollars for reporting and London-listed share trading. Compass switched its reporting currency from British sterling to US dollars in October 2023 and changed the trading currency of its London-listed shares to US dollars in April this year.

The Financial Times reported that Compass could announce an intention to move its primary listing from London to New York as soon as Tuesday, when it reports annual results. The Telegraph reported that the company planned to retain a secondary listing in London and would say that there was no threat to its staff in the UK.

Compass is a £43.6bn ($57bn) catering company, one of the biggest in the FTSE 100, and has been listed in London since 1988. Headquartered in Surrey, it is a major government contractor with more than 600,000 staff around the world.

Compass provides meals in more than 2,000 schools, academies, colleges and universities across the UK and in some NHS hospitals, as well as in private-sector offices. It also provides grab-and-go foods for fast-food chains and cafes such as Starbucks, Costa and Taco Bell.

The Guardian said Compass's potential departure "would be the latest in a string of exits by companies seeking deeper pools of capital and higher valuations in the US." In recent years, equipment rental company Ashtead, building material supplier CRH and Flutter Entertainment, the owner of Paddy Power, have all moved their primary listings to the US.

The UK's biggest drugmaker, AstraZeneca, listed shares directly on the New York Stock Exchange this year, putting its US shares on an even footing with its London-listed stock.

Several companies also left London's market after agreeing to takeover deals. This year's transactions included warehouse landlord Segro's £14bn takeover by US rival Prologis, easyJet's £5.7bn takeover by US private equity company Apollo and asset manager Schroders' £9.9bn takeover by a US investor.

Other deals included the £10bn takeover of lab testing group Intertek by a consortium led by Swedish private equity company EQT, agreed in June. Beazley, the specialist insurer with clients including Lloyds of London, agreed an £8bn sale to larger rival Zurich in February.

Fewer companies are also joining London's market. EY data showed seven listings in the first half of this year, raising £577m in total.

Airtel Money, the mobile money arm of Airtel Africa, was one of the few companies to join London this year. The £5.4bn payments processor listed at £1.96 a share in October and traded at about £2.05 on Monday.

Compass makes about three-quarters of last year's profit in North America and uses dollars for reporting and London-listed share trading. The reported primary-listing move remains unconfirmed: The Guardian said it had approached Compass for comment.
