## Interim CEO Kress says Nvidia's strength has always come from its people

President Donald Trump offered his own recommendation for Nvidia's chief executive seat on Thursday: former Apple chief executive Tim Cook, who has similarly remained in Trump's orbit in recent years. "There is only one man to take his place," Trump wrote on his Truth Social platform Thursday. "Legend replaces a Legend!" Critics quickly decried Trump's comments. House Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries said the Republican administration had "repeatedly undermined the free market economy" and that Thursday marked the latest example of "Donald Trump weighing in on behalf of his views connected to corporate America, as opposed to focusing on working class America."

In a statement about her new post on Thursday, Kress said Nvidia's "strength has always come from its people" and that she is "committed to building on the extraordinary foundation Jensen created over the last 33 years." Kress is Nvidia's chief financial officer and executive vice president since 2013. Nvidia announced Thursday that Huang resigned as chief executive, effective immediately. Huang co-founded the California-based chipmaker in 1993 alongside Chris Malachowsky and Curtis Priem and had led the company ever since, with no clear successor in sight. Before joining Nvidia over 13 years ago, Kress worked at Cisco, Microsoft and Texas Instruments.

Huang's departure has set off a scramble for a permanent chief executive. Nvidia said it is considering both "internal and external candidates." The company's board of directors also said Thursday that it has named its first-ever chairman, Harvey C. Jones.

The resignation followed a week in which reports of Huang's extramarital affairs came into public view. The New York Times reported that Huang had an affair with a businesswoman U.S. intelligence suspects to be a Chinese spy, which Beijing denies. Separately, the outlet and later others reported that Nvidia employees said Huang had another affair with an executive who reported to him directly — compounding calls for him to step down.

Over the weekend, after news reports of Huang's relationship with a woman identified as former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang, the then-CEO apologized for being "unfaithful to my wife."

"My actions hurt her and our children," Huang wrote in a Sunday email to employees, adding that his colleagues also "deserve better" from him.

Neither Huang nor Nvidia identified who he had been intimate with by name, but the apology arrived shortly after Nvidia said it was investigating whether Huang had been "the target of a foreign intelligence effort." Last month, the Times reported that U.S. intelligence agencies discovered Huang had developed a close relationship with Alice Huang, who also goes by the name Huang Le, and determined she was feeding information directly to Beijing. Huang is a common surname and the two are not related.

Nvidia initially maintained that Huang would stay on as CEO as the company conducted its investigation. But scrutiny grew late Wednesday after reports emerged that Huang had a separate affair with an Nvidia executive, and his resignation arrived shortly after. The company didn't address either relationship in its Thursday announcement. Later on Thursday, Reuters reported that Huang had an intimate relationship with a second employee, citing unidentified sources.

Nvidia hasn't provided any notable new updates in its internal review announced Sunday. At the time, the company said it had "no reason to believe" that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised, but that its inquiry would examine all available information.

Trump was reportedly briefed about intelligence findings involving Huang well before the scandal erupted publicly. When asked over the weekend whether he wished he had acted sooner on that information, the president said "I don't think so" and maintained that Nvidia's CEO was an "honorable guy." Trump later said the FBI is looking into Huang's affair with Alice Huang, although where exactly that probe stands is unclear. Lawmakers from both sides of the aisle on Capitol Hill are calling for further investigations.

Many eyes have been on Nvidia's ties to Singapore-based Megaspeed International, which prominently touts the companies' partnership on its website. Last year, Megaspeed was accused of illicitly shipping advanced Nvidia computer chips into China, but in December said there was "no evidence" of such exports. On Thursday, Megaspeed maintained it takes its obligation to comply with American export control laws "extremely seriously" — and that it was committed to working "constructively and transparently" with the U.S. government.

Singaporean authorities said they questioned Alice Huang and confiscated her passport last year as part of an investigation into Megaspeed. They concluded there wasn't enough evidence that she had committed a crime, but suspected she provided "inaccurate information" to downplay her role at Megaspeed and barred her from returning to the country. China, meanwhile, has denied that it attempted to ensnare Jensen Huang. Its Foreign Ministry earlier this week called the spying allegations "unsubstantiated" and said they amount to a smear campaign against Beijing.

Huang has maintained a close relationship to Trump throughout the Republican president's second term in office. He successfully lobbied for looser AI regulation overall — including less strict limits on sales of Nvidia chips to China — and is among tech CEOs who have dined at the White House, and even traveled on Air Force One. Trump has called Huang "smart" and "amazing," repeatedly insisting he join him on foreign trips. After news of his resignation broke on Thursday, the president maintained that Huang had "done a fantastic job."

The shockwaves of Huang's exit underscore how few figures hold so much power in the AI race. Nvidia is one of the world's most valuable companies today — and Huang, often wearing his signature leather jacket, has risen to fame across the tech world as a key face in the AI race, both across the industry and often on Trump's side in Washington. Nvidia's high-end chips have emerged as the leading building blocks for artificial intelligence. Nvidia shares fell more than 4% by market close Thursday, after plummeting earlier in the day.

Stephanie Balaouras, group research director for Forrester, said Huang's resignation from Nvidia isn't just a test for the company but the AI boom as a whole. "If Nvidia stabilizes quickly under new leadership, the AI infrastructure boom will have demonstrated resilience," she said. "If its stock, financing relationships, or ecosystem commitments remain under sustained pressure, that would add evidence to the fragile side of the AI bubble ledger."

Some analysts suspect the foreign intelligence allegations will strengthen calls in Washington for stricter export controls with China, which could in turn renew trade strains overall. Others have called it a watershed moment for Nvidia, the chipmaker that has long been seen as the crown jewel for powering AI. And questions of security risks facing the companies at the forefront of this technology are piling up.
