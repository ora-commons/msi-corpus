## Network stabilized after L3Harris blocked two locations, FAA says

Work at a radio communications site in south Arkansas has been identified as a source of the problems that led federal officials to ground all flights in the United States for several hours last week.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy identified a "remote radio facility" in El Dorado where workers were deploying a new router as a source of the communications problems that led to the nationwide ground stop on Friday.

Duffy described the work on NBC's "Meet the Press" on Sunday. "There was an old fiber line that was going to be decommissioned by our telecom provider. We are moving over to fiber," he said. "And what we saw when we turned this facility on was that the router we used was flooding the rest of our network with data."

The FAA identified the El Dorado site and a radar site in Amarillo, Texas as two potential sources of excessive data that flooded the nation's air traffic control system.

L3Harris, the FAA's network service provider, had been doing work at the El Dorado site, the agency said. The network stabilized after L3Harris blocked the two locations, and the broadcasting function on new routers was also disabled, the agency said.

"We are supporting the FAA in its investigation to understand the conditions that led to the Nov. 13 ground stop of our nation's air travel," L3Harris said Saturday in a statement on its website. "L3Harris takes the nationwide ground stop and its impact on travelers, airlines and air traffic operations seriously."

Duffy said he wants $50 billion to address the nation's aging air traffic control system. Bryan Bedford, head of the Federal Aviation Administration, also stressed the importance of airspace modernization during a visit to Northwest Arkansas National Airport in March.

Local officials took the national attention aimed at El Dorado in stride. The El Dorado-Union County Chamber of Commerce shared a link on its Facebook page to a Wall Street Journal article about the site's role in the ground stop, along with a guide on how to pronounce the city's name.

The correct pronunciation, the chamber said, is "El Duh Ray Duh."

"And while we're at it – tell the masses that our community is growing, we are adding jobs, your children can get their college tuition covered through the El Duh Ray Duh Promise, and we are home to Fortune 200 Retailer Murphy USA," the chamber's post said.
