## Russian missiles and suspected drone come down in Poland during exercises

- Two Russian missiles and a suspected jet-powered drone came down in Poland during European military exercises on Friday, Polish officials said.
- There were no immediate reports of injuries or damage from the three landings.
- Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said the landings appeared to be an intentional provocation by Russia.
- NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte called the incident a grave violation of Polish airspace.
- President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Russia's simultaneous overnight attack on Ukraine killed at least eight civilians and wounded more than two dozen.
- All 32 NATO members must agree politically before Article 5 of the alliance's founding treaty is triggered.

## Tusk calls the landings a likely intentional provocation

Two Russian missiles and a suspected jet-powered drone came down in Poland on Friday during a two-week European military exercise, Polish officials said. There were no immediate reports of injuries or damage.

One Russian missile was believed to have crashed around 35 kilometers (22 miles) from where the military exercises were taking place, officials said. A Kh-101 cruise missile went down roughly 50 kilometers (30 miles) from the Ukraine border, and what officials believed was likely a jet-powered drone hit about 30 kilometers (20 miles) from the border with Ukraine.

Images from closed-circuit cameras and a mobile phone in the region, collected by Poland's PAP news agency, showed a large, loud explosion and a bright flash, apparently in a wooded area, in one of the crashes.

European troops were taking part in a two-week military exercise that began Nov. 9 in southeastern Poland. The forces were preparing for a possible postwar mission in Ukraine aimed at helping stabilize the country and rebuild its military.

Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said it appeared that “we are dealing with an intentional provocation” by Russia. Tusk's comments linked the timing to the European military exercises in Poland. Moscow had not immediately commented on the missiles, the suspected drone or Friday's drills.

NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte called the development a “grave violation” of Poland's airspace. “This incident is clearly dangerous - a consequence of Russia's ongoing war against Ukraine,” Rutte wrote on X. He was expected to go to Poland this weekend in response.

## NATO treaty procedures and political agreement

Under NATO's collective security guarantee, Article 5 of the alliance's founding treaty provides that an attack on one member should be considered an attack on all 32 members. It obliges members to provide support to the affected ally, most likely military backup, but does not limit them to that alone.

Article 5 is not triggered automatically. Ultimately, it is a political decision that all members must agree to. Poland also has the option to invoke Article 4, which it did last year. Article 4 provides for emergency consultations only.

## European condemnations and intelligence comments

European countries had previously expressed alarm at what they described as Russia's attempts to destabilize the continent. European governments condemned the incident after the objects came down.

British Prime Minister Andy Burnham called the incident “utterly unacceptable.” European Council President António Costa said Russia “continues to spread (its) threats,” while Germany's Foreign Ministry called the incident “yet another example of Russia's ongoing provocations.” Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said the incident in Poland “demonstrates the threats posed by Russian aggression to the whole of Europe and the trans-Atlantic community.”

Later Friday, the United Kingdom's chief of defense intelligence, Lt. Gen. Matthew Jones, said he was confident Russia “does not actively seek war with NATO.” Speaking at a college in Cambridge, Jones said his greatest fear “is the risk of a significant miscalculation.” He did not directly mention the incidents in Poland.

“There are too many recent examples of reckless Russian behavior where, if the incident had been slightly different, the results could have been extremely serious,” Jones said.

## Ukraine strikes and diplomacy

The incidents occurred while Russia carried out a major nighttime bombardment of neighboring Ukraine. The Associated Press said the development stoked tensions between Europe and Moscow as the United States tried to add momentum to talks it was mediating on ending the nearly five-year war that began with Russia's invasion of Ukraine.

Overnight strikes killed at least eight Ukrainian civilians and wounded more than two dozen, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said. He said Russia fired more than 400 drones and over 110 missiles, 27 of them ballistic, and that some of the missiles carried cluster warheads designed to scatter dozens of smaller bomblets across a wide area.

Russia's Defense Ministry said the sites it hit Friday were linked to the Ukrainian military. The ministry said it hit a facility where missiles were assembled, a plant making components for rocket fuel and a facility where drones were assembled. Ukraine said the attacks also wounded more than a dozen people as apartment blocks, medical facilities, warehouses and three bridges were hit.

The barrage also affected Ukraine's power grid ahead of winter. Strikes on power plants, substations and transmission lines forced Ukraine's three operational nuclear power plants to reduce generation and brought emergency outages in 11 regions, Deputy Energy Minister Artem Nekrasov said.

Russia's nighttime attack on Ukraine came hours after Russian President Vladimir Putin vowed to retaliate for Ukrainian strikes that week on the Kerch Bridge, which links occupied Crimea with the Russian mainland and is vital to Russia's war effort. The Associated Press's analysis said the attack followed a pattern of Russian responses to Ukrainian strikes and added uncertainty to the prospects for talks brokered by Washington.

U.S. President Donald Trump discussed the war at a summit in China on Thursday with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese leader Xi Jinping, according to Trump's envoy Steve Witkoff. Another envoy, Trump's son-in-law Jared Kushner, told European leaders that the United States believed Russia was “comfortable” with NATO-like security guarantees for postwar Ukraine, which could be a step toward a compromise.

Russia and Ukraine remained far apart on the possible terms for a peace deal. Russian Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov said Friday that even a quasi-membership in NATO for Ukraine would be unacceptable, Russia's Interfax news agency reported. “We discuss security guarantees primarily in terms of ensuring the security of our own country, and that's what we're talking about,” Ryabkov said.

Putin has said that Russia will not accept any European troops deployed to Ukraine after a prospective peace deal and would view them as legitimate targets.

The Associated Press also reported that President Donald Trump, after calling Putin his friend, said he “can't speak to” whether Putin was a friend to America.
