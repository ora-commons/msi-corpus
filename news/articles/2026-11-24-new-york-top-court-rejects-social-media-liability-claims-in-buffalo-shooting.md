## Three judges dissent, saying the plaintiffs’ case should proceed

New York’s highest court ruled Monday that social media companies cannot be held liable for the 2022 racist mass shooting at a Tops Friendly Market in Buffalo in four lawsuits filed by survivors and victims’ relatives. In a 4-3 decision, the Albany-based Court of Appeals upheld a lower court’s decision to dismiss claims that accused Facebook, YouTube and other platforms of helping radicalize Payton Gendron.

The lawsuits accused the companies of offering platforms engineered to promote extreme content and encourage compulsive use, and of helping radicalize Gendron. The majority said the four lawsuits “fail to establish that the shooter’s actions were a natural and foreseeable consequence of these defendants’ decisions about how to design and operate their platforms.”

Three judges disagreed, saying the survivors and families had built enough of a case for it to go forward. Writing for the dissenters, Judge Jenny Rivera noted that Gendron described himself in his diary as an online “addict” whose “mental health has seriously degraded.”

The ruling affected Facebook and Instagram owner Meta, Google and YouTube parent Alphabet, and Reddit. Under different reasoning, the decision also upheld the dismissal of claims against other messaging and social media apps and Amazon, which owns Twitch. Gendron used Twitch to livestream the rampage.

Gendron, a 23-year-old white man, absorbed racist conspiracy theories online before killing 10 Black people and wounding three others at Tops Friendly Market in May 2022. He is serving a life sentence after pleading guilty to state-level murder charges. In 2023, he told a court that he “believed what I read online and acted out of hate.”

Matthew Bergman, who represents victim Heyward Patterson’s family and estate in one of the cases, said Tuesday that the group was assessing its legal options after the ruling. Bergman said in a statement, “The families affected by the tragic May 2022 Buffalo grocery store attack deserve accountability when dangerous product design choices contribute to real-world harm.”

Messages seeking comment were sent to lawyers for the other plaintiffs and for the companies.

Jury selection is ongoing for Gendron’s federal hate-crime trial, which carries the possibility of a death sentence if he is convicted. He has pleaded not guilty in that case.

The 10 people Gendron killed ranged in age from 32 to 86. The victims were Heyward Patterson, Celestine Chaney, Geraldine Talley, Katherine Massey, Pearl Young, Andre Mackneil, Ruth Whitfield, Roberta Drury, Margus Morrison and Aaron Salter.
