## Russian media cite humanitarian grounds; sister denies mercenary allegation

Russian state media said Russian President Vladimir Putin agreed to release American citizen Stephen Hubbard on humanitarian grounds. Russian prosecutors accused the retired English teacher of signing up to serve in the Ukrainian military in Kharkiv province under a contract that stipulated he would be paid $1,000 a month. His sister, Patricia Hubbard Fox, said the allegation was false.

Hubbard, a Michigan native who had been unwell, was expected to be flown to Texas for medical treatment. In total, he had been held for four and a half years since being detained in April 2022.

President Donald Trump confirmed Hubbard's release and said it was the direct result of a phone call with Putin last week in which he requested Hubbard's freedom. Trump said the effort had cost the United States nothing.

The release came as U.S. Special Envoy Steve Witkoff held talks with Putin in St. Petersburg. Witkoff thanked Putin for the move to set Hubbard free, according to the reports, and UPI quoted him: "I want to thank you very much, the president sends his thanks... the whole nation sends his thanks because of Stephen Hubbard. Because of you, he's going home to his family."

Hubbard was on his way home to the United States on Wednesday after being freed from a Russian prison. Russian state media said Hubbard, 74, had been transported to Moscow's Pulkovo Airport after Putin agreed to release him on "humanitarian" grounds two years into an 82-month prison sentence for allegedly fighting as a mercenary for Ukraine.

Trump also said he had raised the retired teacher's case with Putin. Separately, UPI's report noted a deal for Russia to supply the United States with 1.8 million tons of diesel.

Hubbard was an English teacher who relocated to eastern Ukraine in 2014 after spending many years living and working overseas.

Following former U.S. Marine Robert Gilman's release on health grounds in August, the State Department said it was hopeful the move would lead to "Americans unjustly held in Russia" being set free. At least five other U.S. citizens continued to be held in Russian prisons.


Hubbard's release extended direct U.S.-Russia engagement over the war in Ukraine.
