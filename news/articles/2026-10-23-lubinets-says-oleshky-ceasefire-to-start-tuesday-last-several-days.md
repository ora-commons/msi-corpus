## Russia's top general orders Kherson-axis ceasefire at 4 a.m. Tuesday

Dmytro Lubinets, the Ukrainian ombudsman and his country's chief negotiator in the talks, told The Associated Press that a local ceasefire with Russia will start Tuesday — two days before the mission begins — and last several days. Russia's chief of the general staff, Gen. Valery Gerasimov, said Russian forces "will cease fire on the Kherson axis" starting at 4 a.m. Tuesday in Moscow, or late Monday in the United States, "in connection with the humanitarian operation near Oleshky." Russian forces will also carry out "engineering reconnaissance" and ensure "safe movement of the humanitarian convoy" into the city, Gerasimov said.

The White House announced that first lady Melania Trump brokered the ceasefire between Russia and Ukraine in Oleshky, a city she said is "on the brink of disaster," so that humanitarian aid can be delivered and civilians can be evacuated. Ukrainian, Russian and U.S. negotiators reached the deal Friday to provide humanitarian relief to the Russian-occupied town in the war-divided region of Kherson in southern Ukraine. The localized ceasefire is set to begin next week.

Lubinets said the ceasefire would take effect across the Kherson region and neighboring areas essential to the operation, and that the initiative could act as a model for future humanitarian missions. Russian officials also confirmed the agreement.

In a video message posted online earlier Friday, Lubinets thanked Melania Trump and said the deal would be tough to accomplish. He said the agreement calls for a ceasefire, followed by the delivery of much-needed humanitarian aid, and that civilians will be allowed to leave Oleshky. "The operation will be carried out gradually and is extremely challenging to implement," Lubinets said.

Lubinets said the operation would involve removing mines on the way to Oleshky before the physical delivery of humanitarian supplies and the arranging of evacuations. He gave no further details on how the ceasefire or the aid mission would be monitored or implemented, citing the sensitivity of the negotiations, and declined to comment when asked whether Melania Trump would travel to Ukraine.

"When Melania Trump learned about the situation in Oleshky, the negotiation process began under her coordination," Lubinets said. "For several weeks, we discussed many questions, initiatives and ideas. We established new approaches, mechanisms and tools to do everything possible to help civilians in Oleshky survive."

The White House said Melania Trump initiated the talks about Oleshky after learning about the situation at one of her events during the U.N. General Assembly session in September, and that she had previously worked with officials from both countries on a separate matter. She and a representative worked directly with Russian and Ukrainian leadership to define the terms of the ceasefire, the White House said. As President Donald Trump departed the White House, he was asked whether she would be part of trilateral negotiations.

Russia's human rights ombudswoman, Yana Lantratova, confirmed that Melania Trump had brokered an agreement on "organizing a humanitarian mission" in Oleshky. Lantratova, who said she participated in the negotiations, said providing such assistance is Russia's priority. The first step will be to clarify what people need and organize the delivery of food, medicine, hygiene products and other essentials, she said in an online statement. Safe passage will be guaranteed for anyone who wants to leave the combat zone, she said, adding that it will require abiding by the ceasefire and creating safe conditions for humanitarian vehicles.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Moscow was grateful to the first lady, and said Russian President Vladimir Putin and President Donald Trump had recently discussed the effort. Peskov said "those who want to leave it would be able to do so." He would not say whether evacuees would be taken to Russian- or Ukraine-held territory, and he demurred when asked how long the evacuation would last, saying only that it would most likely take several days.

U.N. Secretary-General António Guterres welcomed the first lady's announcement. Through spokesperson Stéphane Dujarric, Guterres said he hopes the local pause in fighting can become a step toward "an immediate, full and unconditional ceasefire" leading to "a comprehensive, just and lasting peace in Ukraine in line with international law."

Melania Trump has made helping children in conflict zones one of her signature issues in her husband's second term. She hosted a U.N. Security Council meeting on the issue earlier this year, and last year wrote to Putin about efforts to reunite families with children displaced by the war. She has helped reunite nearly three dozen children, according to her senior adviser, Marc Beckman. "What she wants to do is save children, and that's her focus," President Donald Trump told reporters at the White House.

The development comes as tensions have increased to new highs between the Trump administration and Ukraine's government. Kyiv has faced restrictions on the satellite internet system it relies on to knock down Russian attacks, with Trump and other top U.S. officials sending mixed messages about who was responsible for throttling the Elon Musk-made Starshield system.

Trump also recently waived new Russia sanctions, reached an agreement with Russia to import diesel fuel and pushed Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy to make a deal to end the war that Russia began in February 2022. In renewed efforts to revive the peace process, Trump envoy Steve Witkoff traveled to Norway this week for talks with Zelenskyy before meeting Putin in St. Petersburg, Russia.

Zelenskyy did not mention the ceasefire in Oleshky during a remote appearance at the Hudson Institute, a Washington think tank. He noted that American Patriot missile interceptors were still on hold and that Starshield and Starlink speed limits were still in place, and he spoke of the importance of the United States to renewed diplomatic efforts to end the war, including a possible joint meeting with Trump and Putin.

Oleshky lies in the war-divided Kherson region of southern Ukraine and is occupied by Russia. Food and medical supplies have been scarce for months in Oleshky, which sits on the Dnipro River that separates Ukrainian forces from the city's Russian occupiers. There is no electricity, heat or running water. Roads leading from town are mined and dotted with burned-out cars, and drones buzz overhead.

Once a city of about 24,000 residents, Oleshky was captured by Moscow's forces on the first day of their full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022. Its buildings were shelled and burned, and its infrastructure was damaged. In June 2023, floodwaters inundated the city after the Kakhovka Dam collapsed from an explosion that an Associated Press investigation found was likely caused by the Russian military. Hundreds of residents were killed.

Those left in the city along Ukraine's southeastern front face death from hunger or disease as another harsh winter looms. Ukraine has appealed repeatedly for a local ceasefire to organize evacuations. Russia, as the occupying power, is obligated by international law to ensure civilians under its control have what they need to survive, according to The Associated Press.
