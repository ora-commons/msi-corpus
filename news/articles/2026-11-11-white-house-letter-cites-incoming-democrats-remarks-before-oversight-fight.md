## NBC exit poll found 90% of voters called corruption crucial or major

A letter from White House counsel William Scharf warned that Democratic pledges to investigate President Donald Trump “may cross the line into blatant criminality,” and it pointed to public remarks by incoming Democratic lawmakers. It said the Representative-elect Richard Ojeda of North Carolina wants to see defense secretary Pete Hegseth “squirm,” that Texas representative-elect Katy Padilla Stout told witnesses to “put makeup on for the cameras,” and that Representative Yassamin Ansari of Arizona described accountability as eventually ending in jail. The letter also took aim at Garcia's “Lawyer Up” campaign, saying his intent was “not to legislate, but to intimidate.”

Corruption became a key issue in last week's midterm elections. While the economy was the issue voters named most often as the country's top problem in the NBC News exit poll, 90% of voters said corruption was a crucial or major factor in their votes.

Having promised during the campaign to tackle those issues, Democrats won back control of both chambers of Congress, and will arrive in January with their largest House majority since 2008. Trump wrote on social media that he was not responsible for the Republican losses, and later posted an image of a cap embroidered with the word “veto.”

The warning put the incoming oversight leadership on notice before the change of control. Rep. Robert Garcia, the incoming Democratic chair of the House oversight committee and its ranking member, rejected it on Wednesday, saying threats against planned Democratic inquiries would not stop Congress. Garcia said it appeared that Trump had “something to hide.”

Scharf argued that congressional inquiries need a legitimate legislative purpose. He said they cannot be used to punish targets or lay the ground for prosecutions.

Garcia said Congress's authority comes from Article I of the Constitution “not from the permission of the White House,” and described that power as inherent in the job of writing laws. “The American people voted overwhelmingly for us to pass laws to root out corruption and abuse,” Garcia said.

Garcia also accused the administration of continuing to weaponize the government with “threats of unconstitutional attacks on Congress.”

Garcia was unmoved. “We are a co-equal branch of government and will not be deterred by baseless threats from a weaponized DoJ or from the president's lawyers,” he said.
