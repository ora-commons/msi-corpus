## State appeal pauses as federal jurisdiction is reconsidered

A three-judge panel of the 2nd U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals on Monday reversed a lower-court decision that kept President Donald Trump's hush money criminal case in New York state court. The panel ordered a lower court to decide promptly whether the case should remain in federal court. If it remains there, Trump could argue that the verdict should be discarded because of presidential immunity. The ruling also set in motion a halt to proceedings in his simultaneous state-court appeal.

The order did not itself dismiss the conviction. It sent the legal question of removal back to a lower court while leaving the 34 felony counts in place. Trump was convicted of falsifying business records to obscure a $130,000 payment to adult film performer Stormy Daniels. He received no punishment and became the first U.S. president convicted of a crime, though he continues to deny wrongdoing.

In a two-page order, the panel said it disagreed with late U.S. District Judge Alvin Hellerstein's denial of Trump's request to move the case from state court to federal court, a process known as removal. The appellate judges ruled that Hellerstein "erred in holding that removal is categorically unavailable in a state criminal case" and erred in denying Trump good cause for two attempts to move the case.

Hellerstein had rejected removal for a third time in August. He died last month at 92. In that decision, he wrote that "paying hush money to an adult film star or engaging in a cover-up of an embarrassment are not subsumed in presidential immunity."

Trump first sought to move the case to federal court weeks after his indictment in March 2023. He tried again after his conviction and after the Supreme Court's July 2024 ruling that presidents and ex-presidents cannot be prosecuted for official acts and that prosecutors cannot use official acts as evidence that unofficial actions were illegal. Those were the two attempts for which the appeals panel found good cause.

After Trump appealed Hellerstein's second denial, another 2nd Circuit panel ordered the judge to reconsider, finding that he had failed to weigh "important issues relevant" to Trump's request. That earlier panel, which included Raymond J. Lohier Jr., said it expressed no view on how Hellerstein should rule but instructed him to closely review evidence Trump claimed related to official acts and whether Trump could argue the actions were taken as part of his White House duties.

At last week's argument, Trump's lawyer Robert Giuffra Jr. said prosecutors had used the type of official-act evidence later barred by the Supreme Court's immunity ruling. Giuffra identified testimony about Oval Office conversations, workflow and social media posts. He also challenged the prosecution's description of the hush money payment as an illegal campaign contribution under the Federal Election Campaign Act, arguing that such allegations belong in federal court rather than state court.

For the Manhattan District Attorney's Office, which prosecuted the case, Steven Wu, chief of the appeals division, urged the 2nd Circuit panel to uphold Hellerstein's ruling and leave the case in state court. The office declined comment.

The panel that issued Monday's order included Raymond J. Lohier Jr., John M. Walker Jr. and José A. Cabranes. The judges spent more than an hour questioning Trump's lawyer and a state prosecutor during arguments last week. Lohier and Cabranes were appointed by Democrats, while Walker was appointed by a Republican.

Trump's state-court appeal included arguments on Nov. 5 before the mid-level Appellate Division of New York's state court system. That court had yet to rule, and Monday's order could prevent it from doing so indefinitely. Giuffra had urged the federal judges to rule swiftly, arguing that without federal intervention Trump "will likely have to wait years" for state appellate courts to rule before he could take the matter to the U.S. Supreme Court.

In a statement, a spokesperson for Trump's legal team said the president "appreciates the 2nd Circuit's prompt and correct ruling, as he continues to win against Democrat weaponization of our Justice System."

Under the order, a lower court must decide whether the case should remain in federal court. If it does, Trump could renew his argument that presidential immunity requires the conviction to be discarded. The 2nd Circuit did not grant dismissal, and the ruling set in motion a pause in the parallel state-court proceedings.
