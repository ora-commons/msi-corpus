## French defends himself on radio as Paxton, Talarico trade attacks

The Longhorns had just beaten Ohio State on Saturday night when Bo French, the Republican nominee for Texas railroad commissioner, turned his attention from the scoreboard to the fans in the stands.

On Sunday, French posted a photograph showing nonwhite University of Texas supporters cheering for their team, accompanied by a caption that pivoted from football to graduation: "I heard UT graduation this year looked like this. I didn't believe it. The problem is now obviously far worse than anyone imagined."

The post drew swift condemnation from within French's own party. Sen. John Cornyn, a Texas Republican who lost his primary to Attorney General Ken Paxton earlier this year, wrote that French is "the Republican nominee for statewide office" and asked whether other Republicans would condemn the "intolerance and racism" or "look the other way while it becomes normalized." Cornyn added: "This is how great political parties self-destruct. Sad."

A number of other Texas Republicans joined Cornyn in criticizing French. French subsequently suggested that foreign-born students were flooding the flagship university — a claim that runs counter to a state law requiring 90 percent of UT freshmen to be Texas residents.

In a Monday radio interview reported by the Texas Tribune, French did not back down. "It's no surprise that the less-conservative people in Texas are the ones attacking me," he said.

The episode was quickly absorbed into the Texas Senate race. Paxton, now the Republican nominee, and Democratic nominee James Talarico both waded into the matter and used it to attack each other.

Meanwhile, French is fundraising off the controversy. He says he has been "inundated" with requests for yard signs.

The Wall Street Journal's newsletter drew a parallel on the Democratic side, noting that Democrats have spent months distancing themselves from progressive streamer Hasan Piker, who once said the United States "deserved" the Sept. 11, 2001 attacks. Piker later tried to distance himself from those comments. He has since campaigned with Abdul El-Sayed, the Democratic candidate for U.S. Senate in Michigan, and was present at El-Sayed's primary night party.

Both parties are struggling to define what should be off limits.
