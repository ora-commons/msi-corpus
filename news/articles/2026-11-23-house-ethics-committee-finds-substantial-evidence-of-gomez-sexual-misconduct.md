## Gomez announces Dec. 1 resignation while disputing report conclusions

The House Ethics Committee said in a report released Monday that it found “substantial evidence” that Democratic Rep. Jimmy Gomez of California engaged in sexual misconduct by making unwelcome sexual advances and sexual contact toward a House staffer. The committee’s report said the conduct caused the staffer significant and ongoing distress and contributed to her decision to leave House employment altogether.

The report connected Gomez’s conduct to a broader workplace issue. “Representative Gomez’s conduct also reflects a broader environment of sexual misconduct and toxic working conditions in some Capitol Hill offices which the Committee is determined to address,” the report stated.

Gomez announced that he would resign from the House on Dec. 1, even as he disputed the committee’s conclusions. In the announcement, Gomez said he had “strong disagreements with the Committee’s report and its conclusions.”

Gomez was already set to depart Congress at the end of the year after being defeated in his bid for reelection this month by fellow Democrat Rep.-elect Angela Gonzales-Torres.

Gomez said he recognized the seriousness of the ethics report and took full responsibility for the “personal mistakes I committed outside of my marriage.” He said he cooperated with the investigation and responded voluntarily to requests for information. Gomez also apologized to his family and constituents.

Before the report was released, Gomez had said he would cooperate with the investigation but also stressed that his actions were consensual in nature and did not violate the law or House Ethics rules.

The House Ethics Committee did not recommend any particular sanction for Gomez, who was serving his fifth term in the House. The panel said the Constitution charges the House with policing the behavior of its own members. “The House should take whatever action it deems appropriate in light of the Committee’s findings,” the Ethics panel said.

The Gomez finding was the latest House case involving allegations of sexual misconduct by a lawmaker. Separately, two lawmakers resigned earlier in the year rather than face the prospect of an expulsion vote, and another member was censured for interactions with two female aides on his staff.

The report issued a finding but no recommended sanction. Gomez’s announcement established Dec. 1 as his resignation date, and the committee’s findings and his disagreements remained on record after the committee said the House should take whatever action it deems appropriate.
