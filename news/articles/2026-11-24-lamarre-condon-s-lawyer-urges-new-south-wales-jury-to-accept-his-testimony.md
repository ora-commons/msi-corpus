## Stratton says cross-examination did not establish new lies

John Stratton SC urged a New South Wales Supreme Court jury on Tuesday to accept Beau Lamarre-Condon's testimony as truthful as the defence closed its case in the former police officer's double murder trial. Lamarre-Condon is accused of fatally shooting Jesse Baird and Baird's partner, Luke Davies, with his police service weapon in Baird's Paddington home on 19 February 2024.

Lamarre-Condon has pleaded not guilty to two counts of murder and one charge of aggravated break and enter. Stratton's closing submissions focused on the fact that the former officer had chosen to give evidence and had subjected himself to questioning by the crown.

Stratton said Lamarre-Condon was not obliged to assist the prosecution. “He didn't have to give evidence. He could have said to the crown, ‘Do your worst but I'm not going to help you’,” Stratton told the New South Wales supreme court.

Stratton used Lamarre-Condon's decision to testify to support his request that jurors accept the testimony as truthful. The lawyer described the former officer's choice to enter the witness box as a voluntary act made after he could have remained silent.

Stratton told the court: “He didn't do that. He took that long walk to the witness box, and he did it voluntarily and he subjected himself to a detailed, fierce cross-examination by a very experienced crown prosecutor that went over six days.”

The defence claimed the cross-examination neither broke down Lamarre-Condon nor established that he was telling new lies. Stratton said the questioning had not discredited his client or supplied a basis for rejecting his evidence.

Stratton also addressed the legal burden facing the prosecution. “The fact the accused has chosen to give evidence does not mean he needs to prove his case … the onus of proof is always on the crown,” he said.

The trial was expected to wrap up in the coming days, and the jury was soon set to begin deliberations. Justice Natalie Adams was scheduled to spend Tuesday afternoon giving jurors her instructions.
