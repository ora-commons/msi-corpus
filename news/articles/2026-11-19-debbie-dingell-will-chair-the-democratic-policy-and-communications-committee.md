## McDonald Rivet will succeed Susie Lee in House Democrats’ battleground role

Rep. Debbie Dingell (D-Ann Arbor) will continue as chair of the Democratic Policy and Communications Committee, and Rep. Kristen McDonald Rivet (D-Bay City) has secured the position of House Democrats’ Battleground Leadership Representative. The appointments come as Democrats prepare to take control of the U.S. House in the next congressional term and caucus members finalize leadership roles.

## Dingell’s policy and communication agenda

Her office announced Thursday that she would once again serve as chair, continuing a tenure that began in 2024.

Dingell said, “The most important work we will do with our new Democratic majority is pass policies that lower costs and help people who are hurting.”

Dingell also said the work would remain unfinished if people never hear about it, cannot see how it changes their lives, or do not believe anyone in Washington is listening. She said Democratic leadership needed to provide members with support, resources and tools to communicate effectively with their distinct districts.

“And that’s the work I will continue to do in the new Congress,” Dingell said.

## McDonald Rivet takes the battleground leadership role

McDonald Rivet secured the position after making her case to her Democratic colleagues in a letter on Nov. 5. She will succeed Rep. Susie Lee (D-Nev.), who created the role in 2022. Lee created it to ensure House Democrats representing hotly contested congressional districts have a seat at the leadership table.

“The American people have demanded real change that will actually make their lives better. They sent us an amazing class of members for the 120th Congress,” McDonald Rivet said in a statement.

She added, “It’s my honor to serve as this group’s representative to leadership. Now the work begins: to do everything we can to deliver on the opportunity we’ve been given.”

## Rivet’s district and election background

McDonald Rivet spoke at her swearing-in ceremony in Flint, Michigan, on Jan. 27, 2025.

She represents Michigan’s 8th Congressional District, which Democrats and Republicans alike targeted as a key district during the 2024 election. She was recently elected to a second term.

Her opponent, Thomas Smith, was a retired GM engineer. Smith dropped out of the Republican primary weeks before voters selected him to represent the party in the general election, and McDonald Rivet defeated him.

The announcements place two Michigan Democrats in House Democratic roles tied to policy, communications and contested districts as the caucus finalizes its leadership ahead of the next congressional term.
