## Trump, Xi and Putin end Shenzhen summit with divergent priorities

- Trump sought nuclear weapons reductions and said he wanted to prevent Iran from obtaining nuclear weapons.
- Putin sought U.S. and Chinese support for a settlement tilted in Moscow's favor in the war in Ukraine.

## The leaders offered broad peace messages but few concrete solutions

President Trump joined Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen, China, on Thursday, capping two days of talks that brought the three countries' leaders together in person for the first time in American history. NPR reported that the peace language contrasted with the conflicts associated with the leaders' governments. Trump entered focused on reducing nuclear weapons and preventing Iran from obtaining them, Putin on a Ukraine settlement favorable to Moscow, and Xi on showcasing China's growing global clout through general calls for peace and economic stability.

Xi opened before cameras with a statement linking great-power relations to global security. “History tells us when great powers are at peace, the world is secure,” he said. “When great powers clash, the world is in chaos.” Putin said their nations could make a “significant contribution to strengthening security and stability in the world.” Trump said, “In that sense and I think in lots of other ways, we are making history,” and called cooperation among the three countries a “special responsibility.”

Despite the Chinese hosts' description of the event as a summit of “groundbreaking significance,” the formal sessions were relatively brief. Trump's meeting with Putin lasted 42 minutes, his talks with Xi about an hour and the trilateral group session an hour and a half. Simultaneous interpretation took part of the time.

The leaders also displayed personal familiarity. Greeting Putin before their bilateral meeting, Trump said, “Who's he? He's my friend.” Trump added, “We've known each other for a long time” and “I think a lot of wonderful things can come of this. Or at least start.” To Xi, he said, “as long as you and I are in charge of things, we're never going to have a problem.” NPR interpreted the remarks as highlighting the approaching change in Washington, with two years remaining in Trump's term and Democrats taking control of Congress in January.

The gathering was first proposed by Putin during an October phone call with Trump, when China was preparing to host the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation conference in November in Shenzhen. NPR said the summit also showed that, with Trump and Xi's help, Putin had chipped away at Western attempts to isolate him politically over his invasion of Ukraine. Russian state media repeatedly aired footage of the three leaders together as proof that Russia had regained its rightful place among global superpowers.

The arrival and seating arrangements supplied a more complicated picture of power. Xi greeted Trump personally on the tarmac, returning a gesture Trump had made during a recent visit to Washington, while Putin received a smaller delegation in Shenzhen. Trump was placed beside Xi for the APEC group photo and dinner, with Putin at a separate table. NPR raised the possibility that the arrangement was a slight to Putin, an effort to flatter Trump or a reflection of Russia's vastly weaker economy, but the report did not settle among those explanations.

Xi and Putin also met privately for two hours on Wednesday, a gathering China-Russia watchers estimated was their 50th meeting in all. Their public speeches similarly invoked the dawn of a “multipolar world” and criticized sanctions as a sign of Western influence on the wane.

Trump hoped to include China in arms-control talks, which he calls “denuclearization,” at a time when key landmark nuclear treaties between the United States and Russia had all expired. Beijing had repeatedly rebuffed U.S. attempts to bring China into negotiations with Russia, noting that its arsenal remained far below those of Cold War-era rivals Moscow and Washington. Xi also insisted Europe have a seat at the table in any negotiated settlement over Ukraine.

Putin's aims for Ukraine went further. He sought U.S. and Chinese backing for a settlement tilted in Moscow's favor, including a redrawn security map for Europe that would amount to a rollback of NATO from Eastern Europe. Trump had largely relied on Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner to work through the complexities of any peace deal. After Trump's meeting with Putin, a Kremlin aide gave no further detail, saying Ukraine had been the focus and that Putin had left “in an excellent mood.”

Hours earlier, Ukraine carried out a missile strike on the bridge linking mainland Russia to the annexed Crimean peninsula for the third time that week, according to NPR. The report said the strike effectively disabled the structure for months. NPR described the bridge as a symbol of Russia's claim to territory illegally seized from Ukraine in 2014.

One topic was explicitly avoided. Trump refused to engage with journalists' questions about China's alleged role in an espionage plot to smuggle in high-end Nvidia chips.

From the outset, critics voiced concern that Trump, Xi and Putin would seek to carve the world into each country's perceived sphere of influence. In Russia, the event was billed as a potential “new Yalta,” a reference to 1945, when the leaders of the United States, Soviet Union and United Kingdom hastily agreed on a post-World War II order for Europe. The Shenzhen summit instead reflected the participants' far more fragmented priorities, NPR reported.

NPR said the summit's key deliverable was arguably the image itself: the president of the United States gathering with the leaders of China and Russia for the first time in American history. Xi and Putin praised great-power cooperation, Trump called the gathering historic and described cooperation as a shared responsibility, but the leaders offered few solutions toward peace.
