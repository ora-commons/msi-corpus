## More shoppers choose stores while AI becomes a holiday buying tool

Americans are expected to spend more on Black Friday than on any other day of the year, according to the holiday-season forecasts discussed by NPR business correspondent Alina Selyukh. The expectations point to another season of record spending through the end of the holiday season, though surveys indicate consumers may need to reduce spending elsewhere to finance gifts and family activities.

Selyukh said she has made similar Black Friday forecasts for several years. This year, Adobe Analytics, which tracks online spending, projects online sales will jump 6.7% compared with last year. Visa predicts holiday spending will grow 4.5%, and the National Retail Federation, described as the most commonly cited forecaster, puts growth at 4.2%.

The forecasts come as households confront inflation. Surveys cited by Selyukh indicate shoppers may need to cut back in other categories to maintain holiday spending, with that pressure more pronounced at lower income levels. National Retail Federation chief economist Mark Matthews said families are protecting spending on one another even as they have less room for other purchases.

Matthews said, "There's almost a moat around that form of spending. They care about spending on their family. And they're choosing to do that rather than go for a meal out. Or maybe they'll travel a little bit less."

Selyukh identified gas prices and housing costs, with groceries also contributing to some extent, as the biggest current drivers of inflation. Goods such as gifts have not been rising in price as dramatically. Survey respondents said they might tighten budgets but would not skip the holidays, continuing a recurring pattern in which celebration spending receives priority.

The shopping channel is also changing. The National Retail Federation found that the majority of people still do their holiday shopping online, but that share is declining. Selyukh said the pattern is moving back toward the way it was about 10 years ago, with more shoppers choosing to shop in stores.

She said shoppers want to see products, touch them and try them on, as well as take part in the holiday atmosphere.

Artificial intelligence is playing a larger role at the same time. A majority of shoppers said they expect to use AI to find the right gift or the best deal, Selyukh said. NPR colleague Stephan Bisaha has reported on shopping with AI agents and advised consumers to think carefully before handing over a credit card because the technology remains new and can make very expensive mistakes.

Visa tested another aspect of AI-assisted gifting by asking what people would do if AI helped them choose a gift. The survey found 41% would not tell the recipient that they had outsourced their gift ideas.

Selyukh said the average expectation is for top discounts of 18% to 30% this year. NPR host Martínez said prices on newly released sneakers were heavily slashed, while expressing uncertainty about whether the discounts would benefit companies.

Taken together, the forecasts describe a holiday season with record spending projections and tight household choices. Shoppers are expected to preserve holiday purchases by scaling back meals, travel and other expenses, while more visit stores and use AI to find gifts and deals.
