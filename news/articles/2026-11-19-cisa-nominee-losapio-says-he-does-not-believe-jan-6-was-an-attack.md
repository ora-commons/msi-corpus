## LoSapio also declines to identify the 2020 popular-vote winner

At his confirmation hearing Thursday, Michael LoSapio, President Donald Trump’s nominee to lead the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, declined to characterize the violence at the U.S. Capitol on Jan. 6, 2021, as an attack. “No, I don’t believe it was an attack,” LoSapio told U.S. Sen. Richard Blumenthal. “I wasn’t involved in politics. I know what I saw on the news.”

The exchange came as Blumenthal, a Democrat from Connecticut, made inquiries about the Capitol violence and the 2020 election part of his questioning for a number of Trump nominees.

It also unfolded as Republicans work to confirm Trump’s picks before Democrats take power in Congress early next year. The White House did not immediately respond to a request for comment on LoSapio’s remark.

Blumenthal also asked LoSapio — currently the chief information security officer at artificial intelligence firm Palantir — who won the popular vote in the 2020 presidential election. Trump has falsely claimed that he won. LoSapio said, “I’m not going to answer the question about an election six years ago,” and later added that his “only involvement” in the election was voting for Trump. His response did not identify the popular-vote winner.

LoSapio would inherit an agency at the center of partisan tensions over the role it should play in combating false claims about voting or election fraud. Formed in 2018 during Trump’s first administration and housed within the Department of Homeland Security, CISA is charged with protecting the country’s dams, nuclear power plants, banks and voting systems.

CISA has received bipartisan praise from many state and local election officials, but Trump and his allies remain angry over its efforts to counter misinformation about the 2020 presidential election and the coronavirus pandemic. LoSapio’s confirmation hearing took place against that backdrop.

The agency’s leadership has also been unsettled. Its first director, Chris Krebs, was fired by Trump after highlighting a statement from election officials that called the 2020 election the “most secure in American history.” CISA has had no Senate-confirmed director for nearly two years: Jen Easterly left the agency as Trump returned to office last year, Nick Andersen has led the agency in an acting capacity since February, and Sean Plankey withdrew in April after his nomination stalled.

LoSapio is not the first Trump nominee to give a similar answer about Jan. 6. At his June confirmation hearing for Department of Justice inspector general, Don R. Berthiaume — also during questioning from Blumenthal — acknowledged “violence on the Capitol grounds” on Jan. 6, 2021, but said, “I don’t know if I would use the term attack.” Several Trump judicial nominees also avoided characterizing the violence as an attack during their confirmation hearings.
