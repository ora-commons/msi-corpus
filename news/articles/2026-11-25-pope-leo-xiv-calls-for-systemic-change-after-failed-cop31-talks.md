## The Antalya talks ended over disputes on fossil-fuel language

Pope Leo XIV expressed concern on Wednesday over the failure of United Nations climate talks to reach an agreement, saying that "extreme environmental events linked to the climate crisis are becoming increasingly frequent." Speaking during his weekly audience in St. Peter's Square in Vatican City, the pope appealed to "never forget the human face of the climate crisis, which affects the lives of millions of people, strikes entire communities and undermines the prospects of future generations."

He called for "fundamental changes in behavior, socioeconomic systems and the governance of our societies," directing his appeal to "the international community and to each individual."

The talks he was addressing, COP31 in Antalya, Turkey, ended Sunday without reaching an overarching agreement. The failure came amid disputes over whether to mention the fossil fuels that cause global warming.

Leo has embraced defense of the environment during his papacy, taking up one of the core teachings of his predecessor, Pope Francis. Francis's landmark encyclical, Laudato Si (Praised Be), cast care for the planet as an urgent, existential moral concern.

During a recent trip to Peru, Leo demanded justice for environmental defenders killed for protecting the Amazon.

Cardinal Pietro Parolin, Leo's No. 2, said in a statement to COP31 that the pope's earlier appeal to "bear in mind the human face of the climate crisis" was a reminder that the talks were not "merely technical … but concern people's lives."

In the Wednesday address, the pope named behavior, socioeconomic systems and governance as the areas requiring change, directing his appeal to "the international community and to each individual."


Pope Leo XIV's appeal came after MSI reported that [UN climate talks collapse in Turkey without agreement on fossil fuels](/articles/2026-11-22-un-climate-talks-collapse-in-turkey-without-agreement-on-fossil-fuels/). The failed COP31 outcome and the unresolved dispute over whether to mention fossil fuels formed the backdrop to the pope's Wednesday address.
