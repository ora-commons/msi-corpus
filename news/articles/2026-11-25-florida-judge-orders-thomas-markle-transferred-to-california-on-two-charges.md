## Grand jury alleges Markle knowingly possessed 35mm negatives depicting two minors

A Florida magistrate judge has ordered Thomas Markle, the father of Meghan, the Duchess of Sussex, to be transferred from custody in Florida to California to face formal charges of possessing child sex abuse images. The transportation order was issued by Magistrate Judge Lindsay Griffin on Wednesday, according to documents seen by the Press Association.

A federal grand jury on the west coast has paved the way for Markle's prosecution, charging him with two counts of possession of child pornography. The 82-year-old is expected to appear before a Los Angeles judge in the coming weeks, where he will hear the charges and is likely to be asked to enter a plea.

The grand jury filing alleges that on or about 30 October and 4 November, Markle "knowingly possessed 35mm film negatives that contained child pornography." The filing says the negatives include images that "involved a prepubescent minor and a minor who had not yet attained the age of 12 years old." If convicted, Markle faces up to 20 years in prison on each count.

FBI agents detained Markle on 6 November after seizing a cache of material from two Los Angeles storage units he had rented for nearly two decades. He remains in custody in Florida.

Markle came under investigation after his son, Thomas Markle Jr, reported him to police in the Philippines and to the FBI last month. Markle Jr allegedly found illegal images in his father's belongings in Cebu City. He told the Sun newspaper that he had tipped off authorities about the Los Angeles storage facilities.

After his arrest in Florida, Markle was initially released on conditional bail. He was detained again just days later after removing his electronic monitoring tag.

In an interview, Markle said he did not understand the allegations against him. He also said he did not have "any child porn" and "no pictures of naked kids."

The US attorney's office for the central district of California said: "Every defendant is presumed to be innocent until and unless proven guilty in court."

Meghan, who moved back to the UK with the Duke of Sussex in August, has previously said she was "deeply distressed" by the accusations against her father, from whom she is estranged.

She has also said she was sent a letter via the Metropolitan Police and Royalty and Specialist Protection in 2019 that made "troubling and disturbing allegations" about her father, and that she was given assurances the allegations were being investigated.

The Met has admitted that it never shared the details with other law enforcement agencies. The Independent Office for Police Conduct is now investigating the force's conduct.
