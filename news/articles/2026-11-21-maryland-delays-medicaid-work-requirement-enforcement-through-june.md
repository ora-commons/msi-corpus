## Maryland delays Medicaid work-requirement enforcement through June

**Subtype:** fact

- The Maryland Department of Health adopted an optional federal hold harmless period allowing Medicaid recipients to retain coverage through June despite unmet work requirements.
- Federal Medicaid rules require recipients to prove they are working or in school at least 80 hours a month.
- Federal Medicaid rules require some recipients to prove eligibility twice a year instead of annually.

## Other federal eligibility and enrollment changes remain set for January 1, 2027

Maryland Department of Health officials said Friday that Medicaid recipients in the state can keep their coverage through June even if they do not meet new work requirements scheduled to begin in 2027. The state is using an optional six-month hold harmless period recently offered by the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services, which gives officials and recipients additional time to adjust.

The Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services announced last week that states could use an optional hold harmless period to work through implementation challenges before ending coverage for people who fail the work requirements. The option gives states and affected residents an additional six months to adjust.

The extension applies only to enforcement of the federal Medicaid work requirements. In its Friday notice, the state health department said other federal eligibility and enrollment changes scheduled for January 1, 2027, will be implemented. Maryland said it would continue implementing those requirements under federal law while working to ensure eligible residents maintain access to health coverage.

Under the federal rules, recipients must prove they are working or in school at least 80 hours a month. Some recipients also must prove eligibility twice a year rather than annually. The changes came through HR 1, budget reconciliation legislation Congress passed in August 2025 that is sometimes called the “One Big Beautiful Bill Act.”

State officials and healthcare advocates warned that the new requirements could cost thousands of Marylanders their Medicaid coverage. Medicaid is the joint federal-state health insurance plan for low-income families and households facing other challenges.

Healthcare advocates welcomed the delay but continued to worry about Medicaid coverage once the hold harmless period lifts in July. Gene Ransom, CEO of MedChi, the Maryland State Medical Society, said in a written statement Friday: “No Marylander should lose access to needed medical care simply because of paperwork or administrative barriers.”

Ransom said the pause gives Maryland time to implement the rules but does not resolve concerns about potential coverage losses. “This six-month period gives the state an important opportunity to get the implementation right, but it does not eliminate our concerns about the potential loss of coverage under HR 1,” he said.

Without an adequate runway to adopt the requirements, state officials and healthcare advocates warned that upwards of 100,000 people from Maryland could lose coverage. They said recipients struggled to keep up with the increased paperwork while state agencies rushed to adapt to the additional administrative burden.

The six-month period gives Maryland more time to adjust its operations and gives recipients more time to prepare for the monthly work-or-school documentation and twice-yearly eligibility reviews. The hold harmless protection ends in July, when enforcement of the work requirements resumes.
