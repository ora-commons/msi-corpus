## Rubio names Kirsten Davies as national cyber director

Sean Cairncross, the U.S. government's top cybersecurity official, is leaving his post after a string of technology outages that hit the National Security Agency, the U.S. air traffic control system and some banks. He has served as national cyber director since August 2025.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in a post on X that Department of Defense chief information officer Kirsten Davies is the new national cyber director.

The White House has not publicly confirmed Cairncross's departure or given a reason for it.

CBS, the BBC's U.S. news partner, reported that Cairncross had been fired after losing the confidence of White House chief of staff Stephen Miller. CBS and AFP said sources had told them the firing was supported by President Donald Trump. The story was first reported by Axios.

BBC News also linked related coverage saying U.S. bank services were returning to normal after a major outage, the NSA's classified internal network was reportedly offline, and Microsoft said services were fully restored after a day-long global outage.

Earlier this week, Rubio said there was "zero evidence" the outages were connected, adding "we are not under systemic attack". The report said the rapid succession of outages nevertheless left some people worried about the safety of technology heavily relied on in all aspects of daily life.

The report also raised concerns about the ability of advanced AI models, mostly from U.S. companies, to discover vulnerabilities that humans would not be able to find on their own. The report framed possible explanations as hacks, AI or overdue updates and asked why so many outages had occurred in quick succession.

Cairncross was appointed by the Trump administration. Before his latest return to government, he was a lawyer in the Republican Party and then the Republican National Committee's chief operating officer.

During the first Trump administration, Cairncross was part of the White House chief of staff team. He later worked in the private sector before returning to government in August 2025.
