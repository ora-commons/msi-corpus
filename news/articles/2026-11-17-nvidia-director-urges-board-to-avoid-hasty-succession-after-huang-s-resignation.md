## Huang's first remarks make return conditional and admit 'serious mistakes'

In a Monday letter that circulated widely among Nvidia executives and was seen by The Wall Street Journal, a high-level Nvidia employee told board chairman Harvey Jones that Nvidia could not rush a leadership transition. The employee, Nader Khalil, director of developer technology, called for Huang’s return as CEO.

“I believe Nvidia can one day find a successor, but we cannot make that transition haphazardly,” Khalil wrote. “Nvidia will not navigate the crucial next couple of years well without Jensen.”

Khalil also said the entire advanced computing ecosystem and AI market depend on Jensen’s “visionary leadership,” according to the letter. His warning coupled the possibility of a future successor with his assessment that Nvidia could not navigate the next crucial years without Huang.

In his first public remarks since stepping down Thursday, Huang made any return contingent on the board finding it in the best interest of shareholders, employees, the company and America. “Since my departure, I’ve seen an outpouring of calls from Nvidia’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO,” Huang said in a short statement. “If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.”

In the same statement, Huang apologized anew to his family and staff for his infidelities and admitted that he had made “serious mistakes” in his personal life. “I did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at Nvidia,” Huang wrote. “I created problems for the Board that I deeply regret. I own my mistakes completely.”

Huang also said he was proud to be an American and praised the country for enabling his career. “Nowhere but in America could a company like Nvidia be possible,” he wrote.

The Wall Street Journal reported separately that Nvidia’s board of directors was not considering reinstating Huang as chief executive, despite calls for his return from some investors and employees.

During the week after Huang stepped down, thousands of Nvidia employees, prominent investors and technology billionaires such as Elon Musk weighed in on his future. President Trump told reporters on Monday that he would support Huang’s reinstatement as chief executive. “I would certainly not mind seeing that,” Trump said, adding that he believed Huang had “learned a big lesson.”

The Wall Street Journal also reported that the revelations surrounding Huang’s resignation prompted Nvidia’s board to announce early Tuesday that it had formed a new committee to oversee the company’s national security policies and compliance with export controls.

Huang resigned Thursday after news reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese spy and another with an employee, The Wall Street Journal reported. On Nov. 8, Huang apologized to employees for marital infidelity, and Nvidia’s board began an investigation into whether he had shared secrets with Alice Huang while they were having an affair. “I know an apology cannot undo what I have done,” he wrote at the time. “I hope our family will heal and that, over time, I can earn back your trust.”

His resignation had ended a 33-year career as Nvidia’s first and only chief executive. Huang’s Tuesday statement did not reinstate him; it left any return to a board that The Wall Street Journal reported was not considering reinstatement.
