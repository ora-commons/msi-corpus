## Spanish high court pauses extradition review over translation dispute

Spain released activist and donor James “Fergie” Chambers on conditional bail from Madrid’s Aranjuez prison on Friday after a Spanish appeals court acted on problems in the legally required translation into Spanish of core documents in a US extradition request. A separate three-judge panel at Spain’s high court had paused the proceedings on Thursday, extending its deadline indefinitely while the judges evaluate the translation changes. The US request remained undecided, and the Thursday decision gave the prosecutor an opportunity to respond in writing.

The appeals court said the translation issues caused “a diminishment, at least for the time being, in the solidity or foundation of the extradition request.” The order concerned the legally required translation into Spanish of the request’s core documents, an issue first reported by The Guardian. Chambers had been detained since 10 July, when Spanish authorities arrested him in Ibiza.

The Guardian described Chambers’s case as the first known instance of the United States seeking to extradite a citizen over alleged support for Hamas. The allegation is apparently tied to an international money-laundering charge in the criminal complaint underpinning the request and to Chambers’s donations to organizations helping Palestinians in Gaza.

Speaking outside Madrid’s Aranjuez prison upon his release on Friday, Chambers called the charges against him “baseless” and said his release was “hopefully the first step in recognizing that internationally.” He also said: “Spain is a country that has stood on the right side of this most important issue of our time, that has stood on the right side of the genocide, and where this is some reason and some justice.”

Separately, the Irish Garda Síochána, acting on a request from US authorities, raided a Dublin house owned by Chambers and Schnabel on Wednesday, destroying the front door and removing electronic items and other possessions, according to their attorney in Ireland, Ciarán Mulholland.

Schnabel, who is five months pregnant, said: “It is difficult to see this raid as anything other than a desperate fishing expedition by the US authorities to find evidence to bolster their case against Fergie.” Mulholland said the raid resulted from a “mutual legal assistance” request from the US and that such a request “wouldn’t give [the Garda Síochána] permission to kick in the doors of someone’s dwelling in Ireland.”

“It’s unusual for the Irish state to be acting in such a clandestine manner,” Mulholland said. He has asked Irish authorities to produce a search warrant and an itemized list of what was taken. “My fear is that whatever was taken has been put on a plane and taken to the US,” he added. “This [extradition case] shows the complete disregard for due process … that the Trump administration seems to have,” Mulholland said.

The background to the high court’s pause was a dispute over alterations the US made to the original, machine-made translation. The Guardian detailed four examples of the alterations on 7 November. The US admitted altering the translation on 17 November, cited the same four examples and described the changes as “substantial [and] inadvertent.” Chambers’s defense team has asserted for weeks that there are as many as 30 alterations.

The defense team submitted a 345-page report identifying 30 “incriminating changes” that Trump administration officials made to a translation prepared by DeepL, “an AI-first translation platform,” according to its website. The panel had been expected to rule on the extradition that week following a 13 November hearing.

Aitor Martínez, a member of Chambers’s defense team, told The Guardian earlier in the week that the changes introduced prejudicial notions with specific legal implications that wound up in the Spanish prosecutor’s report. The judges must consider the prosecutor’s report in deciding on the extradition request. The decision also gives the prosecutor an opportunity to address the issue in writing, after dismissing it at the 13 November hearing by referring to “supposed translation errors.”

The Guardian reported that Chambers’s views and donations to progressive and humanitarian projects have been extensively profiled in US media, including his public comments on Israel and Gaza. A week after the 7 October 2023 Hamas attacks on Israel, Chambers posted on X about Israel’s response: “Stop calling it ‘collective punishment’/There’s nothing to be ‘punished’ for/No faction of the Palestinian resistance, Hamas or other, has done *anything* wrong/It’s simply genocide/Like it has been.” A Guardian photograph showed Chambers in Tunis in 2024.

Chambers is an heir to $250m from his family’s business, Cox Enterprises. His defense team has asserted throughout the case that US authorities are seeking his extradition because of his views.

At the end of the reported week, Spain’s high court had not ruled on the extradition request. The decision gave the prosecutor an opportunity to respond in writing, and Mulholland was still seeking the search warrant and itemized inventory for the Dublin search.
