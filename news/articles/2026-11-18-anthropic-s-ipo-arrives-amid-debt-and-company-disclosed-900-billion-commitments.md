## Newly elected Democrats call for tighter rules on the AI industry

Anthropic's initial public offering is set to begin trading on Nasdaq on Thursday, with 825 million shares slated to trade under the ticker symbol ANT, as the company carries $6.2 billion in debt that its financial filings describe as “substantial.” Anthropic said it has already signed leases and other deals requiring $900 billion in computing and infrastructure spending in coming years, including $70 billion over the next 12 months. The offering comes as Dario Amodei cautioned in September that the AI industry should slow its fast-moving development, while the Associated Press reported that newly elected Democrats are calling for tighter rules on the AI industry amid opposition to data centers.

The Associated Press, reporting from Oakland, California, and citing multiple reports, said Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 per share on Wednesday, above the $70 to $78 range it had set, valuing the San Francisco-based maker of the Claude AI tool at $1.5 trillion. The AP said the higher-than-anticipated price signaled broad investor enthusiasm for AI stocks even amid some market uncertainty stemming from the ouster of former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and worries about an AI bubble. The report described Huang, one of the industry's biggest cheerleaders, as falling from prominence over extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy.

Anthropic is offering 825 million shares that are set to begin trading Thursday on Nasdaq under ticker symbol ANT in the company's Wall Street debut. The company is also selling another $26 billion of stock in a private placement. The AP said Anthropic pressed ahead despite investor jitters over market volatility, possible stricter regulation of AI companies by a Democratic-led Congress next year and growing anti-AI sentiment among the public.

The IPO would also let Anthropic employees begin selling vested stock, a payout the AP said would make many of them millionaires. The offering could give Anthropic resources to accelerate expansion and potentially add billions of dollars to the fortunes of CEO Dario Amodei and his sister and co-founder, Daniela Amodei.

Anthropic already has taken on $6.2 billion in debt, which its financial filings acknowledge is “substantial.” The AP said the company is unprofitable, at least under standard accounting rules, suggesting its resources are limited for paying interest and principal.

The AP said Anthropic has ample cash for now but would likely run through it quickly if it cannot raise more money soon, given its outsized spending plans. Anthropic said it has already signed leases and other deals requiring $900 billion on computing power and infrastructure in the coming years, including $70 billion in the next 12 months.

An IPO is essentially a way to raise money instead of going deeper into debt. The AP said borrowing has become more expensive, partly because of inflation fears and partly because of the AI sector's massive cash needs, which have drawn comparisons to the Gilded Age splurge on railroads. Lenders fear AI companies have taken on too much debt to expand while having no clear path to profitability, the AP reported.

## Regulation, competition and investor demand

The Associated Press reported that the regulatory environment for Anthropic could change once the new Congress is in session. Newly elected Democrats are calling for tighter rules on the AI industry amid a national backlash against data centers, giving new impetus to a congressional effort begun last year to enact a national moratorium on new data centers.

Texas and Georgia, two big states offering incentives for data centers, just elected Democratic governors who vowed to stop new construction, the AP reported.

Anthropic was formed in 2021 by former OpenAI leaders and is locked in a fight for dominance with OpenAI, the AP said. Both companies have plenty of other competitors, including Google, startups in the U.S. and China, and SpaceX, which has an AI business. Anthropic, OpenAI and SpaceX's AI division have all lost more money than they have made because of the huge costs associated with building AI, fueling growing concerns that a bubble is forming in the sector, the AP reported.

Demand for AI shares could drop or even collapse, the AP said, as investors remain anxious about the sector's massive financing needs. Several technology companies have pulled IPO plans recently, and OpenAI is not planning to go public until next year.

## Dario Amodei's safety warning and Anthropic's benefit trust

Dario Amodei cautioned in September that the AI industry should slow its fast-moving development to give safety measures time to catch up. Without such a slowdown, he warned, AI could become capable of letting loose a swarm of agents that could take over the entire internet within six to 12 months.

The AP said investors will have less direct control than at a conventional company, where the main goal is generally to benefit owners through profits and growth. Anthropic says it will consider stockholders' interests but balance them against another fundamental goal: “the responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity.” Like its rival OpenAI, Anthropic is a public-benefit corporation, a legal designation used by companies that pledge to balance shareholder interests with the public good. How that will play out for companies worth potentially trillions of dollars is uncharted territory, the AP said.

Anthropic has set up a long-term benefit trust with the authority to elect or remove a majority of the board of directors. The trust will not run Anthropic's day-to-day operations, the AP said. Its influence means Anthropic could choose strategies that may not make the most money for investors.

Associated Press business writers Bernard Condon and Michelle Chapman contributed to this story.
