## CalHR remains available; SEIU ready to escalate if state refuses to negotiate fairly

California state workers began a one-day strike Wednesday in major cities across the state to protest what they say are unfair labor practices by Gov. Gavin Newsom's government.

The workers' contract expired June 30, and the Service Employees International Union said Newsom's government rejected the union's proposals. SEIU Local 1000 members had been without a contract for over three months, the union said. In a statement on its website, the union said: "The Newsom administration waited until the last minute to reject our proposals in bulk. Now, SEIU Local 1000 members have been without a contract for over three months. Enough is enough!" Cal Matters reported that the union was demanding better pay, more affordable healthcare coverage and more flexibility to work from home.

CalHR, the state agency that handles labor negotiations, said it "remains focused on achieving a fair and responsible agreement and continues to be available at the bargaining table." The union said it was prepared to "escalate and hold the line" if the state refuses to "negotiate fairly."

Union president Anica Walls asked Californians "to give our employees some grace," Cal Matters reported. "If you happen to go to the DMV office and the services are limited, make an appointment for another day," Walls said.

SEIU last authorized a strike in 2016 but did not go through with it.

Tom Perata, a former state senator, told Cal Matters the union was taking a risk by going on strike. Perata said he supports labor and wants the workers to get a good deal. He also said: "Somebody ought to pay attention to them. The strike costs those people a lot more than anyone else."

Walls also said: "Realize that we are standing up for 100,000 state workers who have not had a good living wage. ... It's not just about state employees. It really is about the working class."
