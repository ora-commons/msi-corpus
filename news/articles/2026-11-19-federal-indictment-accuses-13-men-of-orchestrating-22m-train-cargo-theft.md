## Alleged method: brakes busted, goods moved to trucks for resale

LOS ANGELES — A federal indictment unsealed Thursday charges 13 men with conspiracy and theft of interstate railroad cargo, alleging that they organized a train heist operation that took more than $22 million in cargo from major retailers. The stolen goods included Nike sneakers, Crocs footwear, power tools and other consumer goods.

The indictment alleges the group targeted more than three dozen trains carrying goods imported into the United States through Los Angeles and Long Beach, which are home to the busiest ports in the country. The freight those trains carry moves to stores and customers around the country.

Rail shipments have become a prominent target for large-scale theft in recent years, especially at large U.S. ports that receive hundreds of millions of dollars of shipments every day. The relatively low-tech robberies have demanded attention from the world's largest retailers, including Amazon.

According to the indictment, the men would disable the trains at a planned location by busting the brakes, then offload the goods onto trucks. The stolen merchandise was then peddled to an unnamed company that would resell it for profit.

In group chats, the men shared numerous photos and videos of themselves busting into the hulking train cars and posing with wads of cash and stolen merchandise, the indictment said.

They also shared AI photos of themselves, captioned “Los Trenes” — a phrase that means the trains in Spanish — beneath glamorized photos of men throwing boxes of shoes off trains into nearby cars while police helicopters descended overhead.

The 13 men were indicted for conspiracy and theft of interstate railroad cargo. Two of them were also charged with violence against railroad carriers.

If convicted, the men could face anywhere from five to 20 years in federal prison.

First Assistant United States Attorney Bill Essayli said, “The scale of the theft is staggering,” and promised to prosecute any future efforts to steal the merchandise.
