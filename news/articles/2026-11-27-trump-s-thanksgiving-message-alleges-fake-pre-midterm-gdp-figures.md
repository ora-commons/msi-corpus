## Holiday greeting called the upward GDP revision 'three weeks late'

President Donald Trump used his Thanksgiving message on Truth Social to wish a happy Thanksgiving “to even those who issued fake Economic Numbers, just prior to the Election, and corrected them yesterday, three weeks late, showing that the GDP was actually 4.3%, versus 3.4%.” The Guardian said the holiday greeting alleged, without evidence, that figures published by the US federal government before this month’s midterm elections were fake.

Trump’s economic accusation was tied to the timing of the estimate. He asked: “Why wasn’t the correct number issued before the Election? I am asking for an immediate investigation of this situation,” he wrote on Truth Social. The Guardian described the revision as an upward adjustment from 3.4% to 4.3%. Trump presented the three-week delay between the election and the release of the revised figure as grounds for questioning the earlier estimate.

Trump also described the scale and purpose of the alleged error. He wrote: “This is not a small error, this is a massive, and purposeful, error”. His wording characterized the change as both substantial and intentional.

According to Trump, the government revised the GDP figure from 3.4% to 4.3% after the election. His requested remedy was an investigation into why the earlier estimate had not been issued before voters went to the polls. The Guardian’s account did not identify evidence that the pre-election figure was false or that the revision was politically motivated.

The Thanksgiving message also addressed the “Radical Far Left” and wealth redistribution. Trump wished “a very Happy Thanksgiving to all of those people that have worked so hard to bring our Country to the Radical Far Left,” as well as “those who want to redistribute Property, so that all of the hardworking people that have built up, over a lifetime, a certain Wealth, will now be forced to distribute it throughout the Country, even to those who refuse to work.” The economic accusation and those political attacks appeared in the same holiday greeting.

The investigation demand came after voters roundly rejected Trump’s Republican party in the midterm elections. The Guardian said Trump had tried repeatedly to challenge trust in economic data that did not support his claims that a historic boom was under way in the United States.

Revisions to official economic figures are commonplace, The Guardian said. The report did not identify evidence supporting Trump’s claim that the earlier number was false; it instead noted the routine nature of revisions and Trump’s repeated challenges to economic data that did not support his boom claims.
