## Chinese records do not establish that Megaspeed's chips reached Kang Wei's centers

The Wall Street Journal reported that Kang Wei built a network of Chinese government-backed data centers with proposed investments topping $9 billion from 2021 to 2024. His wife, Alice Huang, cultivated a romantic relationship with then-Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang to secure advanced-computing chips for Megaspeed. The newspaper characterized the couple's work as two tracks aimed at the same goal of advancing China's artificial-intelligence capabilities ahead of the United States.

U.S. authorities are investigating whether export controls on Nvidia's most powerful chips were violated, and Jensen Huang has since resigned from Nvidia. Public scrutiny followed news of the couple's romantic relationship and reports that U.S. authorities suspected Alice Huang was providing information to Chinese intelligence agencies, The Wall Street Journal said. Chinese public records do not establish that Beijing directed Megaspeed's procurement or that the chips ended up in data centers linked to Kang Wei.

Chinese officials hailed Kang's projects as part of China's national effort to expand computing power across the country. The Wall Street Journal said the details of Kang's work for state-backed AI efforts in China had not previously been published. The roots of his data-center business trace to late 2021, well before the U.S. began restricting sales of advanced AI chips to China. At the center of the network was Century Longteng, a company headquartered in Shanghai.

A late-2021 regulatory filing showed Century Longteng's investors included a local Shanghai government body with a 10% stake and an investment vehicle for the private-equity arm of Kang's main business venture, Hexi Capital, with 27%. A controlling 63% stake was held by Century Huatong, a Shenzhen-listed conglomerate known primarily for online gaming that had also expanded into data-center infrastructure. Kang later assumed an executive role at Century Longteng, which did not respond to a request for comment.

In early 2022, Century Longteng signed a framework agreement with the Chinese telecommunications giant Huawei Technologies to collaborate on cloud data centers, energy infrastructure and computing, according to a Huawei announcement. At a signing ceremony, Kang stood alongside Huawei's senior executives. By then, the U.S. had already blacklisted Huawei, barring it from accessing American technologies. The agreement was the first in a series of business deals that gave Century Longteng a sprawling portfolio of data-center investment backed by the Chinese government.

A publicly announced project in summer 2022 was in Wuhu, a city of nearly four million people on the banks of the Yangtze River in east-central China. Century Longteng agreed to build what government records describe as an “intelligent-computing industrial park.” The $4.5 billion planned complex included eight data-center buildings across nearly 4.1 million square feet, larger than 70 football fields, and some 24,000 server racks. Wuhu government records showed the project received state support for electricity and infrastructure under Beijing's “East Data, West Computing” strategy, a plan to spread computing capacity across the country. As of April, at least one data-center building, roughly 500,000 square feet, had been registered as completed in Wuhu.

In July 2024, Kang appeared at the groundbreaking of a second major project, a roughly $5 billion data-center development in Liangshan, a remote area of Sichuan province in southwestern China. Local official accounts identified him as chairman of a Century Longteng-affiliated data-services unit. Corporate filings also linked Century Longteng interests to yet another data-center development in Shenzhen, the southern tech hub.

As Kang built the data-center network, Alice Huang was working to establish a pipeline of Nvidia chips in 2023 and pursuing a romantic relationship with Jensen Huang. By November 2023, Megaspeed had told Nvidia that Alibaba, China's giant online retailer, would be an anchor customer and had earned a designation as an Nvidia cloud partner, giving it priority access to chips, The Wall Street Journal previously reported.

Alice Huang's registered directorship at Megaspeed ended that month amid an ownership restructuring, but her relationship with Jensen Huang continued. They were photographed together at a Singapore restaurant in late 2023 and again the following year at the Computex tech conference in Taipei.

Kang connected the two worlds, having known Jensen Huang from both men's days in the videogame industry. Kang told people who knew the couple that he had introduced his wife to Jensen Huang and appeared to accept their romantic relationship, The Wall Street Journal reported.

Between late 2024 and June 2025, the couple offered several data-center projects in Shanghai and Southeast Asia to outside investors, The Wall Street Journal previously reported. They told potential investors that they could expect to triple their money in three years and expressed confidence they had ways to bring Nvidia chips into China.

At least some Nvidia chips obtained by Alice Huang's company, Megaspeed, were shipped to China, The Wall Street Journal reported, citing people familiar with the matter. Chinese public records, including securities filings and government announcements, do not establish that Beijing directed Megaspeed's procurement or that the chips ended up in data centers linked to Kang Wei. The records place the couple on both sides of the U.S.-China technology divide: Chinese state money was invested in Kang's domestic ventures while Megaspeed gained access to chips Washington sought to keep out of China.

In February 2025, Singapore launched an investigation into Megaspeed and its key officers over suspected false declarations on server purchases, the city-state's authorities said. Authorities ultimately found insufficient evidence of domestic criminal offenses but issued Alice Huang a stern warning and barred her from re-entering Singapore. Investigations into Megaspeed and six other individuals remain ongoing, Singapore's Ministry of Home Affairs said.

Kang had been detained in Shanghai earlier this year in connection with stock-market manipulation inquiries, The Wall Street Journal previously reported. He has recently been released and shared a photo of himself at Stanford University with friends, according to people who know the couple.

Attempts to reach Kang and Alice Huang were unsuccessful. Megaspeed stated that it operates as a “legitimate and lawful business” and that Alice Huang currently has no role with the company.
