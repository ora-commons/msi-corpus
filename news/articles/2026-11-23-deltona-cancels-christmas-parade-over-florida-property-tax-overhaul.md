## City expects more than $23 million in cuts through 2030

Deltona officials in Florida have canceled the city's Christmas parade, an event that typically includes floats, marching bands, dancers and Santa Claus appearing in the finale. A photograph credited to Deltona TV shows a float in Deltona's Christmas parade on 13 December 2025. City manager Doc Dougherty said the cancellation and broader reductions in city programming were necessary as Florida's voter-approved Amendment 3 property-tax overhaul takes effect on 1 January.

Florida voters approved Amendment 3 earlier this month with 62% of the vote. The constitutional amendment lowers property taxes on “homestead,” or primary-residence homes, and commercial properties by reducing the cap on annual assessment increases. Drafted by outgoing governor Ron DeSantis, the measure was conceived as an effort to rein in government spending.

Deltona said the amendment's effect on the city's tax base requires immediate reductions. The city said that, unlike other communities in the state, “Deltona’s historic reliance on low property values with little commercial development, the city needs to reduce its budget by approximately $12m this next year and then another $5m the second.”

Deltona is terminating construction contracts and withdrawing vehicle orders to reduce spending and expects to cut more than $23m by 2030. Dougherty warned that all city events, except two self-supporting long-distance running races, are “effectively canceled.” The city said funds would be redirected toward essential services as officials worked to “ensure that Deltona remains a fiscally responsible operation, while trying to meet the needs of our community.”

Dougherty said, “Working through the financial challenges created by Amendment 3 will take time, and there will be many more difficult decisions ahead.” He also said the legislation meant funding for all cities and counties across Florida was being reduced. “It is unfortunate that among all the cities in Central Florida, Deltona will be most affected, which removes millions of dollars’ worth of City services,” he said.

The projected effects extend beyond Deltona. State economists estimated that Florida's cities, counties and special districts could lose $5bn a year, with the annual hit rising to nearly $12bn within five years. The Economist estimated that Amendment 3 would cut 8,000 of the 10,000 homes off the tax roll in Gadsden County, a rural area in the Florida panhandle.

Miami-Dade County Mayor Daniella Levine-Cava said the amendment could cause the county, which includes the city of Miami, to close parks, fire stations and libraries. She estimated that her district could lose $378m in the first year and nearly $700m in the second.

“Property taxes fund the essential local government services we depend on, from garbage pickup to paramedic response,” Levine-Cava said in an interview with WLRN. “A lot of people who support Amendment 3 are under the impression that there’s a plan. There is no plan. We don’t have a source that has been identified by the [Florida] legislature, by the governor or by the amendment itself. So every local government is going to be on their own scrambling to figure out: do we cut here, do we raise there?”

Levine-Cava said the measure would shift costs rather than eliminate them. “There is no free lunch here. There will be a cost shift and not really a tax cut.”
