## Anthropic closes IPO debut at $97 after nearing $2 trillion

## The speed of AI investment growth has fueled ongoing bubble concerns

Anthropic ended its first Nasdaq trading session on Thursday at $97 per share, below an immediate rise to $111.5 that briefly pushed the company's market value to almost $2 trillion. The close left Anthropic with a roughly $1.76 trillion valuation, making the Claude developer one of the world's most valuable public companies and the first purely AI company to go public. Shares had flown past the $83 allocation price, which BBC News said showed plenty of excitement around AI. The offering raised $81bn in a record-breaking stock market debut, and Anthropic also made one of the quickest trips from start-up to IPO in history.

In just a few hours of trading, Anthropic's stock seemed to show that investors remained uncertain about where exactly AI was heading, BBC News reported. Some investors said the debut seemed to be a success as shares rose roughly 17% during the day, but the broader technology market did not perform as well. The Nasdaq Composite Index closed down nearly 2.3%. Alphabet, Google's parent company, fell 3%, and Amazon fell 3.9%.

BBC News reported that Anthropic would remain under intense scrutiny in the months ahead over "the key question surrounding AI: is it all worth it?" BBC News described the build-out as among the most expensive endeavours in the history of technology, with more than $1 trillion spent in the previous year by a handful of major technology companies. The outlet said the speed at which investment had grown had produced ongoing concerns about a financial bubble.

"Bubbles don't burst on the day of the largest deal," Richard Whittle, an economist with the University of Salford, said. "They burst later, if the positive future becomes suspect."

The basics of AI have been around for decades, but the more recent arrival of more powerful computers and related components has allowed more data to be processed than ever. The result has been AI chatbots that simulate human speech and operate more quickly on tasks such as software coding, digital image generation and internet searches. BBC News described the result as a new way of using computers that had led to Anthropic's rapid rise. The company started just five years ago and is best known as the maker of the popular Claude chatbot.

## Wilson sees no obvious peer for Anthropic

As the first purely AI company to go public, Anthropic will almost certainly see significant swings in its share price on the stock market going forward, according to Ben Barringer, who leads technology research at Quilter Cheviot. Barringer said the "initial exuberance" will wear off and pointed to SpaceX's experience earlier this year. Led by Elon Musk, SpaceX shares jumped about 10% on their first trading day, producing a record-breaking $2.2 trillion valuation. Within a month, the shares had fallen below their debut price and had mostly fallen further since.

Most technology companies see their shares fall after reaching the stock market, often after reporting quarterly results for the first time. Many such companies, including Meta, Amazon and Google, have gone on to become the largest and most profitable corporations in the world. BBC News said investors in Anthropic were evaluating a notable technology company with a major valuation and a potentially large business future, as well as the fact that it was the first company of its kind on the stock market.

"There's no obvious peer to compare it to," Neil Wilson, an investor strategist at Saxo UK, said. BBC News reported that, as individual investors, researchers and investors at large funds considered whether Anthropic was worth $1.5 trillion or more, few answers would be available. "We'll have a stab at it," Wilson said. "I don't think anyone really knows."

## Proposed AI laws are among factors that may affect Anthropic's stock

BBC News said several factors were likely to affect Anthropic's success on the stock market in the near term, including new laws governing the use of AI tools. In the United States, where Anthropic is based, Democrats would hold many more positions of power in 2027, the outlet reported. Several politicians had already said they would seek to ensure AI tools were safe and had some guardrails attached.

On Thursday, the same day Anthropic went public, a bipartisan group of lawmakers formed the House AI Safety and Innovation Caucus to work specifically on AI regulation and safety considerations.

BBC News tied the scrutiny to Anthropic's own public warnings about advanced AI risks. Company executives, including co-founder and CEO Dario Amodei, had been outspoken in their purported fears that future AI tools would become so capable they would outsmart humans, to the point where extinction was possible, the outlet reported. Anthropic has always presented itself publicly as an AI company that takes the safety of its technology very seriously.

Daniela Amodei, Anthropic's president and Dario Amodei's sister, said Thursday that the company "had an AI safety team before we launched our first product". She added, "AI carries real risks." Despite the company's statements that rapidly developing technology could be dangerous, Anthropic made one of the quickest trips from start-up to IPO in history.
