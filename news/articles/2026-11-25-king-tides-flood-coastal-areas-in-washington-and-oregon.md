## National Weather Service expects three to four feet of inundation in Washington

Some of the highest tides of the year brought flooding to the Washington and Oregon coasts on Wednesday, the second day of the king-tide event. The high, seasonally occurring tides were not expected to cease until Nov. 30.

In Washington, the National Weather Service issued a flood warning stretching from Grays Harbor County to Whatcom County, the northernmost county along the state’s coast. The warning covers Wednesday, Thursday and Friday.

Oregon also had a National Weather Service flood warning in effect for the northwest coast and the south central coast.

Immediate flooding was expected along the water, but the extent could stretch farther depending on elevation.

Anna Lindman, a meteorologist at the National Weather Service, said forecasters generally anticipated three to four feet of inundation in Washington.

“There could certainly be closed roads,” Lindman said. “We’re just trying to urge people to never drive through water because one, you can’t tell how deep it is, and two, these are strong tides, so there might be a strong current associated with it.”

On Tuesday, the first day the king tides arrived, a video of a man and his dog getting swept up by waves at Cannon Beach circulated online. Both were uninjured. The video demonstrated how strong the pull of the tides can be.

Washington was facing the combination of a storm system, seasonally occurring high waves and El Niño, which warms water and pulls it toward the coast.

Lindman said, “we’re lucky” because the weather event did not turn into a bomb cyclone.
