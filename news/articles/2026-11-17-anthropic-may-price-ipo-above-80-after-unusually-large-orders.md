## Hedge funds placed bids as venture backers plan more investment

People familiar with the matter told The Wall Street Journal that Anthropic expects to price its initial public offering higher than planned after strong investor demand. The maker of the Claude chatbot could price the shares at more than $80 each, above the initial range of $70 to $78, the people said. Advisers have discussed numbers that would put Anthropic's valuation at around $1.5 trillion or $1.6 trillion, compared with roughly $1.33 trillion at the initial range.

Anthropic is not expected to finalize the share price until Wednesday, ahead of its planned Thursday listing on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. One of the people said much depends on the market reaction to Nvidia's Tuesday earnings call. That call was to be the first since the revelation of former Nvidia Chief Executive Jensen Huang's affair with an alleged Chinese spy, one of the people said. The Wall Street Journal reported that Anthropic had been pitching investors as Wall Street and Silicon Valley reacted to Huang's exit from Nvidia.

Anthropic received larger-than-usual orders from hedge funds and similar investors despite sharp volatility in AI-linked stocks over the past month following the midterm elections, the people said. The orders arrived despite the market tumult and upheaval at Nvidia.

Altimeter founder Brad Gerstner said on CNBC on Monday that his firm requested $5 billion worth of Anthropic shares. Investors in IPOs typically submit orders above what they hope to receive because full requests are unlikely to be filled in high-demand offerings such as Anthropic, The Wall Street Journal said.

Anthropic's venture-capital backers, including Bessemer Venture Partners, Menlo Ventures and Accel, plan to increase their investments in the IPO, according to people familiar with the matter. Venture capitalists often exit long-held investments around the time of an IPO, but Anthropic's rapid growth makes its backers' plan unusual, the people said. Because Anthropic is not yet six years old, many of its largest investors have held stakes for two years or less, giving them more room to double down, The Wall Street Journal said.

At the initial price range, Anthropic planned to sell around $61 billion worth of shares, in addition to a $26 billion private placement alongside the offering. Investors in the private placement include Google and Nvidia, The Wall Street Journal said. Anthropic has said it would sell up to 20% of the offering to individual investors.

The retail investor crowd has not clamored for Anthropic shares quite like it did for Elon Musk's SpaceX when the rocket maker staged a record-breaking IPO in June, The Wall Street Journal said. Some individuals said they planned to buy Anthropic shares to avoid being left behind as AI sets the pace for the stock market in coming years.

Bloomberg earlier reported that Anthropic had told investors it expected to price the listing at the high end of the $70-to-$78 range or above. The final price and resulting valuation remained unsettled, with Anthropic not expected to finalize its offering until Wednesday.
