## Democrats win 258 House seats, largest sweep since 1992

**Subtype:** fact

- House Democrats have won at least 258 seats, gaining 44 so far, their largest general election result in decades.
- Democrat Chuck Hubbard defeated Republican Representative Virginia Foxx in North Carolina to push the Democratic tally past 258 on Sunday.

## Jeffries weighs a 100-day affordability push and Trump investigations

House Democrats have secured their best general election result in decades, winning at least 258 seats in a midterm sweep unseen since 1992, according to the Guardian. The party has gained 44 House seats so far, four more than the 40 it picked up in Donald Trump's first midterm in 2018. Republicans will hold at least 173 seats in the minority.

The 258-seat milestone was reached on Sunday after Democrat Chuck Hubbard defeated Republican Representative Virginia Foxx in North Carolina. Four other House races remain outstanding: one each in Minnesota and Tennessee, and two in Louisiana that are heading to a December runoff.

The result sets up divided government, with the Senate also in Democratic hands and a Republican president in the White House. Senate Democrats won a 56-seat majority in the 100-member chamber, their biggest advantage in over a decade.

The Democrats' House majority will be bigger than any the party has seen since Bill Clinton was first elected president in 1992, when it also held 258 seats, or since the election of Barack Obama in 2008, when it held 257 — though not quite the high-water mark of the Franklin D Roosevelt era, when House Democratic majorities swelled past 300.

The party's wins spanned from the strongest Republican states to the midwest and east coast, the Guardian reported, as voters angry over high prices and the Iran war, and concerned over corruption, made clear their discontent with Trump.

Emboldened Democrats are preparing a 100-day agenda to address high costs of living. As part of that agenda, they plan swift votes to reverse the Republicans' Medicaid cuts and restore the Affordable Care Act tax credits that Republicans allowed to expire.

Democrats have also vowed to marshal their subpoena powers and launch a flurry of investigations to exert Congress's role as a check on presidential power. Those plans include probes into how Trump and his family have become wealthier since he returned to office. Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries of New York, who is in line to become House speaker, has not ruled out trying to impeach Trump for a third time.

Representative Jason Crow of Colorado, a former army ranger, said Democrats should welcome the scrutiny that comes with power.

"Like, hell yeah, we should feel pressure," Crow said at the Capitol. "There are people dying in this country and people losing their healthcare, losing their livelihoods, losing their farms, being terrorized from ICE," he said, referring to US Immigration and Customs Enforcement. "If you don't feel a sense of urgency and pressure in this job, and a desperation to deliver for folks, then you don't belong under this Capitol dome."

Representative Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, the progressive New York congresswoman, called the result "a historic moment for our country." She said at the Capitol that it was "one of the largest classes we have seen" and described the numbers as "a mandate for change."

Representative Tom Suozzi, a centrist Democrat from New York and an accountant by profession, said House Democrats should focus 75% of their time on affordability issues and 25% on accountability. "Everything should be tied back to the people – and how it's affecting the people," he said. As Democrats launch investigations into Trump's financial dealings, he said they should be connecting the dots to how those matters affect "people's pocketbooks."

The wide margin gives the new leadership far more room to maneuver than Republicans had. After Republican House speaker Mike Johnson struggled to keep the House functioning for the past two years with a narrow 218-214 majority — meaning any few GOP lawmakers could derail the agenda — Jeffries will be able to lose dozens of Democrats on any given vote and still have the 218 needed for passage.

Jeffries is poised to make history as the first Black speaker of the House if his colleagues elect him to the job on 3 January 2027.

Republicans saw their number of House seats fall to one of the lowest tallies in decades. Representative Jim Jordan of Ohio, who was elected by his colleagues to be the Republican leader in the minority, vowed to fight their way back to power. "Over the next two years, House Republicans will fight for American families," Jordan said.
