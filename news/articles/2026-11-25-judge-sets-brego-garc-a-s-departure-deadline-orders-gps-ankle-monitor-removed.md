## Associated Press timeline records no ruling on Justice Department’s appeal

An unsealed Nov. 20 order set Nov. 24, 2026, as the deadline for Kilmar Ábrego García's departure from the United States. A federal judge issued the deadline and ordered García's GPS ankle monitor removed before he boarded the flight. The Associated Press reported that García left voluntarily from Washington Dulles International Airport that day for Spain.

The order said the government had informed the judge that García was free to self-deport “to any country that is willing to accept him.” The Associated Press said the legal fight galvanized both sides of the debate over President Donald Trump's immigration policies and described the self-deportation as potentially ending “a defining saga” of the Trump administration's immigration crackdown.

García's legal fight had two court tracks. In a Maryland civil case, he challenged the Department of Homeland Security's attempts to deport him to a series of African countries. In Tennessee, the government accused him of human smuggling, and a federal judge dismissed the case after finding evidence of “vindictive prosecution” by the Justice Department.

The Associated Press timeline begins around 2011, when García fled El Salvador for the United States as a teenager. Police arrested him outside a Maryland hardware store on March 28, 2019, accused him of being a gang member and turned him over to U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement. On Oct. 10, 2019, a Maryland immigration judge ruled that García could not be deported to El Salvador, where a gang had threatened his family. The judge gave him a work permit and placed him under federal supervision.

U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement detained García in Maryland on March 12, 2025, while he was driving home with his 5-year-old son. On March 15, the agency deported him to El Salvador by mistake despite the 2019 ruling and held him in a prison that The Associated Press described as notoriously brutal.

On April 10, 2025, the U.S. Supreme Court said the Trump administration must work to bring García back. On April 17, Democratic Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland met him at a hotel in El Salvador. The Associated Press described the visit as “In defiance of Trump” and said it was “helping to establish hope and momentum” for García's family.

On June 6, 2025, the government returned García to the United States and charged him with human smuggling based on a Tennessee traffic stop from 2022. On July 23, ICE announced plans to remove him to a series of African countries, but an injunction from a Maryland federal judge blocked the plan.

On Aug. 22, 2025, García left the Tennessee jail, where he had been held since June, to return to his family in Maryland and await trial. Within minutes of his release, ICE sent notice that it intended to deport him to Uganda. On Aug. 25, he reported to an immigration office in Baltimore and was taken into custody.

On Dec. 11, 2025, a Maryland federal judge ordered ICE to release García immediately. On Feb. 17, 2026, a Maryland federal judge ruled that ICE could not re-detain him.

On May 22, 2026, a federal judge in Tennessee dismissed the human-smuggling case against García after finding evidence that the Justice Department had engaged in “vindictive prosecution.” On Aug. 17, the Justice Department told a federal appeals court that the Tennessee judge had erred in the ruling, which The Associated Press described as “scathing,” and argued for reinstating the charges. The timeline records no decision on the appeal.

García's Nov. 25, 2026, landing at Madrid's international airport confirmed the departure. He did not speak to reporters but raised a fist as he entered a van. The Associated Press timeline ends with the arrival and records no decision on the Justice Department's appeal.
