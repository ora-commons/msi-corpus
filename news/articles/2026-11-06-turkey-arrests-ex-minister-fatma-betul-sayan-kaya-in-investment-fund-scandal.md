## Kaya resigned from Erdogan's party leadership in September amid share-sale allegations

Turkish authorities have arrested Fatma Betul Sayan Kaya, a former family and social affairs minister who served as a deputy chair of President Recep Tayyip Erdogan's ruling party, over her alleged involvement in a share-price manipulation scandal involving several investment funds.

Kaya was taken into custody late Thursday pending a possible trial on charges of membership in a criminal organization and violating Turkey's Capital Markets Law, the state-run Anadolu Agency reported.

Her husband was released on condition that he regularly report to police, following a second round of questioning for the couple.

Kaya, who served as family and social affairs minister from 2016 to 2018, resigned as deputy chair of the ruling Justice and Development Party in September.

Her resignation came shortly after allegations emerged that she had sold shares worth tens of millions of dollars just before regulators decided to liquidate the investment funds over alleged manipulation.

Turkey's main opposition party has accused Kaya of receiving 2.17 billion liras ($43 million) after reportedly investing 163 million liras ($3 million) in one of the funds.

Kaya and her husband have denied any wrongdoing but have transferred some of the profits to an account designated by the banking regulators, according to media reports.

More than 430 people are under investigation over suspected market manipulation and insider trading, including 112 who have been arrested as part of the scandal, Justice Minister Akin Gurlek said this week.
