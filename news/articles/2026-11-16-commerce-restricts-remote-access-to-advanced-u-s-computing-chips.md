## Five countries face case-by-case licensing for chip sales

The Trump administration on Monday issued new rules to restrict remote access to advanced U.S. computing chips and servers, the latest step in an effort to limit China's ability to use U.S. chips to train its best artificial intelligence models. The rule was issued by the Bureau of Industry and Security under the Commerce Department and took effect immediately.

"The rule is designed to ensure that existing restrictions on access to sensitive American technology cannot be circumvented through remote computing arrangements involving data centers in third countries," the Bureau of Industry and Security said in issuing the rule.

Under the new rule, overseas data centers that serve companies in countries including China, Russia, Iran and Cuba are required to identify their end users. The rule also imposes additional, case-by-case licensing requirements for sales of chips for Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand or Brazil — countries that "present a higher-than-normal risk of diversion" to unauthorized destinations, the rule says.

The rule landed days after Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO amid scrutiny of his ties to a Chinese executive who used to lead Singapore-based Megaspeed International. That company is suspected of diverting advanced Nvidia chips to China in violation of U.S. export controls. Megaspeed provides computing power to Chinese companies using chips housed in Southeast Asian data centers, including facilities in Malaysia.

The United States bans sales of its most advanced AI chips to China, seeking to maintain an edge in its rivalry with China for predominance in artificial intelligence. But many lawmakers and policymakers believe the country's export control rules are not tough enough and are filled with loopholes such as cloud computing that allow China to rent processing power built on American technology.

Last year, Rep. Michael Lawler, a New York Republican, introduced a bill to broaden U.S. export controls to include remote access. He told Congress that "physical export controls" under current rules do not apply to remote access, allowing China to "access our tech through the cloud" and giving it "a real tool to sidestep" U.S. export restrictions. The bill has passed the House.
