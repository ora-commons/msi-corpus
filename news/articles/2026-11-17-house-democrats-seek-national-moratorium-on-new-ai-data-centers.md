## House Democrats seek national moratorium on new AI data centers

## Senate advances separate permitting overhaul as environmental groups oppose

On Tuesday, leading and incoming House Democrats called for a national moratorium on the construction of new artificial intelligence data centers. Members, members-elect and advocates made the appeal at a press conference outside the U.S. Capitol hosted by Food & Water Watch.

Data centers are critical to storing infrastructure for artificial intelligence and have drawn widespread backlash over their effects on the environment and energy costs. The issue was a major one in the midterm elections, where Democrats delivered a landslide majority in the House and secured control of the Senate.

The push centers on legislation introduced earlier this year by independent Sen. Bernie Sanders of Vermont and New York progressive Democratic Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez in their respective chambers. The measure would impose a moratorium until “legislation is enacted that safeguards the public from the dangers of artificial intelligence,” according to the bill text.

Rep. Jim McGovern of Massachusetts, the top Democrat on the House Rules Committee and an original cosponsor of the House version, said, “We are here because it is time for Americans from across the political spectrum to stand up and to demand a nationwide moratorium on data centers.” McGovern called for “an immediate change to the status quo.”

McGovern also rebuked President Donald Trump’s administration, which has pushed for the expansion of data centers. He said, “We are here because the alarm bells are flashing, and the president and the White House are asleep at the switch when it comes to AI.”

Rep. Chellie Pingree of Maine said data centers are driving up costs, straining the grid, dirtying air and water, and degrading health and quality of life. She is the top Democrat on the House Appropriations subcommittee that oversees U.S. Interior Department funding and is in line to serve as its chair in the next Congress. Pingree said that “when energy prices are already sky high in places like Maine and across America, Americans don’t want data centers.”

Rep.-elect Adam Hamawy of New Jersey said “data centers are an issue that covers so much — it covers our bills, it covers our environment, it covers our communities and our farmlands.” He added that “the American people are really the ones who have a say in this, not the mega-corporations.” Hamawy also said House Democrats have an opportunity to prove they meant their promise to fight for the public.

Rep.-elect Will Lawrence of Michigan acknowledged that AI is a “marvelous technology” and “capable of amazing things.” He said that under current incentives, “it is going to be used to put people out of work, to harm the environment, to undermine our democracy and to put us all at terrible risk.”

Meanwhile, a bipartisan bill moving through the Senate would overhaul the federal process for permits to construct energy projects, including data centers. The measure advanced in the upper chamber Monday on a 70-27 vote and aims to hold data centers accountable for energy costs.

Food & Water Watch and other environmental groups opposed the Senate bill. In a statement earlier in November, Food & Water Watch policy director Jim Walsh said the “bad bill would only encourage and permit greater buildout of data centers and the polluting fracked gas power plants and pipelines required to serve these enormous facilities.” The two measures address data-center development differently: the House campaign seeks a pause on new construction until AI safeguards are enacted, while the Senate bill focuses on permitting rules and energy costs.
