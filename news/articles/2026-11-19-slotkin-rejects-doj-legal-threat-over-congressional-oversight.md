## Todd Blanche says what Slotkin said was not a crime in and of itself

Aaron Reitz, the U.S. Attorney for the Southern District of Texas, posted an X letter directed at U.S. Sen. Elissa Slotkin a week before Thursday afternoon’s press conference. Reitz alleged that statements Slotkin made during a public podcast taping were unconstitutional and could potentially open Slotkin to a criminal investigation. Slotkin had said on the podcast that congressional oversight could send a “deterrence message” to people considering working in the Trump administration.

Reitz wrote that Slotkin’s comments reflected “soon-to-be-realized fantasies about weaponizing congressional investigations for the purpose of personal aggrandizement and punishment of people close to the President.” He said Slotkin’s oversight plans “seem to have no legitimate legislative purpose and may therefore be unlawful.” Reitz also wrote: “The American people are entitled to freely associate without their elected officials terrorizing them with aggressive, harassing government action.” He said Slotkin’s stated intent to “deter” Americans from associating with President Trump may run afoul of a constitutional right.

Slotkin responded Thursday with her own letter and a press release telling the Department of Justice to “bring it on.” In a video posted to social media, she said all Americans have constitutional freedom-of-speech rights, while sitting senators have further privileges and responsibilities for oversight that protect Slotkin and her colleagues as they investigate Trump.

Slotkin wrote: “The Constitution’s Speech or Debate clause bars prosecution of a legislator for conducting an investigation or performing other legislative acts. As such, your threat of investigation is baseless, and you should withdraw your letter immediately.”

She also wrote: “Your public threats to prosecute a sitting Senator for speech and legislative oversight also violate Justice Department policy. Your breach of these rules — publicly posting your letter instead of providing it to me, my attorney, or Senate Legal Counsel — is evidence that your threat of investigation is more about publicity and your political ambitions than any sort of legitimate inquiry.”

Attorney General Todd Blanche said in a radio interview responding to questions about whether Slotkin’s statements could be illegal: “what the senator said in and of itself is absolutely not a crime.” NBC News reported that Blanche said at a press conference the previous week that there were “no ongoing investigations, criminal investigations, into words that incoming senators or congressmen have said.”

The Thursday afternoon press conference came almost exactly one year after Slotkin and the other legislators posted their video reminding military service members of their responsibility to refuse illegal orders. All of the legislators were military veterans and former intelligence officers. Slotkin and her four colleagues decried what Slotkin called “intimidation” and threats from President Donald Trump and his allies against people who speak out against him.

Slotkin recounted what she called the past year of military and intelligence actions taken by Trump, citing federal immigration enforcement deployments in Los Angeles, Chicago and Minneapolis, the ongoing war in Iran, and military strikes against boats in the Caribbean. She said: “It was important for us to come together a year later to say: number one, we stand behind the video we made. Number two, we won’t be intimidated out of calling out threats to our democracy.” She added: “We stand behind our men and women in uniform for the amazing work that they do, and I think we feel strongly that if this is the thing we need to do to stand up for the country we love, we have no problem doing that.”

Throughout her response letter and Thursday’s press conference, Slotkin referred to the result of an investigation into the video and the federal government’s failure to obtain a criminal indictment against Slotkin and her colleagues as demonstrating that her speech was constitutionally protected. “It’s again similar, just going after someone for basic freedom of speech, and then on top of that, for speech and debate, for those of us who are legislators, we’re sort of doubly protected against that,” she said. She added: “It’s just a demonstration again that what began a year ago of the weaponization of the government continues.”

U.S. Sen. Mark Kelly, a Democrat from Arizona who appeared in the 2025 video, spoke at the press conference alongside Slotkin. “Donald Trump is trying to fundamentally change the nature of this country, it hasn’t worked on us. I will tell you that none of us are backing down,” Kelly said. He continued: “Just like the service members who have a duty to uphold the Constitution and refuse illegal orders, we have a duty in Congress to hold this president accountable and always stand up for the Constitution and the American people. That’s our job, and no amount of threats from Donald Trump or the people around him are going to stop us from doing that.”
