## Banks commit $42 billion after demanding stronger terms

Broadcom CEO Hock Tan spent the weekend racing to bring a $63 billion chip-financing arrangement for Anthropic across the finish line, according to The Wall Street Journal. His intervention followed a push by banks for revised terms before they committed $42 billion.

The package would let Anthropic lease specialized chips. Tan had spent months wrangling over its terms, according to the report.

The Wall Street Journal described the proposed transaction as the largest AI debt deal in history. Its scale made the banks’ demand for revised terms a central part of the weekend negotiations.

Negotiations unfolded just as Anthropic filed for its IPO. The company was moving toward a public-market offering while Tan was still trying to complete the debt financing for its chip lease.

Two concerns drove the banks’ push for better terms: rising interest rates and the potential for overexposure to AI. The banks demanded those improvements before committing $42 billion to the arrangement.

Tan made the 11th-hour concessions. He agreed to higher interest rates and to guarantees that would cover an Anthropic default, according to the report.

The revised terms paired the $42 billion bank commitment with a higher borrowing rate and added default protection for the lenders. The broader $63 billion package would finance Anthropic’s lease of specialized chips.

Alexander Saeedy and Anissa Gardizy reported that the scramble illustrated a shift in AI finance: Wall Street was still committing cash, while lenders were raising the bar even for the industry’s biggest players. Capital remained available for AI, but on terms that banks viewed as more protective.
