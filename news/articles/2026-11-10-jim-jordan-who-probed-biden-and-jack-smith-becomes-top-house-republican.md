## John Thune takes Senate minority leader post unopposed despite past Trump frustrations

Jim Jordan built his House Republican career on investigations: he helped lead a two-year impeachment probe into former President Biden that Republicans ultimately did not pursue, and he investigated former Special Counsel Jack Smith and Democratic officials who had challenged President Trump. NPR reported Nov. 10 that House Republicans chose the Ohio congressman as their minority leader.

The committees where Jordan did that work — House Oversight and Judiciary — are poised to play a central role in the new Congress, NPR reported, as Democrats plan a long list of investigations into President Trump and his administration.

Jordan reached the post by defeating Louisiana's Steve Scalise, the No. 2 House Republican behind Mike Johnson, in a 106-73 vote, NPR congressional correspondent Claudia Grisales reported. He succeeds Johnson as the chamber's top Republican. Republicans in Congress are regrouping after last week's midterm election losses, and NPR described the leadership votes in the House and Senate as an important first step ahead of Democrats assuming the majority.

Grisales described Jordan as a former college wrestling coach who has brought a hard-charging style to his fights with Democrats — a quality she said a lot of Republicans were looking for in the fallout after the midterm losses. Republicans wanted a fresh face at the top, she said. Jordan co-founded the House Freedom Caucus, which NPR described as ultraconservative, and served as the top Republican on the House Oversight Committee when his party was in the minority. His most recent leadership post was chairman of the House Judiciary Committee.

In his pitch to colleagues, Jordan said the conference had for too long operated without a "clear plan to survive the next hour or day in front of us" — a remark Grisales described as clearly a hit on the current leadership team that included Scalise. Jordan said that kind of approach would not work with emboldened Democrats. Grisales said Democrats are coming in with a significant House margin that will leave Republicans with little leverage to shift the floor agenda.

In archived remarks cited by NPR, Jordan said:

> "Over the next two years, House Republicans will fight for American families. When Democrats offer more government, we'll offer more freedom, and we will bring that fight in committee, on the floor and on the stump, every single day."

Senate Republicans made a less surprising choice: Senate Majority Leader John Thune will become minority leader. Thune has held the top Senate Republican job since last year, when he succeeded Mitch McConnell, and he did not face a challenger. NPR reported that Thune has sometimes had a tense relationship with President Trump, who was frustrated with him for failing to advance his long-sought voting restriction bill and end the filibuster, but that Thune has a lot of support inside the conference.

Democrats have more time to reflect on their decisions. House Democrats vote next week, and House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries is poised to become the first Black speaker in history, NPR reported. In the Senate, Vermont Independent Senator Bernie Sanders, who caucuses with Democrats, on Nov. 10 became one of the more high-profile senators to call for new leadership at the top. It is unclear who could challenge Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, who NPR reported seemed to be on an easy track to become majority leader after Senate Democrats blew past expectations with their midterm wins and are on track for a comfortable majority.
