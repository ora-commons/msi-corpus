## Trump presented Huang the National Medal of Science in October

The US Commerce Department has added Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia partner, to a blacklist amid an investigation into whether it diverted AI chips to China. The action is separate from Huang's scheduled 3 December appearance before the Senate banking committee.

Republican lawmakers roundly criticized the Nvidia board's decision, which The Guardian reported was apparently brokered by President Donald Trump. Elizabeth Warren called the decision to reinstate Huang "reckless."

A Reuters photograph published with The Guardian's report shows Donald Trump presenting the National Medal of Science to Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang in October.

Office of Government Ethics disclosures indicate Trump might have owned as many as 370,000 Nvidia shares as of 30 September. According to The Guardian, Nvidia shares rose 31.5% on Monday, and Trump made up to $15m on his holdings after Huang's return. The disclosures do not establish what he held after 30 September.

The Guardian reported that Huang had been forced to step down briefly over allegations of extramarital affairs, including one with a suspected spy. He returned to Nvidia's headquarters in Santa Clara, California, where the newspaper described his reception as a "hero's welcome."

Maryland Democrat Chris Van Hollen pointed to a series of ties between Huang and Trump's circle as evidence of Huang's influence over administration policy on advanced chips. Van Hollen's examples included Nvidia's $25m donation to Trump's White House ballroom project, Huang's personal holdings of Nvidia stock, Huang's contribution to Trump's inauguration fund and Huang's attendance at a $1m-a-plate dinner at Mar-a-Lago. The Guardian said Nvidia and Trump appear to have other conflicts of interest beyond the reported stock gain and the board decision.

Taken together, the report centers three connected but distinct areas of scrutiny: the reported financial conflict of interest between Trump and Nvidia, the corporate-governance decision that returned Huang as chief executive, and Van Hollen's claim about Huang's influence over administration policy on advanced chips. The Megaspeed blacklisting and Huang's 3 December committee appearance add export-control and congressional tracks to the broader debate.
