## Target offers more than 8,000 gifts as retailers push affordability

Outstanding credit card balances hit $1.17 trillion as of last month, up 5.1% from a year earlier, based on data covering 178.5 million consumers who held an active credit card, according to Charles Wise, head of global research and consulting at TransUnion. "More Americans are carrying credit cards than ever, and they're handling them well," Wise said. Navy Federal Credit Union chief economist Heather Long said that after adjusting for higher prices, consumer spending in October was "negative." The Commerce Department's report released Tuesday, which put overall retail sales up 0.2% in October, down from a 0.8% gain in September, supplies the broader context for the month's spending.

Long wrote Tuesday that "The cautious consumer is back," adding that "October retail sales were tepid. Budgets are tight for many households." She noted that the drop in gas prices in November will help free up some much-needed cash this holiday season, but middle-class and moderate-income households are "feeling the strain." Even after that recent decline, a gallon still costs nearly 20% more than at this time last year.

The Commerce Department's report showed sales at gas stations rose 1.5% in October after spiking 30.3% in September; excluding gas stations, retail sales rose just 0.1%. Motor vehicle and parts dealers had a 1.6% drop in sales in October, reversing a 1.3% increase in September, and excluding auto-related stores, retail sales rose 0.6%. The control group, which excludes food services, autos, building materials and gas station sales and is used to calculate economic growth, rose 0.6%.

The data offer only a snapshot of consumer spending and do not include travel and hotel stays, and the lone services category, restaurants, registered a 0.2% increase. Business was down at clothing stores and at furniture and home furnishing retailers, and sporting goods, musical instruments, books and hobby stores also saw sales decline, having performed strongly through the spring and summer as consumers treated themselves to little splurges, Long noted. Online retailers were a bright spot, up 1.2% on a boost from a two-day Amazon Prime Day event in early October.

Higher energy prices were ricocheting into other parts of the economy, with airfares, public transportation and shipping all jumping in price. Grocery prices rose 0.4%, a painful uptick after years of increases, according to new Labor Department data, and the Consumer Price Index rose 0.4% in October, according to data released last week, hotter than expected. Associated Press reporting also said that massive investments in artificial intelligence have exacerbated inflation by driving up computer chip prices and, in turn, prices for laptops, phones and other consumer electronics.

Retailers were preparing for a surge in business over the Thanksgiving weekend, the unofficial start of the holiday shopping period, and stores heavily advertised deals to lure customers who increasingly buy when they think prices have dropped. Kohl's, Target and Walmart are trying to drive the affordability message home through marketing. Target said it is offering more than 8,000 new gifts for the winter holidays, with more than half exclusive to the retailer; most of the new holiday items at Target are $20 or less, including thousands of items that are priced at $5 and under. Kohl's has increased the number of gift sets across different types of merchandise.

Americans, despite the rising cost of credit, continue to rely on cards to finance spending, and credit experts say defaults have been stable. Wise said serious delinquency rates held steady. That signals that "consumers remain disciplined borrowers heading into the holidays," he added.

Stephen Lebovitz, CEO of CBL Properties, a Chattanooga, Tennessee, mall owner, remains optimistic about the holiday shopping season, noting that higher gas prices haven't derailed business. He believes a solid job market is still propping up spending among his middle-class customers. Malls have also been helped by a resurgence in business at movie theaters, fueled by Generation Z shoppers. "People are working and earning, and they're still spending," Lebovitz said.

Using a year-over-year transformation, [FRED's advance retail sales series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/RSAFS), which includes retail and food services, showed 6.82% as of Nov. 17, 2026. The [all-urban Consumer Price Index series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAUCSL) showed a 3.72% year-over-year change as of the same date. These broader comparisons provide context for the Commerce Department's monthly figures but do not measure the same month-to-month changes.
