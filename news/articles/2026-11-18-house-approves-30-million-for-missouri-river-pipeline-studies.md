## House approves $30 million for Missouri River pipeline studies

**Subtype:** fact

- The U.S. House passed Rep. Dusty Johnson’s bill allocating up to $30 million to study three proposed Missouri River water pipeline projects.
- Dusty Johnson’s bill would direct the U.S. Department of the Interior to evaluate the three projects’ viability before construction.
- The Lewis and Clark Regional Water System proposal would expand an existing system that could supply about 350,000 people.
- The Dakota Mainstem Regional Water System would serve nearly 60 water providers in South Dakota, Iowa, Minnesota and Nebraska.
- The Western Dakota Regional Water System would bring Missouri River water to western South Dakota, including the Rapid City area.
- South Dakota Republican Sens. John Thune and Mike Rounds sponsored similar Senate legislation that awaits a full vote after committee approval.

## Senate legislation awaits full vote after committee approval

The U.S. House passed legislation on Tuesday sponsored by Republican Rep. Dusty Johnson of South Dakota that would allocate up to $30 million for feasibility studies of three proposed Missouri River water pipeline projects. The action advances the plans to the study stage and does not approve construction.

The South Dakota Water Feasibility Studies Act would direct the U.S. Department of the Interior to evaluate whether each project is viable before construction moves forward. The bill would allocate $10 million for each study.

The Lewis and Clark Regional Water System proposal would expand the existing Missouri River drinking water system serving communities in southeastern South Dakota, northwestern Iowa and southwestern Minnesota. The expansion could supply water to about 350,000 people.

The Dakota Mainstem Regional Water System would create a new Missouri River water supply for nearly 60 water providers in South Dakota, Iowa, Minnesota and Nebraska. Its proposed capacity is 195 million gallons per day.

The Western Dakota Regional Water System would bring Missouri River water to western South Dakota, including the Rapid City area. The proposal is intended to reduce the region’s reliance on groundwater.

In a press release, Johnson said, “Water is an essential resource, and as South Dakota continues to grow, we will need more of it.”

Separate, similar legislation sponsored by South Dakota Republican Sens. John Thune and Mike Rounds has received committee approval and awaits action by the full Senate. That Senate version stands at a different procedural stage from the House measure.

Johnson ran unsuccessfully for the Republican nomination for governor in June rather than seeking reelection to the House, and he will leave office in January.

The House action leaves the feasibility questions unresolved. The studies would assess whether the three proposals can move forward, while the Senate’s similar legislation still awaits full Senate action.
