## The defense argued both men were shot in self-defense

A New South Wales supreme court jury found former police officer Beau Lamarre-Condon guilty of two counts of murder on Wednesday. The charges concerned the shooting of television presenter Jesse Baird and his partner Luke Davies with Lamarre-Condon’s service weapon. The jury also found him guilty of aggravated break and enter over an August 2023 incident and reached its decision after two hours of deliberation.

Jesse Baird was 26 years old and a television presenter. Luke Davies was a Qantas flight attendant who had begun dating Baird in early 2024. The pair were shot at Baird’s home in Paddington, Sydney, on February 19, 2024.

Lamarre-Condon and Baird began casually dating in late 2022. Prosecutors told the court that the former officer developed an “infatuation and obsession” with Baird and argued that the relationship produced compulsive behavior that escalated when the pair stopped seeing each other. Baird asked Lamarre-Condon to stop contacting him before they broke up for the second and final time in November 2023.

On February 19, 2024, the prosecution said, Lamarre-Condon dressed as an Uber Eats rider and entered Baird’s home with what it described as a surreptitiously copied key. The prosecution case said he shot Baird in the back and then fired an “execution style” shot through his temple. Davies was in the bedroom and attempted to call Triple Zero, but Lamarre-Condon shot him in the head through the door, the prosecution said.

During the days that followed, Lamarre-Condon cleaned up the crime scene and eventually left the bodies near a Greek monastery in Bungonia, about 150km away. He did not surrender to police until February 23, 2024. The bodies of Baird and Davies were found with his help four days later.

The jury’s second guilty finding concerned aggravated break and enter on August 17, 2023. The jury found that Lamarre-Condon had broken into the terrace house and gone through Baird’s phone.

The defense did not dispute the cleanup or the disposal of the bodies. It argued that the men were shot as Lamarre-Condon acted in self-defense.

The case was tried over 10 weeks. More than 50 people gave evidence from the witness box, including Lamarre-Condon, and the court was shown hours of CCTV and numerous exhibits. The jury then deliberated for two hours before returning its findings.
