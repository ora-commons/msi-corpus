## Saudi output reportedly reaches 10.5 million barrels per day

Oil prices fell as expectations of an oversupplied market and bearish U.S. inventory data outweighed continued tanker attacks in the Middle East. In early European trading on Nov. 18, 2026, Brent crude fell 1.9% to $91.38 a barrel and WTI futures declined 2.6% to $83.80, according to The Wall Street Journal's market update.

The update, timestamped 4:07 am ET, traced the decline to the supply outlook and bearish inventory readings. At 0004 GMT, front-month WTI futures were 0.2% lower at $85.86 a barrel. By 0900 GMT, Brent was down 1.9% at $91.38 and WTI was down 2.6% at $83.80 in early European trading.

Supply expectations were central to the move. Analysts at ING said, “Further disruptions around the Strait of Hormuz remain an important upside risk, but rising OPEC+ output, resilient Russian exports and building U.S. inventories point to a more comfortable supply picture.”

The ING analysts also said, “We continue to expect the oil balance to loosen as supply recovers, limiting the scope for a sustained rebound in prices.” That assessment pointed to a looser market while retaining renewed disruption around the Strait of Hormuz as an upside risk.

API data showed U.S. crude inventories rose by 5.3 million barrels last week, according to analysts. The bearish inventory reading and expectations of a more comfortable supply picture outweighed the continuing risk from tanker attacks in the Middle East.

Production reports supplied another supply-side factor. “According to the latest reports, Saudi Arabia has increased production to 10.5 million barrels per day,” FxPro chief market analyst Alex Kuptsikevich said in an email.

Kuptsikevich also said, “There are also reports of a significant increase in production in Iraq and Kuwait.” The additional production reports contributed to the broader expectation that supplies would recover.

Geopolitical risk remained a stated counterweight, and The Wall Street Journal said Middle East tensions could limit oil-price losses. On Tuesday, Iran said its drones hit a U.S. radar base in Bahrain. U.S. Central Command said on X that the drones were intercepted “without any damage to U.S. equipment or injury to American personnel.”
