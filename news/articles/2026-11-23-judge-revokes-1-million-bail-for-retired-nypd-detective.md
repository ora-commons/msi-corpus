## Sears described cleanup video and a matching pistol at Monday’s hearing

At Monday’s arraignment, a New York judge revoked retired New York City police detective Victor Romero’s $1 million bail and sent him to jail to await trial. Romero, 62, pleaded not guilty to a murder indictment in the Oct. 20 shooting death of Koran Irvine, 46, on the George Washington Bridge.

Romero had initially been charged with manslaughter and was released on $1 million bail last month. Prosecutors said a grand jury then indicted him on a murder charge at their suggestion.

According to prosecutors, Romero and Irvine were involved in a near-collision on an on-ramp to the bridge. They said Romero tailed Irvine’s car, pulled up near it in Irvine’s blind spot and shot him through the back passenger window while both motorists headed toward New Jersey.

Irvine was an electrical utility employee on his way home from work. He was shot in the torso, with the bullet piercing his heart and both lungs. Authorities said Romero drove off.

Manhattan Assistant District Attorney Matt Sears said prosecutors now have more evidence, including video of Romero wiping down the driver’s side of his car and the driver’s seat area on the morning after the shooting while not cleaning the rest of the vehicle. When police approached him later that day and asked about the car, he told them he “didn’t get into an accident,” according to a court filing from prosecutors.

Sears said a semiautomatic pistol found in Romero’s home matched a bullet from Irvine’s body and a shell casing discovered in Romero’s own car. He asked for Romero to be held without bail.

Romero spent six years with the New York Police Department before retiring in 2001. He had been wounded in an off-duty shootout with men attempting to rob his family’s laundromat.

Irvine’s family had publicly pushed for a murder charge before Monday’s arraignment. Relatives and their lawyer said the outcome was a step toward justice.

“But it’s not going to bring him back,” sister Zeria Irvine said, choking up, to journalists outside the courthouse.

A message seeking comment was sent to Romero’s attorney. Romero remains in jail while the murder case proceeds to trial.
