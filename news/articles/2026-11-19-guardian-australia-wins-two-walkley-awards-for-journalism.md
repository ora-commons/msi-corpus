## Martin, Smee and Barrett were also finalists in other categories

Guardian Australia won two 2026 Walkley awards for excellence in journalism. Sarah Martin won the award for print/text news report, while Queensland correspondent Ben Smee and the Guardian team won for coverage of Indigenous affairs. The winning work concerned Pauline Hanson’s sponsored trip to the US and Kardell Lomas’s repeated appeals for help before she was killed.

Martin’s award recognized an exclusive story reporting that One Nation leader Pauline Hanson and her chief of staff, James Ashby, traveled with mining billionaire Gina Rinehart on Rinehart’s Gulfstream 700 on 27 October last year. The pair stayed at Rinehart’s Palm Beach mansion, close to US president Donald Trump’s Mar-a-Lago resort.

After Guardian Australia published details of Hanson’s flight, Hanson’s office lodged an amendment with the registrar of senators’ interests declaring the sponsored travel.

Ben Smee and the Guardian team’s award for coverage of Indigenous affairs recognized reporting on Kardell Lomas, who repeatedly sought help from domestic violence support services and police to escape the violent and dangerous man who killed her. The Guardian Australia team described the case as one of systemic betrayal before her death.

The award-winning coverage reported that Lomas had 24 interactions with various agencies. The coverage also said Lomas repeatedly disclosed fears for her safety, death threats, serious violence and other abuse.

Martin and Smee were finalists in two other categories. Martin’s finalist work was *Inside One Nation*, while Smee’s was *‘Starting to frighten me’: the final weeks of Hannah Clarke’s life*.

Business editor Jonathan Barrett was also a finalist in the print/text feature writing category for *‘A child goes to bed and doesn’t wake up’: the families left in shock after the sudden death of their healthy children*.

The 2026 Walkley print/text award for feature writing under 4,000 words went to Australian Financial Review’s Sam Buckingham-Jones for *Maccas in a Rolls: inside Kyle Sandilands’ pivot to $100 tradie plans*. Feature writing over 4,000 words went to Melissa Fyfe and Carla Hildebrandt of The Age and The Sydney Morning Herald for *Virginia: The Final Chapter*.

The scoop of the year went to the ABC team behind *Bondi: what ASIO knew*, including Sean Rubinsztein-Dunlop, Olivia Caisley and Michael Workman. Tony Wright, an associate editor and special writer for The Age and The Sydney Morning Herald, took home the award for outstanding contribution to journalism.
