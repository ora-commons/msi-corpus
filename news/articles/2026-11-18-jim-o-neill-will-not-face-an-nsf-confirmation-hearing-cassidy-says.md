## Committee objections center on O’Neill’s CDC and HHS record

Jim O’Neill was slated to appear before the Senate committee on Wednesday for a confirmation hearing to lead the US National Science Foundation, but he will not undergo the hearing. Bill Cassidy, a Republican from Louisiana and chair of the Senate committee on health, education, labor and pensions, said O’Neill “did not do enough to stand up for science” in his previous position.

The committee’s action ends O’Neill’s run for the top US scientific organization, an independent federal agency that has seen major budget cuts and grant freezes under the Trump administration. The next NSF nominee will face a Democratic-led Senate.

Cassidy made the case in opening remarks before the committee on Wednesday, hours before O’Neill was slated to appear. He said O’Neill, who served as acting director of the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention from August 2025 to February 2026, failed to push back against “irresponsible decisions and statements from Secretary Kennedy.”

“His role in the firing of Susan Monarez raised further concerns about the independent judgment he would bring to NSF,” Cassidy added, saying several committee members had concerns about those lapses.

Cassidy said he liked O’Neill personally, “but this time requires leadership and fortitude in standing up for what is right.”

O’Neill also served as deputy secretary of the US Department of Health and Human Services from June 2025 to February 2026. During that period, HHS slashed recommendations for routine childhood immunizations.

The White House did not pull O’Neill’s nomination. Cassidy noted that the White House withdrew Heidi Overton’s nomination for commissioner of the US Food and Drug Administration, saying she lacked the votes to pass confirmation, which he called “pretty odd.”

Cassidy said, “I think she would’ve had the votes,” and said anti-abortion senators “sought reassurances from the administration” about Overton’s stances against abortion. He said the administration chose not to make such commitments and ultimately withdrew Overton’s nomination.

The Republican-led Senate is pushing through confirmation hearings before it loses the majority in January. The NSF’s chief of staff, Brian Stone, is currently performing the duties of NSF director; in October, he announced plans to retire at the end of the year.
