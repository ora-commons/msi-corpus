## Sanon's lawyer says Sanon intends to appeal the verdict

Moïse's wife, Martine, who was injured in the attack that killed her husband, announced this week that she intends to stand in Haiti's presidential election next year. Separately, CBS, the BBC's US partner, reported that Christian Emmanuel Sanon's lawyer said Sanon maintains his innocence and intends to appeal the Miami verdict in the case of the 2021 assassination of Haitian President Jovenel Moïse. The UN said last month that more than 5,700 people were killed or injured in Haiti in the year to 11 September.

A presidential election is scheduled for next year. BBC News said the murder of Moïse has left Haiti without a leader for five years and that the political vacuum has intensified gang violence in the impoverished Caribbean nation.

The UN's figure came as the multinational force prompted by Moïse's assassination struggled to penetrate areas where gangs hold sway. The assassination ushered in a period of worsening instability that emboldened gangs.

According to CBS, Sanon was found guilty on Wednesday following an eight-week trial in federal court in Miami. The 67-year-old Florida pastor and dual US-Haitian national was convicted on all eight charges against him, including conspiracy to kill or kidnap a person, and faces a potential sentence of life in prison.

Prosecutors said Sanon had "political aspirations" in Haiti and met several other co-conspirators in April 2021 to devise a plan to oust Moïse in an armed raid on the presidential palace in the capital, Port-au-Prince.

Sanon was accused of being involved in a plot to replace Moïse as president with himself in 2021, allegedly so he could award lucrative contracts to a Florida-based security outfit once in place. Among the co-conspirators were the heads of Counter Terrorist Unit Federal Academy and Counter Terrorist Unit Security, collectively referred to as CTU, who would finance the coup in exchange for government contracts for infrastructure projects and provide security forces and equipment.

A few months later, the other conspirators realized that Sanon had "neither the constitutional qualifications nor the popular support" to become president and shifted to another candidate, the U.S. Department of Justice said. Investigators said the plot developed at the same time from forcibly removing Moïse from office to assassinating him.

Dozens of people were implicated in the conspiracy, which prosecutors said involved co-ordinating funding for and delivering weapons and mercenaries to Haiti to carry out the attack. On 6 July 2021, a group of Colombian mercenaries entered the presidential palace and killed Moïse. Martine, who was also injured in the attack, said at the time that the assailants had "riddled" her husband with bullets.

The successful assassination plot raised questions in Haiti about the relative ease with which the mercenaries gained access to the president. Haiti, the poorest country in the Americas, had been ravaged by gang violence for years.

Sanon and several other co-conspirators were arrested by Haitian authorities, while three mercenaries were killed in a gun battle with police. Eighteen people were transferred to Florida in September to stand trial over the plot. Four men, including the heads of CTU, were convicted of their role earlier this year. Sanon had been scheduled to be tried with them, but his case was delayed because of health reasons, according to CBS.

Sanon was born in Haiti but moved to the US as a teenager. He told the court he had worked in churches in Haitian immigrant communities in several parts of the US, including Florida, according to US media.
