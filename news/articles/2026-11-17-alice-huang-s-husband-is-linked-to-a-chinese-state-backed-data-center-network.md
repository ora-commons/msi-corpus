The report appeared in advance of Nvidia’s scheduled earnings release.

Nvidia was set to report earnings after the bell. The newsletter described the release as the chipmaker’s first major test of investor confidence after Jensen Huang’s scandal-marred exit.

The newsletter directed readers to an investigation by Lingling Wei as the starting point in its Nvidia coverage. The piece focused on Alice Huang, her marriage to the data-center executive and her cultivation of a personal relationship with Jensen Huang.

Alongside Wei’s piece, the newspaper included reporting on Nvidia’s internal culture, a warning from a senior employee and the board’s financial gains. Those reports addressed the company’s employees and directors as well as its former chief executive.

Separate reporting examined the demanding work culture Jensen Huang fostered inside Nvidia. The Wall Street Journal’s summary described the culture as marked by tough eviscerations and the rise of what the newsletter called Jensen Huang’s “royal family”.

A senior employee warned Nvidia executives and directors that Nvidia could lose “irreplaceable talent” if Jensen Huang was not reinstated. The warning was addressed directly to the company’s executives and directors.

The Journal also published charts and graphics examining how Nvidia’s board made billions while overseeing the company. The board’s finances were presented alongside the newspaper’s other examinations of Nvidia’s leadership.

Taken together, the accounts placed leadership continuity, employee treatment and retention, and board finances in the same frame as the China-backed data-center connection described in Wei’s piece. Nvidia’s scheduled earnings report was the investor-confidence test the newspaper placed around those questions.

Tenants paid an average of $2,700 a square foot to lease space on the upper portion of New York’s famed shopping strip over the past year.
