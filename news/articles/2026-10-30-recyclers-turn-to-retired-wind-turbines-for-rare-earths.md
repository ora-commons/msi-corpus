## Paladin EnviroTech taps Iowa venture's leaching technique to reclaim metals

Paladin EnviroTech, a recycling company eyeing a new role as a supplier of rare-earth metals, has identified old wind turbines as its top target, The Wall Street Journal reported. The generators on those machines can be the size of a room and can house huge rare-earth magnets.

The company's focus lands as the United States aims to become less reliant on China for rare earths that go into everything from drones to MRI scanners. Against that backdrop, a new type of venture is looking to the scrapyard for supply.

The supply Paladin is chasing is a product of the wind industry's own age. There are thousands of retirement-age turbines waiting to be harvested as America's wind industry ages, according to the Journal's Ed Ballard. Only a fraction of earlier models contain rare earths, but the metals became commonplace in the more-powerful turbines that are now starting to be replaced.

Metal recyclers — primarily targeting copper — have been harvesting what they can. The rare-earth metals, which are harder to separate and less valuable, were going to waste.

One major hurdle lies in the economical separation of materials: isolating them, grinding them up and converting rare earth elements into metal for next-generation magnets.

Paladin collaborates with Critical Materials Recycling, a Boone, Iowa-based venture scaling a leaching technique pioneered at Ames National Laboratory to reclaim virtually all rare earths from electronic waste.
