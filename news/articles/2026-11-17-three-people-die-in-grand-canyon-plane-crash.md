## Night-vision search locates wreckage in Grand Canyon's North Rim

Three people died in a plane crash in Grand Canyon National Park in Arizona, the National Park Service said Monday. The aircraft was a Piper Comanche, and officials said all three people aboard were killed.

The Grand Canyon regional communications center received a report of the missing aircraft at about 11:30 p.m. Sunday, according to a statement from officials. The Piper Comanche was last known to be in the Basin area of the park's North Rim, a higher-elevation and less-visited section.

Search crews began looking for the aircraft in the early morning hours on Monday. At about 10:15 a.m., officials said they located the wreckage and a debris field in the Basin area.

The National Park Service had not released the victims' identities, saying that withholding them was out of respect for their families.

According to officials, the Arizona Department of Public Safety conducted an aerial search using night vision and forward-looking infrared technology. Coconino County Search and Rescue, Kane County Search and Rescue and the Air Force Rescue Coordination Center also assisted with the operation.

The reported crash area sits between 8,000 and 8,800 feet, or 2.4 and 2.7 kilometers, above sea level. It is about 1,000 feet, or 305 meters, higher than the South Rim.

Officials said the National Transportation Safety Board will investigate the cause of the crash with assistance from the National Park Service.
