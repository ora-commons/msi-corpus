## Police recovered 96 vehicles worth $4.5m bound for export

Canadian police have arrested 12 suspects, including eight Nigerians, in an investigation into an alleged car-theft syndicate accused of stealing luxury vehicles and shipping them to West Africa. Peel Regional Police in Ontario said officers recovered 96 high-value vehicles concealed in shipping containers. The vehicles were valued at $4.5m (£3.4m) and were destined for export via Canadian ports.

The 12 suspects face 231 charges, according to Peel Regional Police. Police said arrest warrants had been issued for others connected to the alleged network. Some of the accused may face deportation after their criminal proceedings are concluded.

The arrests followed months of what police described as an "extensive auto theft investigation." The investigation began in March in partnership with Canada's border agency after police identified a warehouse allegedly used to store stolen vehicles.

Investigators believe the network was led by five people who coordinated the theft, transportation and export of stolen vehicles, with other members playing supporting roles. In a statement, police said, "Together, investigators linked the network to loading sites, container movements and the transportation of stolen re-VINed vehicles."

Re-VINing is the illegal practice of altering the Vehicle Identification Number (VIN) to conceal a vehicle's identity and make it harder to trace.

Canadian authorities have described organised vehicle theft as a growing threat and said it was linked to wider criminal networks. Peel Regional Police Deputy Chief Nick Milinovich said, "Organised auto theft is a predatory business model run by transnational syndicates that terrorise families and fund broader criminal enterprises."

Last year, Interpol said more than 100 luxury vehicles stolen from Canada and European countries had been recovered, exposing a network involved in trafficking stolen cars to West Africa.

In a separate operation the same year, Ghanaian security authorities recovered 43 luxury vehicles stolen from Europe and North America. Ghanaian authorities attributed the operation's success to improved cooperation between national and international law-enforcement agencies.

The trafficking of vehicles from North America and Europe to African countries has been a long-standing concern for law-enforcement agencies, according to the report.
