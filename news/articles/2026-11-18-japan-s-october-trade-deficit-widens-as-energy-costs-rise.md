## Exports rose 20.5%, but imports increased faster and widened the gap

Japan's Ministry of Finance reported a 476.5 billion yen ($3 billion) trade deficit for October, according to government data released Wednesday. The deficit came as soaring energy-import costs continued to outpace exports.

Japan's imports rose 21.6% from a year earlier to 12.25 trillion yen. Exports increased 20.5% to 11.77 trillion yen, leaving the total value of imports above exports for the month.

The trade deficit was 58% larger than in October 2025, marking Japan's sixth consecutive month with a year-over-year deficit. The figures showed that stronger export growth did not offset the increase in import costs.

Japan imports virtually all its oil. The Associated Press reported that import costs had soared over the past year because the war in Iran effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz. Much of the oil Japan had imported previously traveled through the strait from the Middle East.

Japan has had to find alternative sources, and its oil imports from the U.S. soared over the past year. The higher import total still outpaced export growth in October.

Exports grew to North America, Europe, South America and the rest of Asia, including China. Import growth remained strong from the Middle East, while imports also rose from other regions.

By commodity, exports increased in computer parts, semiconductors, machinery, ships and cars. Imports grew in iron and steel products, cereal and nonferrous ore.

According to analysts, the October deficit was lower than expected because imports, though still greater than exports, were smaller than anticipated while exports remained strong.
