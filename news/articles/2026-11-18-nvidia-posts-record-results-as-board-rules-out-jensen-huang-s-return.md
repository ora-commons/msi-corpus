## Colette Kress pledges disciplined financing as Nvidia searches for a permanent CEO

Nvidia reported record results on Tuesday and said revenue would grow beyond its previous forecast, sending shares up about 4% after the announcement. The company also said its new Vera Rubin servers had reached the market quickly and were boosting revenue in the data-center segment.

Interim CEO Colette Kress told investors on the earnings call, “Our momentum is real. Our demand is accelerating. Our supply is growing. Our customers and our partners are with us. We’ve got this.” She described conditions at the company’s Santa Clara headquarters as “business as usual.”

The leadership question remained central to the announcement. Chairman Harvey Jones said, “The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO,” at the start of the earnings call. Nvidia’s board retained Heidrick & Struggles to find a permanent chief executive.

Hours before the earnings report, Huang said in a statement that he would return as CEO if asked and agreed to testify before Congress. Kress acknowledged “diverse opinions” within Nvidia’s workforce, including a vocal faction calling for Huang’s return. She said, “Our employees, though, we believe, are committed,” adding, “They are committed to doing their life’s work at Nvidia.”

The Wall Street Journal reported that the Securities and Exchange Commission is probing Nvidia’s disclosures. The report also said the Commerce Department is restricting shipments of chips from Nvidia and other U.S. companies to countries in Southeast Asia, where Chinese companies can access them.

The Wall Street Journal reported that, despite Nvidia’s financial strength, the company’s near-term future is likely to be dominated by questions about the former CEO’s behavior and legacy. The report said Huang’s extramarital affairs included an employee and a customer whom U.S. officials believe was working with Chinese intelligence. The report also said Huang’s relationship with Alice Huang, then an executive at the Singapore-based data company Megaspeed, raised fresh questions about Nvidia’s business in Asia.

In Tuesday’s question-and-answer session, Kress faced intense interest over how Nvidia would approach future financial guarantees for its cloud-computing partners. Financing those partners was a key part of the business model Huang created to sell as many chips as possible, and Kress’s comments reflected a more reserved approach to that practice.

Last quarter, Kress defended Nvidia’s practice of investing in AI infrastructure after some critics dubbed it “circular financing.” She said the investments would facilitate the development of “one of the most important technologies in human history” and that money invested in companies buying Nvidia chips would come back “in spades.”

On Tuesday, Kress warned that certain Nvidia-brokered financial guarantees would not “exist anymore” if the relevant data-center projects were delayed or failed to find additional financing. She said Nvidia would “remain thoughtful” and “disciplined” in committing capital. Nvidia would still provide financing and guarantees for “strong customers who need financing and some kind of guarantee,” Kress said, while acknowledging that the company had “a lot of work to understand if those are the right kind of investments that we need to make.”

Stacy Rasgon, a Bernstein tech analyst, said that because so many of Nvidia’s financing deals with partners such as cloud companies were negotiated by Huang personally, with Kress in the CEO’s chair, the broader AI infrastructure world could be starved of capital. Rasgon said some people were concerned that Kress could be less enthusiastic about such deals. “There’s this idea that Nvidia has been backstopping the entire ecosystem, and if they stop doing that, it will have major effects on AI demand,” he said. “The question is, how dependent is the entire AI trade on Nvidia’s support?”

Kress’s broader challenge, according to the report, is to persuade investors that Nvidia is in steady hands going forward while she crafts a new strategy for shifts already under way in America’s AI build-out and on Wall Street. Nvidia’s board retained Heidrick & Struggles to find a permanent chief executive, and Kress remained interim CEO while the search proceeded.
