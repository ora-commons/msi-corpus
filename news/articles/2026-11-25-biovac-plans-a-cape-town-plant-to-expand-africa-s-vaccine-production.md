## Biovac seeks approval for end-to-end cholera vaccine production

Biovac announced Wednesday that it plans to build a new vaccine manufacturing facility next to its current Cape Town base, aiming to dramatically increase African vaccine production by the end of 2028. The company said the project is intended to reduce the continent's heavy reliance on imported doses.

Africa needs more than 1 billion vaccine doses a year, but only around 1% of them are manufactured on the continent, according to the Africa Centers for Disease Control and Prevention.

Biovac is a public-private pharmaceutical company that is part-owned by the South African government. It distributes or performs end-stage production for vaccines against polio, tuberculosis, tetanus and other diseases. The proposed facility would expand the company's overall vaccine production base.

Biovac plans to upscale its output through the Cape Town project with support from the European Investment Bank. The bank has lent Biovac 75 million euros ($82 million) to help finance the new production center, and European Investment Bank Vice President Ambroise Fayolle visited Biovac on Wednesday.

“It is important because we believe in science and we believe the benefits can reach beyond vaccine production,” Fayolle said. He added that health resilience should be a global priority.

Company officials said Biovac is also seeking regulatory approval to start end-to-end production of an oral cholera vaccine by late 2028. If approved, the product would be the first cholera vaccine manufactured entirely in Africa and only the second vaccine produced end-to-end on the continent, following a yellow fever vaccine made by Senegal's Pasteur Institute.

CEO Morena Makhoana said Biovac acquired the base technology for the cholera vaccine from the International Vaccine Institute in South Korea. The vaccine is currently undergoing clinical trials, and the company expects to have initial safety and efficacy data by June.

Chief operations officer Mark MacNaughton said the new facility would give Biovac the potential to produce around 150 million vaccine doses a year. Biovac said that capacity could include 30 million to 40 million doses of the new cholera vaccine if the vaccine receives regulatory approval.

Cholera is a waterborne bacterial disease that kills thousands of people every year in Africa. A global shortage of cholera vaccines from around 2022 caused the suspension of immunization campaigns in parts of Africa, illustrating the continent's dependence on imported vaccines despite its population of more than 1.5 billion.

The World Health Organization reported more than 7,000 cholera deaths in Africa last year, the most in more than two decades. Cholera vaccination campaigns on the continent restarted only this year.

During the COVID-19 pandemic, African governments were left at the back of the vaccine queue and had to scramble to secure doses. Health officials said the ongoing Ebola outbreak in Congo, which has killed more than 5,000 people, is another indication of Africa's need to increase local vaccine production.

The African Union has set a target for the continent to produce 60% of its vaccines by 2040, although that target has been seen as a steep challenge.
