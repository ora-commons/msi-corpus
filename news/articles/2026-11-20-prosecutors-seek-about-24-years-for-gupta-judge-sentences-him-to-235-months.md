## Yadav is not in U.S. custody; court papers show $15,000 cash payment

Prosecutors with the Manhattan U.S. Attorney’s Office recommended about 24 years in prison for Nikhil Gupta in the case involving the failed plot to kill Gurpatwant Singh Pannun. U.S. District Judge Victor Marrero imposed 235 months, or more than 19 years, on Friday at a hearing in a Manhattan federal court.

Gupta, 55, pleaded guilty in February to murder-for-hire, conspiracy to commit murder-for-hire and conspiracy to commit money laundering.

*A courtroom sketch of Nikhil Gupta in court in New York City on 17 June 2024. Photograph: Jane Rosenberg/Reuters.*

The Guardian reported Gupta’s admission that he orchestrated the failed plot to kill Pannun in New York City and plotted with Indian government official Vikash Yadav to hire a hitman. Prosecutors accused Gupta of plotting with Yadav to kill Pannun, a dual U.S.-Canadian citizen and lawyer at Sikhs for Justice in New York who has advocated for a sovereign Sikh state in northern India.

The Guardian characterized the failed plot as Indian government-backed. India’s government denied involvement in any plot against Pannun and said the plot was against government policy.

Court papers show Gupta and Yadav arranged to have $15,000 in cash delivered to a purported hitman who was actually an undercover U.S. law enforcement officer.

Yadav is also charged in the case but is not in U.S. custody, and it is unclear whether he has hired a defense lawyer.

The discovery of alleged assassination plots against Sikh separatists in the United States and Canada has tested relations with India, which has denied involvement in such plots. U.S. and Canadian authorities have called the cases part of a broader effort to target Indian dissidents.

Prosecutor Ashley Nicolas said in court, “This case cuts to the very core of American values.” “We don’t condone the use of violence to silence voices in this country, ever.”

Addressing the court on Friday, Gupta said he regretted straying from the non-violent example set by the Indian independence leader Mahatma Gandhi. “I’m sorry to Mr Pannun, I’m sorry to the communities I hurt, I’m sorry to my own family,” Gupta said.

Pannun told the court that he was grateful to the United States for prosecuting Gupta and said he would keep advocating for Sikh sovereignty. “Self-determination for my people and my homeland, Punjab, is my lifetime goal,” he said.
