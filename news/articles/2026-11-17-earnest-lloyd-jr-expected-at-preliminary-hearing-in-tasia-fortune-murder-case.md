## Authorities say suspects staged Fortune’s hanging after a drug dispute

Earnest Lloyd Jr., 25, is expected to appear in court Tuesday for a preliminary hearing on a murder charge in the death of Tasia Fortune, according to the Associated Press. Tasia Fortune was a 29-year-old Black woman, and her body was found hanging from a tree in Jackson, Mississippi, on Aug. 3 last summer.

Lloyd is one of three men held on murder charges in the case. Eric Clark, 45, and Jarques Ratliff, 51, are also being held on murder charges, and Jackson police have said Ratliff had an “association” with Fortune.

The positioning of Fortune’s body stoked fears that she had been targeted because of her race and evoked memories of Mississippi’s history of lynching. The Associated Press reported that all three suspects are Black. Authorities said the three staged the scene after a drug dispute.

Some officials had called for a federal civil rights investigation. Jackson Police Chief RaShall Brackney said props were left at the scene “as part of the staging” of Fortune’s death. Brackney said the fear that the image of Fortune’s hanging body evoked had damaged the community.

Over the past two years, about a half-dozen Black women and men have been found hanged across the South. The Associated Press reported that none of those deaths had been found to be criminal, though in some cases critics said law enforcement appeared to move quickly to label them suicides before having sufficient evidence to rule out foul play.

Brackney described the fear in broader terms: “We know we have a very sordid history around lynchings and racial terror across this nation and particularly around the South. That is still a very current narrative. It is still a very raw narrative,” she said.

Judge Jeffery Reynolds, who set bond at $5 million for Clark following his arrest in late September, called Fortune’s killing “heinous.” “People justifiably didn’t know if we had a killer on the loose who might hang people for racial reasons,” Reynolds said.

Before the arrests, Fortune’s mother, Christy Spivey, told the Associated Press that she did not believe race had anything to do with her daughter’s death. Spivey is white, and Fortune’s father is Black.

Spivey said she believed the killing was personal and insisted that someone acquainted with Fortune was involved. “I think this is something very personal, very personal,” she said.

Spivey said Clark hugged her at the courthouse after a hearing for Ratliff earlier in September. She told reporters following Clark’s hearing later that month: “He never said he did it, but he said he was there to turn himself in for murder.”
