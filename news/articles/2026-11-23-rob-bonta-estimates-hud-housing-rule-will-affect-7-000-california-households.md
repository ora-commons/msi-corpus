## Landlords and housing authorities would report failed tenant checks to DHS

At a press conference, California Attorney General Rob Bonta said, “No child should lose their home over a parent’s immigration status,” and, “This rule is cruel, and it’s unlawful.” He illustrated the potential effect with a hypothetical: “Picture a mom with two kids in public housing. All U.S. citizens. Their dad is not eligible. Under this rule, all four of them lose their home,” Bonta said.

Bonta estimated that HUD's rule would affect 7,000 households in California. He also said one in five children in California is part of a mixed-status family.

Under the rule, a family would lose federal housing assistance if any household member lacked an eligible citizenship or immigration status. For more than three decades, Congress has authorized prorated assistance for mixed-status families, whose members have different citizenship or immigration statuses; those families currently receive less assistance when a member is ineligible because of immigration status. The new rule would bar mixed-status families from receiving any assistance.

The rule would also require public housing authorities and private landlords to report tenants who fail verification to the Department of Homeland Security. Bonta said: “Congress never gave HUD the power to throw mixed-status families out of their homes or turn private landlords into immigration enforcement. But that’s exactly what this rule does.”

Earlier this year, HUD conducted an audit with DHS and identified 20,000 mixed-status households receiving federally funded housing assistance nationwide. HUD said “illegal immigrants” were using “loopholes” to access benefits while the department struggled to provide housing for all who needed it.

HUD defended the rule by arguing that “illegal immigrants” were taking advantage of the current eligibility requirements. In an emailed statement Monday, HUD Secretary Scott Turner said: “HUD’s final rule fully implements the 1980 Housing and Community Development Act and ends the exploitation of taxpayer dollars. This rule guarantees ONLY AMERICAN citizens and certain eligible noncitizens will reside in HUD-funded housing. This change represents a reallocation of up to $218 million which will now go to eligible Americans that are currently on a waitlist.”

Turner first announced the proposed rule in January, but HUD finalized it only last week. The change is scheduled to take effect Dec. 14 and requires people to verify their citizenship or eligible immigration status to receive federal housing assistance.

Twenty-one states and Pennsylvania's governor have sued HUD over the rule. California, New York, Arizona and Massachusetts joined the suit. The states say the change could split up families or evict U.S. citizens from public housing.

The Associated Press report characterized the rule as the latest in a series of moves by President Donald Trump's administration targeting legal immigrants. The report said those broader moves included restricting health care, nutrition benefits and other subsidies, stripping legal protections from some immigrant groups and making more people eligible for deportation.

The rule remains scheduled to take effect Dec. 14 as the states challenge it. Bonta's estimate puts the number of potentially affected California households at 7,000, while the reporting requirement would require housing authorities and landlords to report failed tenant verifications to DHS. HUD says the change would reallocate up to $218 million to eligible Americans on a waitlist.
