## Poundland enters administration after Gordon Brothers rejects rescue bids

**Subtype:** fact

- Poundland entered administration on Wednesday after Gordon Brothers failed to secure a buyer for the discount retailer.
- Poundland's collapse puts 11,000 jobs at risk just weeks before Christmas.
- Gordon Brothers rejected offers to buy Poundland as a going concern if the buyers repaid a £22.5m shareholder loan.

## £22.5m shareholder loan complicated Poundland rescue negotiations

BBC News reported that Poundland formally entered administration on Wednesday afternoon, putting 11,000 jobs at risk just weeks before Christmas. The discount retailer failed to secure a last-minute rescue after its US-based owner, Gordon Brothers, held talks with several bidders but reached no transaction.

The administration filing came ahead of a deadline for creditor protection that was due to expire at midnight. Gordon Brothers had held discussions with several bidders over a last-minute rescue deal, but no proposal produced a transaction before the deadline.

A central stumbling block was a shareholder loan that Gordon Brothers inherited when it bought Poundland from Pepco for less than £1 last year. Gordon Brothers was owed £22.5m of that loan, while Pepco was owed £7.5m. Gordon Brothers rejected various proposals to buy the business as a going concern if the deals paid off the loan.

Martin Property Group, an Irish firm, offered to pay £5m up front and defer the remaining loan payments until 2030; Gordon Brothers rejected the proposal. The London-based Xyra group offered £12.5m up front but ran out of time to get a deal over the line. Zachert Private Equity, a German investment firm, was also understood to have made a late offer, which was rejected.

The crisis came to a head over the preceding weeks after Gordon Brothers restricted access to some funds for day-to-day operations. According to BBC News, stock at Poundland was not being replenished at the pace needed during the vital run-up to the festive period.

BBC News also reported that insiders said Gordon Brothers was driving a viable business into the ground to make money through insolvency. Gordon Brothers denied the claim. The criticism focused on how the US investment firm handled Poundland's sale process; Gordon Brothers specializes in buying and restructuring distressed companies.

Gordon Brothers had described Poundland as "challenged and vulnerable to any potential downturn in future trading." Poundland boss Barry Williams said the business was making a "strong recovery," presenting a different assessment of the retailer's condition as the deadline approached.

Gordon Brothers said it had put £85m into Poundland over a year to help save the company from insolvency. Poundland nevertheless entered administration after its owner failed to secure a buyer, leaving 11,000 jobs at risk.


Before the administration filing, [MSI reported that Gordon Brothers was weighing three bids for Poundland while restricting access to cash](/articles/2026-11-23-gordon-brothers-weighs-three-bids-for-poundland-amid-cash-restrictions/). The administration filing followed the failure to secure a transaction by the creditor-protection deadline.
