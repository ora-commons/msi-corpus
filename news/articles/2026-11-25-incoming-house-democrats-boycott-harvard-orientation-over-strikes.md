## Harvard graduate students seek raises as academic workers bargain for contracts

Fifty-three incoming House Democrats told Harvard University on Tuesday that they will boycott an informal orientation program at the Kennedy School next month in support of the graduate students’ union strike. In a letter to university administrators, the lawmakers said attending would require crossing a union picket line and that any trip to Harvard would be to stand in solidarity with striking workers.

The lawmakers thanked Harvard for inviting them to the Bipartisan Program for Newly Elected Members of Congress, which more than 800 members have participated in since 1972. In the letter, the lawmakers wrote: “However, as much as we would like to attend, we have been informed that to attend your event we would be required to cross a union picket line.”

The letter said thousands of UAW members belonging to the Harvard Graduate Students Union, HGSU-UAW Local 5118, are on strike. The incoming Democrats said the union is seeking basic provisions, including a cost-of-living increase.

The letter also said thousands of members of the Harvard Academic Workers, or HAW-UAW, have been bargaining for a first contract for over two years and recently went on a week-long strike. The lawmakers wrote: “While we appreciate the invitation to Harvard’s Institute of Politics, we will not cross a picket line.” They added that any trip to Harvard while the workers remained on strike would be to stand in solidarity with union siblings.

A photograph published with the report showed members of the Harvard Academic Workers picketing on 10 November 2026.

Sam Forstag of Montana, Clare Valdez of New York and Bob Brooks of Pennsylvania posted a social media video statement in support of the striking workers. Forstag said some Harvard workers had been fighting for more than two years to obtain a contract with “basic protections against harassment at the workplace, basic discrimination protections and a cost of living raise to $50,000 a year.”

Forstag also said, “Harvard has an endowment of $68 billion; they can afford to pay people a living wage. Our brothers and sisters there deserve a fair contract.” He said members of Congress traveling to Harvard in December would be going to join the picket line.

Valdez wrote on social media, “This freshman class stands with the working class.” She added: “Solidarity to my @UAW @hgsuuaw siblings at Harvard who are holding the line for what every worker deserves: living wages, workplace protections, and dignity on the job.”

In a post on X, incoming Democrat Richard Ojeda of North Carolina wrote: “I’ll never cross a picket line. You can find me shoulder to shoulder in solidarity fighting the good fight with our union siblings, full stop.”

The incoming Democrats are part of the largest freshman class in the U.S. House in three decades. More than 100 new members will be sworn into Congress’ lower chamber in January.
