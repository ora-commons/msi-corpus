## AOC says an El-Sayed win would 'break open the entire political calculus'

Abdul El-Sayed, who narrowly won Michigan's Democratic Senate primary two months ago, has drawn attention from the top leaders in both parties since Monday, with less than a month to go in the U.S. Senate race, according to the WSJ Politics newsletter.

The WSJ Politics newsletter, written by Damian Paletta, the Wall Street Journal's Washington coverage chief, characterized the shift in El-Sayed's standing as a transformation from "a potential anvil for the Democrats" to "a game-changer within the party."

President Trump used a rally to criticize Fox News for how the network handled a recent interview with El-Sayed. "El-Sayed was interviewed just a little while ago on a certain network…They let this guy talk, talk, talk. They hardly fight him at all. Why are they putting these people on our television networks?" Trump said, according to the newsletter.

At a private event in New York, Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez (D., N.Y.) said an El-Sayed win would reshape national politics. "What we do is that we break open the entire political calculus in America—period," Ocasio-Cortez said, according to the New York Times, as quoted by the newsletter. "Because if you can win, if Abdul can win in Michigan, anybody can win anywhere. Anywhere."

Former President Barack Obama announced he would campaign with El-Sayed in Michigan later this month. The newsletter reported that El-Sayed did not have support from many in the Democratic establishment during his primary battle against Rep. Haley Stevens, and that former Vice President Kamala Harris has already campaigned with the Democratic nominee. The newsletter said Obama's planned appearance "brings the Democratic seal of approval onto a candidate that didn't have support from many in the Democratic establishment" in the primary.
