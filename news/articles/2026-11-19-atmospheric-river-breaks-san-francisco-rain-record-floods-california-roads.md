## Los Angeles county fire department urges burn scar residents to leave

An atmospheric river moved from north to south across California on Wednesday, and San Francisco broke a rain record previously set in 1917, according to The Guardian. Heavy rain and high winds battered the US west coast, temporarily grounding planes at San Francisco airport, flooding roads in Napa county and forcing evacuations in southern California.

Crews worked at the flooded area on Alemany Blvd at the Highway 101 entrance during heavy rain in San Francisco on Wednesday.

San Francisco's fire department responded to hundreds of calls, many of them related to flooding along with downed trees and electrical wires. Flights at San Francisco airport were delayed for hours because of wind, and officials canceled several ferries leading from the city because of the storm.

Areas along the city's Embarcadero flooded as waves crashed over barricades recently installed to prevent flooding near the Ferry Building, a waterfront landmark.

A car rested in floodwater near the 101 freeway on Wednesday in Sausalito.

Tens of thousands of households across the region lost power. Several highways were blocked by flooding or debris in Napa, Marin, San Mateo and Santa Clara counties. A 47-mile (76km) segment of Highway 1 near Big Sur was closed on Wednesday for flooding or debris.

As the storm moved south, authorities in southern California issued evacuation orders. The Los Angeles county fire department urged residents in the Palisades, Hurst and Sunset burn scar areas to leave, warning of a risk of debris flows.

In the mountains, heavy snow forced the closure of highways in the Tahoe area. Snow accumulations of up to 4 feet (1.2 meters) were forecast through Friday in the highest portions of the Sierra Nevada.

The Guardian described atmospheric rivers as huge currents of water vapor that can carry as much or even more water than an actual river, and said they have been growing stronger and deadlier in recent years. In the relatively dry western US, the storms provide both essential precipitation and flood risk.

California was bracing for more weather challenges in the week ahead, when fresh rain is set to coincide with high tides, The Guardian reported.
