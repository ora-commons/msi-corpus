## Medicaid programs paid $10 billion for autism care last year

The Wall Street Journal analyzed billings from five of the largest private-equity-backed providers of autism therapy to Medicaid patients. The group’s average weekly therapy hours for those patients were 29% higher than competitors’ in 2024, and all but one of the companies delivered more hours than local rivals, according to Anna Wilde Mathews, Christopher Weaver and Tom McGinty.

The Journal also reported that Medicaid plans and private insurers will often pay for up to about 40 hours of autism therapy a week. At some large chains backed by private-equity investors, former employees told the Journal they felt pressure to maximize therapy hours; the companies dispute those claims. Medicaid programs financed by state and federal taxpayers paid $10 billion for autism therapy last year, up from around $2 billion in 2021. The Journal said the increase was driven in part by increasing treatment hours per patient.

The Journal said the New York Stock Exchange and Nasdaq have spent years trying to outdo each other as they compete for “the hottest new listings,” pitching companies on their ability to generate buzz and offering “goodies such as billboards and lavish galas.” It said, “Lately, it’s not much of a fight.”

Dealogic counted 59 traditional IPOs by Nasdaq raising nearly $120 billion so far this year through Friday, while the NYSE landed 32 companies raising $20 billion.

Some NYSE officials privately said Nasdaq’s decision to fast-track the addition of newly public companies like SpaceX to its popular Nasdaq-100 index “made it all but impossible to compete,” the Journal reported. Corrie Driebusch reported that the Texas Stock Exchange was also “an emerging threat.”

In a Journal video, Alphabet’s Demis Hassabis, who co-founded Google DeepMind and whom the Journal considers a pioneer in machine learning, discussed the most pressing question in science, his Nobel Prize and why his brain always needs a problem to solve. The segment came from a full conversation with Ben Cohen.

Anthropic disclosed a net loss for the first half of this year in a draft IPO prospectus viewed by the Journal. The loss was much larger than the $13 billion loss recorded in the same period last year, but the Journal said the red ink largely stemmed from an accounting adjustment tied to financing agreements. The disclosures offered a rare financial look as the AI company prepared for an IPO that could help it reach a valuation of $2 trillion.

The Journal wrote, “Loneliness used to be a shameful secret.” It said people now disclose isolation to thousands of strangers on TikTok and Reddit, hoping one of them might want to hang out. The classifieds-like posts were candid: “This is going to seem sad, but I really need to find some friends” or “I’m tired of being lonely.”

Some posts drew internet trolls, but far more drew dozens of supportive replies, and a surprising number yielded real-world best friendships, the Journal reported.

The newsletter’s separate Starlink account directly attributed the Oct. 14 slowdown to Elon Musk: “Our reporting shows Elon Musk authorized the Starlink slowdown.”

The military-grade Starlink service relied on by Ukraine was throttled on Oct. 14, triggering panic in Kyiv and confusion inside the Trump administration over who had ordered the restrictions, the Journal reported. President Trump denied ordering the restrictions, while SpaceX founder Musk had been discussing Ukraine with Trump. Volodymyr Zelensky’s complaints that Ukrainians could die if the unrestricted service was not restored led to an informal investigation inside the White House.

The episode had pushed U.S.-Ukraine tensions to their highest level since the previous year’s Oval Office confrontation between Trump and Zelensky, the Journal reported. The newsletter wrote, “But it has also highlighted Musk’s singular influence over the war.” It added, “With a seeming flick of a switch, he has the power to alter the course of a deadly conflict.” The Journal’s headline said, “Musk Ordered Starlink Slowdown That Crippled Ukraine’s Drones and Sowed Panic in Kyiv.”
