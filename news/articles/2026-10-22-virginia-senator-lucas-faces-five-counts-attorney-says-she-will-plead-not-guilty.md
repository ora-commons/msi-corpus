## Lucas says prosecution is politically motivated; Spanberger criticizes election timing

Federal prosecutors have charged Democratic Virginia state Senator L. Louise Lucas with five counts — racketeering conspiracy, conspiracy to distribute marijuana, conspiracy to launder money, conspiracy to commit honest-services fraud and maintaining a drug-involved premises — and her attorney, Anthony Capozzolo, said she would plead not guilty and that she denies the charges in their entirety. Lucas, 82, said in a statement on X that the prosecution was politically motivated. Lucas has not entered a formal plea, and BBC News reported that federal prosecutors arrested her on Thursday.

The five counts form part of a sweeping racketeering case that allegedly involves fraud, drug violations and bribery. Lucas and six other people, including family members, have been charged in relation to the case, which alleges that Lucas and her co-conspirators obtained and illegally distributed large amounts of illegal marijuana. Prosecutors also accused her of using her political influence to benefit herself and her family.

Capozzolo said in a statement that the defense was “surprised and disheartened” to learn of the indictment and that Lucas “denies these charges in their entirety.” He told the BBC that Lucas would plead not guilty.

Lucas, 82, claimed the prosecution was politically motivated in a statement on X. “I will not be intimidated,” she wrote. “I will not be bullied. And I will not back down.” In a post on X responding to the charges, she wrote: “I grew up under Jim Crow,” referencing an era of legal segregation in the US. “I’ve seen what happens when people in power try to silence those who stand up to them. I spent my entire life fighting back, and I’m not stopping now.”

At a press conference on Thursday, US Attorney General Todd Blanche accused Lucas of overseeing a criminal “enterprise,” arranging for the “acquisition and distribution of marijuana,” and using her power as a lawmaker to influence legislation that would benefit herself and her family. Other alleged participants were charged with a firearm charge and wire fraud.

Lucas is not running for re-election. Her daughter, Lisa Lucas-Burke, is also charged in the indictment and is running for the city council of Portsmouth, Virginia.

Lucas has served in the Virginia State Senate since 1992 and assumed the role of Senate president pro tempore in 2000. She has publicly spoken about her cannabis dispensary and her support for legalizing marijuana, as well as her opposition to President Donald Trump and Republican lawmakers. In a 2022 post on X she wrote: “I’m a 78 year grandma who legalized pot and now has my own cannabis store. And I’m the last thing standing between The GOP and total control of Virginia. That’s worth an RT!”

Small amounts of cannabis are legal in Virginia for personal or medical use, although major restrictions continue to apply to sales and consumption. Lucas has backed legislation to legalize recreational marijuana and is an owner of The Cannabis Outlet in Portsmouth, Virginia. WAVY News reported that the shop opened in 2021, after Virginia decriminalized simple marijuana possession and legalized personal possession of certain small amounts of marijuana.

The Virginia Mercury reported in 2022 that products sold in Lucas’s shop may have contained substances and dosages that conflicted with state law at the time. That report predates the racketeering indictment and does not establish a connection between the products and the current charges.

The Federal Bureau of Investigation raided Lucas’s office and a cannabis business in May as part of a long-running inquiry, The Washington Post reported at the time.

The case came weeks before the November 3 US midterm elections, with early voting already underway. BBC News reported that it is unusual for the Justice Department to announce major actions involving lawmakers, candidates or other electorally sensitive matters in the weeks before an election. The Justice Department also recently announced inquiries involving lawmakers and election officials, including a raid on the home of Democratic Congresswoman Ilhan Omar as the government investigates her immigration status.

Virginia Governor Abigail Spanberger, a Democrat, wrote on X that the Lucas indictment’s timing undermines a longstanding Justice Department practice. “The timing of this indictment undermines the US Department of Justice’s previously longstanding policy of not taking legal actions — to include indictments or significant investigative steps — close to an election,” she wrote. She added: “And sadly, this timing further erodes the trust the American people deserve to have in the Department of Justice.”

Lucas has not entered a formal plea. Capozzolo said she would plead not guilty.
