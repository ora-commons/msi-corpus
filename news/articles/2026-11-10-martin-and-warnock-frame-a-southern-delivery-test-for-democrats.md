## Both parties could face crowded 2028 candidate fields

Two years after voters ousted Democrats from power in Washington, the 2026 midterms appeared to send a new message. Democrats are set to enter the next Congress with one of the largest majorities in a generation after flipping dozens of House districts and at least seven Senate seats in mainly Trump-won communities. The party also controls a majority of governorships and has gained control of several additional state legislative chambers. The immediate test for that power was delivery; the early outlook for 2028 included crowded candidate fields for both parties.

## Delivery becomes the test for expanded Democratic power

Democratic National Committee Chairman Ken Martin said the breadth of the victory surprised him. "I think one of the big surprises for me on Tuesday night was just how deep and wide our election results were," he said. "This was the biggest election cycle for Democrats in the history of our party." Martin, who had faced criticism from some inside the party throughout his term as chairman, said the victories across the country reflected the party's focus on issues like affordability and ending the war in Iran.

Martin said the midterms were "bigger than politics and bigger than party," and he described the results as a "massive repudiation of Donald Trump and the MAGA Trumpers and the Republican Party." He turned the result into a warning about governing: "I think it's an admonition for Democrats, for sure: Now that we're in power, we have to deliver," he said. "But it was a complete message to Republicans. You had the ability to help us and you did nothing."

With an expanded congressional majority and a lame-duck president, Martin said the South would be one of the frontlines for determining whether Democratic party power would hold. The immediate post-election debate over that power was also visible as newly elected members of Congress arrived in Washington for orientations. Competing visions for Democratic governance appeared in postmortem event panels called "What Worked" and "What Just Happened?" and in event names like "What's Next," while party strategists argued online about which wing of the party had been most successful.

## Southern coalition and demographic shifts reshape the party's position

Martin tied the region's importance to demographic change. "Six of the 10 fastest growing parts of the country right now are below the Mason-Dixon line, and 40% of the U.S. population is going to live in the South after the next Census," he said. Looking toward 2032 and reapportionment, he said Minnesota, Michigan, Wisconsin, Pennsylvania, New York, Rhode Island, Illinois and California would lose electoral and congressional votes to the South.

The midterms brought the South's political power, especially with Black voters, into focus. Black voter turnout helped elect the country's first Black female governor in Georgia and may elect the first Black U.S. senator from Mississippi since Reconstruction. NPR reported that a slew of Republican-led states seeking to redraw House maps for partisan advantage backfired, as Democrats gained several seats across the region.

Georgia Democratic Sen. Raphael Warnock said in an interview with NPR that the Democratic victories in 2026 were a "proof of concept" that the South is central to the future of American politics. "You saw a multiracial coalition of voters stand up on Tuesday night beyond anything I had even hoped for, and they offered a silent rebuke of where we are now," he told NPR. "It's now up to us to provide the leadership, even as we listen to people, and point the way forward into how we build an economy that works for everyday working families."

Warnock said Democrats' direction in the next two years should aim to serve as a cure for the last decade of Trump-dominated politics, which he described as more top versus bottom than left versus right. "Ordinary people have had their hopes dashed time and time again, cycle after cycle, across Republican and Democratic administrations," he said. "They've seen the widening chasm between the haves and the have nots." He called the midterms a "moral moment" that would help determine what kind of nation America wants to be.

## 2028 candidate lanes and swing-state map widen

In the early days after the 2026 elections, NPR's look ahead to the presidential race found both parties with the potential to see crowded fields of candidates in several new lanes reacting to signals sent by the electorate. Warnock, who will be up for reelection in 2028, was among the names floated as a presidential contender. The list also included former Transportation Secretary Pete Buttigieg, California Rep. Ro Khanna, Illinois Gov. JB Pritzker, Kentucky Gov. Andy Beshear, former Vice President Kamala Harris and others who had spent significant time in the South campaigning for other Democrats.

Martin said the 2026 victories also scrambled the traditional map of swing states. He suggested Democrats would start off competitive in places like Florida and Texas and as favorites to win the White House. "Texas is no longer a red state. It is a blue state. They didn't even go from red to purple to blue, which is a normal sort of progression," he said. "If Texas is now a blue state, and you look at Georgia and you look at other parts of the South, how do the Republicans get to 270 votes?"
