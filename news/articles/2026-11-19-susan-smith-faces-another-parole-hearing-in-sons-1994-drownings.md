## Boys' father and prosecutors plan to oppose release

Susan Smith will join Thursday's virtual parole hearing by video from prison, according to The Associated Press, as the South Carolina Department of Probation, Parole and Pardon Services considers whether to release the 55-year-old. A jury convicted Smith of the drownings of her sons, 3-year-old Michael and 14-month-old Alex, and chose not to give her the death penalty. She was sentenced to life in prison.

Under South Carolina law at the time, Smith could request parole after 30 years and could ask again every two years after a denial. Her parole request was unanimously denied in 2024. The board has seven members, and release requires support from two-thirds of them.

State agency statistics cited by The Associated Press show that the board granted parole in only 4% of cases before it, with most grants involving nonviolent cases. The Associated Press described Smith's case as one of the most notorious cases to come before the board.

David Smith, the boys' father, vowed at the first hearing to oppose his ex-wife's release for as long as he could. He and the original prosecutors plan to make an early-morning trip to Columbia to ask the board to keep Smith in prison. The Associated Press reported that David Smith was likely to pin the same photograph of his young sons to his jacket. The photograph had been sent around the world when people thought a carjacker had kidnapped the boys.

In 1994, Smith made international headlines by insisting for nine days that a Black carjacker had driven away with Michael and Alex at the bottom of John D. Long Lake in Union County. The Associated Press said the claims by Smith, who is white, played into a centuries-old racist trope of Black men being a danger to white women. The report said the claims also stoked concerns about crime that were prevalent in 1990s America and remain today.

Prosecutors have long argued that Smith killed her sons because she believed they were the reason the wealthy son of the owner of the business where she worked broke off their affair.

Smith and her attorneys blamed her mental health at her trial 30 years ago, saying she planned to go into the lake with her sons but changed her mind at the last moment. The Associated Press reported that her lawyer was likely to repeat that argument from her first parole hearing, along with the claim that Smith has no other criminal history and would be a low risk to ever commit a crime again.

At her first parole hearing, Smith said God had forgiven her and asked for the same mercy. She cried, said what she did was horrible and said she would give anything to bring her sons back.

For nine days in 1994, Smith made numerous and sometimes tearful pleas asking that Michael and Alex be returned safely before she confessed.

Investigators' re-creation showed that it took six minutes for Smith's Mazda to dip below the surface. Cameras inside the vehicle showed water pouring in through the vents and rising steadily. The boys were still strapped into their car seats. Their bodies were found dangling upside-down in the seats, with one tiny hand pressed against a window.
