## White House withdraws FDA nominee ahead of Senate committee vote

Geoff Bennett reported for PBS NewsHour that Robert Garcia, the incoming chairman of the House Oversight Committee, was taking his first steps toward investigating Trump officials and Trump family members. Garcia called on Vice President Vance, White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller and President Donald Trump's sons Donald and Eric, among others, to preserve documents related to their political and business activities.

The FDA remained without a full-time leader after the White House pulled its nominee for the post ahead of a Senate committee vote scheduled for the same day. Dr. Heidi Overton, a physician and domestic policy adviser to President Trump, did not appear to have enough support even among Republicans. Overton helped shape some of the administration's most controversial health policies, including efforts to scale back the number of vaccines routinely recommended for children. It remained unclear whether the White House would nominate someone else before Democrats take control of Congress in January.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced a new military agreement with Venezuela covering joint military training and intelligence sharing between the two countries. Venezuelan state television showed footage of Hegseth's visit to the presidential palace in Caracas, where he met interim President Delcy Rodriguez. The Pentagon had not previously disclosed the visit. Hegseth was the latest Trump administration official to visit Venezuela since U.S. forces ousted then-President Nicolas Maduro in January.

House Democrats put House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries of New York forward for speaker in a closed-door ballot that drew 251 votes in favor and 11 against. The nomination puts Jeffries within reach of the gavel, but a separate vote of the full House is required when the new Congress convenes in January. Jeffries would be the first Black American to hold the speaker's gavel.

A group of incoming Democrats who opposed Jeffries's nomination said they had secured commitments to discuss their concerns in good faith. Jeffries pointed to the size of his victory and said the results spoke for themselves. He said: "We're going to work hard throughout every corner of the caucus to deliver for the American people. Our job is to fight MAGA extremism, not each other, to fight MAGA extremism, and to get big things done for the American people."

House Democrats also elected Katherine Clark as majority leader, Pete Aguilar as majority whip and Joe Neguse as assistant speaker. All three ran unopposed.

Democrats also secured a 56-to-44 majority in the U.S. Senate after Mary Peltola won the last outstanding race in Alaska. Peltola flipped the seat, defeating two-term Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan in a contest called the previous night. In a victory speech posted on social media, Peltola focused on the rising cost of living, especially fuel prices: "From Adak to Metlakatla to Utqiagvik, you have made your voices heard. And it's clear that the status quo in D.C. cannot go on any longer. We need to bring prices down. We need to lower fuel costs and build new homes."

Peltola also made history as one of the first two Native American women elected to the U.S. Senate, alongside Minnesota Democrat Peggy Flanagan. Alaska Public Media's Liz Ruskin told PBS NewsHour that Peltola's roots helped her claim victory. Ruskin said: "I think, for a lot of Alaskans, it's significant to see an Alaskan native person elected to the U.S. Senate. And beyond that, she was raised on the Kuskokwim River in a rural community that's off-the-road system. For some Alaskans, they see this as a homegrown win, despite the hundreds of millions of dollars that were poured into Alaska for this race."

It took nearly two weeks for Alaska's race to be called, in part because of the state's lengthy timeline for receiving mail and ballots. The state uses ranked-choice voting, under which a candidate must secure a majority of the vote to win.

In the Middle East, Iran-backed Houthi rebels fired missiles at Saudi Arabia after Saudi-led forces launched airstrikes in Yemen. One strike set a factory on fire in the port city of Hudaydah, where Houthi health officials said at least 15 people were killed. Another airstrike hit a shoe factory in Yemen's capital, the report said, killing at least two people.

Separately, Iran said it launched a drone attack on a military base in Kuwait used by U.S. forces. Kuwait said it shot down the drone before it reached its target. The U.S. military said no U.S. service members had been injured in Iranian attacks during the week.

Wall Street stocks ended mixed ahead of Anthropic's public listing the next day. FRED's raw readings for Nov. 18 put the [Dow Jones Industrial Average](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DJIA) at 50,759.42, the [Nasdaq Composite](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NASDAQCOM) at 24,328.38 and the [S&P 500](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SP500) at 7,323.63. The Dow Jones industrial average fell more than 500 points, the Nasdaq gained less than half a percent, and the S&P 500 posted its fifth straight losing session.

Gebhard Galle, one of the last Pearl Harbor survivors, died earlier in the week on the same day as Freeman Johnson. Galle was 20 years old when he served aboard the USS Nevada during the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor on Dec. 7, 1941, and a torpedo struck his ship. He later helped save four fellow sailors while serving aboard another vessel. Galle was 105 years old, and fewer than a dozen Pearl Harbor survivors are known to be alive.
