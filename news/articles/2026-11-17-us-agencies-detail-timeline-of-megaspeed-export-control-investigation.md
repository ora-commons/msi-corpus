## Nvidia records request preceded June site check at Megaspeed

The US Department of Justice and Department of Commerce confirmed Monday that they are investigating Singapore-based Megaspeed for suspected violations of US export controls and released a timeline showing that the inquiry began in 2024. The agencies suspect Megaspeed of helping divert advanced chips to China, potentially in violation of US controls.

Megaspeed is the biggest buyer of Nvidia chips in South East Asia. Questions have also been raised about the possibility that high-end AI chips ended up in China despite US export restrictions. Singapore does not prohibit transfers of advanced AI chips to companies abroad, but such transfers can breach US export controls.

Megaspeed said on Sunday that it had launched an internal investigation into its business. On Monday, the Justice and Commerce departments said that "it is in the public interest to provide the facts and address inaccurate information" regarding the investigation. The agencies used the announcement to lay out the inquiry's timeline.

The Justice and Commerce departments said they began investigating Megaspeed in 2024 "for potential export control violations." In May 2025, Commerce investigators requested records from Nvidia "concerning direct or indirect sales to Megaspeed."

In June 2025, Nvidia placed Megaspeed on its 'no sell' list. That month, Bureau of Industry and Security agents, working with Singaporean law enforcement officials, conducted an on-site check at Megaspeed and interviewed Alice Huang, who was the company's chief executive at the time.

Following that enforcement action, the Justice Department took the lead role in the criminal investigation of Megaspeed. The government said it would "bring the full extent of the law against Megaspeed for any criminal conduct that may have occurred."

The departments also said: "Sensitive technologies like semiconductors are among the most valuable and strategic assets of the US economy, and the Department of Justice and the Department of Commerce will remain vigilant in pursuing justice for those who want to compromise America's technological edge."

Concerns about possible chip transfers emerged in recent weeks after Nvidia founder Jensen Huang revealed he had an extramarital affair, allegedly with former Megaspeed executive Alice Huang. US authorities suspect Alice Huang of spying for Beijing, which China denies. A Reuters image caption said Alice Huang and Jensen Huang were pictured together in Taiwan in June 2024.

Jensen Huang resigned from his role as chief executive last week following revelations that he had two affairs. BBC News described the reported affairs as creating a crisis for Nvidia, which it called one of the world's most valuable firms and a major company in the AI industry central to US competition with China.

The affair disclosures at Nvidia have created tensions between the US Treasury and Commerce departments over shipments of high-end chips to Megaspeed. On Friday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent blamed the Commerce Department, led by Howard Lutnick, for failing to properly oversee chip exports. Lutnick has been called to testify before the Senate Banking Committee next month about the department's role in allowing Nvidia to export advanced chips.

Jensen Huang has agreed to testify in Congress, including before the House Select Committee on China, to discuss whether US national security was compromised by his affair.

The White House said on Monday it would further restrict China's access to AI chips from firms like Nvidia.
