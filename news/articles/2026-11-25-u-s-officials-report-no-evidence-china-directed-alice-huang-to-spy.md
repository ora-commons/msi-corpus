## Briefing says China's spy agency approached Alice Huang under law

U.S. officials told congressional staffers in a highly classified briefing Tuesday that no evidence shows Chinese intelligence directed Alice Huang to spy on Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang. People familiar with the discussion said current evidence collected by the United States suggests the relationship between Alice Huang and Jensen Huang may have been amorous rather than a coordinated operation by China, but those people said new information could change that or any other assessment.

The session was a broad overview rather than a detailed intelligence rundown, and it came one day after President Trump helped reinstall Jensen Huang as Nvidia's chief executive following a pressure campaign. Trump had assured Nvidia that Huang posed no national-security threat. Huang had stepped down earlier this month after reports that Nvidia's board was preparing to oust him and that he had other affairs, including one with an Nvidia executive.

The CEO's allies have said there is not sufficient evidence proving Alice Huang is a spy or that Nvidia knew about alleged efforts to smuggle chips to China. Some current and former U.S. officials have said that regardless of what information changed hands, an affair between one of America's most influential businesspeople and a woman connected to Chinese intelligence posed national-security risks.

Some U.S. officials insist that passing any information to China makes Alice Huang a spy, regardless of the reason for her actions. Others said her links to Chinese intelligence were more circumstantial, given how Chinese business leaders are often compelled to remain in contact with the country's national-security apparatus.

The briefers said China's Ministry of State Security, the country's premier spy agency, approached Alice Huang to provide the agency with information, as compelled under Chinese law, and that she was in contact with the service during the affair. U.S. officials said they had no indications she had disclosed intelligence of significance to the Ministry of State Security. The New York Times earlier reported on the briefing's contents.

The briefing also addressed Kang Wei, a man described as Alice Huang's husband. Briefers said Kang Wei was a Ministry of State Security asset who was often in touch with Chinese intelligence. People who know Kang Wei and Alice Huang have described them as husband and wife.

Officials from the Central Intelligence Agency, Federal Bureau of Investigation, Office of the Director of National Intelligence and National Security Agency attended the session for congressional staffers from the House and Senate Intelligence Committees and chamber leaders. The sensitive discussion followed weeks of speculation about the nature of Jensen Huang's relationship with Alice Huang.

National security adviser and Secretary of State Marco Rubio oversaw the drafting of a classified memo about the relationship in the spring, and Trump was briefed on the concerns at least twice. Trump maintained a close working relationship with Huang, who has helped shape the administration's AI policies.

U.S. intelligence agencies continue to investigate what influence, if any, the affair had on the alleged smuggling of Nvidia chips into China in violation of U.S. export controls. Nvidia's board is also still investigating the matter, and national intelligence director Jay Clayton urged the board to continue.

Alice Huang was a top executive at Megaspeed International, an Nvidia partner accused of smuggling Nvidia chips to China in violation of U.S. export-control rules. The Justice Department is investigating her as part of two probes into chip diversion by Megaspeed and another company, Super Micro, and lawmakers are seeking more information about her relationship with Jensen Huang.

The briefing came ahead of Huang's Capitol Hill appearances next week. He is scheduled to be privately interviewed by three House committees, including the House Intelligence Committee, on Tuesday, before appearing at a public Senate Banking Committee hearing on Dec. 3. Nvidia's general counsel is also scheduled to brief House lawmakers.
