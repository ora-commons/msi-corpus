## Vance's four-point roadmap comes as Democrats take both chambers

Vice-President JD Vance broke with President Donald Trump over the Iran war on Wednesday, blaming the war for the Republican Party's loss of the House of Representatives and the Senate and calling the decision to go to war a clear political mistake. In a 600-word statement on X, Vance wrote: "The reason we lost is the war." He added: "The president himself said so last night." The statement also acknowledged that Trump's 2024 voters had deserted Republicans in the midterm election.

Trump had defended the war in interviews late Tuesday night and Wednesday while acknowledging that it played a factor in Democrats flipping control of both chambers. After Vance's post, the president said he agreed with Vance that the midterms were "largely" decided by the war and that he would "do it again" if given the same choices. Asked about Vance's criticism, Trump said: "I know how he feels about it. I think it was -- I think it was fine." Trump also cited the nuclear question, saying "we stopped them from having a nuclear weapon, and they will never have a nuclear weapon."

The split came hours after Trump and the party were handed a bruising election defeat. Vance had repeatedly backed the conflict since the US and Israel attacked Tehran in February, but he has reportedly expressed opposition in private meetings with administration officials. BBC News described that disagreement as an open secret in Washington, kept out of public view until now.

BBC News said the statement was striking and had no recent precedent, adding that it was hard to recall a similar moment in recent US political history when a sitting vice-president had so sharply broken with the president on a signature foreign policy issue.

Vance pressed his point in terms of political cost. "If you'd like to argue it was worth it, that losing the Senate for the next 6 years was worth it, then do so. Argue it was a worthwhile cost to pay. Agree or disagree, that's at least honest," he said. The remarks were widely interpreted as an effort to distance himself from Trump ahead of a potential 2028 White House run.

Alongside the criticism, Vance offered a roadmap that could preview a possible presidential campaign, distilling the party's strategy for winning back voters into four points: "1) End the war 2) Focus on cost of living/inflation 3) Put America First 4) Change our relationship to Israel." BBC News said the list encapsulated much of Vance's political worldview and read like the core argument in a future stump speech.

The statement was carefully constructed. Vance did not mention Trump by name and attributed much of his analysis of the election results to unnamed pollsters.

Analysts read the move as distance. Daniel Byman, a foreign policy expert at the Center for Strategic and International Studies, said Vance's statement seemed like "a way of separating himself from Trump," and that drawing contrasts with Trump would be a delicate balancing act if Vance sought the presidency. Ashley Davis, a Republican strategist, said Vance "put his toe in the water to see what he's going to do."

The 2028 race remains unsettled. Vance is expected to run but has not made an official decision, and aides insist he is still mulling it. Trump has been circumspect about who he would support as the 2028 Republican presidential nominee, having praised Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio on several occasions without saying when, or if, he would make an endorsement. Trump has previously floated the idea of continuing to a third term, which the US Constitution bars.

The New York Times reported that Vance showed the message to Trump before posting it online, and BBC News said it remained an open question exactly how much Vance had broken with the president. Trump could change his mind and make clear that he would brook no dissent from Vance or anyone else. BBC News called the exchange an early sign that Trump, who has prized loyalty from his advisers, may give Vance a wide berth if Vance launches a presidential campaign.

BBC News said it had contacted Vance's office for comment.

What Vance's post amounts to is not settled. BBC News said the statement might not prove significant in the end, or could be "the starting gun for the 2028 race and the battle for the post-Trump Republican Party."
