## UAE treaties formalize closer cooperation with Western law enforcement

International fugitives are looking for new hideouts after the United Arab Emirates signed extradition treaties designed to deepen cooperation with Western authorities, according to The Wall Street Journal. The report said the circumstances for crime lords sheltering in the country were shifting.

Dubai, the desert emirate in the Persian Gulf state, had long offered refuge to international fugitives. According to the report, loose financial regulations allowed those fugitives to park illicit fortunes in the emirate.

Generous visa rules also helped them live walled off from criminal investigations in their home countries. The combination of financial and immigration access had made the emirate a place where fugitives could remain beyond the reach of investigations at home.

The UAE has recently signed a raft of extradition treaties designed to demonstrate a new level of cooperation with Western authorities. The report said the ground under crime lords sheltering in the country was shifting.

One treaty led to the extradition of Daniel Kinahan, whom the report described as an alleged Irish crime boss and one of the world’s most powerful suspected kingpins. In August, Kinahan was handcuffed and flown on a military plane back to Dublin.

His arrest and removal posed a direct question for others sheltering in the UAE. The report said: “If one of the world’s most powerful suspected kingpins wasn’t safe there, would they be?”

The case makes the treaties’ practical consequence visible: the UAE has moved from being a place where fugitives could live walled off from home-country investigations to becoming a partner in extradition with Western authorities.

For fugitives still in the country, the immediate issue is whether they can remain safe under the new arrangement. The report said international fugitives were seeking new hideouts. Whether the UAE’s new treaties would change the position of other powerful fugitives remained an open question.
