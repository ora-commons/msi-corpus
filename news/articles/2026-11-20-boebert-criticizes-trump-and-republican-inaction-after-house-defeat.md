## Boebert criticizes Trump and Republican inaction after House defeat

**Subtype:** fact

- In an Associated Press interview after her Colorado House defeat, Lauren Boebert said President Trump had done things that hurt her voters before the election.
- Boebert said Republicans had solutions but did not act, alluding to high fuel prices after the Iran war, Republican infighting and unfulfilled promises.
- Democrats flipped nine Senate seats and about four dozen House seats in the 2026 midterms, leaving the Republican Party without control of Congress.
- Boebert said she still respected Trump and wanted to continue working with him.
- Boebert said she planned to make money after Congress but gave no details about her next role.

## Democrats flipped nine Senate seats and about four dozen House seats

## The Friday AP interview and Colorado's narrow result

Boebert spoke with reporters at the U.S. Capitol in Washington, D.C., on Nov. 9, in a photograph credited to Nathan Posner/Anadolu/Getty Images. The Associated Press interview published Friday was among some of her first comments to the news media after Democratic challenger Eileen Laubacher defeated her by less than a 1% margin in a race called 10 days after the polls closed. In that interview, Boebert said she was not angry with the president but that, before the election, he had done things that hurt her voters: “I’m not mad at the president. [But] the president did some things that hurt my voters.”

The midterms cost Boebert her U.S. House seat and the Republican Party control of Congress. Boebert first joined Congress in 2021 and had been widely favored to win a fourth consecutive term. She said the result could carry more than one meaning: “This could be a referendum on President Trump,” she told the AP. “It could be the referendum on Congress or the Republican party. I think it’s all of it.”

Boebert also said in Friday’s interview that she respected Trump and wanted to continue working with him. “I respect President Trump, I still work with him, I want to continue working with him. Not for him, with him,” she told the AP. She referred only vaguely to what comes next, telling the outlet: “Too many people come to Congress and get rich. I am not one of those. I’m gonna go make some money now.”

## How Boebert allocated the blame

Boebert divided responsibility for the outcome. In Friday’s interview she pinned some of the blame for the outcome on the agenda Trump set for the first half of his second presidency, and she also said, “it’s all of the Republican party,” which had held majorities in both congressional chambers dating back to the latter part of Joe Biden’s lone term in the Oval Office.

“I think it’s more all of us,” Boebert told the AP, alluding to high fuel prices after the Iran war, Republican infighting and unfulfilled promises. “We had solutions, and we didn’t take action.”

The Guardian wrote that “low approval ratings for Trump after he started the war in Iran, pursuing an aggressive mass deportation campaign and other unpopular initiatives created an environment in which Democrats flipped nine seats in the US Senate and about four dozen in the House – including Boebert’s.”

## Earlier disputes over Trump and congressional conduct

The Guardian reported that Boebert began her congressional career by supporting Trump’s claims that the 2020 election he lost to Biden was unduly rigged by fraudsters; the Guardian characterized those claims as lies. In 2023, Boebert and a man with whom she was on a date were kicked out of a performance of the stage production *Beetlejuice* in Denver for inappropriate behavior, including vaping and groping each other. She later apologized for the conduct, which was recorded on surveillance video, saying: “I simply fell short of my values.”

Her criticism of Trump was not new. The Guardian reported that she was among the Congress members who “most loudly called for the full disclosure of files related to the prosecution of Jeffrey Epstein” — even after the Trump administration tried to convince her to drop her support for a measure that would compel such a release. The Guardian described Epstein as “the late accused sex trafficker whose social circle encompassed the US and global elite.”

Trump responded to Boebert by calling her “dumb.” He also kicked off his second presidency by vetoing a Boebert-sponsored bill that would have funded a drinking water project in her district after the legislation unanimously cleared the House and Senate. Boebert then said: “If this administration wants to make its legacy blocking projects that deliver water to rural Americans, that’s on them.”

Just weeks before her contest against Laubacher, an ethics complaint filed against Boebert accused her of having sexual relationships with three staffers, including one who was paid to stay silent. Boebert denied the allegations cited in the complaint and dismissed it as “politically motivated.”
