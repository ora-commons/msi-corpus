## Metropolitan Police says withheld material can be published without prejudicing inquiry

The UK government is preparing to publish additional documents about Lord Mandelson's appointment as U.S. ambassador. The Cabinet Office confirmed earlier this month that preparations were under way, and further documents are expected later on November 26.

Some documents were published earlier this year after MPs voted to compel the government to release them, but others were withheld at the request of the Metropolitan Police, which is investigating the former ambassador. The Metropolitan Police has since informed the government that the withheld documents can be published without prejudicing its investigation.

Mandelson became British ambassador to the US in February 2025. He was dismissed in September of that year after Downing Street said new information about the extent of his relationship with the late convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein had emerged.

The government, then led by Sir Keir Starmer, was heavily criticised for giving Mandelson the job. In February, Members of Parliament voted to force the government to publish papers relating to his appointment.

The first batch of files, released in March, ran to 147 pages. The documents showed that Starmer had been warned before Mandelson's confirmation that his relationship with Epstein posed a "reputational risk."

More documents were published in June. The 1,500 pages covered communications about Mandelson's appointment and included messages between Mandelson and ministers that strongly criticised Starmer.

The former Labour peer was arrested in February on suspicion of misconduct in public office. Mandelson has repeatedly said he believes he did not act criminally or for personal gain and that he is cooperating with police.

Following the Cabinet Office's confirmation that preparations for another release were under way, the Conservatives warned: "we still haven't got to the bottom of the Mandelson scandal."


As [MSI reported on May 31](/articles/2026-05-31-uk-government-to-release-second-tranche-of-mandelson-appointment-files/), the government was preparing a second tranche of Lord Mandelson's appointment files. The planned November release advances that disclosure sequence after the March and June batches and follows the Metropolitan Police's notification that withheld material can now be published without prejudicing its inquiry.
