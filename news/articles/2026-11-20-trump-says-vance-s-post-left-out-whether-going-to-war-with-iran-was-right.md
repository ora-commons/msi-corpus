## Vance’s three assignments included Iran talks, fraud and tech outages

During an interview in China, President Trump praised Vice President JD Vance’s work, saying the vice president was doing a “very good job.”

Fox News asked Trump why Vance had been pulled out of the Iran talks. Trump immediately raised Vance’s post, although he had not been asked about it.

It initially looked as though Vance was going to negotiate with Iranians about how to end the war. Trump canceled that plan and said he wanted Vance focused on rooting out fraud in Democratic-run states. Right before he was to speak at a New York Times event, Vance then took on the government’s response to a series of tech outages that had conspiracy theories flying.

The post at issue came the morning after the midterm elections. Vance wrote a lengthy post on X saying heavy GOP losses were due to one reason: the Iran war. Some read the post as an implicit critique of Trump, though Vance did not explicitly make that connection.

Vance also did not say in the post that going to war with Iran was the right decision. Trump said, “He basically just left one thing out,” then asked, “If we had it to do again, would you do it again?” and answered, “absolutely yes.”

The Wall Street Journal said Vance had “whipsawed” between three roles over the past few weeks, in part pushed and pulled by Trump himself. Its chronology began with what looked like prospective Iran negotiations, followed by a fraud-focused assignment and then the assignment to address the government’s response to tech outages.

Beyond the immediate policy assignments, the episode raised questions about Vance’s standing with Trump. The Wall Street Journal said it was not yet clear whether Trump would back Vance if Vance ran for president in 2028; the answer did not appear to be “definitely.”

In an October interview with Mark Halperin, Vance said he thought Trump would endorse him if he ran for president. Trump was asked about that assessment and said the two had never discussed the matter. Trump declined to say whether he would support Vance over other candidates.

Trump’s remarks paired his praise for Vance’s performance with his observation that Vance’s post left out whether going to war was right. The Wall Street Journal said their dynamic would be very closely studied, and that Trump had not given a definite answer about backing Vance in 2028.
