## Sentencing is set for 17 March following the federal arson verdict

A jury found the 30-year-old former Uber driver Jonathan Rinderknecht guilty of two federal arson charges on Friday in the retrial of the case involving the January 2025 Palisades fire in Los Angeles.

The fire killed 12 people and devastated the Pacific Palisades, an affluent enclave on the California coast, destroying thousands of homes and businesses.

The Friday verdict came months after the first trial ended with a hung jury. A fresh batch of jurors heard the prosecution’s case for two weeks during the retrial.

Prosecutors argued that Rinderknecht used a lighter to set the Lachman brush fire in a hillside area in January 2025. Firefighters initially suppressed the fire, but its embers simmered underground. According to the prosecution’s account, powerful winds swept the embers above ground, where they erupted into the Palisades inferno.

Prosecutors also cast Rinderknecht as a disgruntled young man who sought retribution against the world for personal gripes, including failed romances, and perceived societal injustices such as wealth inequality.

Rinderknecht maintained his innocence, pleading not guilty to the revised charges of destruction of property by means of fire and arson affecting property used in interstate commerce.

During the retrial, jurors were instructed explicitly not to consider the adequacy of the fire department’s response to the Lachman fire and the Palisades blaze. The department had faced criticism for failing to fully extinguish the Lachman fire before it eventually reignited.

Some jurors from the first panel were wary of pinning the blame squarely on Rinderknecht and cited the fire department’s past actions. The deadlocked panel included two votes for guilty and 10 jurors who were not convinced.

Rinderknecht’s defense team advanced an alternative explanation during the retrial: the initial brush blaze had been ignited by fireworks. The proposed explanation included a defense witness whose testimony would reference stains at a private residence that supposedly indicated fireworks had been discharged.

Prosecutors dismissed the theory as “baseless” in a court filing. They raised concerns about the reliability and late introduction of the defense witness.

Rinderknecht will be sentenced on 17 March.
