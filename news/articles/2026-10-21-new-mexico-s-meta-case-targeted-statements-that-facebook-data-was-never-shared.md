## Judge Mathew said the lies went to the heart of Facebook's core business

New Mexico's lawyers sued Meta in 2021 over statements the company made saying that Facebook user data was never sold or shared with third parties, though it was. The lawsuit was based on the Cambridge Analytica scandal, in which the firm used Facebook user information in political consulting that included the 2016 presidential election.

A jury in the State District Court in Santa Fe ruled against Meta after a weeklong trial, finding that the company had spent years lying to the public about how it handled Facebook user data.

"The lies extend beyond Facebook's data practices as well," Judge Francis J. Mathew said, adding that they went to "the heart of Facebook's core business practices."

The jury said Meta had violated New Mexico's consumer protection laws 43 million times. Each violation carried a penalty of up to $5,000. The original penalty was $219 billion, but Mathew reduced it to $25 billion on October 21.

In his judgment, Mathew wrote that "the harm in this matter is difficult to overstate, and goes to the heart of Facebook's basic business proposition." He said the "extraordinary number of willful violations" that repeated over years "support the highest level of penalty."

Meta, which owns Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp, has faced thousands of lawsuits about child safety, social media addiction, censorship and data use.

In August, a New Mexico judge ordered the company to pay $567 million in damages after a jury ruled that it had enabled harm to young users, on top of a $375 million penalty from that same case in March.

Separately, Meta reached a settlement in August with 47 states and the District of Columbia and U.S. territories. In it, Meta agreed to pay up to $17.1 billion. It also agreed to change the way children interact with the platforms.

New Mexico Attorney General Raúl Torrez said in a statement that "For too long, powerful technology companies have operated as though their size and influence put them beyond the reach of the law." He added: "Today's result makes clear that no corporation is too powerful to be held accountable."
