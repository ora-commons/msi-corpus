## Budget speech says Russia wants more than Ukraine

Friedrich Merz used a budget speech to connect Germany’s defense decisions with what he described as Russia’s broader escalation. He warned that “Russia knows exactly what it wants and Russia wants more than Ukraine,” and said Europe must set clear boundaries for Russia.

Merz’s wide-ranging speech also addressed what he called Germany’s long-neglected priorities. He said the country had neglected fundamental issues for too long, listing defense, infrastructure, demographic change and the economy, and said those issues required urgent attention.

Merz turned to the external situation, particularly the threat from Russia, to explain why some decisions, especially on defense, were needed. His warning over Russia’s aims provided the central external rationale for those choices.

He then addressed people who did not grasp that assessment: “Anyone who fails to grasp this either does not understand the world as it is, or is pursuing an entirely different agenda – one that stands in opposition to the freedom, openness, and liberal values of our own country.”

Merz’s warning extended across several areas. “Russia is escalating tensions in the Baltic region, in Poland, in the Black Sea, and even in the outer space.” He also said escalation was occurring inside Germany’s borders at the cost of human lives.

“It is escalating on a massive scale – including here within our own borders, even at the cost of human lives,” Merz said. His conclusion was collective: “And that is why we, as Europeans, must—and will – set clear boundaries for Russia.”

The speech joined Germany’s domestic budget argument to a European security response. Merz treated defense as one part of a broader set of choices involving infrastructure, demographic change and the economy, while using Russia’s regional escalation to explain the urgency of defense decisions. His proposed boundaries were for Europeans collectively.
