## Preowned luxury gains ground as brands struggle in China

China's secondhand luxury market expanded last year even as the country's overall luxury market contracted. The Wall Street Journal's Hannah Miao visited ZZER in Shanghai, one of China's largest secondhand luxury stores, to examine the growing appetite among Chinese shoppers for preowned luxury goods.

Luxury brands were struggling in China as the broader market weakened, Bain reported. China's overall luxury market contracted last year, but the market for secondhand goods moved in the opposite direction.

Bain estimated that the luxury resale market grew more than 15% to roughly 30 billion yuan, or more than $4 billion, last year. The figures placed the resale segment well above its previous size even as the wider market shrank.

Miao, a reporter for The Wall Street Journal based in Singapore who covers China's economy and its ties with Asia and the world, visited the Shanghai store for a report and video on the resale market.

ZZER, which The Wall Street Journal described as one of China's largest secondhand luxury stores, carried preowned products from top-tier brands including Hermès and Chanel. The store offered those goods at a fraction of their original retail prices.

Miao's visit was intended to examine what was behind the growing appetite among Chinese shoppers for secondhand luxury goods. The store visit provided a concrete example of the segment's appeal while Bain's estimates showed its scale.
