## FBI investigating apparent hack of national crime database

**Subtype:** fact

- The FBI is investigating an apparent hack of the National Crime Information Center, a person familiar with the matter said Saturday.

## Scope of breach unclear; FBI declines to comment

The person who confirmed the investigation to The Associated Press was not authorized to speak by name and spoke on the condition of anonymity. The FBI declined to comment Saturday on the apparent hack, which CNN first reported.

The National Crime Information Center, known as the NCIC, is a computerized database of criminal justice information that is available to local, state and federal law enforcement agencies. Its records cover fugitives, missing persons, criminal history data, stolen property and other topics.

The scope of the problem was not immediately clear.

Asked about the subject on Saturday, President Donald Trump said in a Fox News Channel interview: "We just heard about it and I will say that it's not good."

News that the NCIC was targeted comes as the country has encountered a series of digital outages in the government and private sector. Trump administration officials have said those outages were caused by routine efforts to upgrade computer systems and fix vulnerabilities, rather than by a cyberattack. There was no immediate indication that the FBI incident was related to those outages.

The bureau has historically been a frequent target for hackers. In September, the FBI said it was investigating a hack of its job portal website after a criminal hacking group known as ShinyHunters claimed it had obtained a massive trove of data on bureau employees and applicants.

And in March, the FBI disclosed that it was investigating "suspicious activities" on an internal system that contains sensitive information related to surveillance operations and investigations.
