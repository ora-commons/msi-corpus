## Nvidia stock lost about $573 billion before earnings, Mulvey says

Nvidia's new AI supercomputers began shipping during the quarter and had already made about $20 billion, according to NPR's Ricky Mulvey. Mulvey called it the fastest product launch in the company's history. He said the high-powered systems help scientists look into black holes and large technology companies develop large language models, and that part of Nvidia's revenue was tied to them.

Mulvey said Nvidia stock had lost about $573 billion since scandal stories broke, before earnings. He compared the loss with the value of Mastercard. Shares rose a bit after hours, Mulvey said, but the increase was not enough to recover from the scandals.

Mulvey said Huang had made a lot of people a lot of money, which he said was why many people were upset. He offered an investor example: “If you invested a grand in NVIDIA 10 years ago, you've made well over $100,000 today.”

Mulvey said Nvidia's operating fundamentals did not fundamentally change during the quarter or with the scandals. Setting the scandals aside, he said, “the business itself is firing on all cylinders.” When Sacha Pfeiffer asked whether losing Huang had affected the company's basic fundamentals, Mulvey answered, “It does moving forward.” He described Huang as the company's “chief storyteller” and “one of AI's central bankers,” saying Huang had been in the middle of many circular deals.

Nvidia's November 17 quarterly results also included about $116 billion in revenue, about double the year-earlier level and above expectations, Mulvey said. He said most of what Nvidia took in became pure profit and that the company was making a lot more dollars. He described Nvidia as an extraordinarily profitable business on the bleeding edge of technology.

Pfeiffer said Nvidia appeared to be moving past the scandal involving former CEO Jensen Huang's extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged spy. She also said the company had confirmed it would not reinstate Huang, that its business had remained strong for some time and that the earnings were high stakes for many investors.

On the morning of the earnings, Huang posted on X that he was open to returning. Mulvey said the timing made the post part of the earnings conversation. Huang wrote that he had “made some serious mistakes” in his personal life and “deeply regrets them.” He said an outpouring of support had given him hope about returning and that he had availability to step back into the CEO seat if doing so was in the best interests of shareholders, Nvidia and America.

Mulvey said Nvidia's earnings call began with board chair Harvey Jones saying the board did not intend to reinstate Huang as CEO. Mulvey characterized the board's answer as leaving no wiggle room.

But Mulvey said that might not be the whole story because a shareholder revolt was brewing that could potentially remove the board.

Mulvey said Huang had powerful supporters, including big-money shareholders, President Trump and tech giants such as Elon Musk. Despite that support, prediction markets viewed Huang's return to the CEO seat as unlikely. Colette Kress, who had been Nvidia's chief financial officer, was serving as interim CEO, and Mulvey said the company still had to determine who would lead it next.
