## Gabbard testified in 2025 that Iran was not building a nuclear weapon

Vice President JD Vance stopped short on Friday of endorsing President Donald Trump's claim that Iran was so close to obtaining a nuclear weapon in February that it was essential for the United States to go to war with the Islamic republic, telling reporters at the White House that it is not his place to second-guess the president.

The exchange came in the White House briefing room, where Trump appeared alongside Vance and Marco Rubio. Trump was asked about reporting this week that he was unhappy with the vice president's statement blaming Republican losses in the midterm elections on the Iran war.

"I didn't say I was unhappy with it," Trump said. "I thought the statement was fine, except I would have had one other thing, that if it were asked, I would have said that: 'Would you do it again?' And absolutely, the answer is yes, because we cannot allow Iran to have a nuclear weapon."

Trump has made the claim that Iran was close to a nuclear weapon dozens of times in the past nine months. At a campaign rally in Alaska last month, he insisted that he had to join Israel in attacking Iran in February because "they were two weeks away from having a nuclear weapon."

That assertion conflicts with what his then director of national intelligence, Tulsi Gabbard, told Congress on March 25, 2025, two months into Trump's second term. Gabbard said US intelligence agencies reported that Iran had not tried to make a nuclear weapon for more than two decades, and that the intelligence community "continues to assess Iran is not building a nuclear weapon and supreme leader Khamenei has not authorized the nuclear weapons program he suspended in 2003."

Three months after that testimony, on June 21, 2025, Trump ordered the bombing of Iran's nuclear enrichment facilities. Trump told the public that night that the strikes "were a spectacular military success" and that "Iran's key nuclear enrichment facilities have been completely and totally obliterated."

Eight months later, when Trump announced the start of a full-scale war on Iran early on February 28, 2026, he said in a video statement from Mar-a-Lago that the war was necessary to "ensure that Iran does not obtain a nuclear weapon."

The morning after this month's midterm elections, Vance said in a statement posted on social media: "The reason we lost is the war."

"If you'd like to argue it was worth it, that losing the Senate for the next 6 years was worth it, then do so," Vance added. "Argue it was a worthwhile cost to pay. Agree or disagree, that's at least honest. But everyone knows why we lost."

In the briefing room on Friday, after Trump said Vance should have said the war was necessary, a reporter asked the vice president whether he would argue that the war in Iran was worth it.

"Look, what I've said is the goal of keeping Iran from getting a nuclear weapon is absolutely what we need to be doing. It's the biggest state sponsor of terrorism. If they had a nuclear weapon, they would use it as leverage or, God forbid, they would attack and kill thousands, hundreds of thousands, of innocent people," Vance said.

"I think the president is the one who makes the decision, and the president has said he would do it again," he said.

"My view is that my goal is, and my job is, to support the president of the United States," the vice president said.

The vice president's office did not respond to a request to comment.
