## Donald Tusk says Putin is not his friend; Schifrin calls meeting scripted

State media reported that Xi Jinping warned Donald Trump to "keep doing the right thing on Taiwan," a reference to Trump's ongoing delay of a Taiwanese weapons sale. Amna Nawaz described the gathering as the first-ever trilateral meeting between the leaders of the United States, China and Russia and said Trump was largely stone-faced as he walked with Xi and Putin. Nick Schifrin, reporting for PBS NewsHour from Shenzhen, said the meeting lasted just over 80 minutes and called it "a scripted summit."

The leaders released a joint statement promising to release more oil and, for the first time, hold formal nuclear-weapons talks. Schifrin said the declaration was light on specifics and the countries still had deep divisions, even as U.S. officials emphasized its arms-control language after a remarkable and memorable meeting that the entire world was watching. The declaration set out plans for future dialogue to reduce nuclear risks and proliferation after years in which China resisted American requests for arms control. Trump said, "The United States, Russia, and China are uniquely positioned to discuss how to reduce the number of nuclear weapons on Earth, and also to lessen the risk that those weapons will ever be used. And I think, as long as you and I are in charge of things, we'll never have a problem."

The declaration also admonished the International Criminal Court. Schifrin said the United States was trying to destroy the court while the court was trying to arrest Putin for war crimes in Ukraine.

Trump said, "No peace was ever forged by declining opportunities for engagement." Schifrin said the winners of World War II divided up Europe in 1945 and embraced spheres of influence, and Putin embraced the historical echo. Through an interpreter, Putin said, "Our three states are the victors of World War II, and we hold joint responsibility for maintaining international peace and security." Schifrin noted that Yalta, a summit of victorious allies, lasted eight days, while the Shenzhen meeting lasted just over 80 minutes. He characterized the event as "a scripted summit."

Schifrin said the United States, China and Russia had held their first formal meeting as the world's three preeminent nuclear powers, with the gathering focused on what Xi called "upholding world peace." He described the scene as "one of his most indelible images" and said Trump had already lived "a lifetime of made-for-TV moments." Trump said the meeting "went great" and built "fantastic relationships." Putin said it was "the beginning of a new era of global diplomacy."

Schifrin said much of the summit's focus was on a president who prioritized personal connections and on the leaders' friendly body language rather than rivalry. At one point, Xi held Trump's arm. When Xi's hand slipped, Trump reached out to reconnect. Trump asked, "Who is he? He's my friend. Hi." Earlier, Trump joked with and embraced Putin at a separate bilateral meeting after the Kremlin said Putin had left the trilateral meeting in an "excellent mood." Trump said, "We've had a great relationship for many years. We know each other for a long time. And I think a lot of wonderful things can come out of this, or at least start."

The United States and China remained at loggerheads over export controls. Schifrin said the United States and TSMC, described as a Taiwanese chip giant, were blocking China's access to high-end chips, while Beijing was restricting the release of heavy earth magnets vital to sophisticated manufacturing. State media reported that Xi Jinping warned Trump to "keep doing the right thing on Taiwan," a reference to Trump's ongoing delay of a Taiwanese weapons sale. No agreement was reached to extend the U.S.-China trade truce, which Schifrin said was set to expire in just two months. Trump had asked Xi to make more agricultural purchases for U.S. farmers, saying, "And I will be asking you to do some more for our farmers, because you have -- China has an insatiable appetite. I can tell you that. And we can fill that appetite."

During an hour-long press conference, Putin asked, "What is democracy? Is it a liberal attempt to monopolize power, and, in reality, it's a liberal dictatorship?" Schifrin said Xi and Putin had spent years trying to build "an axis of authoritarianism," which Schifrin described as "a direct counterweight to American values," and said Putin did not hide that position during the press conference. Schifrin said the sentiment traditionally divided the countries, but Trump, Xi and Putin were in their 70s and 80s, focused on their legacies and convinced they could define the course of history together. Schifrin said Trump argued that only his connections with Xi and Putin could produce progress, then separately observed that even an American president could not defy "some international relations gravity."

Schifrin said Trump's affinity for authoritarians concerned U.S. allies, as Poland's Prime Minister Donald Tusk put it. Through an interpreter, Tusk said, "Mr. Putin is not our friend, nor will he ever be."
