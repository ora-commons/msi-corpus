## Pentagon plans eight consolidated opinions ahead of a fiscal 2028 department-wide opinion

An inspector general report released Monday said the Pentagon failed a department-wide financial audit for the ninth consecutive year in fiscal 2026. The finding came as 14 of the Pentagon's 25 standalone reporting entities passed their financial audits, up from 11 the prior year, and an official said a majority of units passed for the first time.

Jules Hurst, the U.S. Department of Defense's chief financial officer, told Reuters that an independent auditor found financial statements for those 14 entities passed in fiscal 2026, which ended in September. The entities account for 56% of the department's assets and 86% of its liabilities. “For the first time ever, a majority of assets from the department are on clean opinions,” Hurst said. The audits check whether financial statements are fairly presented and whether financial systems and processes are sound.

The Pentagon as a whole was first audited in 2018 and has consistently failed to pass annual audits because of system and accounting problems. The department has struggled with some 149 financial management systems, many of which are inadequate and outdated, including technology built on 50-year-old computer code.

Progress at individual entities offers a way around some of those limitations. The Pentagon's Medicare-Eligible Retiree Health Care Fund passed its audit for the first time in fiscal 2026. To complete the audit without relying on antiquated systems, officials drew from 61 core systems in the Pentagon's more modern cloud-based war-data platform and obtained statistically significant data samples for the audit.

The department is also using artificial intelligence tools from Palantir and xAI, along with in-house applications, to automate financial reconciliations and documentation. “Passing the audit is ultimately a time problem,” Hurst said, adding that AI allows auditors to test more data.

The administration is seeking a record $1.5tn in national defense spending from Congress. Congress gave the Pentagon 10 years to pass an audit, with the deadline falling in the fiscal year beginning in October 2027. Modernizing the department's systems has become a priority as the Pentagon seeks to show lawmakers it can be trusted.

Beginning this year, the Pentagon plans to consolidate 25 separate audit opinions into eight, ahead of seeking a full department-wide opinion in fiscal 2028.

Congress phased in audit requirements for federal agencies over decades, and most major agencies must have their financial statements audited annually. The Pentagon remains unable to secure a clean department-wide opinion as it works to modernize the systems auditors test.
