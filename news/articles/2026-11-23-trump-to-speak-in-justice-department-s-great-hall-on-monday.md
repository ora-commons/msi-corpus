## Some investigations of Trump's opponents rejected by grand juries and judges

President Donald Trump is set to address the Justice Department on Monday in the building's ceremonial Great Hall, his second speech there in less than two years. The Associated Press reported that the department has moved aggressively to fulfill the White House's agenda and pursue criminal cases against Trump's adversaries.

The White House said the speech would be about protecting American families. It remained unclear to what extent the address would also function as a forum for the Republican president to again encourage the prosecution of his political opponents and revisit years-old grievances about a Justice Department that repeatedly investigated him over the past decade.

Trump will be joined by Attorney General Todd Blanche, his former personal defense lawyer. The Associated Press reported that Blanche has been unapologetic in his view that the president is entitled to exert his will over the Justice Department.

Sweeping Republican losses in this month's midterm elections gave Democrats control of Congress. The Associated Press reported that the result set up possible House and Senate investigations over everything from the mass firings of agents and prosecutors who investigated Trump to a series of politically charged investigations by the Trump administration Justice Department that were rejected by grand juries and judges alike.

Blanche was elevated from deputy attorney general to Attorney General in April after Trump ousted Pam Bondi. The Associated Press reported that Bondi struggled to satisfy the president's demands for retribution and endured bipartisan criticism over the handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files.

Presidential addresses at the Justice Department are rare but not unheard of. Trump last visited in March 2025 for a speech meant to rally support for his administration's policies on violent crime, drugs and illegal immigration.

At that time, he aired complaints about the criminal cases he faced during the administration of Democratic President Joe Biden. Prosecutors charged him with hoarding classified documents at his Mar-a-Lago estate in Palm Beach, Florida, and with conspiring to overturn his 2020 election loss to Biden. Trump vowed retribution for what he described as the “lies and abuses that have occurred within these walls.” He also pledged to expose the “egregious crimes and severe misconduct” of his foes.

The Associated Press reported that the Justice Department accelerated its efforts to investigate Trump's opponents after that speech but encountered multiple setbacks. Cases were thrown out by judges or spurned by grand juries that refused to issue indictments.


The Associated Press described Trump's sway over the department as embodied by a banner featuring his face hanging outside the building. In February, [a banner featuring Trump's face was displayed outside Justice Department headquarters](/articles/2026-02-20-trump-banner-displayed-on-justice-department-headquarters/). The Associated Press reported that the banner still hangs outside the building as Trump prepares to return for Monday's speech, with critics saying it indicates the erosion of the institution's historic independence.
