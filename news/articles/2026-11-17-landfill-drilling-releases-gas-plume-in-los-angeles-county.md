## Six people taken to hospital with minor injuries after the gas release

A large plume of gas billowed high into the air over Castaic on Monday after crews drilling at the Chiquita Canyon Landfill in Los Angeles County caused a rush of gas to escape along with decomposing garbage.

Six people were taken to the hospital with minor injuries, according to Jonathan Torres, a spokesperson for the county’s fire department. Torres did not provide details on who the six people were.

The crews were conducting routine drilling about 150 feet (46 meters) below ground, according to Capt. Aaron Katon, a spokesperson with the fire department. Katon said the crews ran into gas that occurs naturally in landfills because of decomposing garbage.

Katon said the escaping gas carried small pieces of decomposing garbage, making the release appear as a large cloud. “As that gas escaped through that hole, it carried with it small pieces of decomposing garbage, which made the appearance of a large cloud that went into the air,” he said.

Officials monitored the air after the release, and Katon said it posed no hazard to anyone in the community.

The city of Santa Clarita warned the public to avoid the area around the landfill and told some nearby residents to prepare to evacuate.

Gov. Gavin Newsom’s office said in a statement that it was monitoring the situation.

The Chiquita Canyon Landfill is a 639-acre (259-hectare) solid waste landfill, according to California’s Department of Toxic Substances Control. By Monday night, Katon said, the plume had diminished significantly.
