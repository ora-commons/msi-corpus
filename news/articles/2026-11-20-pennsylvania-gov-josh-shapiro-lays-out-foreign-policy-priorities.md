## Shapiro urges Democratic leverage to end the Iran war

Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro sat down with PBS News interviewer Amna Nawaz at Perry World House after delivering a foreign policy speech at the University of Pennsylvania. His reelection gave him another four years in office, and Democrats won majorities in the Statehouse and in Washington. Shapiro argued that global stability and investment in the states were connected, and he tied his policy prescriptions to the security and prosperity of Pennsylvania.

“It matters to governors what is happening across the globe. It matters that we have stability across the globe to get investment in our states,” Shapiro said. He also said foreign policy mattered for the citizens Pennsylvania’s government represents.

On the Iran war, Nawaz asked how Democrats could act without a veto-proof majority. Shapiro said Congress has a war powers resolution and the power of the purse. He urged lawmakers to use that leverage to end the war and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and he expressed confidence in the speaker-designate, the House majority and the new Senate majority. Shapiro described the conflict as creating chaos overseas and in the Arab world while driving up prices for people at home.

Shapiro’s speech included this criticism of the administration: “The chaos, the cruelty, the corruption of this administration has made the U.S. less secure at home and abroad.” Nawaz used the recent historic trilateral meeting with the presidents of China and Russia to ask whether dialogue was necessary to address Ukraine, a nuclear arms deal or other issues. Shapiro said he was not opposing dialogue, but said the meeting did not address the problems he raised.

He said President Donald Trump failed to address AI, push back against Russian aggression toward Ukraine or respond to what he described as China’s efforts to undermine the U.S. economy. “I actually thought that he ended up looking like the junior partner on the international stage,” Shapiro said. He added that it was clear Xi was dominating the scene when the three leaders walked together and that Trump looked small.

On Israel, Shapiro said the issue was in many ways being resolved because President Donald Trump and Israel’s current prime minister had indicated plans to end military aid that had existed for decades. “I support that. I think it is important to end that military aid,” he said. Shapiro also said Israel needed weapons to defend against Hamas, the Houthis and Iranians, and tied that position to America’s national security interest.

Nawaz then asked how Shapiro could give allies confidence, citing Canada’s new deal with China and what she described as allies working around or without the United States. Shapiro said he regularly meets foreign leaders, ambassadors, foreign ministers and, in some cases, prime ministers. He wants those officials to know that other governors and members of Congress favor alliances, and he said those alliances need to deliver prosperity, defense and security benefits for the American people.

Shapiro said the current president was setting foreign policy poorly. He rejected the idea that a future president, Congress or governors could rely simply on the message Joe Biden used when he took office, saying, “I don't think words will cut it anymore.” He added that it might take generations to repair the destruction he said Trump had done.

The conversation then turned to the election. Nawaz cited Democratic wins in Kansas, Mississippi and South Carolina, along with Senate races, and said Shapiro’s reelection win was bigger than his previous one by 22 points. Shapiro called it a great night for Democrats and a complete rejection of Donald Trump and his enablers. He described Pennsylvania as “the swingiest swing state in the country,” said Trump had won it in two of the last three elections, and said he had set a record in every election he had run.

Pennsylvania now has its first Democratic trifecta in three decades, Nawaz said. Shapiro said his administration would carry out a long agenda over the first 100 days and then during the next two years. The first bill to reach his desk in January, he said, would raise Pennsylvania’s minimum wage from $7.25 an hour to at least $15 an hour.

Nawaz also raised Pennsylvania’s measles outbreaks, citing the Pan American Health Organization’s conclusion that the United States had lost its measles elimination status because the country was unable to control the outbreaks. She said Pennsylvania had reported about 1,529 cases that year, more than double any other state, and she described the state as the center of the outbreaks. Shapiro added that eight people had died and said measles had been eliminated in America 26 years earlier.

Shapiro attributed the resurgence to vaccine misinformation and disinformation. He said medical experts and data showed the MMR vaccine was the best protection against measles, particularly severe or fatal cases. Shapiro said RFK had undermined confidence in medicine and vaccines for a generation, including during the last two years as health secretary. He also said that work had been enabled and promoted by President Trump.

On his own political future, Shapiro did not say whether he would run for president in 2028. He said he wanted to help shape the future of the Democratic Party and the country without Donald Trump and his enablers, and that he wanted to remain part of the 2028 conversation. He said a professional decision would involve his wife and their four children and would be made soon, but his immediate focus was his work as governor.
