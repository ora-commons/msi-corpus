## SpaceX shares closed at $127.44, valuing the pledge at nearly $18bn

Elon Musk said on Wednesday that he would give every child in the United States two shares in SpaceX, the space and artificial intelligence company he runs — one issued by the company and one donated from his own holdings — in a pledge The Guardian reported was worth nearly $18bn. The announcement expands an October commitment by Musk to hand a single SpaceX share to every child in America, and it arrives as technology companies debate how to share the spoils of the AI boom.

In a post on X, the social media platform he owns, Musk said children would receive one share from the company itself and one from him personally. SpaceX announced earlier on Wednesday that it would contribute one newly issued share of its Class A common stock to every currently active Trump Account, and that Musk would match the number of shares it donates.

The Trump Account program was designed to give every American under 18 a stake in the stock market, to help build wealth from an early age. The White House said last month that it had opened almost 70 million accounts.

SpaceX shares closed at $127.44 on Wednesday, valuing the pledge at nearly $18bn. The Guardian described Musk as the world's richest person, with a net worth of around $824bn.

The pledge is not yet fully delivered. In a separate post, Musk said the company shares would be transferred next month, with his own contribution to follow later. "The share directly from me will be transferred when my lockup expires in June next year," he wrote.

SpaceX did not immediately respond to a request for comment on whether Musk's own donations would also be linked to Trump Accounts.

In October, Musk announced his intention to give one of his SpaceX shares to every child in America. "This will be the first of many," he said in a post on X at the time. As of June, he held about 6.42bn shares in the company.

Most of Musk's philanthropic efforts have been conducted through the Musk Foundation, which reportedly holds around $16bn in assets. In October, SpaceX president Gwynne Shotwell and her husband pledged a share of their own SpaceX stock to each of more than 2 million children under the Trump Account initiative.

Musk and rival AI company Anthropic have recently detailed different approaches for sharing the spoils of the AI boom. Anthropic, which went public earlier this month, said it would commit 5% of its shares to a new fund for the "public good." The company said the initiative, worth around $54bn at current prices, would give it more flexibility and scale to make donations for the benefit of humanity.

Other major, long-running philanthropy ventures include those of Berkshire Hathaway chair emeritus Warren Buffett, who is reported to have given away $60bn, with the intention of doing the same with most of the rest of his $150bn fortune. The Gates Foundation has distributed more than $100bn since its founding in 2000, with some of that fuelled by Buffett.

Musk tied the timing of his own contribution to his lockup expiring in June next year. Whether those shares would be distributed through the same Trump Account mechanism SpaceX will use for the company-issued shares remained unsettled.
