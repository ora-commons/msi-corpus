## Helena Moreno teases plan for festival with top Black producers

Festival organizers and Essence Communications, which founded the annual event, announced Tuesday across their social media platforms that Essence Fest would relocate next year from New Orleans. The festival has celebrated Black American culture in the city for more than 30 years. The statement did not give a reason for the decision or mention where the festival was moving. The statement mostly expressed gratitude to the city and its residents for helping grow the event. It said, “You didn’t simply host us. You embraced us,” and added, “And in doing so, you helped shape one of the most beautiful celebrations of Black culture in the world.”

On the same day, New Orleans Mayor Helena Moreno teased in a social media video that the city would announce details on Dec. 9 about a new “world-class” festival dedicated to Black American culture. Moreno said she was working to team up with “some of this country’s top Black producers” to bring in a festival that would realize how important it is to be in the city of New Orleans and that the city itself is a draw. She added, “We need to make sure that there’s widespread benefit for our local businesses, particularly our local Black businesses.”

The multiday festival takes place around the Fourth of July weekend and draws major musical acts. Usher, Megan Thee Stallion and Janet Jackson are among the many performers who have headlined in recent years. The festival was launched in 1995 as the Essence Music Festival, when Essence magazine staged a concert event in New Orleans to mark the publication’s 25th anniversary.

Essence magazine is part of Essence Communications, a media company that includes digital and video content. The magazine was acquired by Time Inc. in the early 2000s before becoming Black-owned again in 2018, when the founder of Sundial Brands purchased it.

The festival has also been a political draw, with panels focused on politics and voter mobilization. Politicians, both Black and white, who are in the middle of major campaigns have made Essence Fest a stop. Then-Vice President Kamala Harris spoke there in 2024 during the presidential campaign.

Essence released a report earlier this month saying the festival generated $1.3 billion in economic impact from 2022 through 2025. The report said the event supported 11,000 jobs and led to $179.3 million in tax revenue.

Local businesses and residents have questioned how much the festival benefits the community. During the festival, a “Clean Zone” ordinance prohibits smaller events in certain areas unless they are authorized.

A legal dispute arose in 2023 when a Black-owned bookstore and coffee shop sought to host an event promoting Black authors during the festival. The business and Essence organizers later resolved the dispute.

A similar incident occurred in 2022, when Spotify and a New Orleans real estate developer wanted to put on an event at a music club. Essence dropped the lawsuit the following year, according to NOLA.com.

The Rev. Al Sharpton, founder and president of National Action Network, has participated in every festival since it began in 1995. The civil rights leader said he was saddened that such a cultural institution was leaving New Orleans but pledged continued support. “My commitment to ESSENCE is not contingent on its geography,” Sharpton said in a statement Wednesday. “Wherever the Festival lands, I will be there.”
