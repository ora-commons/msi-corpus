## Musk left DOGE after 180 days amid a public dispute with Trump

Elon Musk, the chief executive of SpaceX and Tesla, said he is not MAGA and that he regrets nearly $500 million spent supporting Republicans in the 2026 U.S. midterms. Speaking by video from China during the New York Times Dealbook summit, Musk was among several U.S. business leaders accompanying Donald Trump on a trip to Asia. He also said Washington would benefit from split control between the two parties.

Musk said he was unsure what MAGA meant, but rejected the idea that it meant blind adherence to Republican policies. “I’m not sure what Maga means, but if that means sort of blind adherence to a set of policies by the Republican party, then I certainly don’t have that,” he said. The comment came after Musk aligned with Donald Trump’s Make America Great Again movement in 2024. At a campaign rally in Madison Square Garden that year, he had declared, “I’m not just Maga. I’m dark, gothic Maga.”

The remarks came during a 45-minute interview with Andrew Ross Sorkin, a New York Times columnist. Musk said it would be good for Washington to have split control between the two parties and that he thinks “there are positives and negatives in both.”

Musk also accused Ukraine’s president, Volodymyr Zelenskyy, of “lying” about restrictions on access to his firm’s Starshield satellite system. The allegation extended the conversation beyond U.S. party politics to Ukraine and SpaceX’s satellite operations.

Musk praised Jensen Huang, the ousted Nvidia chief executive. Asked about his previous negative comments about Anthropic, Musk said, “no one I met at Anthropic set off my evil detector.” He said he met with a lot of people at Anthropic before moving forward with an agreement to rent the AI firm computing power, and he described the team as good people who seemed principled, diligent and concerned about the future.

Musk’s government role began after Donald Trump took office for the second time in 2025. He served 180 days heading the administration’s so-called “department of government efficiency” (Doge) before stepping down following a very public falling out with the president.

The falling out culminated in a June 2025 post on Musk’s social media platform: “Time to drop the really big bomb: @realDonaldTrump is in the Epstein files. That is the real reason they have not been made public.” The two men later reconciled. During his DOGE tenure, Musk frequently appeared in the Oval Office and at cabinet meetings alongside Trump, often wearing hats that read “Make America Great Again” or “Trump was right about everything.”

Musk’s political spending began before his government service. He donated at least $250 million of his own money to help Trump win the 2024 election, then spent nearly $500 million in the U.S. midterms to support Republicans, many of whom lost.

At the summit, Musk said the spending had “failed” and that he felt “pretty foolish.” He described the nearly $500 million as the most anyone had ever spent in a political cycle by someone who did not run for office. “It certainly shows that American elections are not just about money,” he said. “If I could rewind the clock, I would have spent less money.”
