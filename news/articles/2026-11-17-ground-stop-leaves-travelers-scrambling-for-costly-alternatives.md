## Federal guidance values passenger delay time at about $66 an hour

The Friday ground stop lasted four hours and disrupted flights for as many as a million travelers; the effects continued to ripple beyond airports for days. Angelina and Kyle Phoenix were flying from Jacksonville, Florida, to Detroit for an anniversary trip to Chicago when the outage began. At Detroit airport, officials had stopped announcing which canceled flights would send checked baggage to which turnstiles, leaving passengers without clear information as they scrambled to decide what to do next.

Kyle Phoenix recalled the uncertainty: “A lot of confusion, a lot of scrambling from a lot of people.” He added, “I think that was probably the roughest part, not knowing anything.” The couple's new airline tickets cost $300, they waited four hours for luggage and retrieved it at midnight, and they then spent $400 on a four-hour Uber ride to Chicago. They forfeited a rebooked flight and a hotel room, and Angelina Phoenix said, “We just took the loss.”

The wider bill included emergency child care, high taxi fares, replacement tickets and lost vacation rentals. Travelers also said they did not know which expenses might be reimbursed. Beyond those direct charges, people described stress and grief over time lost, costs that generally were not eligible for reimbursement.

William Ware, 24, encountered the disruption at LAX. Traffic outside the airport kept his Uber from dropping him off in time for his flight home to Chicago, where he needed to care for his child. Ware was rebooked for the next day and found a hotel in Long Beach more than 20 miles away. He described it as “scary, but that was the only hotel that was available.” He also had to arrange two Uber rides for a round trip back to the airport.

Ware said he never made it home that night. “I actually had to get home to my kid and I couldn’t, so it was horrible for me,” he said. His family was able to watch his child, which he described as a strong support system: “I got a great support system, so my family was able to watch my child.”

Other disruptions accompanied and followed the air-travel failure. United Airlines suffered a malfunction and halted its flights for 90 minutes while travelers were still describing Friday's ground stop. Several top U.S. banks reported significant slowdowns. On Tuesday, an internal National Security Agency network used to communicate classified information was offline after what was described as “routine maintenance.” The NSA outage came after “AI chaos and Microsoft’s global meltdown” a few days earlier. Ahead lay busy Thanksgiving travel week, when TSA agents typically screen more than 2 million passengers nationwide.

The International Air Transport Association reported in July that delays were accelerating faster than the number of scheduled passenger flights had increased since the COVID-19 pandemic. Between 2015 and 2025, delays affecting scheduled passenger flights generated about $10.9 billion in costs for airlines and travelers. The association said holdups began rising sharply in 2021.

Federal guidance issued a decade ago valued airline passengers’ time at $47.10 an hour, expressed in 2015 dollars — about $66 an hour today. The estimate counted travel time that otherwise would have been available for work or leisure.

Russell Wong, an economist at the Federal Reserve Bank of Richmond, estimated that last year's 43-day federal government shutdown caused roughly 1 million additional hours of passenger delays at six airports. Wong calculated the lost time's value at $66 million, using metro-area wage data and the higher-than-average incomes of people who take flights. His calculation excluded canceled flights.

The report said losses also reached hourly workers, freelancers and caregivers downstream from airports, where the loss of time was harder to measure.

Trust in government, already low for years, was tested by the official accounts of the shutdown. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the air-traffic shutdown, described as the nation's longest in 25 years, could cost billions of dollars. He blamed a contractor and aging infrastructure. President Donald Trump pointed to Democrats, who demanded answers about how the administration had spent billions already provided and why its funding request had risen to $50 billion.

Describing the event as a glitch involving a faulty router raised questions. Shannon A. Bowen, a journalism and mass communications professor at the University of South Carolina, told The Conversation: “It wasn’t simply a crisis, it was an actual emergency of impending disaster-level scale.”

Travelers were not easily persuaded by official explanations. Sonia Saini Simpson, 56, whose flight from Orlando, Florida, landed in Philadelphia after hours of uncertainty, said she considered renting a car to drive the nearly 1,000 miles. “I’m super skeptical about what we’re being told as to why this event happened at all,” she said. “I look forward to getting some answers that are believable.”

Glenn Anthony Cooper, a disc jockey, said he was told his flight from Philadelphia to Toronto on Monday had been delayed nine hours for “a hole in the tire.” “It’s like, what’s really going on, you know? Is there something behind it, or what are they doing?” he said. He added, “It’s just a bummer to just have these types of situations happen and you can’t really control it.”

Some social media users advised travelers to buy travel insurance, avoid checking luggage when possible, be kind to airline and security workers, and accept what they could not control. For Angelina and Kyle Phoenix, the loss included money, the time required to retrieve luggage and the stress of a disrupted anniversary trip.
