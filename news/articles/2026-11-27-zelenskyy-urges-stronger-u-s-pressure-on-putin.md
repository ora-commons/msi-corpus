## Peskov says Zelenskyy is not willing to pursue peace

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Friday that Washington should apply more pressure on Russian President Vladimir Putin to stop the invasion of Ukraine, a war that has lasted almost five years despite President Donald Trump's efforts to broker a peace deal. Speaking in an interview with the BBC broadcast from Kyiv, Zelenskyy said the United States needed to do more to stop Russian attacks.

Zelenskyy's frustration focused on Trump calling Putin “my friend” at a recent summit in China. He called the remark “strange” because, he said, Putin was “an aggressor and a killer.” “He can't be a friend,” Zelenskyy told the British broadcaster, likening the description to someone calling Adolf Hitler a friend during World War II.

The International Criminal Court has issued an arrest warrant for Putin for war crimes, accusing him of personal responsibility for the abductions of children from Ukraine. The report said the Trump administration has imposed sweeping sanctions on the court as part of its campaign to dismantle the tribunal over investigations into the United States and its allies, including Israel.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Zelenskyy's remarks were “insulting his counterpart” and showed the Ukrainian leader “is not willing to follow the path of (a) peace settlement.”

Zelenskyy's comments were broadcast a day after Jared Kushner, Trump's son-in-law and a U.S. envoy trying to mediate an end to the war, complimented Putin on being a “grandmaster” of politics. Weeks earlier, Kushner's partner in the peace efforts, Steve Witkoff, described Putin as a “humanitarian.”

At the same time, Zelenskyy complained that the Pentagon was not delivering to Ukraine paid-for Patriot missiles that could stop Russia's ballistic missiles. He also complained that SpaceX's military-grade version of the Starlink satellite internet service, the backbone of Ukraine's drone operations and communication, was not working properly.

Europe had previously vowed to stay the course in helping Ukraine fend off Russia's invasion. But European Union leaders failed on Thursday to reach consensus after fraught talks over a new $49 billion loan for Ukraine's economic and military needs next year. The report said elections across the EU over the next 12 months could amplify the influence of parties reluctant to send more money to Ukraine and may fray the continent's support.

Moscow's invasion has killed hundreds of thousands of troops and thousands of Ukrainian civilians. It also brought heavy sanctions on Russia and diplomatically isolated Putin in the West before Trump resumed relations.

A bus pulled up at Ukraine's northern border on Friday, and an 8-year-old boy ran out of it. “Mom!” he shouted into the crowd. His mother, Alina Sydorenko, was there to reunite with him after nearly five years apart. She hugged him tight as he kept saying, “Mother, mother.”

The boy was one of nine Ukrainian children returned Friday from Russia and Russian-occupied territories of Ukraine. “I want to show him everything that he missed and was taken away from him,” Sydorenko said. Some of the children had lost their parents, who died in occupied territory, and relatives met them off the bus.

Zelenskyy announced the latest Bring Kids Back UA operation on social media and thanked U.S. first lady Melania Trump for her role in mediating wartime reunifications. The White House said on Wednesday that the reunifications had brought 43 children home. Ukraine has so far managed to bring home 2,875 children, according to Zelenskyy. “We remember each and every one of them. We are doing everything we can to find them and return them to their families,” he said.

Kyiv officials said Ukraine remained determined to keep fighting for its independence and that Ukraine had launched attacks against Russian assets.

A recent strike on a Russian navy vessel docked at a port in Libya raised alarm in Europe and Washington that the war could spread and engulf other countries.

In an apparent attempt to defuse tensions, Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha said he held a phone call with Libyan Foreign Minister Al-Taher Al-Baour, informing him that Ukraine had no intentions of drawing Libya into the war. Sybiha said in comments posted on X late Thursday that he reaffirmed Kyiv's support for Libya's sovereignty and efforts to reunify the country.

The grinding aerial bombardment between Russia and Ukraine has escalated recently. Russia launched close to 300 aerial attacks a week through Nov. 20 on Ukraine's civilian infrastructure, according to the Armed Conflict Location & Event Data Project, or ACLED, which tracks violence.

Zelenskyy warned Thursday that a major Russian attack is looming.

Ukrainian air defenses intercepted 110 of 155 Russian drones launched overnight Friday, including 51 jet-powered Shahed-type drones, according to the Ukrainian air force. Hits were recorded at 27 locations across Ukraine, it said.

Ukrainian Prime Minister Serhii Koretskyi said the attacks targeted railway infrastructure, energy facilities, civilian businesses and residential buildings across nine Ukrainian regions, killing at least one person and wounding at least six.

Meanwhile, the Russian Defense Ministry said Friday that its forces overnight intercepted 318 Ukrainian drones over 10 Russian regions and illegally annexed Crimea.

Residents of St. Petersburg reported explosions in eight districts of the city, the Russian online news outlet Astra said.

Also, 70 apartments in a multi-story building were damaged in a drone attack on the Black Sea port of Novorossiysk in Russia's Krasnodar region, local officials said Friday. Officials reported that Ukraine's attacks killed at least two people and wounded six others.

Associated Press writer Elise Morton reported from London, with AP writers Hanna Arhirova in northern Ukraine and Barry Hatton in Lisbon, Portugal, contributing.
