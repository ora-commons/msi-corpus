## Chris Bowen describes three sleepless nights of final negotiations

NPR's Rachel Waldholz reported from the almost-empty Antalya Expo Center on Nov. 22, where parts of the venue were being disassembled; the center had been packed with about 70,000 people during the previous two weeks. Australia climate chief Chris Bowen, who led the negotiations, said, "I haven't slept for the last three nights, been negotiating all the way through, but more importantly, people on delegations came at it and gave it everything they had."

The climate talks had run two days past their scheduled end. By the time the gavel fell, many negotiators had already left to catch their flights home. Waldholz said many negotiators crossed the expo center dragging their suitcases on their way to the airport.

Delegates came to Antalya with long wish lists, from electrifying more of the global economy to money for developing countries to help deal with the impacts of climate change. Those broader goals remained unresolved as negotiators failed to reach an agreement.

Many countries wanted the final agreement to explicitly call for a transition away from coal, oil and natural gas. Waldholz reported that big oil-producing countries, including Saudi Arabia, blocked any mention of fossil fuels. The European Union ultimately decided that no deal was better than the deal on the table.

Ireland's environment minister Darragh O'Brien said, "We will not endorse a cover decision or an Antalya pact that does not even mention fossil fuels, which is the key driver of climate change."

The summit ended without an agreement. In his introduction, NPR host Rob Schmitz said international climate talks in Antalya, Turkey, had ended after negotiations fell apart.

COP31 was the first summit to squarely face the reality that the planet will miss a key target of the Paris Climate Agreement: limiting warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius, or 2.7 degrees Fahrenheit, above pre-industrial levels. The United Nations said limiting that overshoot requires cutting the use of fossil fuels nearly in half in the next 10 to 15 years.

Waldholz said delegates would pick up negotiations the next year at the next climate summit in Ethiopia.


Before the summit ended without an agreement, [COP31 talks had run into overtime with fossil fuels left out of the draft deal](/articles/2026-11-21-cop31-talks-run-into-overtime-as-fossil-fuels-stay-out-of-draft-deal/), leaving delegates divided over the final text.
