## White House expects no agreement on reducing nuclear weapons stockpiles

Edgard Kagan, a former ambassador to Malaysia and a senior adviser at the Center for Strategic and International Studies, said China strongly opposes trilateral nuclear discussions with the United States and Russia.

"The Chinese strongly disagree with the idea that they should be involved in any kind of trilateral discussions with the United States and Russia on nuclear weapons," Kagan said.

"China wants to avoid this because they don't want to be locked into a subservient position. And they also don't want to create a precedent. I think the main thing for the Chinese is they know if they do a meeting and nuclear issues are discussed, it becomes much harder for them to refuse to do it in the future," he said.

A White House official said the administration does not expect any agreement on denuclearization to come out of the meeting. "We do not expect to get to any kind of agreement," the official said, calling the trilateral an "entry point" for the three countries to talk about reducing nuclear weapons stock.

Another U.S. official, not authorized to speak on the record, acknowledged that denuclearization is a "difficult" topic for China. "But we do think it's time for China to sort of step up to the adult table, if you will, and have adult conversations about adult things," the official said.

NPR's report assessed that the meeting could benefit Vladimir Putin as Russia's war with Ukraine nears its five-year mark. The report described Trump's recent approach toward Russia as friendlier, said he had been siding with Putin and reported that Trump struck a deal to release more Russian oil into the market ahead of the midterm elections.

Trump said last month that he was fine with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy not being present at a potential meeting with Putin about the war. "Anything I can do to end the war, whether somebody's there or not," Trump said.

A White House official said Ukraine is not intended to come up in the meeting with Xi and Putin, and no separate meeting between Trump and Putin is planned. Kagan said the gathering could end up shorter if there is little all the parties are willing to discuss.

"But at the same time, I could see this going longer because President Trump likes dealing with Putin and Xi. He sees them as his peers. He sees them as important world leaders," Kagan said. Either way, Kagan said, a photograph of the three leaders together is enough to make headlines. "That picture alone is going to be worth well over a thousand words."

On trade, this is the second time Trump and Xi are meeting on the sidelines of the APEC summit. This year's meeting differs from last year's in Busan, South Korea, when tensions between the United States and China were fraught amid an escalating trade war.

The Busan meeting established a sort-of truce. The United States immediately lowered some tariffs on Chinese goods, and China agreed to loosen exports on rare earths, though the agreement's execution has been spotty.

"There's friction in this truce. It's not smooth, but it's basically functional," Kurt Tong, managing partner at The Asia Group, said. A White House official said the United States is not getting rare earths from China at the pace or level it would like. "We really want to focus minds among our counterparts in China on compliance with this," the official said.

Tong expects the Busan agreement to be renewed past its January expiration. According to Tong, the two sides are likely to agree to the renewal either in Shenzhen or later this year at the G20 summit Trump is hosting in Miami.

The White House confirmed Friday that Trump is expected to meet with Xi and Putin together. Before traveling to China, Trump will first go to the Philippines, where he will meet allies at the ASEAN summit in Manila. Trump is expected to remain in the region for just about three days, and the trip marks his second visit to China this year.

For weeks, Trump has said he is keen to discuss the Iran war and denuclearization with Putin and Xi. Expectations for major deals or pronouncements from this week's meetings are low, but the gathering is still expected to make waves over the imagery of the three leaders sitting down together.

The trip comes as Trump and his party face widespread backlash from voters in the recent midterm elections, in which his party lost control of Congress. NPR said the result could weaken Trump's bargaining position across the Pacific.

Trump's traveling party also reflects changed circumstances since his earlier China trip. Jensen Huang traveled to China with Trump earlier this year, while tech billionaire Elon Musk is joining Trump in Shenzhen.

Before arriving in China, Trump is holding meetings with several allies in Manila, including the other leaders of the Quad nations: India, Japan and Australia.

"I think it's quite significant symbolically and politically that the U.S., Australia, Japan and India, the major democracies of Asia and the Pacific, get together… it's especially significant that they're doing it just before the meetings in China," Tong said. The White House said Trump will also sign a trade deal with Thailand while in the Philippines.

NPR described the Manila stop as an opportunity for Trump to repair and reinforce relationships with countries such as Japan and India and with the host country, the Philippines. The ASEAN summit is also the region's first look at Trump since his party lost control of Congress and a chance to see how the last two years of Trump's presidency may look.

"I think the real thing that people will be looking at first is, how does he present himself?" Kagan said. "How does his behaviour towards Modi and towards India change? Those are things people will be looking at very, very closely for clues for what the next two years are likely to be like."

A White House official said Trump is not expected to raise Huang's affair and resignation with Xi, with trade the focus of the meeting. Huang resigned last week after admitting an extramarital affair with Alice Huang, NPR reported. Citing The New York Times, NPR also said U.S. intelligence officials believed the woman had been recruited by China's top spy agency; NPR said it had not independently confirmed those reports.

After Huang's resignation, President Trump and administration officials, including Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, face scrutiny and questions about how much they knew about Huang's affair and how much access Huang had to Trump and his orbit. Lutnick agreed last week to appear before the Senate Banking Committee to answer further questions. Trump has continued to defend Huang and called him an honorable person.

Tong said the main impact would be in the United States, where there are calls for accountability over Huang's activities with the U.S. government and questions about what happened.

"I think the main impact is here in the United States, where there are calls for accountability with respect to Mr. Huang's activities with the U.S. government and questions about what happened… the net result of that is to provide a tailwind to persons who are interested in tightening up the technology denial regime toward China," Tong said.
