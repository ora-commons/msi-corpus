## Justice Department opens criminal investigation into Alaska Senate-race matter

An undisclosed source sent text messages supporting Democrat Eric Hafner during Alaska’s U.S. House race. Bill Hill’s campaign and the state Democratic Party objected after voters received the messages.

Hill’s campaign manager Kevin Groh and the state Democratic Party’s executive director Jenny-Marie Stryker said they never learned who sponsored the text messages. Stryker said the party spent “thousands of dollars to send texts clarifying that the real Alaska Democratic Party endorsed Bill Hill.”

In the final weeks of the race, Hill’s campaign had expressed concerns over Hafner’s candidacy and distributed stickers and yard signs labeling Hafner a “fake ‘Democrat.’” Hafner submitted a candidate biography that appeared in state election materials without mentioning his incarceration.

Hafner received nearly 8% of the vote after the Nov. 3 general election. He is incarcerated in a federal prison in New York after his 2022 conviction for threatening to kill judges, police officers and others.

Eric Hafner’s mother, Carol “Kitty” Hafner, responded this week to an Associated Press email sent to her son. She said it would seem “next to impossible” for voters not to know Eric Hafner was in prison, given coverage of his candidacy for Alaska’s House seat in 2024. She also said the “media has gone well overboard identifying his incarceration status.”

She wrote that “Absolutely no one encouraged” Eric Hafner to run, adding that his campaigns had been “entirely independent of any influencers.” She also wrote, “Tell President Trump to Pardon Eric Hafner!”

The Associated Press reported that neither Carol Hafner nor Eric Hafner has ties to the state. Carol Hafner ran in Alaska’s Senate primary in August and finished sixth with 0.6% of the vote; both have run for office in Alaska and other states before.

The major candidates in Alaska’s U.S. Senate race were Mary Peltola and Dan Sullivan, but the race also included a second Sullivan, Dan J. Sullivan. Peltola faced allegations that members of her team helped recruit Dan J. Sullivan, and she denied involvement in any such effort. “I personally know that I am innocent, so I don’t spend a lot of time hand-wringing” about it, Peltola said in an interview Wednesday after her win.

The U.S. Justice Department has opened a criminal investigation into the Senate-race matter, but Alaska’s U.S. attorney has not said what potential crimes authorities were looking into.

Those disclosures came as Republican U.S. Rep. Nick Begich won reelection to Alaska’s lone U.S. House seat Wednesday through the state’s ranked-choice tabulation, finishing with nearly 51.4% of the vote while Hill had nearly 48.7%. The race was one of the last House races called nationally.

The result was a rare bright spot for Republicans in a midterm election in which their ranks in the House were decimated and Democrats regained control of both chambers of Congress. It came a day after Democrat Mary Peltola flipped one of Alaska’s U.S. Senate seats by defeating two-term Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan.

Begich, a Republican seeking a second term, was endorsed by President Donald Trump. He pitched himself as an effective leader who was in lockstep with Trump’s efforts to expand oil and gas drilling and other resource development in Alaska. In a statement after his victory, Begich said he took the job of U.S. representative seriously and thanked the other candidates. “This process requires engagement, and the perspectives brought forward during the campaign added value for Alaskans as they made important decisions about their vote in this race,” he said.

Bill Hill, Begich’s main challenger, was an independent, a commercial fisherman and a retired teacher. Endorsed by the state Democratic Party, Hill sought to focus on affordability, limiting the influence of big money in elections and pushing back against the billionaire class in the United States. Hill said he called Begich to congratulate him. “I wished him well as he continues representing Alaska in Congress, because all Alaskans have a stake in his success,” Hill said in a statement.

With Begich’s House victory settled, the sponsor of the Hafner-supporting text messages remained unknown to Groh and Stryker.
