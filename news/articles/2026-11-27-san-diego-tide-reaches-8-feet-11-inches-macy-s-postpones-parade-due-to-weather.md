## Los Angeles tides could surpass October record amid coastal flood warnings

According to The Guardian, San Diego recorded a tide of 8 feet 11 inches, or 2.7 metres, on Wednesday morning, while tides in Los Angeles reached approximately 8.19 feet and could potentially surpass a record set at the end of October. The readings came as king tides and huge waves battered both U.S. coasts during a turbulent Thanksgiving period, with severe weather threatening millions across the country.

The high tides also affected Southern California. Along the California coast, king tides caused considerable damage. In Pacifica, south of San Francisco, large waves battered the coastline on Wednesday, destroying part of a pier and sending debris into the ocean. Several beachfront properties in Pacifica also experienced flooding.

The National Weather Service issued coastal flood warnings covering parts of the Pacific coastline and shorelines around San Francisco Bay and San Pablo Bay. Marin County authorities temporarily issued an evacuation warning for residents of beachfront homes at Stinson Beach.

King tides are unusually high natural tides that occur when the gravitational forces of the sun and moon combine around their alignment with Earth. According to The Guardian, unusually warm Pacific waters associated with the 2026 super El Niño were heightening their impact.

On the East Coast, a Nor'easter brought heavy rain, damaging winds and coastal flooding across the eastern United States. The system created hazardous conditions for millions of Americans traveling to celebrate the holiday.

The severity of the forecast forced an unprecedented decision in New York. Macy's postponed its 100th annual Thanksgiving Day parade, originally scheduled for Thursday, until Saturday. It was the first weather-related postponement in the event's history. The postponement was intended to safeguard performers and spectators, particularly because powerful gusts posed a danger to the parade's enormous character balloons.

Photographs accompanying the report showed large waves crashing against the Marine Room restaurant's waterfront building in San Diego, California. A separate photograph showed a flooded street in Sea Isle City, New Jersey. The first image was credited to Sean M Haffey/Getty Images, and the second to Seetheview.com/Reuters.
