## US MMR coverage falls to 92.4% as cases hit 5,400

The Pan American Health Organization (PAHO), a specialised service of the United Nations, has determined that the US has lost its measles elimination status after the same strain circulated uninterrupted for more than 12 months. The determination comes after 26 years in which the US maintained elimination status and follows two years of record case numbers and deaths. PAHO director Dr Jarbas Barbosa announced that Mexico and Bolivia have also lost their status, while several European nations lost theirs earlier in 2026.

US states have reported 10 measles-related deaths in 2026, the highest toll the country has seen since the 1990s. BBC News reported 5,400 measles cases this year, the highest count for a year since 1991. BBC News also reported that the national MMR vaccination rate has fallen to 92.4%, below the 95% level associated with herd immunity. Herd immunity limits spread and protects people who cannot be vaccinated, including newborns.

At a news conference Tuesday, Dr Jarbas Barbosa announced that Mexico and Bolivia had also lost their measles elimination status. He attributed the regional resurgence to persistent "gaps in vaccination" and said, "These figures are a serious warning for our region." Daniel Salas, PAHO's executive manager of immunisations, said the US and other countries that lost status would be required to submit corrective action plans outlining how they would attempt to improve vaccination rates.

Canada lost its elimination status last year after several large outbreaks but has made strides this year in interrupting transmission. The BBC also listed the UK, Spain and Austria among European nations that lost their measles-free status earlier in 2026, along with countries including Russia, Azerbaijan and Uzbekistan.

William Moss, a paediatric infectious disease specialist at the Johns Hopkins Bloomberg School of Public Health, said the US was the first country to eliminate measles in the Americas thanks to vaccination campaigns. The country recommends two MMR shots for children between ages one and six, and the regimen is 97% effective at protecting against the illness. Nuwan Gunawardhana, an assistant professor of medicine at Columbia University Irving Medical Center, said public health officials and clinicians made massive efforts to build patient trust and increase vaccine uptake, leading to decades with few infections and deaths.

Gunawardhana said trust has steadily eroded since the Covid-19 pandemic, when patients were confronted with misinformation about vaccine safety and lost even more trust in public health systems. He called the US loss of elimination status "a huge leap backwards." Measles is extremely contagious and can be fatal. Some patients can experience long-term complications, including encephalitis and immune amnesia. Immune amnesia resets the immune system and leaves it with only a limited ability to respond to new infections. Before a vaccine was invented, the virus infected millions of children a year, and it has a signature blotchy red rash.

Pennsylvania officials said an outbreak infected more than 1,400 people, likely a large underestimate, and killed seven people. US Health Secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr has sparred with Pennsylvania officials over how the state counts measles deaths, while the US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has declined for now to record some of the deaths in its database.

Kennedy has attempted a series of sweeping changes to US vaccine recommendations and policies and has frequently cast doubt on the safety of the MMR shot, while vaccination rates have declined in recent years. Last year, he fired all members of a top vaccine advisory panel and replaced them with his own appointees, some of whom were vaccine critics. The panel recommended changes including no longer requiring the Hepatitis B vaccine for all newborns, but a federal judge blocked the recommendations. Kennedy argued that the rise in measles cases started before he took the helm at HHS and that neighbouring countries have also seen more infections. The US Department of Health and Human Services did not respond to a BBC request for comment.

President Donald Trump cast doubt on the safety of the MMR vaccine this year and issued an executive order for the shot to be split into three separate immunisations for measles, mumps and rubella. Public health experts said the move was costly and could sow confusion about an already safe vaccine. Doctors and public health officials said misinformation from Kennedy and Trump played a major role in falling vaccination rates.

"It's confusing patients. It's confusing families," Dr Gunawardhana said. "We need a clear message from the top." The federal government's vaccine manoeuvres have left public health officials and states to fill the gaps, offering their own separate recommendations that uphold decades-long practices.
