## Core inflation held at 2.8% and services price growth remained at 3.7%

U.K. consumer inflation accelerated in October, with prices 3.6% higher than a year earlier compared with 3.5% in September, the Office for National Statistics said Wednesday. The result matched a consensus of economists polled by The Wall Street Journal.

The Office for National Statistics attributed the acceleration to high energy prices amid the conflict between the U.S. and Iran. Energy prices continued to put upward pressure on inflation as the escalation threatened further disruption to oil and gas supplies flowing through the Persian Gulf.

Core inflation remained steady at 2.8% in October, while price growth in the services sector was also unchanged at 3.7%. Central bank policymakers watch both measures to determine the extent of second-round effects from higher energy costs and the risk that inflation becomes entrenched.

The acceleration was likely to support expectations that the Bank of England would soon raise its key interest rate for a second time. Earlier in November, the bank raised the rate for the first time since the outbreak of the war to 4% from 3.75%. Governor Andrew Bailey acknowledged that indirect or second-round effects had not yet emerged.

The central bank signaled that further increases could be necessary if the conflict persisted, noting that the risk of higher energy prices feeding through to other parts of the economy had increased.

The Bank of England expects inflation to peak at 5% around the middle of next year before falling back to its 2% target in 2028. In an adverse scenario that assumed another sharp rise in energy prices, inflation would reach 5.5%, the bank said.

The inflation report came against a backdrop of unexpected economic strength. U.K. gross domestic product grew 0.7% in the third quarter, boosted by artificial intelligence-related demand and household consumption. That strength has added to concerns that inflation could prove sticky.

Signs of stabilization in the labor market have also prompted concern at the Bank of England that workers might secure compensation for rising prices through higher salaries. The U.K. unemployment rate fell in the three months through September, while an anticipated jump in household energy bills in early 2027 could prompt an uptick in wage renegotiations.

The Bank of England's next interest-rate decision is scheduled for Dec. 17. Deputy Governor Clare Lombardelli said last week that a second consecutive rate increase remained on the table, citing the rise in inflation and a more persistent energy shock.

Investors expect the central bank to go further and are currently pricing in an increase in interest rates to 5%. The National Institute of Economic and Social Research recently said investors were right to expect such an aggressive rate path, noting that second-round price effects from the energy shock could keep inflation above the bank's 2% target until at least 2030.
