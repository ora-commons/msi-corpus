## Judge has not ruled on request to dismiss Morales García’s lawsuit

The Justice Department acknowledged in federal court filings on Friday that Brian José Morales García was a U.S. citizen whom authorities incorrectly identified as undocumented before his deportation to Mexico. Attorneys for the department made the concession while seeking dismissal of Morales García’s damages lawsuit against the federal government.

The Trump administration denied Morales García’s attorneys’ assertions on Friday and said he went to Mexico voluntarily, leaving no basis to sue. In the same court filings, however, the administration said Morales García “was permitted entry” back into the United States on August 30 after it reviewed “information concerning plaintiff’s place of birth.”

Justin Simmons, the U.S. attorney for the western district of Texas, wrote in a court filing first reported by the Texas Tribune: “Plaintiff is a dual citizen of the United States and Mexico.”

The administration had earlier said in a media statement that Morales García was in the country illegally. His attorneys disputed that statement. The Guardian later characterized the media statement as incorrect and said it was among several related immigration-enforcement statements that had come under scrutiny.

Morales García, 25, was born in Denver, raised in Mexico and was from Austin, Texas. On April 3, he was riding with his boss to work as an air-conditioning installation technician when state and local officers pulled them over near Fredericksburg, Texas. Morales García was the passenger and did not have his identification with him.

Morales García told the officers that his birth certificate and Social Security card were at home. The officers did not believe him and called immigration agents, who detained Morales García.

Court filings recount that Robert Gil Jr, a U.S. Customs and Border Protection agent, consulted records from Mexico that listed a Mexican birthplace for Morales García. Gil also accused Morales García of saying he was born in Mexico and observed that he only spoke in Spanish, according to the court records.

Morales García was deported on April 7. His attorneys sued on May 1, seeking an unspecified amount of damages. The lawsuit says he falsely said he entered the United States illegally and signed papers for a rapid deportation in response to threats of imprisonment by agents. It also says family and friends tried to deliver documents proving his U.S. citizenship before the deportation, but their efforts were unduly rebuffed.

Judge Robert Lee Pitman had not immediately ruled Friday on the government’s request to dismiss Morales García’s lawsuit. The suit also names unspecified local and state Texas law enforcement officers as defendants.

Americans are not required to carry proof of citizenship when going about their daily business. The Guardian reported that lawyers across the United States have seen cases like Morales García’s arise after encounters with local authorities that generally are not aware of immigration-policy nuances. Texas’s public safety department is among many state and local agencies that collaborate with federal immigration authorities under the 287(g) program.

The case came as the White House faced bipartisan criticism of the mass-deportation campaign pursued by Donald Trump throughout his second presidency. The Guardian described the campaign as “aggressive” and said it was “widely seen” as a factor in the Republican Party’s electoral losses in the 3 November midterms, which cost the party control of Congress.

An October 2025 ProPublica report found that immigration agents had detained more than 170 U.S. citizens during the first nine months of Trump’s second presidency.

In a July analysis, The Guardian reviewed Trump administration press releases on 26 cases in which cars were allegedly used to assault immigration-enforcement agents. Nearly all of those cases were either disproven or dropped.
