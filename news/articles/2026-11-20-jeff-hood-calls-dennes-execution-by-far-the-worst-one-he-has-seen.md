## Pike survival renewed scrutiny as federal ban left state choices intact

Reverend Jeff Hood, a witness to Reinaldo Dennes's execution in Texas, said the execution was "by far the worst one" he had seen. Hood told USA Today that Dennes "screamed out, 'my leg, my leg, my leg'" and "screamed out twice, 'it's not working like it's supposed to.'"

The Texas execution took place on the same day another inmate was put to death in Florida. It also came weeks after Christa Pike survived two lethal doses during two attempts at execution by lethal injection in September. Pike, a 50-year-old woman convicted of murder, was taken to a hospital in critical condition and later returned to prison.

BBC News reported that Pike was believed to be the only individual to have survived an execution attempt by lethal injection.

The use of pentobarbital has been under renewed scrutiny since Pike survived the attempts, BBC News said. The outlet also reported that the drug's use in lethal injections came under renewed scrutiny last year after a US federal government review during the Biden administration raised concerns about the drug's potential for "unnecessary pain and suffering." The Biden government banned its use in federal executions, but the ban did not bind individual states, which can choose their own methods.

Pentobarbital is a potent sedative that slows the body, including the nervous system. Doctors sometimes use it to treat certain conditions, while high doses can also be used as a lethal injection to cause death.

Dennes, 70, was a US death row inmate whose Texas execution lasted more than an hour. According to witnesses, as he was being injected with pentobarbital, Dennes said the process was "not working the way it should be."

Michael Graczyk, an Associated Press reporter who had witnessed many such executions, described how the witnesses waited an unusually long time before being taken into the chamber. Graczyk said such delays usually are due to an inmate resisting the procedure or difficulty finding veins. When the witnesses were finally allowed in, Dennes almost immediately exclaimed, "The pain! Go! Go!" Dennes then uttered an expletive and asked "the father" to "take this pain."

Dennes shouted, "My right leg is killing me!", and described the sensation as a cramp. After another expletive, Dennes grunted and said, "I don't think this is normal."

A Texas Department of Criminal Justice spokeswoman said there had been unsuccessful attempts to find veins for the injections. She added that Dennes had reported pain in parts of his body where there were no attempted injections. BBC News said executions with a lethal drug usually last from a few minutes to over an hour.

BBC News separately displayed a related-story headline reading, "I've seen nearly 500 executions - but never one like Christa Pike's."

Dennes was sentenced to death for the 1996 murder of Janos Szucs, a diamond dealer who was found shot multiple times.
