## Axios says Vance remains undecided on a 2028 presidential bid

Arwa Mahdawi urged JD Vance to run for president in 2028, arguing that Republican midterm losses should not end his political ambitions. In a Nov. 17 opinion column in The Guardian, Mahdawi told the vice president not to shelve his bid: “Please run in 2028, JD, habibi!”

The appeal came as reports described Vance as considering whether to enter the race at all. CNN, citing people close to the vice president, reported that he was giving “serious consideration to not running for president in 2028.” One unnamed person close to Vance described him as 42 and said, “He’s young enough that he can have a comeback should this environment make success impossible.”

Axios, citing two sources with knowledge of Vance’s plans, reported that “People familiar with Vance’s thinking say he’s genuinely undecided about running.” The outlet said a definitive answer might not come until late spring. Axios also reported that Vance was “wary that launching a campaign too early could shift attention away from Trump and risk alienating him.”

Mahdawi described Vance, once considered Trump’s heir apparent, as increasingly unlikely to become the 48th president after the midterms and said he did not appear to be in Trump’s good books. She wrote that Vance had posted that the war in Iran was why Republicans had lost badly. Trump said in a post-election 60 Minutes interview that he had no problem with the critique and made a similar statement to the Wall Street Journal, but Mahdawi said Trump had removed Vance from Iran negotiations. She also noted that Trump had not endorsed a successor.

Republican criticism also reached Vance after the election. Sen. John Kennedy told CNN, “I don’t think the vice-president did himself any favours by sending out that email or tweet or whatever it was after the election.”

Mahdawi said Vance’s rapid ascent—from his work as a writer to becoming one of the youngest vice presidents in U.S. history—owed part of its speed to billionaire friends. Peter Thiel hired Vance at his investment company in 2016, contributed millions to his Senate campaign and brokered his first meeting with Trump, she wrote. Elon Musk and Tucker Carlson also helped bring Vance closer to Trump, Mahdawi said.

Mahdawi said Vance’s political luck was turning as the Trump administration moved from failure to failure and he remained tied to it. During a nationwide flight stoppage on Friday, Laura Ingraham challenged Vance on Fox News after he deflected blame. “You guys have been in charge for the last two years,” Ingraham said. “Why hasn’t it gotten fixed?”

Mahdawi nevertheless urged Vance to run rather than wait for a future attempt. “Unless Trump miraculously manages to fix cost-of-living problems, I’m not sure any Republican could win in 2028,” she wrote. She predicted that Vance would face a difficult effort to distance himself from Trump while serving as vice president and could land on his feet if his political career faltered.

The decision remained unresolved in the reporting. CNN and Axios described an undecided Vance weighing the risks of an early launch against the option of a later comeback, while Mahdawi made the case for running despite the midterm losses.
