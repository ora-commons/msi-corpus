## Wittman Watch research circulated across Virginia's 1st District

**Subtype:** fact

- News From The States credited Wittman Watch's research and targeted outreach with helping Shannon Taylor defeat Rob Wittman in Virginia's 1st Congressional District in 2026, but said no one could measure the group's exact effect.
- Wittman Watch formed during a June 2025 Zoom call among a handful of Virginia citizens.
- Wittman Watch received no money from the state Democratic Party and had no formal affiliation with any political party or advocacy group.
- Jen Tierney said Wittman Watch supplied research that a campaign would have paid tens of thousands of dollars to produce.
- Rural GroundGame, a political group, received permission to use Wittman Watch content in two digital advertising campaigns.
- Shannon Taylor defeated Rob Wittman by 2.8% in the 2026 election for Virginia's 1st Congressional District.

## Shannon Taylor won by 2.8%; Wittman Watch's exact impact could not be measured

Wittman Watch began during a June 2025 Zoom call among a handful of ordinary Virginia citizens discussing how to make lawmakers accountable to their constituents. The grassroots political advocacy organization was not affiliated with the state Democratic Party and received no money from the party.

The report said online trolls accused the volunteers of being “Democratic Socialists” or “communists” paid to spread lies, although Wittman Watch was not formally affiliated with any political party or advocacy group. A small crew worked from their homes for dozens of hours a week and never collected money. David Cassidy, who operated the group with his wife, Shana, and a few others, said the volunteers sought to represent their district rather than either national party.

“We weren’t trying to say all Republicans are bad and all Democrats are good,” Cassidy said. “We were rooting for the 1st District. We did this because we care about where we live.”

The report characterized Wittman Watch as an information engine that produced reliable data at a high rate through an intensive information campaign and targeted outreach. Shanna Cassidy described the group's broader goal: “The hope was to flip a seat,” she said. “But our real goal was to shine a light on things people should know. We did shine that light, win or lose.”

The Cassidys said Wittman had stopped holding in-person public town hall meetings with rank-and-file constituents over the past year as he voted for President Donald Trump's budget and policies, which they said hurt people the congressman supposedly represented. They said Wittman's staff was “unfailingly polite” during limited mobile office hours, but responsiveness to constituent concerns remained a problem. Wittman Watch's research also found that many of Wittman's stated positions did not align with his voting record or behavior.

Jen Tierney, chair of the Historic Triangle Democrats, said Wittman Watch supplied research outside the Democratic Party that would have cost a campaign tens of thousands of dollars to produce.

“It was the first time we had a group outside the party doing really high-quality research,” Tierney said. “You could count on it. A campaign would have to pay tens of thousands of dollars for this kind of research.”

The research reached Democratic officials beyond the group's core volunteers. Cayanne Yates, the Democratic chair in Republican-leaning Essex County, first encountered Wittman Watch on a public Facebook account. She also ran a private Facebook account where Essex Democrats gathered, also called Wittman Watch. Yates said she called Dave and told him they already had a Wittman Watch; Cassidy replied that his was different.

“They were smart people. They kept us informed,” Yates said. “They gave us factual information. If someone said we had said something wrong, we showed them the record. I depended on Dave and Shana. Other Democratic chairs in Republican counties would use them, too. All of us moved the needle.”

Rural GroundGame, a political group, received permission to use Wittman Watch content in two digital advertising campaigns. Tierney said the facts Wittman Watch found allowed Democrats to begin conversations with voters who were still deciding whom to vote for or who assumed Wittman was so entrenched that their vote would change nothing.

Wittman had represented Virginia's 1st Congressional District for 19 years. Shannon Taylor became the first Democrat in nearly two decades to beat him, winning by less than 3%. Wittman had won the district by roughly 13% in 2022 and 12% in 2024; in 2026, he lost by 2.8%.

In 2024, Wittman won James City County by 3% and York County by 15%. In 2026, he lost James City County by 11%, a 14-point flip, and York County by a few votes, a 15-point flip. Although traditionally Republican counties in the district had backed Wittman, the report's review of voting records found that all but two of them recorded more Democratic votes in 2026 than in Wittman's 2024 reelection campaign.

The report also said national election results showed what it called Trump's “failed policies” — the Iran war, tariffs, inflation that drove up gas and grocery prices, and mortgage rates — brought out Democratic voters. People working in Virginia's 1st District said Wittman Watch made a difference.

The report said no one could measure the exact impact Wittman Watch had on those numbers. A small group of unpaid researchers from the upper Virginia Peninsula had spent 16 months conducting the research, and the report presented their work as a model for people across the country seeking to hold leaders accountable. Tierney said, “They definitely mattered.”
