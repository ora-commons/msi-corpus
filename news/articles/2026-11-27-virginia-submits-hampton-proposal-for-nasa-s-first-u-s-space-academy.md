## Virginia submits Hampton proposal for NASA’s first U.S. Space Academy

**Subtype:** fact

- Virginia submitted a proposal to NASA to host the first U.S. Space Academy at NASA Langley and Fort Monroe in Hampton.
- Virginia's proposal identifies a 600-acre campus at NASA Langley and Fort Monroe and a Wallops Island satellite learning campus.
- Virginia's proposal targets approximately 300 cadets in 2028 using existing academic, residential, laboratory and student-support capacity.
- President Donald Trump directed the establishment of the academy in an August executive order.

## The plan targets 300 cadets using existing facilities in 2028

Virginia has submitted a proposal to NASA to host the first U.S. Space Academy at NASA Langley and Fort Monroe in Hampton. The proposal identifies a 600-acre campus at NASA Langley and Fort Monroe and adds the Virginia Spaceport Authority's Mid-Atlantic Regional Spaceport, located at Wallops Island, as a satellite learning campus. The plan targets approximately 300 cadets in 2028.

The competition follows an August executive order from President Donald Trump directing the establishment of the academy. NASA's invitation calls for “a NASA-led, civilian Federal academic institution that combines rigorous technical education with disciplined leadership development, civic responsibility, and a lasting commitment to public service.” Texas, Pennsylvania, Louisiana and Maryland are among the states that have submitted proposals.

NASA's invitation said the academy would prepare the future multidisciplinary space workforce, including astronauts, engineers, operators and scientists, to advance American interests across the aerospace sector.

Under the proposal, NASA Langley would provide the academy's research, engineering, flight, innovation and technical core. Fort Monroe would support residential life, instruction, leadership development, ceremonies, administration and public engagement.

Mid-Atlantic Regional Spaceport, known as MARS, would provide field-based experience in payload integration, space launch and range operations, mission planning, flight safety, uncrewed systems and post-mission analysis. The proposal says the academy would draw on existing academic, residential, laboratory and student support capacity.

“Virginia is a world leader in aerospace innovation and national defense, and the commonwealth is best positioned to be the future home of the United States Space Academy,” Gov. Abigail Spanberger said in a Nov. 16 statement. She cited Virginia's “unparalleled defense footprint” and “world-class research assets” in saying the state could meet the federal government's goal and begin training the first class of cadets by 2028.

Secretary of Commerce and Trade Carrie Chenery said Virginia would use existing facilities and partnerships with industry, research and academic leaders to work rapidly with NASA. “Through adaptive reuse of existing facilities and a network of industry, research, and academic leaders, Virginia will work with NASA to rapidly realize its vision and meet its needs,” Chenery said in the Nov. 16 statement. “Together, we will build an academy capable of cultivating our next generation of space visionaries.”

All members of Virginia's congressional delegation signed on in support of bringing the academy to Hampton. Multiple universities also support a unified curriculum spanning aerospace engineering, aviation, cybersecurity, data science and power systems. House Technology Committee Chair Cliff Hayes, D-Chesapeake, called the prospect “spectacular” for Hampton Roads, the commonwealth and the nation.

“Hampton Roads has long helped defend America’s interests on the seas with the world’s largest naval base and through the air with Oceana Naval Air Force presence,” Hayes said in a statement. “With the United States Space Academy, we have an opportunity to help prepare the scientists, engineers, and innovators who will advance America’s interests among the stars.”

News From The States describes the academy contest as Virginia's most prominent new federal-site competition since the FBI headquarters fight, which began almost a decade ago. The FBI project was awarded to Maryland in 2023 under President Joe Biden. Trump later reversed course after being elected and kept the FBI in Washington, D.C.; his administration later lost in court after Maryland challenged the decision.

NASA is located in Washington, D.C., and has operations across the country, including Wallops Flight Facility and Langley Research Center in Virginia. Virginia's proposal centers on NASA Langley, Fort Monroe and the Wallops satellite campus; an announcement on where the academy will be headquartered is expected in late December.
