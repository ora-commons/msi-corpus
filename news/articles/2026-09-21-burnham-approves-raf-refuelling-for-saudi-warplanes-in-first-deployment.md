## Saudi deployment paired with push to wind down Iran conflict with Trump

UK Prime Minister Andy Burnham said on his flight to New York for the United Nations General Assembly that he has agreed to a Saudi request for Royal Air Force air-to-air refuelling support, his first military move since entering office. He framed the deployment as defensive and tied to keeping Gulf oil flowing and easing pressure on British households.

Burnham told reporters that the UK has agreed to provide defensive air support to Saudi Arabia to help counter Houthi fighters and restore stability in the Middle East while driving down the cost of living at home, ahead of his first meeting with Donald Trump. The prime minister said he had agreed to a request for air-to-air refuelling of Saudi jets to defend energy infrastructure against the Iran-backed rebels and help keep oil flowing from the Gulf to ease global economic turmoil. Burnham also revealed he will use his first meeting with Trump on Tuesday to urge the US president to de-escalate the conflict in Iran, warning that "dark clouds of global instability" are directly affecting people's lives all over the world.

"We have received a request from Saudi Arabia for military support and … I agreed to that request for defensive air-to-air refuelling. On cost of living pressures and acting to secure the interests of Britain, the wider region … we need to keep those pathways open," Burnham said.

The Guardian reported that Houthi forces have advanced to areas adjacent to the strategically important Bab al-Mandab strait, which Saudi Arabia uses as an alternative route for oil exports bypassing disruption in the Strait of Hormuz. An RAF Voyager air-to-air refuelling aircraft of the type that will help refuel Saudi jets will be deployed for several weeks, in Burnham's first military deployment since taking power. The RAF operational support is in addition to the UK Sky Sabre air defence system already operating in the region.

Burnham framed the deployment against a domestic economic backdrop that the Guardian described as marked by intense inflationary pressure.
