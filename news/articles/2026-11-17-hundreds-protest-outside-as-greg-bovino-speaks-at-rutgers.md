## About a dozen protesters inside the building drowned out Bovino’s remarks

The Guardian reported that hundreds protested outside as former U.S. Border Patrol commander Greg Bovino spoke at Rutgers University in New Brunswick, New Jersey, on November 16, 2026. Bovino addressed about 25 people inside the Douglass Student Center while the protesters outside jeered. Police ordered demonstrators to stop disrupting the event or disperse; Bovino left the stage for a few minutes and returned.

The Rutgers University College Republicans had invited Bovino to speak and promoted the event in social posts, calling him the “Great Commander” and the “GOAT.” During the two-hour talk, the room never quite filled and was occupied mostly by journalists, club members and other students criticizing the former Border Patrol commander.

Photographs by Olga Fedorova/EPA and Eduardo Muñoz/Reuters showed Bovino speaking to the College Republicans inside the Douglass Student Center and protesters outside. One Fedorova/EPA image showed an anti-ICE protester holding a photograph of Renee Good, who was shot and killed by an ICE agent in Minneapolis, during a protest before Bovino’s Rutgers appearance.

Bovino asked the audience, “Show of hands, can we remove 100 million illegal aliens by 2028? Do you think we can?” Two or three people raised their hands. He responded, “Well, I’m telling you what, my hand’s raised! American exceptionalism! We can do it before then!”

The Guardian described Bovino as “the face of the Trump administration’s immigration crackdown” and reported that he oversaw Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) surges in Chicago, Minneapolis and other U.S. cities. The Guardian reported that scrutiny and public outrage over agents’ handling of immigrant arrests, including pulling children off streets and taking people from cars, intensified after agents killed Minneapolis residents Alex Pretti and Renee Good in January. The report also said Bovino had argued that Pretti was a “suspect” rather than a victim. He was taken out of the field by the end of the month and retired in March, reportedly while under internal investigation.

A few minutes into the talk, about a dozen protesters inside drowned out his remarks. “What’s so crazy is that you all allowed a Nazi to be here,” one protester shouted over him, standing from her seat. “Your fascist death cult is failing!” yelled another. Several police officers gathered in front of Bovino on stage.

Immigration advocate Gabriela Soto helped lead the disruption. Her husband, Martín, had helped lead hunger strikes over conditions and deaths at the Delaney Hall immigration detention facility in New Jersey earlier this year. The Guardian reported that Martín’s transfer to another facility in May was followed by weeks of intense protests. Soto was pregnant when Martín was taken into ICE custody, and she miscarried while he was held in solitary confinement. At Rutgers, she stood on a chair and shouted, “You’re taking children!” and “It’s not OK!”

Bovino eventually left the stage for a few minutes while police ordered protesters to either stop the disruption or disperse; they largely complied. He returned to continue the talk. The Guardian reported that Bovino repeatedly mentioned a small Michigan city that banned LGBTQ+ pride flags in 2023, made what the report described as a seemingly homophobic pun on Pete Buttigieg’s surname and ridiculed New Jersey governor Mikie Sherrill. Sherrill had issued a statement earlier Monday condemning the event without ordering its cancellation.

Even among the smaller crowd, multiple people disagreed with Bovino during a question-and-answer session. When retired teacher Alan Cummings asked about Bovino’s goal of 100 million deportations, The Guardian noted that the estimated number of undocumented immigrants in the U.S. was a fraction of that total. Bovino argued that Cummings was not proud to be American.

After the College Republicans announced the event last month, it was immediately met with backlash. The ICE Out of New Jersey coalition, which comprised several advocacy organizations, and student organizers called on Rutgers University to cancel the appearance. A student petition opposing the event garnered more than 2,000 signatures.

Dory Devlin, Rutgers assistant vice-president of news and media relations, said the university was committed to freedom of expression. She said providing space for the event did not constitute university endorsement of Bovino or his views: “providing space for a student-organized event does not constitute university endorsement of the speaker or the views expressed.”

At the protest, Rutgers senior and student organizer Brea Yumiguano, who was with Climate Revolution Action Network, said immigrant students, students with immigrant family members and international students feared being targeted by ICE. “We are all about free speech,” Yumiguano said. “The issue comes when it’s Greg Bovino, someone who has been violent in the past, who has eroded our constitutional rights, who’s pepper-sprayed protesters in Minneapolis – that’s not what we want in New Brunswick.”

Earlier that Monday, The Guardian reported that U.S. international student enrollment had dropped for the second consecutive year amid the Trump administration’s ICE campaign. A Rutgers sophomore named Ninnie broke down in tears while saying she did not feel safe bringing her family to campus and describing reports of ICE agents targeting local public transportation. “There are plenty of us students here who are people of color who are afraid to have our family members come visit us at Rutgers,” she told the crowd.

After more than an hour of chanting to drums and bagpipes, most of the protesters left. Those who remained at Bovino’s talk trickled out through a security perimeter established by police, leaving a sign that read “prosecute Bovino” by the exit. Jorge Torres of the National Day Laborer Organizing Network, a member of the ICE Out coalition, was among those who interrupted Bovino. “This is an example of what New Jersey is about: New Jersey is an immigrants’ rights state. We welcome immigrants,” Torres said. “We don’t want no neo-Nazis in our state.”
