## Reform deputy leader Richard Tice calls Putin a murderous dictator

Reform UK and the Conservatives have each moved to distance themselves from Donald Trump after the US president posted a photograph of himself shaking hands with Vladimir Putin, captioned "My friend."

The image, posted by Trump on his Truth Social platform overnight, shows the US president leaning toward the Russian leader as they spoke at last week's Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, southern China. Trump had already described Putin as a friend at that summit, where he also met the Chinese president, Xi Jinping.

Asked for his reaction to the post, Richard Tice, the deputy leader of Reform UK — the British party most closely associated with Trump — replied: "Horror."

"It's just wrong at every level," Tice told Sky News. "It's a grave mistake. He is not our friend, Putin. He is, as others have said, a murderous dictator. And no one is in any doubt about that."

Asked whether Reform leader Nigel Farage, a friend of the US president, would raise the matter with Trump, Tice said the two men had disagreed before and the disagreements were mounting. "There have been more occasions recently when clearly we have disagreed with him and said so very explicitly," he said. "And in a sense, that's the nature of long friendships. Sometimes you'll agree and sometimes you'll disagree. And, I think the disagreements are growing."

Alex Burghart, the Conservatives' deputy leader under Kemi Badenoch, said he was "very sad" about the post. "I'm sad and I'm depressed, because the world that I grew up in was one in which the United States was a defender of freedom," he told Sky News. "I worry that we might be moving into a world where that isn't the case any more."

Pressed on his party's earlier backing for Trump, Burghart pointed to Conservative defence and foreign policy positions, saying the party had committed to raising defence spending to the level Nato wants. "But, I very much hope that we don't enter a world in which large, aggressive powers feel that they can occupy their neighbours and get the approval of the United States," he said.

Both Reform and the Conservatives welcomed Trump's re-election in 2024. The two parties have increasingly tried to dissociate themselves from the US president amid questions over his temperament and judgment, while his attacks on Iran have driven up global energy prices.

Polling last month for YouGov found that just 10% of British people have a favourable opinion of Trump, with 86% holding an unfavourable view.

Hamish Falconer, a Foreign Office minister, also responded to the post, calling Putin "a butcher" and saying Andy Burnham would make the UK's views known. Falconer told Sky News: "The prime minister speaks to the president regularly and is in absolutely no way pulling his punches about the strength of feeling that the UK has as a whole about Russia's invasion of Ukraine."
