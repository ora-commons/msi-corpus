## Contractors who fail to comply risk terminated contracts, White House warns

President Donald Trump signed an executive order late Friday directing federal contractors and subcontractors to consult with his administration about inquiries they receive from Congress and to withhold any communications that the White House considers privileged.

Under the order, companies must coordinate with the White House and U.S. agencies on "appropriate responses" to any such requests. The White House warned that contractors who fail to comply could have their contracts terminated and their future work with the federal government suspended.

The directive covers all contractors and subcontractors — a universe of tens of thousands of businesses.

It is the administration's latest effort to resist what are expected to be wide-ranging and aggressive oversight efforts from Democrats when they take over Congress in January. Democrats have made government contracts one of their top investigative targets, noting that federal money has been flowing to companies linked with Trump's family, particularly at the Pentagon.

The order follows other steps by White House officials to exert more control over how agencies and officials respond to congressional requests, in anticipation of a flood of investigations once Democrats take power in January.

On Monday, White House Counsel Will Scharf told U.S. agencies that they must seek approval from his office and the White House's Office of Legislative Affairs before agreeing to appear before Congress. Scharf also suggested in a recent letter to Rep. Robert Garcia, the top Democrat on the House Oversight Committee, that some congressional investigations may amount to "blatant criminality."

Garcia earlier this week sent letters directing members of Trump's family and top administration officials to preserve documents. Scharf sought to dismiss the requests, noting in a letter to Democratic lawmakers that they are not yet in the majority and cannot compel compliance.

In a fact sheet, the White House framed the new requirements as "protecting executive privileges from congressional overreach," arguing that some congressional leaders have tried to intrude on executive branch privileges by "harassing private companies that contract with the federal government."

Presidents of both parties have long used executive privilege to resist congressional inquiries, but the claims can set up messy, protracted legal fights as the parties dispute whether the protection applies. Executive privilege allows the president to keep information from the courts, Congress and the public to protect the confidentiality of White House deliberations, though the protection is far from absolute.

Friday's order extends the administration's claim on that protection to private companies holding federal contracts, requiring them to route congressional contact through the White House before responding.
