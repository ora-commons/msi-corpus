## Draft communiques focus on Middle East war and South China Sea disputes

Southeast Asian leaders opened their annual summit in the Philippine capital on Sunday, beginning three days of talks that will address the world's most troubling conflicts, led by the war in the Middle East, according to drafts of post-summit communiques seen by The Associated Press. The meetings include expanded dialogues with Asian and Western heads of state, led by U.S. President Donald Trump.

Trump is expected to land in Manila on Tuesday afternoon with U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio, Philippine officials said, after the main summit meetings have concluded and Marcos has turned the regional bloc's rotating annual leadership over to Singapore. His trip to Asia comes after his party's defeat in the midterm elections.

The leaders' draft statement lays out the bloc's posture. "We reaffirmed our shared commitment to upholding international law, including the United Nations Charter," the draft says. It also underlines the "importance of the peaceful resolution of disputes, upholding freedom of navigation and overflight and other lawful uses of the sea, including the right of transit passage in straits used for international navigation."

The ASEAN leaders are likely to renew calls for self-restraint and the peaceful resolution of conflicts, including longstanding disputes in the South China Sea and the civil war in Myanmar. They also are expected to press efforts to shield their economies and citizens from the continuing fallout of intractable global conflicts.

Marcos, speaking before ASEAN counterparts in a heavily secured convention center by Manila Bay, said the 11-nation bloc's diversity makes its sustained unity crucial. "That commitment matters even more as the waters around us turn on shifting tides, geopolitical tensions, economic uncertainty, new technologies, climate change and transnational threats," Marcos said.

He warned that problems across the world "are reshaping our region faster than any one country can respond to alone."

Unity, however, has been an elusive objective for ASEAN, a diverse bloc of democracies and authoritarian states, many of which are aligned with the United States or China. The grouping is known more for photo-ops projecting unity, cultural pomp and pageantry than for effectively dealing with the region's conflicts, including Myanmar and the South China Sea.

Trump will join three key meetings late Tuesday, including one of the Quadrilateral Security Dialogue — the United States, Japan, Australia and India, a grouping largely seen as a counterweight to China. He also will take part in a special summit with ASEAN leaders over cocktails, according to Philippine officials.

On Wednesday, Marcos will receive Trump with a red-carpet welcome and a state luncheon at Manila's presidential palace. The two leaders are expected to renew their countries' decades-old treaty alliance before flying separately to the Chinese city of Shenzhen for the APEC summit hosted by Chinese President Xi Jinping.

"The short stop in Manila will help reassure allies and partners that they aren't being forgotten before he jets off to Shenzhen to focus on meetings with Xi," said Greg Poling, a U.S.-based geopolitical analyst.

Poling said the midterm electoral setback suffered by Republicans will hound Trump in Asia. "In China, the administration has been trying to lower expectations; there will be no substantive progress made between Trump and Xi," Poling said. "With Trump politically weakened after the midterm drubbing here, it is likely that the Chinese side will be even less interested in making any compromises."

China's delegation at the Manila summit is led by Premier Li Qiang, who will meet ASEAN leaders on Monday but leave before Trump arrives, Philippine officials said.
