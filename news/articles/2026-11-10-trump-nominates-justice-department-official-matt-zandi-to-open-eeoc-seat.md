## Zandi's confirmation would give Republicans three of the commission's five seats

President Donald Trump has nominated Matt Zandi, a Justice Department official, to an open seat on the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission, the agency that enforces the country's employment anti-discrimination laws. The White House announced the selection late Monday.

Zandi would fill the seat previously held by Jocelyn Samuels, one of two Democratic commissioners Trump fired before the end of their terms — a move the Associated Press called "an unprecedented move to seize control" of the bipartisan agency Congress created through the 1964 Civil Rights Act.

The nomination advances the administration's effort to dismantle diversity policies it claims discriminate against white workers and others. Zandi serves as chief of staff for the Justice Department's civil rights division, which saw a mass departure of career lawyers as it shifted to investigating employers and schools for what it considers unlawful diversity and transgender policies.

If confirmed, Zandi would serve a five-year term ending July 1, 2031. The commission currently has two Republican commissioners — Andrea Lucas, who is the chair, and Brittany Panuccio — and one Democrat, Kalpana Kotagal, whose term expires next year. Zandi's addition would give Republicans three of the five seats.

Under Lucas, the EEOC has enforced Trump's civil rights priorities, often in tight cooperation with the Justice Department.

The administration has yet to nominate anyone to the commission's fifth seat, left open by the firing of Democrat Charlotte Burrows. The impending Democratic takeover of the Senate majority in January raises the possibility that the EEOC will not have five commissioners for the remainder of Trump's term.

Leading Democratic senators have vowed not to confirm any of Trump's nominees to the agency in protest of the firings, which they say stripped the EEOC of its historic independence and bipartisanship. Sen. Tammy Baldwin, a Wisconsin Democrat, criticized Zandi's nomination as further politicizing the commission.

"For a bipartisan commission, the custom is to pair nominees from the two parties. This would be the third Republican commissioner in a row without a pairing, an overtly partisan decision of the administration," Baldwin said Tuesday, speaking during a Senate committee hearing on the confirmation of Catherine Eschbach to be EEOC general counsel.

The Supreme Court upheld Trump's decision to fire the heads of several independent agencies, a ruling that significantly expanded presidential power. The decision prompted Samuels to drop a lawsuit challenging her dismissal from the EEOC.

Zandi and Eschbach are likely to be confirmed while Republicans control the Senate. Senate Republicans have mostly praised Trump's approach to civil rights enforcement, and Lucas and Panuccio were confirmed despite unanimous opposition from Democrats.

"Under this administration, EEOC has sharpened its focus on even-handed enforcement of employment discrimination laws," said Sen. Bill Cassidy, a Louisiana Republican, during the hearing on Eschbach, whom he praised.

The EEOC had no comment. The Justice Department and Zandi did not reply to a request for comment.
