## Thousands of flights upended, tens of thousands of travelers disrupted

L3Harris Technologies is facing attention after an upgrade it performed on much of the U.S. air traffic communications system set off a nationwide disruption that grounded domestic flights for hours on Friday, according to The Wall Street Journal's Morning Risk Report, published Monday.

The chain of events began with a single act of work. A technician's installation of a router triggered a cascade of failures, and federal aviation officials responded by grounding domestic air traffic for hours, the Journal reported. The stop upended thousands of flights and the travel plans of tens of thousands of Americans.

The Journal described the turmoil as unprecedented, writing that the upgrade of the communications system "caused unprecedented turmoil." L3Harris, the major defense contractor responsible for much of the system, has promoted itself with a single phrase: the "trusted disruptor."

The report frames the episode as a moment of exposure for a company that ordinarily draws little public notice. The contractor is under-the-radar but vitally important, the Journal wrote, and the ground stop put a bull's-eye on it.

The incident lands squarely in the risk and compliance world the Morning Risk Report covers. It ties a technician's installation of a router on a contractor-run communications network to a shutdown of domestic air travel, a reminder that upgrades to infrastructure carrying public traffic can fail at national scale.

For L3Harris, the reputational cost sits alongside the operational one. The company's self-description as the "trusted disruptor" is now measured against a Friday in which the disruption it describes was felt by tens of thousands of travelers whose travel plans were upended.
