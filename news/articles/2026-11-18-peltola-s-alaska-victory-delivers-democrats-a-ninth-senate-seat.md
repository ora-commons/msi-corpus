## Democrats' Senate majority reaches its largest advantage in 16 years

Former Rep. Mary Peltola’s victory over Republican incumbent Sen. Dan Sullivan in Alaska’s competitive U.S. Senate race flipped a ninth Republican-held seat to Democrats. Associated Press reporter Robert Yoon reported that the result delivered the party its largest midterm Senate gain in nearly 70 years and gave Democrats a 56-44 majority when the new Congress begins in January.

Robert Yoon’s historical comparison reached back to 1958, during Republican President Dwight Eisenhower’s second term. Democrats last gained nine or more Senate seats in a midterm election that year. Democrats captured 13 Republican-held seats and won both of Alaska’s newly created seats, producing a total gain of 15.

With Peltola’s victory, Democrats will begin the new Congress with a 56-44 Senate majority, their largest advantage in 16 years. Democrats held 58 Senate seats in the waning days of the 111th Congress, which concluded on Jan. 3, 2011.

Even without the Alaska result, Democrats would have secured their largest Senate majority since January 2015. Republicans took control then after flipping nine Democratic seats in the 2014 midterms during President Barack Obama’s second term.

Democrat Scott Colom’s U.S. Senate victory Wednesday in Mississippi supplied the eighth Republican-held seat before Alaska. Colom’s win gave Democrats their largest number of midterm pickups since President Ronald Reagan’s second midterm election 40 years ago.

Democrats also reclaimed the House, picking up 42 seats as of Tuesday. The House gains continued a historical trend of midterm losses for the president’s party.

The Senate has tended to be less susceptible to the midterm effect than the House. In seven of the last 16 midterms before 2026, the president’s party either held its ground or gained seats in the Senate, though all but one of those midterms occurred in the president’s first term.

Associated Press analysis also noted that a president’s second midterm tends to be more punishing. By comparison, the president’s party had gained House seats in only two midterms since the end of World War II.
