## Compass earns about two-thirds of its profit in North America

The chief executive of the British investment group M&G, Andrea Rossi, called on the UK government to “take a stand” against the long line of companies leaving the London Stock Exchange after Compass Group proposed shifting its primary listing to New York. In a letter to the Financial Times, Rossi said M&G's fund managers would vote against the proposal.

Compass Group proposed making New York its primary listing venue while retaining a secondary listing in the United Kingdom. Shareholders would vote on the plan at a future general meeting, and the company expected the transition to occur in 2028 if approved.

Rossi called Compass Group's decision a “warning about the health of the UK stock market.” He wrote: “when one of the largest members of the FTSE 100 concludes that its interests are better served elsewhere, policymakers cannot regard it as an isolated corporate decision.” He also said the continued departure of major companies from London's stock market should concern anyone who cares about Britain's economic future.

M&G's position gives it a financial interest in the outcome. LSEG data show that M&G Investment Management owns 0.75% of Compass Group's shares, making it the company's 18th-largest shareholder. Compass Group itself is the 18th-largest FTSE 100 company by market capitalization, at £45.9bn, and the world's largest caterer.

The schools and NHS caterer said it had become a “predominantly North American business.” About two-thirds of Compass Group's profit comes from North America, while about two-fifths of its shareholders are based in the United States. Compass Group said a New York primary listing would provide better access to “the world's largest and deepest pool of capital.”

The proposal also comes as only about a third of reported revenue across the FTSE 100 is derived from the UK. There are concerns that other big firms could make the same argument. Rossi said the government should introduce targeted interventions to reduce the number of companies leaving the UK stock market, including reducing or removing stamp duty on UK shares. The Financial Times estimated that the government would lose about £40m in stamp duty revenue when Compass Group moves its primary listing.

Rossi also argued that pension providers could allocate more of their default funds to British stocks. He wrote: “Where taxpayer support is provided to private companies, a future UK listing should form part of the bargain.”

Compass Group's proposed relocation would follow other primary-listing departures by companies seeking deeper pools of capital and higher valuations in the United States. Equipment rental company Ashtead, building-material supplier CRH and Flutter Entertainment, the owner of Paddy Power, have moved their primary listings to the US in recent years. The UK's biggest drugmaker, AstraZeneca, directly listed its shares on the New York Stock Exchange this year, putting its US shares on an even footing with its London-listed stock.

Three FTSE 100 companies — fund manager Schroders, insurer Beazley and product-testing group Intertek — have also left the stock market after being acquired by overseas groups this year.

Compass Group's proposal remained subject to shareholder approval. The UK government was approached for comment.
