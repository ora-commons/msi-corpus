## New York bans manufacture and sale of AI chatbot toys for five years

**Subtype:** fact

- New York Gov. Kathy Hochul signed legislation on Wednesday that bans the manufacture and sale of toys powered by AI chatbots for five years.
- Attorney General Letitia James said her office would “not hesitate to enforce this law and other measures to stop AI systems from doing harm.”
- An NBC News study found the Pixie Paw, a toy marketed to children as young as three, had shared details about firing squads and massacres.
- The US Public Interest Research Group Education Fund’s “Trouble in Toyland” report found AI chatbot-enabled toys that talked in depth about sexually explicit topics and told children where they were likely to find matches in the home.
- A consortium of more than 155 child safety advocates and technology experts asked Amazon CEO Andy Jassy and eBay CEO Jamie Iannone to bar AI toys from their platforms.

## Attorney General James says her office will enforce the law

Gov. Kathy Hochul signed legislation on Wednesday that bans the manufacture and sale of toys powered by AI chatbots for five years, covering talking stuffed animals, dolls and robots that use the internet to hold conversations with children. The law follows an earlier letter from more than 155 child safety advocates and technology experts asking Amazon and eBay to bar AI toys from their platforms, and it gives the state attorney general’s office a stated role in keeping the products out of the state.

The recently re-elected governor said parents shopping for children’s toys “should not have to worry that dangerous chatbots disguised as toys could expose their children to inappropriate conversations, manipulate their feelings or put their privacy at risk.” Hochul said the measure was “deeply personal” and that, as a mother and grandmother, “it’s my job to put New York families first.”

Officials pointed in a press release to “Trouble in Toyland,” a consumer watchdog report from the US Public Interest Research Group Education Fund. The report tested AI chatbot-enabled toys marketed to children as young as three and found products that “talked in depth about sexually explicit topics, told kids where to likely find matches in the home and acted upset when a child had to leave.” The governor said that in some cases the toys had few or no parental controls, and that the safety limits weakened the longer a conversation continued. “Additionally, some toys could record a child’s voice or scan their face,” the statement said.

A study by NBC News found that the Pixie Paw, a toy marketed to children as young as three, had shared details about firing squads and massacres, and that a second product, the Pixie Paw Smart Interactive Plush Cat, explained gang rape.

Attorney General Letitia James said AI chatbots in children’s toys “are exposing young people to inappropriate content and putting their mental health at risk.” Her office would “not hesitate to enforce this law and other measures to stop AI systems from doing harm,” she said, adding: “We won’t allow our children’s health to become the collateral damage of big tech.”

Last month, Hochul signed the “AI Chatbot Restrictions for Minors” legislation, which prohibited companies from offering minors certain unsafe chatbot features, including features that foster emotional dependency, encourage self-harm or engage children in sexually explicit talk. Wednesday’s measure instead targets the manufacture and sale of toys powered by AI chatbots.

The New York measure follows a similar measure in California, which placed a four-year moratorium on the sale and manufacture of toys with AI companions. Earlier this week, a consortium of more than 155 child safety advocates and technology experts sent a letter to Amazon CEO Andy Jassy and eBay CEO Jamie Iannone asking them to bar AI toys from their platforms.

Rachel Franz, an advocate for limitations on tech for children and leader of a program at the nonprofit advocacy group Fairplay, told NBC News: “Just like we don’t release cars without seat belts and test them to make sure they are safe to be driven, we shouldn’t be releasing toys that are available to the consumer before they’ve been rigorously tested.” LaVita Tuff with Mothers Against Media Addiction said that “Parents should be able to feel confident that the toys their children play with are safe, which means that toys containing AI should only be sold if they are safe by design.” State senator Andrew Gounardes said in a statement that “we don’t have to live in a Twilight Zone episode turned real.”

eBay and Amazon, both named in the letter, responded to NBC News. An eBay spokesperson told the network: “We continually evaluate our policies, engage with regulators, and comply with applicable laws to help ensure our marketplace remains safe, trusted, and responsive to evolving safety standards.” An Amazon spokesperson also gave a statement to the outlet: “We require all products sold by third-party sellers in our store to comply with applicable laws and regulations,” and that “Amazon is committed to the safety of children and families. We support efforts across the industry and government to put appropriate guardrails in place as this technology develops.”

Hochul has also placed a one-year moratorium on AI datacenter construction in the state. The “Trouble in Toyland” report tested products marketed to children as young as three. Both the manufacture and the sale are covered by the five-year prohibition, and the state attorney general’s office is the enforcement channel James named.


The demand for a pause came first: [Child safety groups seek pause in AI toy sales this Christmas until proved safe](/articles/2026-11-23-child-safety-groups-seek-pause-in-ai-toy-sales-this-christmas-until-proved-safe/). The five-year prohibition signed Wednesday follows that campaign and bars the manufacture and sale of AI chatbot toys in New York.
