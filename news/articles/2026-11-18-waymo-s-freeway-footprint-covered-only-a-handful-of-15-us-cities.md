## Company data suggests 95% fewer crashes involving serious injury than human drivers

Waymo operates driverless taxis in 15 US cities but offered freeway rides in only a handful of those markets, including San Francisco, Los Angeles and Miami.

Data on Waymo's website suggests that its cars, compared with human drivers, are involved in 95% fewer crashes resulting in serious injury.

In May, Waymo recalled thousands of its self-driving cars in the US over a software issue that could allow vehicles to drive into flooded roads. A related BBC News report said the recall came after a robotaxi swept into a creek.

BBC News reporter James Chater reported that multiple Waymo cars were spotted driving around a cul-de-sac in Atlanta, Georgia, in the early hours of the morning that month.

Waymo temporarily suspended its driverless vehicles from operating on all US freeways after California banned the taxis from state freeways following a series of recent traffic incidents. The company did not halt regular city-street operations.

California's Department of Motor Vehicles said the ban, which came into effect on 11 November, would remain until Waymo demonstrated to the department that it had taken effective corrective actions addressing the deficiencies that precipitated the restrictions. The DMV cited an "imminent hazard to public safety" and said Waymo's driverless taxis presented a risk "due to recurring incursions by its vehicles into active construction zones where construction workers, vehicles and equipment are present".

Earlier this month, several driverless Waymo vehicles entered an active construction zone on US Route 101. Waymo said it was working with state officials "diligently and responsibly to resume freeway operations." The company said no injuries or collisions were reported following the incident south of San Francisco.

In another incident earlier this month, footage widely seen on social media appeared to show a Waymo car trying to overtake patrol vehicles that were deliberately slowing the pace of traffic on a San Francisco highway.

A Waymo spokesperson said: "In alignment with our safety-first principles, we have also temporarily restricted freeway operations across our other operating markets to evaluate our performance."
