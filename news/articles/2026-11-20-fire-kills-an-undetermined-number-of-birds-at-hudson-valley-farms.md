## The cause remains unconfirmed after a response involving 200 first responders

A fire broke out on Thursday at Hudson Valley Farms in Ferndale, New York, and engulfed several barns before firefighters could get inside. The blaze killed an undetermined number of birds. Marcus Henley, manager of Hudson Valley Foie Gras, told Radio Catskill that two buildings housing ducks and geese were destroyed. BBC News described the farm as one of the largest foie gras farms in the US.

The emergency response in the early hours of Thursday morning involved 37 fire departments and 200 first responders, Westchester News 12 reported. Crews managed to rescue hundreds of young birds and hatchlings, and local news outlets reported that no workers or first responders were injured.

The fire spread rapidly, and the metal-clad, wood-frame structure made it difficult for firefighters to stop the flames from spreading inside the barn.

Henley said, "The building isn't there anymore." He suspected an electrical malfunction may have started the blaze, although BBC News reported that no information had been provided about what caused the fire.

Hudson Valley Foie Gras did not disclose how many birds it hosted at one time. BBC News noted that commercial poultry farms can typically house thousands of birds.

Hudson Valley Foie Gras and the local fire department did not respond to a BBC News request for comment.

Foie gras is made from the fattened liver of a duck or goose. Producers of the product have long faced criticism from animal rights groups because the birds are force-fed through plastic tubes, a process that allows them to gain over a third of their body weight in a short period.

New York moved to ban the product in 2019, and Hudson Valley farms have been in litigation with the state since. Voters in Washington DC and Denver backed bans on foie gras sales in this month's midterm elections.

At a city council hearing in 2019, Henley argued against the ban, saying that journalists who visited the farm found "nothing inhumane" about its practices.

PETA told BBC News that it would erect a billboard nearby in memory of the birds that died. The animal rights group also encouraged Hudson Valley farms to donate surviving birds to local sanctuaries.
