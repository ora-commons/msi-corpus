## PolitiFact and Snopes refute claims of a 90-day U.S. blackout starting Nov. 17

**Subtype:** fact

- PolitiFact rated the claim that a 90-day nationwide U.S. blackout would begin Nov. 17, 2026, False.
- PolitiFact traced a manipulated TikTok video of Donald Trump to a Nov. 9 press briefing that did not announce a blackout.
- FEMA scheduled a nationwide Emergency Alert System test for Nov. 17 using radio and TV, with no action required from the public.
- DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin said Americans do not need to stockpile supplies for potential power, water and food disruptions.
- Fox News reported that FORTRESS America was preparing U.S. military bases to operate without outside power for at least 90 days.

## FEMA's alert test uses radio and TV and requires no public action

A PolitiFact report by Maria Briceño, carried by PBS News, rated false social media posts warning of a 90-day nationwide blackout. The posts say the disruption will start Nov. 17, 2026, and urge people to stock food and other supplies. PolitiFact found no evidence that such a blackout is scheduled.

A Nov. 14 TikTok post presented an altered clip of President Donald Trump apparently saying, “A 90-day blackout is going to happen before the end of the year,” from the White House press briefing room. PolitiFact located the original video from Trump’s Nov. 9 remarks. He discussed former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and the Iran war; he did not announce a 90-day blackout. The TikTok account used the purported clip to promote buying cryptocurrency before the supposed blackout and has posted other manipulated Trump videos.

Snopes found similar manipulated Trump videos on Facebook and Instagram. Those posts promoted cryptocurrency and claimed it would make people money during the “upcoming” blackout. A Spanish-language TikTok post said the blackout had already started, citing the Nov. 12-14 Microsoft outage and the Federal Aviation Administration’s Nov. 13 order to ground all flights because of faulty old equipment.

Microsoft said in a Nov. 13 post on X that the outage was a technical issue, not a cyber attack. The FAA allowed flights to resume Nov. 13.

The Federal Emergency Management Agency and the Federal Communications Commission announced on Sept. 17 that they would conduct a nationwide test of the Emergency Alert System on Nov. 17 at around 2:20 p.m. ET. The test will air only on radio and television and will not be sent to mobile phones. Authorities said there is nothing the public needs to do in response.

The alert test is required at least once every three years under federal law to ensure the system remains ready for an emergency. It is not expected to announce a real emergency or blackout.

The scheduled message will last one minute and include an alert sound. It will say, “This is a nationwide test of the Emergency Alert System, issued by the Federal Emergency Management Agency. This is only a test. No action is required by the public.” FEMA said the test would be postponed to Dec. 3 if an actual emergency occurred on Nov. 17.

After the FAA ground stop, Fox News Senior White House Correspondent Peter Doocy asked DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin on Nov. 15 whether Americans should stockpile food and supplies. Mullin said Americans do not need supplies for potential disruptions to power, water and food.

“That's kind of running over the internet right now too, especially throughout social media. 'Should I be worried? The 90 days', absolutely not,” Mullin said. “I'm not stockpiling and I tell my kids all the time, don't worry unless you see me worry. We're in good shape here, the panic that people want to draw from is unfounded.”

The United Kingdom government had advised residents in late October to keep enough supplies to last 72 hours to survive weather, cyber or other serious emergencies. The notice came before the winter storm season and amid concerns that the global phenomenon “super El-Niño” could affect global food supplies and energy systems. Mullin said the United Kingdom does not have the same safeguards and emergency preparedness as the United States.

Some social media users linked the blackout claim to FORTRESS America, which Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced on Sept. 30. FORTRESS stands for Federal Operations for Resilience, Troop Resources, Energy, Supply and Survivability. The project seeks to ensure every major U.S. military installation generates its own independent power so that bases remain operational even if civilian electric grids are taken down by a cyberattack or sabotage.

Fox News reported on Oct. 25 that documents it reviewed showed the project was preparing U.S. military bases to operate without outside power for at least 90 days. The network did not report that a 90-day blackout was coming.

This was not the first time an emergency-alert test had been linked to false claims. PolitiFact said it had previously fact-checked bogus assertions that test signals could turn people into zombies or activate COVID-19 vaccine ingredients in people’s bodies.

PolitiFact separated the Nov. 17 alert test, the Microsoft outage, the FAA ground stop and the FORTRESS project from the unsupported blackout claim, concluding: “We rate this claim False.”
