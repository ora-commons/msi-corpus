## Europe sales fall 28% while U.S. decline narrows

Shein reported third-quarter net income of $4.65 billion, up from $616 million a year earlier, even as group revenue fell 5.9% to $10.3 billion in the July-September period. Adjusted profit rose 27% as the China-founded fast-fashion retailer adapted to trade-related costs, steps that included raising prices and reallocating marketing spend. The regional breakdown was uneven: revenue in the United States declined 9.4%, revenue in Europe fell 28%, and revenue from the rest of the world grew 18%.

Shein framed those figures as a division among its markets. "We took the hit from the change in European trade policy, while the U.S. continued to recover and the rest of the world kept growing healthily," Shein said Wednesday.

The U.S. business has yet to catch up with last year's revenue level, though the decline narrowed on a monthly basis. Shein said order volumes are improving and added: "We believe the adjustment in the United States has passed its most difficult phase."

Europe remained the weakest link for the retailer. Its 28% revenue decline reflected a new customs duty imposed in July on low-value packages. Shein has pivoted to local fulfillment and diversified supply chains to offset the hit from the European Union rule, a response shared by peers PDD Holdings' Temu and Alibaba's AliExpress.

With Latin America a particular standout, sales in the rest of the world continued to grow strongly, the company said. On Europe, Shein said: "We believe that over the medium to long term we will return to, and exceed, our previous peak level of sales in Europe."

Shein reiterated efforts to diversify its supply chain and ensure growth does not depend on any single market. The company now manufactures clothes in Brazil and Turkey to be closer to consumers in North America and Europe.

Price increases have been necessary to offset costs, and Shein acknowledged they would weigh on sales volumes in the short term, though the company still expects consumers to return to the brand over the medium to long term. The Wall Street Journal noted that the increases raise the risk of alienating budget-conscious customers.

Higher freight costs tied to shipping disruptions from the Iran war have become another pain point, though Shein said prices have largely normalized.

Shein built its business on ultracheap, trendy clothing and social-media campaigns that drew global shoppers to affordable online fashion, and at its peak the company commanded a $100 billion valuation. That figure has shrunk to $20 billion as scrutiny of the company's consumer practices and compliance has intensified on both sides of the Atlantic. Sales have slowed in the face of trade barriers in the United States and the European Union and fierce competition from bargain-shopping platform Temu.

The company had a subdued debut in Hong Kong in September, and its stock is down more than 20% since listing. Whether the third-quarter results are enough to convince investors remains to be seen.

Analysts expect near-term headwinds to persist for Shein. Duty-related price increases could weigh on demand and pressure revenue next year before a recovery in 2028, Deutsche Bank analyst Sammi Xu said in a note.
