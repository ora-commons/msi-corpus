## Wright: Poland is a gateway for American LNG into the region

U.S. Energy Secretary Chris Wright urged Western European countries on Wednesday to stop closing power plants in pursuit of lower carbon emissions and instead expand energy production. Speaking in Warsaw at the U.S.-led Partnership for Transatlantic Energy Cooperation conference, Wright called the shift fundamental to the continent’s economic and national security.

The conference brings together governments across Central, Eastern and southeastern Europe and regional businesses in a bid to improve energy cooperation with the United States. Historically, it has served as a platform for U.S. businesses to sign contracts and agreements for energy with countries in the region, and U.S. administrations have used it to encourage construction of regasification plants and pipelines that can transport U.S. LNG across the continent. As Europe weans itself off Russian gas, liquefied natural gas agreements have been prominent in recent years, while nuclear energy has risen up the agenda this year.

Wright said Western European nations have done too little to grow their energy resources and have too often celebrated power-plant closures while pursuing lower carbon-dioxide emissions without expanding their energy mix. He said prosperity requires an energy-addition mindset and that the future of Western civilization depends on energy and energy security.

Wright said Poland is a gateway for American LNG into the region. He said Poland’s role as a gateway was positive for Polish energy security and also for Poland’s immediate neighbors.

Warsaw has been vocal about the need to diversify its energy supply since Russia’s invasion of Ukraine in 2022. Poland has also been one of the most prolific signatories to long-term LNG agreements with U.S. suppliers.

At the conference, Polish Energy Minister Milosz Motyka said there can be no back-to-business with Russia. “Russia used energy sources like a weapon,” he said.

Wright said the future of European economic growth is Poland and Eastern Europe. The Wall Street Journal characterized the region as a natural destination for U.S. gas and American efforts to build energy infrastructure and deepen cooperation.
