## Prosecutors say crew slashed air hoses to force emergency stops

Fourteen people in southern California have been accused of burglarizing more than $22 million worth of goods from moving freight trains, according to a Justice Department case that describes a coordinated crew boarding trains in motion, disabling them, and hauling off cargo.

The indictment charges the group with raiding at least 36 trains and selling thousands of stolen Nike sneakers, among other products, on the black market. First assistant US attorney Bill Essayli described the scope of the operation in a single line: "The scale of the theft is staggering."

Nathan Daniel Angel, 21, is the alleged ringleader of the scheme, which prosecutors say spanned 2024 through 2026 and operated under the name "Los Trenes" — "the trains." Court documents did not list an attorney for Angel or for the other charged individuals.

Each robbery followed a similar pattern, according to a grand jury indictment. Angel's associates, including his father and two uncles, stowed away on moving freight trains. With some of the men aboard serving as lookouts, others slashed the train's air hose, causing a sudden loss of air pressure and forcing an emergency stop of the entire vehicle. The maneuver is perilous given the risks of derailment.

Once a train was disabled, the rest of the Los Trenes crew would move in to load the stolen goods and stash the loot at houses across southern California, prosecutors say.

The indictment is supported by material prosecutors say documents the crew's work. Angel posed for a selfie, throwing up a peace sign, in front of several boxes containing Nike merchandise in a February 2024 photograph attached to court documents. In October 2024, Donta Hedgeman, 27, sawed through the lock of a container on a moving Nike train, according to video evidence.

The heists ramped up in 2025, even as signs emerged that law enforcement was catching on. An unidentified co-conspirator texted 23-year-old Carlos Luis Fernando Martinez on 17 April 2025 to say he had been arrested for receiving stolen property.

"They want me to tell them who did I get them from and who had the other pairs," the person wrote, according to the indictment. Martinez responded by instructing the person to contact him over Signal, an app with encrypted messaging.

Twelve days later, the crew made off with 1,170 tools and 17,000 parts from Milwaukee Tool. Police officers appeared to be examining an unnamed company that had purchased a large number of stolen products from the crew in the fall of 2025.

A second co-conspirator speculated on the group's prospects in a message to Martinez: "Good luck with them wires/if they go deep in the case/You're cooked."

Then, on 16 January 2026, Angel and 25-year-old Fredy Marquina referenced a partner being intercepted by police: "They just got Jorge for buying and receiving/stolen property." Faced with the mounting risk of being caught, the men proceeded with further heists in the summer of 2026.

In July 2026, Angel Sanchez, 24, shared an AI-generated image that appeared to glamorize the robbery spree. It depicted masked men tossing Nike sneakers off a Burlington Northern Santa Fe Railway train into luxury cars inscribed with the words "Los Trenes," with a helicopter circling above and shining a searchlight.

If convicted of the conspiracy charge, the men face at least five years in prison. The theft of interstate railroad charge carries a maximum penalty of 10 years in prison, and a conviction for violence against railroad carriers would carry the most severe punishment, with a possibility of life in prison.

Prosecutors described this week's sweeping indictment as the culmination of a 14-month-long investigation. Nathan Hochman, the Los Angeles county district attorney, said the office intends to protect the freight network the stolen goods moved through.

"We are determined to protect this vital supply chain and ensure that goods reach their intended destinations, not the black market," Hochman said.
