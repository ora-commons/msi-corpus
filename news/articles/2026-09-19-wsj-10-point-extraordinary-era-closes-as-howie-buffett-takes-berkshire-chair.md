## Newsletter cites scrutiny over Berkshire's cash pile, stock and structure

The Wall Street Journal's 10-Point newsletter framed Howard "Howie" Buffett's appointment as Berkshire Hathaway chairman as the close of what it described as an "extraordinary era" at the $1 trillion conglomerate, citing growing scrutiny over its enormous cash pile, its lagging stock and whether its collection of businesses should remain intact.

Warren Buffett has stepped down from the company he built over more than half a century, completing a succession plan years in the making, the newsletter reported. The Journal frames his son's appointment as a signal of what Buffett fears could happen to Berkshire without his direct stewardship.

Reporters Krystal Hur and Gregory Zuckerman wrote the newsletter, which was curated and edited by Bharbi Hazarika and Will Horner in collaboration with Editor in Chief Emma Tucker. The newsletter teased "five key things to know about Berkshire's new chairman."

Howie Buffett is an unconventional choice to help lead a $1 trillion conglomerate: he never graduated from college and has spent much of his career farming and running a charitable foundation, according to the newsletter. His father has long seen him as uniquely suited to protect Berkshire's culture and sprawling structure from pressure for change, the Journal reported.

The scrutiny the newsletter highlights spans three areas: Berkshire's enormous cash pile, its lagging stock and whether its collection of businesses should remain intact. The 10-Point framed the choice of Howie Buffett as a step toward keeping the conglomerate's sprawling structure insulated from pressure for change.
