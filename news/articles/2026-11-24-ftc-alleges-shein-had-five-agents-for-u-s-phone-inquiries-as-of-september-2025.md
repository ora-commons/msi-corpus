## FTC alleges thousands of consumers reported late orders, some never received them

The Federal Trade Commission alleged in its complaint that Shein Global Holdings employed five customer-service agents to handle phone inquiries from U.S. consumers as of September 2025. The complaint said Shein did not provide a phone number for those agents, leaving consumer requests unanswered.

The FTC said Shein marketed fast and reliable shipping but made it difficult for consumers to resolve purchase issues and failed to keep its shipping promises, and that thousands of consumers reported late orders while some never received their orders.

Shein, which was founded in China and is currently based in Singapore, ships nearly one million packages to the U.S. each day.

On reviews, the complaint alleged that Shein prevented certain reviews from being displayed on its website by deleting reviews that criticized the company’s products while publishing positive reviews.

The FTC also alleged that Shein provided no proof that its evoluSHEIN products were sourced from suppliers with above-average ratings in audits designed to protect against child labor. The agency separately alleged that the company issued deceptive claims about which country its cotton came from.

Shein Global Holdings has agreed to pay $100 million to resolve the FTC claims that the fast-fashion retailer engaged in unfair customer-service practices. Under the proposed order, the payment would provide redress to consumers, according to the FTC.

“The agreement does not constitute an admission of liability, and we continue to disagree with aspects of the FTC’s characterization of our business and compliance practices,” a Shein spokesperson said. “Shein has continued to make significant investments in strengthening its consumer practices and compliance efforts,” the spokesperson added.
