## Kuwait says the strike also wounded a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani

An Iranian drone attack in Kuwait killed two Italians on Thursday while they performed maintenance on antidrone equipment, as Tehran continued its attacks to pressure U.S. allies in the region. Kuwait's military said the strike also wounded a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani and blamed the attack on "Iranian aggression." Iran had not immediately claimed the attack.

The Italian Foreign Ministry said the two Italians worked for Rheinmetall Italia. Rheinmetall Italia is the Italian arm of German defense company Rheinmetall, which focuses on air-defense systems. At the time of the strike, the two workers were working on detection antennas for the Skyguard air-defense system used by Kuwait's military.

Kuwait's military said the attack caused material damage but did not say which site was targeted. Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni offered her condolences to the families of the two Italians killed.

The attack came as Iran maintained a stream of strikes to pressure U.S. allies in the region, targeting some of its Gulf neighbors and ships trying to get oil and natural gas out through the Strait of Hormuz. The U.S. military and President Trump have said the strait remains open for business while the United States keeps its naval blockade in place against Iran.

Iran had not immediately claimed the attack and has been threatening to escalate its campaign as negotiations aimed at ending the war remain deadlocked.

The International Institute for Strategic Studies, a London-based think tank, said Tehran launched over 1,200 drones and missiles at Kuwait and over 700 at Bahrain between the war's start on Feb. 28 and the April 8 ceasefire between Iran and the United States.

Both Bahrain and Kuwait have come under repeated attacks during the war, with power plants, desalination facilities and oil infrastructure sustaining damage from Iranian strikes. As Iran stepped up its attacks, both countries came under fire again last week.

The United Arab Emirates has yet to be attacked in this latest period, even though the country saw the most incoming fire during the war, at over 2,800 drones and missiles. Saudi Arabia has also escaped Iranian fire during the latest period, though it faces intense attacks from Yemen's Iranian-backed Houthi rebels.


MSI previously reported on the Iranian strikes that hit Kuwait and Bahrain during the Gulf escalation, including damage to a Kuwaiti water desalination plant and an oil facility: [the June 28 report](/articles/2026-06-28-iran-s-revolutionary-guard-strikes-bahrain-kuwait-as-u-s-hits-10-targets/) and [the July 18 report](/articles/2026-07-18-u-s-iran-escalate-strikes-in-gulf-as-israel-bombs-lebanon/). Thursday's drone attack in Kuwait continued that campaign and killed two Italians working on air-defense equipment.
