## The United States now has three aircraft carriers in the region

Iran launched strikes against Kuwait and Bahrain early Wednesday, widening a conflict already focused on attacks against ships trying to pass through the Strait of Hormuz. Kuwait's military said it faced a wave of incoming drones. Iran said it had targeted Ali Al Salem Air Base, which hosts U.S. forces, but Kuwait had not immediately acknowledged any damage.

The attack on Kuwait marked Iran's first assault on the tiny, oil-rich nation in over two months. In Bahrain, missile alerts sounded twice before sunrise, blasts were heard and air-defense batteries fired. Bahrain's authorities blamed Iran for the attack without elaborating. Bahrain has been targeted three times over the past week by Iran in a series of escalating assaults.

Kuwait and Bahrain have also come under repeated, heavy attacks during the Iran war, with power plants, desalination facilities and oil infrastructure sustaining damage from Iranian assaults. According to the International Institute for Strategic Studies, a London-based think tank, Iran launched over 1,200 drones and missiles at Kuwait from the war's start Feb. 28 through the April 8 ceasefire. Over 700 targeted Bahrain over that period.

During the same period, the United Arab Emirates saw the most incoming fire, with more than 2,800 drones and missiles, according to the institute, but its territory had not been attacked in the latest round. Saudi Arabia had also escaped Iranian fire for now, though it remained under intensive fire from Yemen's Iranian-backed Houthi rebels as they continued to take territory there.

The maritime attacks continued near Oman. On Tuesday, an Iranian attack set the Panamanian-flagged gas tanker Tara Gas ablaze in the Strait of Hormuz. Authorities said they rescued 24 crew members from the vessel; one Indian mariner remained missing. An earlier Iranian attack had disabled the tanker as part of Tehran's guerrilla campaign to disrupt traffic in the Strait of Hormuz, while the U.S. military provides cover to passing vessels.

The United States has continued to increase its military presence in the region. The aircraft carrier USS Theodore Roosevelt arrived in the Middle East and sailed alongside USS George Washington in footage released by the U.S. military, bringing the number of carriers in the region to three. USS George H.W. Bush was also there alongside the California-based USS Boxer, a Wasp-class amphibious assault ship.

Kristian Coates Ulrichsen, an expert at Rice University's Baker Institute who has long studied the region, said the attacks signaled that Iran retained both the capability and intent to target U.S. partners across the Gulf. He said Gulf leaders remained vulnerable to retaliation for U.S. actions in the Strait of Hormuz. As the Trump administration continued its buildup of force, he added, the U.S. did not have a monopoly on the escalation ladder.

The latest strikes came after Iranian state media claimed an American attack targeted Qeshm Island on Tuesday night. A local official said no infrastructure had been damaged there. The Wall Street Journal reported that U.S. forces have quietly carried out a series of strikes on Qeshm Island in recent weeks. The island is Iran's largest, and the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps has used it to launch drones and missiles. The U.S. military's Central Command did not respond to a request for comment.

President Trump has downplayed the strikes. His administration has been struggling to contain the fighting since the U.S. midterm elections. Vice President JD Vance had been considering face-to-face talks with Iran, but Trump halted those efforts last week, writing online that “we don’t need a deal with Iran.”
