## UK sales rose 0.5% as customers scaled back furniture purchases

Swedish flatpack furniture retailer Ikea has abandoned some planned UK store openings, citing the cost of business taxes and complexity within the market. Its UK sales edged up 0.5% to £2.26bn in the year to the end of August, but weak confidence and sustained pressure on household budgets led customers to pull back on large purchases.

UK boss David McCabe said some planned investments would instead go to other European countries. “There are locations where we were planning to open a new unit, and those plans have been abandoned due to the cost that we experience and the complexity within the market.” “I would hope that we’re able to bring those projects back to life,” he added. “But it’s a very real impact here in the UK, when those investments will go to other countries in Europe.”

The abandoned projects were for smaller UK stores, and Ikea declined to comment on the exact locations. The retailer has been pushing further into smaller high-street shops rather than its traditional out-of-town outlets.

McCabe said Ikea's UK arm pays the most business taxes among its operations in Europe and the third-highest in the world, after the US and China. The retailer said UK business taxes were the highest in Europe.

Ikea UK's sales rose 4.7% in the first four months of the year before falling in the second half. The retailer said weak confidence and sustained pressure on household budgets led consumers to pull back on big purchases.

McCabe said he had spoken with customers in stores in recent weeks who planned to replace an entire bedroom but instead bought one or two smaller pieces of furniture and refreshed textiles to save money. “We are seeing a change in the way that customers shop and we do anticipate a difficult period going forward,” he said. “We don’t think that there’s going to be a sudden, miraculous turnaround in confidence.”

McCabe described John Healey’s October budget as a “missed opportunity to back retailers under real pressure,” but said Ikea continued to talk with the government.

The company has spent £54m reducing prices by an average of 20% on 425 products. The products include the popular Billy bookcase, the Hemnes daybed and the cantilever Poäng armchair.

Ikea is also using seasonal offers to attract customers, including discounts on extendable dining tables, folding chairs and soft toys in the run-up to Christmas.

McCabe said smaller stores, between 1,000 and 2,000 sq metres, had been popular with customers at locations such as Oxford Street in London and retail parks.

The Oxford Street shop opened within the former Topshop flagship site. Ikea opened stores in Brighton, Norwich and York last year, followed by a small store in Chester. It also opened a “plan and order” site dedicated to kitchen, bedroom and living room planning in Dundee, and another in Aberdeen last month.

Ikea has abandoned some planned openings, continued to expand smaller shops and reduced prices on 425 products. McCabe described the period ahead as difficult and said he did not anticipate a sudden recovery in customer confidence.
