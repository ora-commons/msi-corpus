## White House says remarks will focus on families and commutations

WASHINGTON — Republican President Donald Trump is scheduled to deliver a Justice Department address on Monday about California Gov. Gavin Newsom's decision to commute more than 500 death sentences to life without parole. The Associated Press reported that Trump was set to criticize the move. The White House said the 4:30 p.m. EST speech in the building's ceremonial Great Hall would focus on protecting American families and Newsom's clemency decision.

The report said it was unclear how much of the address would also function as a forum for Trump to again encourage the prosecution of his political opponents or revisit years-old grievances about a Justice Department that had repeatedly investigated him over the past decade.

Newsom, a Democratic governor who frequently trades barbs with Trump and is considering a run for president, said earlier this month that he would change more than 500 death sentences in California to life without parole. He called the decision among the most difficult choices of his career because it would surface painful memories for crime victims' families.

Trump will be joined by Attorney General Todd Blanche, his former personal defense lawyer, who has said the president is entitled to exert his will on the Justice Department. Blanche was elevated from deputy attorney general to the top job in April after Trump ousted Pam Bondi, who, according to the Associated Press, struggled to satisfy the president's demands for retribution and endured bipartisan criticism over the handling of the Jeffrey Epstein files.

The speech comes after sweeping Republican losses in this month's midterm elections gave Democrats control of Congress. The outcome sets up possible House and Senate investigations into the mass firings of agents and prosecutors who investigated Trump and into a series of what the Associated Press described as politically charged investigations by the Trump administration's Justice Department that have been rejected by grand juries and judges.

A banner of Trump's face hangs outside the building, and the Associated Press described the display as embodying his sway over the department. Critics said the banner signaled the erosion of the institution's historic independence. Presidential addresses at the Justice Department are rare but not unheard of, and Trump's scheduled address will be his second there in less than two years.

Trump last visited in March 2025 for a speech meant to rally support for his administration's policies on violent crime, drugs and illegal immigration. He used the occasion instead to air complaints about criminal cases he faced during the administration of Democratic President Joe Biden.

Those cases included charges that Trump hoarded classified documents at his Mar-a-Lago estate in Palm Beach, Florida, and conspired to overturn his 2020 election loss to Biden. Trump vowed retribution for what he described as the "lies and abuses that have occurred within these walls" and pledged to expose the "egregious crimes and severe misconduct" of his foes.

Since that speech, the Justice Department has accelerated efforts to investigate Trump's adversaries, according to the Associated Press. The wire service has also described the agency as having "moved aggressively" to fulfill the White House's agenda. Those efforts have encountered repeated setbacks: judges have thrown out cases, and grand juries have refused to issue indictments.


Newsom's commutation remains the action at issue, and Trump's scheduled remarks would mark a federal response to it. [MSI previously reported that Newsom announced a universal clemency for California's death row](/articles/2026-11-06-newsom-empties-california-death-row-with-universal-clemency/).
