## Some Zelle payments may be delayed at other banks while the network stays operational

Thousands of customers reported difficulty accessing online accounts at several major U.S. banks on Monday after a technical issue at JPMorgan Chase caused ripple effects throughout the financial system.

Chase said the technical problems were not the result of a cyberattack and that the bank was working to bring banking services back online. "Due to a technical issue, some transactions are taking longer than usual to complete but customers still have access to their accounts and funds," a Chase spokesperson said in a statement. "We are working to resolve this as quickly as possible."

Some Chase customers received a message that read: "Some services aren't available. We're working on them. Please try back later."

The technical issues appeared to be centered on Chase customers and on customers at other banks trying to transact with Chase. Other banks, including Bank of America and Citigroup, were not having issues with their banking services on Monday.

A spokesman for U.S. Bank said its systems are not directly impacted but that "clients attempting to send or receive Zelle payments with another financial institution may experience transaction delays."

Zelle is the peer-to-peer payment system that major U.S. banks use to let customers send money to friends and family at near-instant speed. Zelle is owned by Arizona-based Early Warning Services, which in turn is owned by financial institutions including Bank of America, Capital One, JPMorgan Chase, PNC Bank, Truist, U.S. Bank and Wells Fargo.

A spokesman for Early Warning Services said the Zelle network was fully operational. "An issue at the financial institutions may be delaying Zelle payments for some of their customers," the spokesman said.

The banking disruption came days after two major outages affecting other parts of the economy. Microsoft had an outage Friday that lasted for hours, severely impacting services including Outlook, Teams and AI assistant Copilot. That was followed by an outage with the FAA, which grounded all U.S. flights for several hours over the weekend.
