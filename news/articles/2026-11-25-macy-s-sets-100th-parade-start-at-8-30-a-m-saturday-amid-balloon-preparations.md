## Macy’s says its team is working through “complex operational logistics”

Macy’s said the 100th annual Thanksgiving Day Parade will kick off at 8:30 a.m. Saturday. The Saturday start followed the event’s postponement from Thursday, which organizers announced Wednesday because of forecast extreme weather and concerns about participant and spectator safety. When the postponement was announced, the parade’s iconic balloons were well on the way to being inflated, and Macy’s representatives had repeatedly put off interviews with media on-site.

Macy’s said: “Our team is now working through the complex operational logistics required to bring the Parade to life on Saturday, and we will share additional details as they are finalized.” The company also said: “We are incredibly grateful to the thousands of volunteers, performers and partners who make this extraordinary tradition possible. We can’t wait to celebrate 100 years of Parade magic together on Saturday.”

NBC, which airs the parade, will broadcast a rerun of the 2025 edition Thursday in place of the 2026 event. NBC relayed a statement that cited conversations with the city and concern about the safety of participants and spectators: “Due to the extreme weather conditions, the city of New York, Macy’s and NBC have decided, in the interest of safety for both the participants and spectators, to move the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade to Saturday.”

The parade originally was scheduled to kick off at 8:30 a.m. Thursday. City emergency management officials expected New York City to get wind gusts between 50 and 60 mph (80 to 96 kph) on Thursday, along with steady, sustained winds between 30 and 38 mph (48 to 61 kph). Moderate to heavy rain was also expected.

Separately, the National Weather Service issued a high wind warning and a coastal flood warning for Thursday into Friday.

New York City has long had measures in place that dictate whether to lower the parade balloons or cancel their flights entirely if the wind gets too strong, because strong winds can knock the inflatables off course and into spectators along the parade route.

The city’s balloon rules were put in place after high winds sent a Cat in the Hat balloon careening into a metal pole during the 1997 parade, leaving a woman in a coma.

Bad weather in the past has caused balloons to be flown a bit lower than usual. Each inflatable character has a distinct risk profile that includes its size and weight and optimal altitudes for differing wind speeds.
