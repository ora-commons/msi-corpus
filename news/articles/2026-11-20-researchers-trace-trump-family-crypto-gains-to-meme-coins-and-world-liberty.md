## Democrats vow oversight hearings after the midterms

President Trump and his family made more than $2 billion from cryptocurrency ventures during his second term, according to researchers at the Center for American Progress. The researchers put their total second-term earnings so far at nearly $3 billion. Democrats have promised oversight hearings after the midterms, with planned scrutiny extending to the $TRUMP meme coin, World Liberty Financial and a stablecoin transaction involving Binance.

Will Ragland and his team at the Washington, D.C.-based Center for American Progress spent the last year counting how much money Trump and his family made. Ragland said, “You could probably combine all of the past presidents' income made during their time in office. And it wouldn't come close to what we're seeing here.” The team also tallied more than $1.7 billion in earnings linked to World Liberty Financial and its stablecoin business. World Liberty Financial declined to comment.

Shortly before his inauguration in early 2025, President Trump and now-First Lady Melania Trump each launched their own meme coin. Trump made over $600 million from $TRUMP, according to his own disclosures. In an effort to drum up demand for the coin, Trump held two galas for its top holders, promising the closest access to himself to those who invested the most. A third gala was scheduled for Sunday at his golf course in Virginia.

Many guests at the first event last year were likely to have been foreign, according to media reports. The second dinner, held this year, attracted considerably less interest, though participants also described a significant presence of people from abroad. For Ragland and other anti-corruption activists, the dinners raised concerns about potential conflicts of interest. Ragland said, “This is a way for foreign investors, foreign countries in some instances, to try and get as much influence as they can and use that as leverage for deals that they want to make that are helpful to them.”

The biggest source of Trump's reported wealth over his second term was World Liberty Financial, which his family helped launch before he won the 2024 presidential election. The family started the venture late in 2024 with Steve Witkoff, whom Trump would later appoint as U.S. envoy for the Middle East, and Steve's son Zack Witkoff, now the company's CEO. The venture revolves around a type of cryptocurrency called a stablecoin.

Just days before Trump's inauguration, World Liberty received a $500 million investment in exchange for 49% of the company. The buyer was a firm linked to Sheikh Tahnoon bin Zayed Al Nahyan, a member of the United Arab Emirates royal family and the kingdom's national security adviser; The Wall Street Journal first reported the deal. At the time, World Liberty was a young company seeking a foothold in the crypto sector.

Months later, NPR wrote: “The U.A.E. earned a substantial concession: The U.S. eased export restrictions, allowing the shipment of about 500,000 of the most advanced American AI chips to the kingdom.” NPR added: “The shipment was critical in the U.A.E's ambition to build big AI data clusters and become an AI power.” Democrats seized on the timing, but Trump denied any quid pro quo.

In a recent CBS' 60 Minutes interview, Trump said he does not keep track of how much money he makes from his business ventures. He told 60 Minutes correspondent Ross Douthat, “I devote almost no time to my net worth. I don't care. I make – I have a lotta money, and far more than I ever thought I'd have. And I guess it's gone up. I don't even know.”

NPR described another major World Liberty transaction as helping boost the cryptocurrency's profile and the Trump family's profits. Using $2 billion of World Liberty Financial's stablecoin, a separate firm, also led by Sheikh Tahnoon, bought a small stake in the Binance exchange last year. NPR wrote: “The deal instantly elevated World Liberty Financial's stablecoin into one of the biggest in the market.”

Months later, President Trump pardoned Changpeng Zhao, the convicted founder of Binance who no longer runs the exchange. Zhao, widely known as CZ, still has Binance's biggest stake and weighs in publicly, including when the exchange was hacked earlier this month.

The relationship between Binance and World Liberty then deepened. Binance actively promoted the World Liberty stablecoin to its customers, further elevating the cryptocurrency's market valuation to $5 billion, according to The New York Times. Binance strongly defended the acceptance of World Liberty's cryptocurrency as having been done on its own merits. A spokesperson told NPR in an email, “Any suggestion that Binance did anything to support World Liberty Financial or USD1 to gain political favour or influence a legal outcome is false.” A representative for Zhao declined to comment. Transparency advocates and top Democrats remained suspicious, and Rep. Robert Garcia posted the social-media message “Lawyer up.” amid the scrutiny.

Trump campaigned heavily on an anti-corruption message that resonated with voters. After the midterms, Democrats promised to make accountability for Trump and his administration a critical oversight priority when they take over both the Senate and the House next year. Sen. Elizabeth Warren of Massachusetts and Rep. Robert Garcia of California were among prominent Democrats vowing oversight hearings and accountability.

Garcia warned Trump, his family and his administration in a letter after this month's midterms to preserve records. He said, “Americans should be secure in the knowledge that their government is focused on their best interests—not on making the President's family richer.”

Top Democrats, including Rep. Jamie Raskin of Maryland, launched investigations, and NPR reported the galas were likely to become an aspect of congressional oversight into Trump's crypto ventures next year. When contacted by NPR about the Democrats' oversight plans, a White House spokesperson warned against pursuing “sham investigations.” Lauren Bis said in an emailed statement, “As the President has said, he's willing to work with anyone to continue delivering victories for hardworking Americans,” and warned that “If Democrats choose to pursue sham investigations and radical left policies, they are choosing to work against the American people.”
