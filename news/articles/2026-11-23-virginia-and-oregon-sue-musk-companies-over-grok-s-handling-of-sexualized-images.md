## Virginia and Oregon sue Musk companies over Grok's handling of sexualized images

## Virginia seeks $2,500 per willful violation and surrender of 'ill-gotten gains'

Virginia and Oregon filed separate lawsuits Monday against several of Elon Musk's companies and subsidiaries, including SpaceX and X Corp., alleging that the design and launch of the Grok chatbot exposed consumers to harmful sexualized imagery. Virginia's complaint, filed in state court in Richmond, says the companies stripped out industry-standard safeguards and marketed a “spicy mode” built to produce nudity. Representatives for the companies had not responded to an emailed request for comment.

Virginia's complaint alleges that those choices led to a proliferation of child sexual abuse material and other nonconsensual intimate imagery on the companies' platforms. Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones said in the state's lawsuit that the companies prioritized their business model over the safety of millions of people.

The Virginia lawsuit quotes Jones: “To push its lagging product, Grok, to tens of millions of new users, it stripped out industry-standard safeguards and marketed a ‘spicy mode’ built to produce nudity,” Jones said in the lawsuit. “For these Defendants, weak safeguards were not a failure but a feature.”

Grok, accessible through Musk's social media platform X, rolled out image generation and editing features to all users in December 2025, according to the Virginia complaint. By January, the tool had drawn a global backlash after users were able to create realistic-looking but fake sexualized images of people. Researchers warned that some of the images appeared to depict children, and the Virginia lawsuit says, “X was flooded with sexualized content — primarily of women.”

At least two countries, Malaysia and Indonesia, announced temporary bans, and other nations said they were launching or widening investigations. In response, Grok prevented non-paying users from generating or editing images. Virginia's lawsuit said the change did not stop users from making more nonconsensual sexualized images and claimed it “simply made that generation profitable.” The complaint also said users could create such imagery through a standalone app and website.

Virginia argued that most major AI developers designed their products so they could not be used to produce sexualized images, a measure intended to avoid generating nonconsensual content. The state's complaint said the defendants' published system-prompt materials told Grok to ‘assume good intent,’ stated that terms such as ‘teenage’ or ‘girl’ do not necessarily imply underage, and imposed minimal restrictions on sexual content.

The Virginia lawsuit also said xAI marketed Grok from the outset as a tool that would “answer spicy questions that are rejected by most other AI systems.” The developer of Grok was then known as xAI and has since rebranded to SpaceX Super Intelligence.

Virginia's lawsuit alleges X made “false, deceptive or misleading” statements in January about changes intended to prevent Grok from allowing users to edit images of real people in revealing clothes. It also alleges that users openly traded “jailbreak” prompts to get around Grok's remaining limits and that Musk's companies failed to promptly address the exploits.

Virginia's suit argues that the defendants violated the state consumer-protection law by “misrepresenting that they implemented and enforced safeguards to protect consumers from exposure” to harmful sexualized imagery on their platforms. The state asks for $2,500 for each “willful violation” of the Virginia Consumer Protection Act, with the exact number to be proven at trial. It also asks the court to prevent further violations and force the defendants to surrender any “ill-gotten gains” connected to the alleged violations. Oregon's lawsuit makes a similar request.

The complaints come as concern grows about AI's risks for children, including its impact on brain development and unhealthy relationships many young people have formed with chatbots. Both lawsuits draw heavily on news outlets' investigative reporting about the rollout of Grok, the backlash and the companies' response.

“When xAI allowed Grok’s creators to sideline consent to turn a profit, they prioritized growth and engagement over safety, and they deceived the public about it. Today’s filings are the first step towards holding this company accountable,” Jones said in a statement.
