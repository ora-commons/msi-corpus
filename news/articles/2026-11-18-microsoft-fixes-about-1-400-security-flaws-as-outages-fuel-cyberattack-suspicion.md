## White House response draws criticism after FAA ground stop

The disruptions started last Thursday with a global Microsoft outage that hit nearly half a billion people, according to The Wall Street Journal. On Friday, the FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop. On Monday, banking suffered an outage. Later, the tools United Airlines uses to communicate with pilots went down. On Tuesday, the National Security Agency's internal network went down, and Apple also suffered an outage.

The companies involved blamed botched system upgrades, not hackers, though AI may still have been involved, The Wall Street Journal said. Robert McMillan of the outlet likened the situation to a pressure cooker. He said AI tools are finding software flaws faster than ever, forcing companies to push out fixes at a record pace, and every patch can break something.

Security experts said AI is largely behind this year's record wave of upgrades in North America. Last month, Microsoft's monthly software update fixed about 1,400 security flaws, a record; a year earlier, it fixed about 170. The Wall Street Journal said that at that pace, IT teams cannot install every fix by hand. Companies are leaning more on automation, which can mean new processes and new ways for an update to go wrong.

Attackers are using AI, too, The Wall Street Journal said. It described AI-driven cyberattacks as relentless and fueled in part by serious bugs uncovered in software used across corporate America.

Anne Neuberger, a general partner with the venture-capital firm Andreessen Horowitz and former White House cybersecurity adviser, said: “We have two sets of things. We have systems like the FAA systems, which are known to be ancient and really need an upgrade…In addition to that, we have this whole set of AI attackers that are able to find vulnerabilities in code.”

At a White House news conference Friday night, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin tried to explain the nationwide FAA ground stop. They blamed the country's outdated air-traffic control system but stopped short of pinpointing the cause, The Wall Street Journal reported. Mullin said, “There’s nothing completely certain right now.”

The Wall Street Journal said the response was not helped when Mullin flew the next day for a college-football game while many travelers were still dealing with the disruption. Brian Harrell, who oversaw infrastructure protection at the Department of Homeland Security during President Trump's first term, called the answers “bumbling” and “insufficient.” He said, “The current media strategy is not working and creating less confidence.”

In the absence of an explanation, and amid a flurry of more recent outages, social media filled the gap. Thousands of TikTok videos tagged “cyberattack” drew more than 14 million views in the past week, according to the analytics firm Exolyt.

The Wall Street Journal said companies cannot stop AI from finding bugs or guarantee every patch will work smoothly. Leaders can control how quickly they explain disruptions before a vacuum in information fills with fear.

The same report also examined Nvidia's first investor call under interim chief executive Colette Kress, answering multiple questions hanging over the company and its markets. After more than a year of debate about the strength of AI demand, Nvidia's results showed that demand remained robust and gaining momentum, the outlet said.

The Wall Street Journal called it remarkable that growth was accelerating at such a pace nearly four years after ChatGPT's launch transformed AI and ignited its adoption. The outlet said the acceleration reflected large companies' ongoing commitment to AI investment, often led directly by the chief executive.

The report said some studies suggest that only a minority of non-tech companies are gaining significant leverage from AI. That minority includes large enterprises with an outsize impact on the economy.

The Wall Street Journal assessed that the call cemented Kress's role as interim CEO and that the market accepted her leadership. It said Kress could share credit for the results given her long record as CFO. The outlet noted that Nvidia's shares began to rise during the call in after-hours trading and said the moment felt like the end of former CEO Jensen Huang's era.
