## US intends prosecution under military justice once UK charges are filed

A U.S. Army officer arrested in Britain on suspicion of spying for Russia handed over no information to Moscow, a U.S. official said Thursday, and British charges against him are expected on Sunday or Monday, the official said. Once those charges are filed, the official said, the intention is to prosecute him under the U.S. military justice system.

"No information made it into Russian hands," the official said of Lt. Col. Brandon Tyner, whose name was confirmed by a second U.S. official. Both spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss sensitive military matters.

Tyner was arrested Tuesday on suspicion of assisting a foreign intelligence service and obtaining or disclosing protected information.

The Metropolitan Police announced the arrest this week and said its counterterror forces worked closely with the FBI and the U.S. military.

Tyner was serving as a senior human resources officer with NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps at Imjin Barracks near Gloucester, England. He had been previously deployed to Afghanistan, Iraq and Qatar, the second official said.

The Allied Rapid Reaction Corps is a multinational land unit designed to respond quickly to crises or short-notice operations. According to the unit's website, it is made up of forces from 20 nations but is led by the British.

Police said the arrest was not related to other counterterrorism investigations, indicating no connection with a suspected terror plot linked to Iran in September at RAF Fairford, a U.S.-run base near Gloucester.

British charges are expected within days, after which the case is intended to move into the U.S. military justice system, the official said.
