## Democrats vow greater scrutiny of technology companies and data centres

Democrats won control of the US Senate and House in the midterm elections, with Texas, Ohio and other states swinging away from President Donald Trump. Trump has faced pressure over the impact his policies have had on the cost of living.

One of the most striking midterm results came in Texas, where Democrat James Talarico defeated Republican Ken Paxton for a US Senate seat. According to BBC analysis, Elon Musk had spent at least $45m in support of Paxton.

Democrats have vowed greater scrutiny of technology companies, including over the rollout of controversial data centres. Texas, where key operations for Musk's car company Tesla and rocket and technology firm SpaceX are based, was among the states that swung away from Trump.

Elon Musk, the Tesla and SpaceX chief executive, was the campaign's top individual donor. BBC reported that Musk gave $446m (£339m) to support Trump's party, particularly in Texas, where many of his firms are based. Musk had also backed Trump's second White House bid.

Musk spoke virtually at the New York Times DealBook Summit, an annual conference attended by major business and political leaders. He discussed the spending, the midterm results and the Maga movement, the right-wing Make America Great Again movement associated with Trump.

"I'm not Maga," Musk said. He added: "I'm not sure what Maga means... if that means sort of blind adherence to a set of policies by the Republican Party, then I certainly don't have that."

On Wednesday, Musk said he felt "pretty foolish" about the record sum he spent on the US midterm elections following sweeping Republican defeats. He said he did not see much for the money he had spent on the Republicans' campaign, though he added that it was likely candidates would have fared worse without the spending.
