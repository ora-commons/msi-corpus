## Eyes Up let users share videos of ICE activity before Apple removed it

A federal judge last week permanently barred the Department of Justice and the Department of Homeland Security from formally or informally pressuring Apple to suppress Eyes Up, the mobile application for monitoring U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement activity.

U.S. District Court Judge Jorge L. Alonso granted the permanent injunction in favor of Mark Hodges, a Brown County resident whose application was removed by Apple last year after pressure from the two agencies.

Hodges founded Eyes Up, which allows users to share videos of ICE activities. The app is one of at least two platforms targeted by federal authorities who pressured technology companies to suppress sites tracking immigration raids at the start of President Donald Trump's second term.

Alonso granted a joint motion filed by attorneys for Hodges and co-plaintiff Kate Rosado together with attorneys for the Department of Justice and the Department of Homeland Security — the two sides jointly asking the court to enter the injunction.

The Foundation for Individual Rights and Expression, a First Amendment law firm, filed the lawsuit on behalf of Hodges and Rosado, a Chicago activist who started a Facebook group to document ICE sightings in Chicago and whose Facebook group was also targeted.

Hodges responded to the order in a news release Friday.

"I'm glad the government agreed to stop targeting my speech, but it shouldn't take a lawsuit to get the government to respect our constitutional rights," Hodges said. "People have a right to know what their government is doing. I hope that thanks to this lawsuit, others can share what they see and hear without facing this kind of censorship."
