## More than 2,500 flights were delayed as U.S. disruptions continued into Friday

BBC News reported that severe weather struck both coasts of the United States during Thanksgiving, leaving hundreds of thousands of people without power on Friday. A powerful storm brought high winds, heavy rain and coastal flooding to eastern areas on Thursday, while the West Coast also faced storms. The National Weather Service said conditions would improve but cautioned that the change would take some time.

BBC News video footage showed roofs torn off and streets under water as the severe weather hit the US.

Poweroutage.us said Pennsylvania remained the state worst hit by power cuts early Friday. The number of homes reporting power cuts peaked at close to a million on Thursday evening, with Pennsylvania, Virginia, Maryland and New Jersey badly affected. Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro said utility providers were working tirelessly to restore power.

In Maryland, authorities asked almost two million customers to limit water consumption to essential use only because of “intermittent power disruptions.” Emergency declarations covered parts of several eastern states, including New York.

President Donald Trump said he would consider releasing emergency funds as the storm's effects spread across the East Coast. Severe winds closed bridges in Maryland, while waves battered Delaware beaches as water seeped onto the boardwalk. Thursday was the wettest Thanksgiving on record in Washington DC, when tidal waters surged and soaked runways at Reagan Washington National Airport.

Organizers of Macy's annual Thanksgiving parade in New York City postponed the event to Saturday.

Flight disruption persisted into Friday, with hundreds more delays and cancellations reported on a day expected to be another busy travel day. Flightaware.com reported that more than 2,500 flights within, into or out of the United States were delayed on Thursday and more than 450 were canceled.

One passenger told the BBC that he had been stuck at New York's LaGuardia Airport for 13 hours while trying to fly to see friends in Chicago for Thanksgiving.

The American Automobile Association predicted that 81.5 million Americans would travel at least 50 miles (80km) from home over the seven-day Thanksgiving holiday period.

The concern on the West Coast was exceptionally high tides and the associated risk of flooding. San Diego and Los Angeles reported a high tide of more than 8 feet (2.4 meters) earlier in the week, and beachgoers were advised to avoid coastal areas.

Amtrak said on social media that it canceled several trains on the Pacific Surfliner because of “washout conditions from high tides.” The route runs along the California coast. Police in Oregon said a man and a dog escaped unharmed after footage showed them swept into the sea by a fierce, so-called king tide.

In Miami, video footage showed dirt flying as a FedEx plane overshot the runway the night before, and some travel delays persisted into Thursday. The incident prompted the closure of all but one runway at the airport for a brief period. FedEx said one of its flights had “experienced an issue upon landing.” No injuries were reported, and the Federal Aviation Administration was investigating what happened. An airport employee who spoke anonymously to CBS News, the BBC's US partner, raised the possibility that rainy conditions had been a factor.


In earlier coverage, [MSI reported that the National Weather Service expected the eastern storm to affect airports through Friday morning](/articles/2026-11-26-thanksgiving-storms-disrupt-u-s-travel-and-tear-down-part-of-a-pacifica-pier/). Friday's continuing flight delays and cancellations showed that the expected airport disruption persisted into the next day, while the number of people without power had reached hundreds of thousands.
