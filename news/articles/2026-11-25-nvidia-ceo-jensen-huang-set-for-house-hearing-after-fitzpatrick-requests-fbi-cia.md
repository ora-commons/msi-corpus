## Anonymous source reports no spy evidence but believes Alice Huang passed information

Republican Rep. Brian Fitzpatrick has asked the FBI and CIA to brief House lawmakers before Nvidia chief Jensen Huang appears next week. A former FBI special agent who sits on the House Intelligence Committee, Fitzpatrick told NPR's Morning Edition on Wednesday that federal authorities must explain what they knew about the affair involving Alice Huang and whether they warned the Nvidia chief of potential espionage threats. NPR obtained information about a closed-door briefing after the interview.

The new information came from a person familiar with the matter who was granted anonymity to speak about a closed-door briefing. The person told NPR's Ryan Lucas on condition of anonymity because of the sensitive nature of the matter. U.S. intelligence officials told congressional staff there is no evidence that Alice Huang, the woman who had an affair with the Nvidia chief, was a Chinese spy or intelligence asset.

The source also said there is no evidence Alice Huang initiated the affair to collect intelligence for China's government. The source said, however, that Alice Huang is believed to have passed along information after the affair began. The source did not specify what information she was believed to have passed along.

Fitzpatrick said the inquiry begins with what federal authorities knew and whether they warned Huang of potential espionage threats. “What briefings did they give? When did they give them?” he asked. He said Huang's reinstatement should not put those questions to rest. “The public is rightly concerned, and they deserve answers,” he said.

Fitzpatrick also wants to hear from Attorney General Todd Blanche, who on Tuesday described Nvidia as a good corporate citizen, and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, whose department faces questions over Nvidia's chip exports to China. “Anytime you do an investigation, you follow the facts wherever they lead, and you report those facts with unimpeachable integrity,” Fitzpatrick said. “You leave no stone unturned.”

The oversight request follows Nvidia's decision to bring Huang back to the helm with President Trump's backing, while questions surrounding the AI chipmaker's ties to China remain unsettled. Trump has said he helped arrange Huang's return as CEO, and Nvidia's board maintains that the reinstatement presents no national security risk. NPR reported that some Republican lawmakers want to understand how those conclusions were reached.

The debate over Huang's reinstatement extends beyond Washington, NPR reported. In Silicon Valley, Huang's return “has alarmed women and advocates who have spent years pushing for a better workplace culture, renewing questions about the limits of executive accountability.”

Huang remains scheduled to appear before House lawmakers next week, and Fitzpatrick has asked that the FBI and CIA go first. Ahead of that appearance, the unresolved questions include what federal authorities knew, whether they warned Huang about potential espionage threats and when briefings occurred, along with the content of information Alice Huang was believed to have passed along after the affair began.
