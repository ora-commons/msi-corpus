## Vanuatu says the $500mn agreement bars foreign military bases

Coalition members of a parliamentary committee scrutinising treaties said they did not support ratifying the $500mn Nakamal agreement with Vanuatu in its current form. Their objection centers on clauses they said create binding conditions for renewable energy and climate targets and limit future governments’ ability to change climate and energy policy settings.

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese and his Vanuatu counterpart, Jotham Napat, signed the agreement in Canberra in June.

Under the $500mn deal, Vanuatu stated it would “not permit its territory to be used for any foreign military base or infrastructure.”

The Vanuatu agreement forms part of the Albanese government’s wider effort to build Pacific alliances that deny China a military foothold. The government has forged a number of agreements, including new alliances with Fiji and Papua New Guinea.

The committee’s dissenting comments were released last night. In them, Coalition members said they recognized “the legitimate concerns Vanuatu and other Pacific Island nations have regarding climate change and its impacts on their communities, economies, and national security.”

They nevertheless objected to provisions that could restrict future governments’ policy choices. The members said the clauses limit the ability of future governments to change current climate and energy policy settings by creating binding conditions tied to the achievement of renewable energy and climate targets.

The published objections focused on future climate and energy policy rather than the agreement’s restriction on foreign military basing.

A government spokesperson said the Coalition was “putting culture wars ahead of regional security. It is remarkable that the Liberals and Nationals continue to disrespect Pacific priorities.”
