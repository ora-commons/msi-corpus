## Key House lawmakers said Huang had voluntarily agreed to transcribed interviews

Key House lawmakers said on Tuesday that Jensen Huang had voluntarily agreed to appear for transcribed interviews before congressional committees. Nvidia faces intensifying national scrutiny over sales of its advanced computer chips.

Huang is scheduled to appear before congressional committees on Dec. 1. Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter is scheduled to appear before the committees on Dec. 2.

The California Department of Justice said Thursday that California Attorney General Rob Bonta had issued an investigative subpoena to Nvidia. The subpoena is related to former CEO Jensen Huang's affair with a suspected Chinese spy.

The department said it is asking whether Nvidia's advanced chips or confidential information may have gone to China. It is also asking whether Nvidia misled investors and the public.

In a statement, Bonta said: “The conduct of Nvidia and its former CEO raises bright red flags, including about the ways in which the company may have misled investors.” He said his office is investigating whether Nvidia or Jensen Huang’s actions resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information and if Nvidia misled investors and the public about that risk, in violation of law.

Bonta also said: “Nvidia is in a messy, transnational situation but the underlying principle of our corporate securities law is clear: Companies cannot mislead their investors.”

Nvidia did not immediately respond to a message for comment Thursday.
