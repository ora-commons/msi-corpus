## ERCOT plans to report community-impact findings to the PUC on Dec. 10

The Texas Commission on Environmental Quality (TCEQ) has resumed its normal review of environmental permits for data centers, ending Gov. Greg Abbott’s directive to pause approvals tied to the projects. TCEQ received notice from Abbott’s office on Friday that officials at the state’s electric grid had completed gathering information on potential community impacts.

TCEQ spokesperson Laura Lopez said in a written statement, “The agency has resumed its normal permit review process and will evaluate applications under all applicable state and federal laws and regulations.”

In August, Abbott called on the Electric Reliability Council of Texas (ERCOT) and electricity regulators to audit all data centers trying to connect to the grid. The directive also sought information about tax incentives, power use, water use and potential neighborhood effects, and Abbott paused approval of grid interconnections while the audit was completed.

In a Sept. 21 letter to TCEQ calling for the environmental permitting pause, Abbott said the agency needed to wait until ERCOT “completes its review.” TCEQ handles permits related to air pollution at facilities and stormwater runoff at construction sites.

TCEQ faced criticism in October from Public Citizen and FracTracker Alliance after the groups noticed that some data center-related construction permits were being cleared anyway, counter to Abbott’s order. TCEQ said it suspended the permits, shut down its entire automated electronic filing system and required physical applications to be submitted for staff review.

Public Citizen responded to the resumption by calling on TCEQ commissioners to resign. Kathryn Guerra, the TCEQ watchdog campaign director for Public Citizen, said, “The TCEQ is asserting that ERCOT has concluded its assessment of the community impacts of data centers, and without sharing those results with communities or detailing how the TCEQ will incorporate the findings into permitting decisions, agency leadership has begun unilaterally issuing data center permits.”

Sam Law, a researcher at FracTracker Alliance, said in a written statement shared by Public Citizen, “Texans are tired of this political theatre.” Law added, “We need real leadership, transparency, and a true moratorium on data center development.”

Public opposition had grown across Texas during the year. Democrats and Republicans alike fought projects they worried would destabilize the electric grid, lead to more pollution from new gas-fueled power generators and use large amounts of water in a state with serious drought concerns.

On Nov. 3, Abbott lost his reelection to Democrat Gina Hinojosa, who had campaigned on keeping data center approvals paused until legislators pass new state laws. Neither Abbott’s office nor Hinojosa’s media team immediately responded to a Tuesday request for comment.

At a Nov. 5 update to the Public Utility Commission of Texas (PUC), ERCOT reported receiving responses from 93% of 465 projects. The grid operator said the responses provided enough information for “a statistically meaningful analysis for the final report.”

ERCOT officials plan to report their full findings to the PUC on Dec. 10 and discuss them at the PUC’s next open meeting on Dec. 17.
