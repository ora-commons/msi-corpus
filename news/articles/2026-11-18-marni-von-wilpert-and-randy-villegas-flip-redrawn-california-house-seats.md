## Republicans nationally failed to pick up nearly all the seats they redrew

Marni von Wilpert defeated Trump-endorsed Republican Jim Desmond in a Southern California House district redrawn to favor Democrats, while community college professor Randy Villegas ousted six-term Republican David Valadao in a redrawn Central Valley district. The Guardian reported that the wins formed part of five Democratic flips that will send 48 California representatives to Washington DC for the 120th Congress, leaving Republicans with four of the state's 52 seats.

Von Wilpert's campaign focused on workers' rights and affordability. She will represent California's 48th district, which had been held by Darrell Issa, a longtime Republican congressman who chose not to run for re-election after the maps were redrawn. Desmond had received Trump's endorsement.

The Guardian described Villegas as a progressive Democrat and community college professor. He shunned corporate campaign donations and received endorsements from national progressive leaders including Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez and Bernie Sanders. His victory came in a redrawn Central Valley district.

California voters overwhelmingly supported Proposition 50, which The Guardian described as a redistricting measure meant to counter Republicans' partisan gerrymandering in Texas. The outlet attributed California's result to a successful redistricting effort spearheaded by Democratic Gov. Gavin Newsom and to a broad blue wave across the country that displaced Republicans in districts that previously backed Donald Trump.

Across the country, Republicans' efforts to redraw congressional districts in their favor spectacularly backfired, The Guardian reported. The party failed to pick up nearly all the seats they redrew, the outlet said.

Republicans held on to one seat in the state's Central Valley, one in northern California near the Oregon border, one east of Los Angeles and one that includes parts of Orange County in the southern region. Before the midterms, the party held eight California congressional seats. The four-seat outcome leaves California Republicans with their smallest delegation to Washington in decades, The Guardian reported.

The last time California Republicans held only four House seats was in 1937, when the state had 20 total congressional representatives, according to CalMatters, a local news site. Democrats have long held the majority in California, which The Guardian described as the most populous state in the US, while conservative regions in the state have remained Republican strongholds.

The final California race called by the Associated Press went to Republican incumbent Jay Obernolte, who narrowly defeated Democratic opponent Tessa Lynn Hodge east of LA. Obernolte won 50.8% of the vote despite running in a Republican-majority district, The Guardian reported. The final call left California with 48 Democrats and four Republicans in the House.

California's House delegation will begin the 120th Congress with 48 Democrats and four Republicans. The outcome followed five Democratic flips, a successful redistricting effort and overwhelming voter support for Proposition 50, The Guardian reported.
