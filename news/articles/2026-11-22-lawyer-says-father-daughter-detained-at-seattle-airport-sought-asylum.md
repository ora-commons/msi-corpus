## ICE says father and daughter overstayed visas; passenger may face charges

A father and daughter were detained by masked immigration officers on the jet bridge at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport on Saturday as passengers boarded an Alaska Airlines flight to Los Angeles, a scene that fellow travelers recorded on their cellphones and that was widely viewed on social media. Their lawyer, Olia Catala, told The Associated Press in a telephone interview Sunday that the two were on their way to a baptism ceremony in California and are seeking asylum in the United States, where they have lived since 2015.

Catala said neither the father nor the daughter has a criminal record.

The recordings show the officers, wearing unmarked jackets and khaki pants, struggling with someone on the ground. The flight to Los Angeles was delayed.

Immigration and Customs Enforcement said in a statement that its officers detained Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza, an immigrant from Mexico who it said had overstayed his visa "in violation of our nation's laws."

According to the same statement, another passenger "hurled hot coffee on the officers" during the confrontation. ICE said that woman, Sofia Hernandez Mejia, was determined to be in the U.S. illegally from Mexico and to have also overstayed her visa, and that she could be charged with assaulting a federal officer.

Catala said Mejia, who is in her mid-20s, had married a U.S. citizen and filed paperwork to adjust her status. Mejia's husband was accompanying Mejia and her father when they were detained Saturday and was not taken into custody, the lawyer said. Catala said both of the detainees were being held at the Northwest ICE Processing Center in Tacoma.

The lawyer said Mejia was carrying a cup of coffee when the confrontation began.

"When somebody's charging at you, you're going to drop a cup of coffee," Catala said.

"It was just a lot of commotion, and honestly, if somebody is running at you or charging at you and they're not wearing a uniform, you don't know who they are," Catala said. "There was a lot of panic, chaos, not knowing what's going on."

Catala described the pair as people who had followed the rules. "They've done everything correctly, and these are educated people," she said. "She went to college, to university, she worked for a nonprofit. No criminal history (for) any of them. So it's just heartbreaking."

Alaska Airlines said the ICE officers gave no advance notice of the enforcement action, which came during a busy travel weekend ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday. The airline said it was working with the Port of Seattle, which manages the airport, to determine whether protocols were followed, and that it had raised its concerns "directly with the appropriate authorities."

The Port of Seattle said in a statement that the arrest was distressing not only for the affected family but for other passengers, and that local authorities would "engage federal officials to understand more about what occurred."

ICE has stepped up arrests at airports since the summer, especially targeting people it says have overstayed visas.

Washington Gov. Bob Ferguson, a Democrat, said ICE's tactics were "deeply un-American" and called for the agency's abolition. At a news conference Saturday with labor leaders and local elected officials, the governor described the footage of the detention.

"The video is horrific and it is chilling," Ferguson said.
