## Democratic lawmakers challenge Nvidia after Trump helps reinstate Jensen Huang

**Subtype:** fact

- Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive after President Trump intervened in the chipmaker's leadership decision.
- Sen. Ruben Gallego said Jensen Huang's reinstatement sent a message that national security was less important than Huang's relationship with Trump.
- Rep. Ro Khanna said rules should apply to everyone and that the rich and powerful should not evade export controls or accountability merely because doing so props up the stock price.

- Trump made up to $15m on his Nvidia stock holdings after the company's share price soared following Huang's surprise return.

## Government investigations and lawsuits continued after the return

Democratic lawmakers accused Nvidia of allowing national-security and export-control concerns to be set aside when the chipmaker reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive following President Trump's intervention. Sen. Ruben Gallego criticized the return's message that national security was less important than Huang's relationship with Trump, and warned that corporate America should not draw the lesson that evading national security was acceptable for selling more. Rep. Ro Khanna said the rich and powerful should not evade export controls or accountability because doing so props up the stock price.

Nvidia's board had ousted Huang after allegations that he had three affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese spy and two with employees. The board reinstated its co-founder after the presidential intervention. Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993, and his return was unexpected.

Democrat Sen. Ruben Gallego, an Arizona senator considering a White House campaign, said Nvidia's decision sent a message “that the national security of this country is not as important as your relationship with president Trump.” Gallego also said corporate America needed to avoid drawing the lesson that evading national security was acceptable for the purpose of selling more.

California Rep. Ro Khanna wrote on social media that “rules should apply to everyone,” noting that “the rich + powerful, like Jensen, do not get to evade export controls or accountability just because it props up the stock price.”

On Monday, Huang returned to Nvidia's headquarters in Santa Clara, California, and received a hero's welcome. Crowds of employees chanted “Jensen, Jensen, Jensen!” as the executive returned to the company he co-founded in 1993.

Trump made up to $15m on his holdings of Nvidia stock after the chipmaker's share price soared on Huang's surprise return.

At the time of the return, Huang remained subject to several government and internal investigations and faced shareholder lawsuits, while critics accused him of being a national security threat. The reinstatement settled who would lead Nvidia but did not end the investigations, lawsuits, export-control objections or personal-conduct allegations.

Nvidia's scale gives the dispute broad reach. The company is worth about $4tn and is one of the world's most valuable businesses. The Guardian said the entire AI ecosystem relies on Nvidia. Its engineers develop advanced chips used to train AI models and power technology companies including OpenAI, Anthropic and Google, making Nvidia's leadership and export-control questions relevant across the AI industry.
