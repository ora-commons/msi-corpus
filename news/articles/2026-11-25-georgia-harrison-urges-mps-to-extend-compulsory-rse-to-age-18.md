## Bill would require England’s further education providers to teach RSE to under-18s

Georgia Harrison urged MPs on 25 November 2026 to back a private members' bill that would require relationships and sex education for students under 18 in England. The Only Way Is Essex and Love Island star led a protest outside Parliament, calling for compulsory teaching to continue through age 18 rather than stop at 16.

Harrison said young people were being exposed to “poison” online and that misogynists had sold young people advice she considered harmful. “We need to undo it and the only way to undo it is through education,” she said.

The measure is the Relationships and Sex Education (Further Education Sector) Bill, put forward by Alistair Strathern, the Labour MP for the Hitchin constituency in Bedfordshire and Hertfordshire. It would legally require all further education providers in England to teach relationships and sex education to under-18s. A debate and vote are scheduled for next week.

Compulsory relationships and sex education currently stops at age 16 in England, meaning sixth forms and colleges are not required to teach the subject. Harrison said: “Between 16 and 18 is the age that people first start having sex. It doesn't make sense that we're abandoning them at that time.”

A Department for Education spokesperson said the department had rolled out mandatory relationships, sex and health education in schools and was “exploring how we can extend this to colleges too.” The spokesperson said supporting young people and safeguarding them from harm online and offline was “a job for us all.”

Harrison was a victim of so-called revenge porn. BBC News reported that fellow reality TV performer Stephen Bear was jailed for posting intimate footage of Harrison and Bear online, which Bear had filmed at his home in Loughton, Essex. Harrison said making RSE mandatory to age 18 could help prevent intimate image abuse of the kind she had experienced.

The protest was organised by LADbible. The bill follows a petition by the survivor-led campaign group Make it Mandatory, which called for sex education to be mandated to 18. Faustine Petron said she founded the campaign after surviving a coercive and abusive relationship when she was under 18.

Petron said she reflected on “the lack of education I had about the signs of abusive behaviour, grooming and coercive control.” She said she was “shocked in my research to find out that sex education ends at 16” and added: “I got into the relationship at 16. Why stop and have this dangerous loophole in the law?”

BBC News noted that private members' bills are debated only on Fridays and rarely become law. Strathern nevertheless said he had “a good chance of making real change.” He added: “I've been really encouraged by the response we've got from ministers so far.”
