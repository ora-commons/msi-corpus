## Jefferies sees Keyera facility utilization at 50%, below prior guidance of over 70%

A consortium led by MISC is proposing to take Yinson Holdings private at 2.50 ringgit a share in a move that MBSB Research said could weigh on the energy shipper's share price in the near term.

MBSB said Yinson's preferred shareholders and holders of perpetual securities capture most of its equity profits, limiting potential returns to ordinary shareholders like MISC. The investment would also add to MISC's funding needs amid a major fleet renewal and capital expenditure program, MBSB said.

MBSB Research said the deal could strengthen MISC's position in the floating production, storage and offloading market and improve access to future Petronas projects, although the strategic benefits might take time to materialize. MBSB downgraded MISC to neutral from buy and cut its target price to 8.33 ringgit from 9.22 ringgit. MISC shares were 0.7% lower at 7.95 ringgit.

At Keyera, Jefferies analyst Sam Burwell said the Canadian company's third-quarter results were stronger than expected across its three main segments and that 2026 marketing guidance was raised, but that more issues at its Alberta EnviroFuels production facility countered the beat. Burwell said AEF utilization was expected to be just 50% through the remainder of 2026, from prior guidance of over 70%, and that an additional outage is now required in January.

Burwell said maintenance and growth capital expenditure guidance for 2026 and 2027 also appears higher than expected on the whole. He said he will be looking for more detail on AEF and on future growth projects during the investor call with management.

Raymond James analyst Michael Barth scored Keyera's third-quarter results as neutral but said he would be a buyer on any weakness in the shares given their recent underperformance. Adjusted Ebitda of C$462 million came in 8% above the analyst's forecast, and the total realized margin of C$492 million was 3% above his expectation, Barth said. He noted that offsetting a positive marketing guidance update are continued disappointments at the AEF facility in Alberta, where heat exchanger performance has deteriorated more rapidly than anticipated. Still, Barth said a lot of the AEF concerns are likely already reflected in the share price, and Raymond James has an outperform recommendation on the stock.

Adnoc Gas offers investors near-term earnings recovery and a structural growth plan that has not been priced in by the wider market, Citi analyst Oliver Connor wrote. The company's third-quarter net income beat expectations on improving exports and better margins in its domestic gas business, Connor noted, and its growth trajectory has been largely de-risked by recent project approvals and higher oil output from the United Arab Emirates.

Barclays analyst Lydia Rainforth wrote that Adnoc Gas's earnings recovered across the board despite export restrictions, describing a strong report with improved asset availability, utilization and margins. Exports remain below normal, but the company indicated that September cargo movements had recovered to 88% of 2025 levels, she said.

Oil futures extended Monday's selloff after President Trump reiterated that it is unlikely the United States will re-escalate its conflict with Iran. "We are seeing positive momentum toward an end to this conflict," Trump posted on Truth Social following his meeting with Qatar's prime minister. WTI was down 2.7% at $84.47 a barrel, and Brent was down 3.3% at $89.50 a barrel.

Market analyst Nikos Tzabouras said the Democratic Party's gains in the U.S. midterms and President Trump's signal that the conflict could end fairly soon have revived hopes of a deal, while flows through the Strait of Hormuz are returning. He added that hopes of a breakthrough have been dashed before, so "until diplomacy delivers, geopolitical uncertainty lingers and crude can rebound."

Earlier Tuesday, European energy stocks fell as Brent crude slipped toward $90 a barrel after Trump said further U.S. military strikes on Iran did not seem necessary for now. Front-month Brent crude, the global oil benchmark, fell 2.5% to $90.22 a barrel, and West Texas Intermediate, the U.S. benchmark, fell 2.4% to $84.82 a barrel. In London, Shell and BP were both down around 1.85%. France's TotalEnergies shed 1%, Spain's Repsol fell 2%, and Italy's Eni slipped 0.8%.
