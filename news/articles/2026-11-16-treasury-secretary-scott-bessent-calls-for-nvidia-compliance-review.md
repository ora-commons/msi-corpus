## Commerce delayed restrictions on Nvidia chip exports

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said in a Fox News interview that aired Sunday that the more important issue is whether Nvidia had “a culture of compliance” around U.S. export restrictions and at what level. The Commerce Department has delayed placing restrictions on Nvidia chip exports to a company led by the alleged Chinese government agent with whom Jensen Huang admitted he had an affair, and scrutiny of the Trump administration’s actions involving Nvidia is growing.

In the Fox News interview with anchor Larry Kudlow, Bessent said he would “leave the internal workings of the Commerce Department to the Commerce Department.” He said, “I think what’s more important here, Larry, is to examine whether Nvidia had a culture of compliance around the U.S. export restrictions ... and at what level?” and added, “I think we’re going to see answers.” Bessent also said, “Jensen Huang is an incredible innovator, but that does not abrogate their duties of compliance.”

Trump’s Republican Party also lost both chambers of Congress in the November midterm election. His Asia trip offers an opportunity to change the narrative from those domestic problems, including the air-travel disruption.

The controversy over Nvidia is expected to hang over the China visit despite efforts by administration officials to downplay Trump’s close ties to Huang. Huang resigned as Nvidia’s CEO last week after admitting to an affair with an alleged Chinese government agent. Beijing said it was not involved. U.S. officials who briefed reporters anonymously under White House ground rules said Trump did not plan to raise the matter with Xi Jinping when the two leaders meet.

Huang accompanied Trump on Air Force One during the president’s last trip to China in May and has had a sporadic presence around the White House while speaking with him frequently. Trump has argued that the United States needs to preserve its leadership in artificial intelligence and has warned of dire consequences if China wins that competition.

Elon Musk, the head of SpaceX and Tesla, is among the travelers on the Asia trip with Trump. He is shaping up to be a top investigative target of the incoming Democratic majority in Congress.

Trump is preparing to depart for Asia on Monday, turning his focus to economic deals and historic three-way nuclear talks with Russia and China as the administration adjusts to a new political reality in Washington.

His trip begins in Manila, where Trump will meet with Southeast Asian leaders who, according to AP, are looking to the United States for help countering Beijing’s regional conduct. The White House said Trump plans to sign a trade deal with Thailand during his Manila visit.

The trip then continues to Shenzhen, the Chinese technology hub, for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. At the end of the trip, Trump is expected to hold the planned first-ever three-way talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin to discuss nuclear stockpiles.

Trump and U.S. officials have indicated that the meeting with Putin and Xi will focus on nuclear weapons and that topics of discussion would not include Russia’s war in Ukraine, now in its fifth year. Trump has recently played up the chances of a settlement in that conflict.

The three-way meeting will also put the ongoing U.S. war with Iran in the spotlight. Iran has often allied with China and Russia, and the war has been widely blamed for the Republican Party’s steep losses in the midterms. Trump has tried to play down the chances that he will order a resumption of major strikes against Iran but has also said he does not see the need for talks.
