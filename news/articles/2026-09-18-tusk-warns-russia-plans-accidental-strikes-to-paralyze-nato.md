## Trump announces progress on permanent Army base in Poland

Poland's Prime Minister Donald Tusk said Thursday that Russia plans to send drones or missiles into NATO countries that back Ukraine and label these incidents as "accidental," according to NPR. Tusk said he received the intelligence from NATO, Ukraine and U.S. agencies, though he did not specify which he was citing.

The Polish prime minister said the operations Russia is planning aim to "paralyze NATO" or weaken the alliance's collective response — the principle embodied in Article 5, under which an attack on one NATO member is treated as an attack on all members — to show that Article 5 "exists only in theory." Tusk's warning follows recent incidents along NATO's eastern flank that NATO member countries have attributed to Russia, NPR reported.

NPR reporter Esme Nicholson emphasized that Tusk said "there is nothing to suggest an invasion." But, she reported, the Polish prime minister also said his country faces a critical test as a key ally of Ukraine. Tusk questioned whether NATO members adequately consider the vulnerabilities of the alliance's eastern flank, suggesting Poland may need Article 5 support more urgently than countries such as Portugal or Ireland.

Hours after Tusk's remarks, Trump announced that the U.S. is making progress on establishing a permanent Army base in Poland, which currently hosts American troops on a rotational basis.
