## A previous GAO report found millions wasted after ICE's warehouse pullback

The Government Accountability Office urged Immigration and Customs Enforcement to develop a new plan for detention spending after the agency scaled back an effort to buy and convert 11 warehouses. In a report released Thursday, the nonpartisan agency said ICE needs to reduce potential waste and help ensure the cost-effective use of taxpayer dollars.

ICE budgeted $2.8 billion for detention in fiscal 2025, more than 40% above the previous year. The agency then scaled back what Stateline described as an unpopular move to buy 11 warehouses and convert them to hold detainees.

A previous GAO report examining the Trump administration's push to expand immigration detention found that the pullback resulted in millions of dollars in waste. The new report said ICE still had not made a new plan for the money.

Per-prisoner costs range from $85 a day for state and local jails to $180 a day for federal prisons. The report said ICE needs to make a new plan to “reduce potential waste and help ensure the cost effective use of taxpayer dollars.”

The pullback did not reverse every warehouse-conversion plan. As recently as Nov. 5, ICE informed New Jersey that it still planned to convert a Roxbury warehouse into an immigration detention center after environmental review. Another proposal in Arizona is open for public comment until Dec. 9.

ICE also announced plans to sell warehouses in Georgia, Michigan, Pennsylvania, Texas and Utah.

Stateline reported that ICE's detention expansion had also included private-prison purchases, including one in Minnesota that drew criticism for its high price compared with an earlier offer, and that ICE had announced a deal this week to buy a private prison in Washington state.

ICE still has warehouse conversion plans, has announced sales of warehouses in five states and has purchased private prisons. The spending plan sought by GAO remains unresolved.
