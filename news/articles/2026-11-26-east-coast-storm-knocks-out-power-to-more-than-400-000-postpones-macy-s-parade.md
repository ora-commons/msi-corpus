## Roughly 1,400 flights delayed and 400 canceled, according to FlightAware data

A major storm with heavy winds and rain disrupted Thanksgiving Day plans along the East Coast on Thursday, cutting power to more than 400,000 people as of early Thursday afternoon, according to Poweroutage.us. The National Weather Service warned of major coastal flooding from Georgia up to the Northeast and wind gusts exceeding 50 mph. In New York City, officials postponed Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade because of fears of severe weather.

Heavy rainfall was expected across the region, with 1 to 4 inches projected for the mid-Atlantic and 1 to 2 inches in southern New England.

Power outages were concentrated in Maryland and Virginia. Energy provider Dominion said broken utility poles and large trees on power lines meant it could be days before power was restored in some areas. “Outages are especially difficult on a holiday, and we appreciate your patience as our crews work to get the lights back on,” the utility said on social media.

Another 130,000 power outages were reported in Missouri. The state is grappling with several inches of snow in some areas and a winter-storm warning.

Roughly 1,400 flights within, into or out of the U.S. were delayed and 400 were canceled, according to flight-data specialist FlightAware. The hardest-hit airport so far was Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport in Arlington, Va.

As of Thursday afternoon, the storm's effects were still accumulating: more than 400,000 people were without power along the East Coast, 400 flights had been canceled, and the National Weather Service's coastal flooding warning extended from Georgia up to the Northeast.

In New York City, where fears of severe weather forced officials to postpone Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, wind gusts exceeding 30 mph were recorded across all five boroughs. The city's emergency-management department warned residents to secure loose outdoor items and use caution around trees and scaffolding.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani warned that the city could see wind gusts as high as 65 mph. “If you’re traveling, give yourself extra time,” Mamdani said Wednesday night. “And remember: No destination is worth risking your safety.”
