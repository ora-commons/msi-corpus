## Regulator barred the Ingram-Moores from trusteeships after 2024 misconduct findings

The charity set up by the family of pandemic fundraiser Captain Tom Moore has applied to be struck off, with the application appearing on the Companies House register on Friday. The organisation, now known as the 1189808 Foundation, dropped Moore's name at his family's request last year.

The rename came after the findings of the charities watchdog. A Charity Commission report published in 2024 found "repeated" misconduct and mismanagement by Moore's daughter Hannah Ingram-Moore and her husband Colin in their running of the foundation. The Commission described a "repeated pattern of behaviour" through which the couple made private gains and which the regulator said would have left people feeling "misled".

The report listed the financial benefits the Ingram-Moores enjoyed through their family links to the Captain Tom Foundation, which was launched in May 2020. Those benefits included a £1.4m book deal and an £18,000 awards ceremony appearance fee.

The Charity Commission has already banned both of them from being charity trustees. Hannah Ingram-Moore was prohibited from serving as a trustee or holding a senior management role in any charity in England and Wales for 10 years, and Colin Ingram-Moore was banned from serving as a charity trustee for eight years. The couple have both resigned from the foundation.

The filing records an application for removal from the register rather than a completed strike-off, and no decision on the request was reported.

Moore became a household name during the first Covid-19 lockdown, raising £39.4m for NHS charities by walking laps of the garden at his home in Marston Moretaine, Bedfordshire. He was honoured with a knighthood from Queen Elizabeth II.

The foundation was established after that fundraising. The millions Moore raised and donated to NHS Charities Together for its Covid-19 Urgent Appeal, before the foundation was formed, were not part of the Charity Commission's inquiry.

Moore, a father of two born in Keighley, West Yorkshire, died aged 100 in February 2021 after testing positive for coronavirus.
