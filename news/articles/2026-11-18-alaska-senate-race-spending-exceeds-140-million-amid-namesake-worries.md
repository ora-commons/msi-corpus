## Probes follow alleged effort to recruit Dan J. Sullivan Jr.

The Wall Street Journal reported that both sides spent more than $140 million in Alaska’s 2026 Senate campaign. The final days were marked by Republican worries that voters would be confused by a candidate with the same first and last name as the incumbent.

Under Alaska’s ranked-choice election rules, candidates advanced to the general election as one of four finalists in a nonpartisan primary. The 2026 general-election ballot included Republican Sen. Dan S. Sullivan; Dan J. Sullivan Jr., a retired teacher; Gerald Heikes, another Republican; and Mary Peltola, the only Democrat. The Associated Press said Heikes received 2.1% and Dan J. Sullivan Jr. received 1.7%.

The New York Times reported that Peltola’s campaign and Senate Democrats’ campaign arm had allegedly secretly worked with people to recruit Dan J. Sullivan Jr. to run as a spoiler. The Wall Street Journal reported that federal and state authorities are probing whether anyone broke the law after that account. Peltola has said she was “deeply offended” by allegations that she was involved in any such effort.

The Justice Department, urged on by President Trump, filed a lawsuit in federal court in late October seeking to delay certification of the results if Sullivan lost narrowly. That lawsuit was dismissed, and the Trump administration has appealed.

Sullivan and other Republicans criticized Peltola’s lobbyist work and argued she was too liberal. Sullivan and other Republicans also accused Dan J. Sullivan Jr. of being a Democratic plant intended to confuse voters. Efforts to get Dan J. Sullivan Jr. off the ballot were unsuccessful in state courts, and Alaska state officials said they had taken sufficient steps to alleviate any voter confusion on the ballot.

The Associated Press called the race Tuesday, two weeks after Election Day, with 99% of the vote counted. It reported Peltola had 50.1% to Sullivan’s 45.8%. Peltola’s victory made her the first Alaska Native elected to the U.S. Senate. It will bring Democrats’ Senate majority to 56 seats when they take control of Congress next year. It was the ninth Senate seat Democrats flipped from Republicans in the midterms.

Peltola, a former state legislator and U.S. House member, will return to Washington after running on the motto, “Fish, Family, Freedom.” Her campaign emphasized fish conservation and subsistence rights, along with reducing the high cost of groceries, fuel and other goods.

The Wall Street Journal reported that inflation hit voters especially hard in Alaska, where already-high prices were driven even higher in recent months by tariffs and the war with Iran. Democrats’ focus on affordability in the midterms helped their candidates unseat numerous Republican incumbents in Senate races across the country, including in traditionally deep-red territory.

Peltola won a special election for Alaska’s lone House seat in 2022, following the death of Republican Rep. Don Young. The following year, Peltola’s husband, Eugene “Buzzy” Peltola Jr., died in a plane crash.

Peltola lost re-election to Republican Nick Begich III in 2024, when Trump, at the top of the ticket, won Alaska for the third time by 13 percentage points. Peltola outperformed the Democratic nominee for president, Kamala Harris—whom she did not endorse—by about 10 percentage points.
