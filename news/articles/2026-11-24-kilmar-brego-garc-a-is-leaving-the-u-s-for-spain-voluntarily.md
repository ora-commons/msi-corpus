## Washington Post reports Ábrego García's negotiated departure

Kilmar Ábrego García is leaving the United States for Spain voluntarily, his lawyers confirmed Tuesday, according to The Guardian. The Washington Post reported that his departure would proceed through an agreement with federal authorities rather than another forcible removal.

According to The Washington Post, that negotiated route would avoid forcible removal in the latest stage of a months-long legal battle. Ábrego García had been fighting a second deportation since his return from El Salvador.

The first deportation came in March 2025, when the Trump administration removed Ábrego García to El Salvador in defiance of a court's protection.

He was held in Cecot, which The Guardian described as a “notorious anti-terrorism mega-prison.”

A Guardian caption identified Ábrego García with his wife, Jennifer Vasquez Sura, and his attorney in Greenbelt, Maryland, last December. The photograph was credited to Alex Wong/Getty Images.

Ábrego García was ultimately returned to the United States following a Supreme Court decision three months later. The return did not end his fight against removal; another deportation effort followed.

Ábrego García then fought removal to a series of African countries proposed by Department of Homeland Security officials.

The Washington Post reported earlier on Tuesday that Ábrego García negotiated with federal authorities to leave voluntarily so he would not be forcibly removed. The report supplied the immediate context for the departure his lawyers confirmed.

Tuesday's announcement specifies the immediate form of his departure: Ábrego García is leaving voluntarily for Spain. The Washington Post reported that Ábrego García's voluntary departure followed a negotiated agreement with federal authorities intended to avert forcible removal.
