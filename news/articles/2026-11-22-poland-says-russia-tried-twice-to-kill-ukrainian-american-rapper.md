## Denmark expels Russian defense attaché after thwarting concert plot

WARSAW, Poland — Denmark has expelled a Russian official after it said it thwarted an alleged plot to kill a Ukrainian American citizen on Danish soil. The case took a new turn Sunday when Polish authorities said the same man was allegedly targeted by Moscow's intelligence services last summer in Poland.

The target was Albert Vasiliev, a rapper who performs under the name Kyivstoner and holds Ukrainian and U.S. citizenship, according to Danish media.

The Danish Security and Intelligence Service, known by its acronym PET, said the attempt in Denmark was planned for Sept. 25 during a concert north of Copenhagen. It said the plot was "planned and initiated by Russian intelligence services."

The Danish prime minister's office said Friday that authorities thwarted the plot. PET said two men were charged with attempted murder and assisting a foreign intelligence service in operating in Denmark. They were linked to Loyal to Familia, it said, a banned gang known for "extensive, serious and violent criminal activity."

"In our assessment, the individual concerned was a particular target of the Russian intelligence services," said Rasmus Kieffer-Kristensen, director general of PET. "The case demonstrates that Russian intelligence services are both willing and capable of initiating an assassination attempt on Danish soil — at a public event and using individuals from Danish criminal gangs. This is a concrete indication of the heightened threat posed by Russia."

Russia dismissed the allegations through its embassy in Copenhagen, saying the accusations were unsubstantiated. Denmark responded by expelling Russia's defense attaché.

As Denmark announced the plot against the rapper on Friday, the Polish Foreign Ministry posted on X that it was noting the news with "outrage and concern."

"This is another assassination attempt against this person — the first took place on Polish territory in August 2026 but was thwarted thanks to the efforts of the Polish services and the would-be perpetrator, a Russian citizen, was detained," the ministry said.

The suspect, who was allegedly recruited by Russia's secret services, was detained by Polish authorities on Aug. 7. Maciej Wewiór, a Foreign Ministry spokesman, confirmed to The Associated Press the previous attempt to kill Vasiliev in Poland.

On Aug. 13, Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk disclosed that a plot to kill a Ukrainian American citizen in Warsaw had been thwarted, noting it was the first time that "someone on the order of Russia" sought to attack a U.S. citizen on the territory of another NATO country. Tusk did not disclose Vasiliev's name, saying only that the target "was uncomfortable" for Russian President Vladimir Putin's government.

Vasiliev was born in Kyiv in 1991, according to Polish media. He posted online comedy sketches and was a former member of the Ukrainian band Hryby. He also was popular in Russia before the full-scale invasion of Ukraine. He left Ukraine after the war broke out and lived for two years in the U.S. before moving to Slovakia, and he has expressed his support for Ukraine against Russia. He performed across Europe this year before his concerts were cancelled for security reasons.

Russia's domestic security service, the FSB, accused Vasiliev of being involved in supplying drones for an alleged Ukrainian attack on a defense enterprise in the Moscow region. It said he was "acting under the patronage" of Ukraine's internal security service, the SBU.

He did not comment on social media about the plot against him in Poland, but DR reported that he announced on Instagram that "the intelligence service banned us from holding the concert," referring to the cancellation of his event in Denmark.

Denmark is expected to ask the European Union this week for more decisive action stemming from the case, perhaps restricting Russian citizens from entering and traveling freely within the EU. Poland said it would support such measures.

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen condemned the alleged plot and said Russia's threats were becoming more real, reaching EU soil as the bloc continues supporting Ukraine as Moscow's full-scale invasion nears the five-year mark. "Russia's hybrid threats are becoming less and less hybrid, and more and more real," she said.

The Polish Foreign Ministry said in a statement that "the events in Denmark are another example of Russia's criminal activity on the territory of an EU member state, aimed at intimidating and destabilizing our societies."

Western officials have accused Russia and its proxies of staging dozens of attacks and other incidents across Europe since the invasion of Ukraine. Russia has denied accusations by Western officials that it was conducting a sabotage campaign in Europe.
