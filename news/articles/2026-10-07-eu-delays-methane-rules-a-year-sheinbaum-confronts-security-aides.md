## Tanzanian gold prospector families sue London bullion authority

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen told EU lawmakers on Tuesday that exporters would get an extra year to comply with incoming European Union methane-emission rules, citing high fuel costs, according to the Wall Street Journal's Morning Risk Report newsletter on October 7, 2026. Von der Leyen said the move would save additional costs during a crisis period. Under the rulebook, oil and gas importers in the bloc would have faced stricter methane-emissions reporting requirements on their suppliers from January 2027.

In a separate item, Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum began her workday at dawn last week by grilling Mexico's top security and intelligence officials, according to the newsletter. Sheinbaum confronted some of her most trusted aides over their plans to freeze the local bank accounts of former Mexican officials linked to a state governor from Sheinbaum's ruling party, according to people familiar with the discussion. The U.S. Treasury was about to target those former officials with sanctions for allegedly accepting bribes to protect the Sinaloa cartel's drug-smuggling operations, those people said. To Sheinbaum, her security chiefs were going beyond the close cooperation with American law enforcement that has been the hallmark of her two-year tenure by taking for granted that the U.S. had solid evidence linking the former officials to drug cartels, these people said. According to the newsletter, the episode reflects the harsher tone that Sheinbaum is taking toward American efforts to fight drug trafficking on her country's soil and the Trump administration's targeting of her leftist party, Morena.

Families of two independent gold prospectors allegedly killed by security forces at a Tanzanian mine are taking their grievances directly to global markets, suing the London-based authority that certified metal from the deposit as ethically produced. According to the newsletter, if successful, the legal maneuver — going after the London Bullion Market Association rather than the mining company, the gold refiner, Tanzanian authorities or the alleged killers — could generate questions about whether gold produced amid violence or human-rights violations is seeping into the marketplace.

The newsletter also featured a brief quotation from Daniel Gowan, the co-owner of a Dallas therapy practice that was ensnared by internal conflict about the war in Gaza, speaking at a church guest sermon: "Have you ever pressed the send button for the email right after you thought, 'Maybe I should wait and send it tomorrow, after I've calmed down?'"

The October 7 edition of the Morning Risk Report also covered Disney's federal court challenge to an FCC broadcast-license-renewal request and a Chinese-AI-agent attack on South Korea's biggest banks, and referenced a Riskonnect report on the share of corporate respondents whose organizations trained employees to evaluate AI outputs.
