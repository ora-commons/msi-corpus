## Ontario prosecutors seek 25 years as defense asks for 12

Kenneth Law, a 61-year-old Canadian former chef, apologized Friday during a sentencing hearing in an Ontario court to families of victims in the case. Law had sold toxic substances through online suicide forums, authorities said.

At the end of the hearing, Law addressed the families of the victims. "To the families of the victims, words can never replace the loss of your loved ones. I convey my deepest condolences. I remain remorseful. I'm sorry," Law said. He also said he deeply regretted his actions and would never repeat them.

Law pleaded guilty in May to 14 counts of aiding the suicide deaths of 14 people in Ontario. Law has also admitted causing the deaths of dozens of victims in England and Wales.

Under the plea deal, Law will not face charges in the UK or over any British deaths. The deal saw Canadian authorities withdraw more serious murder charges.

Prosecutors asked the judge for a 25-year prison term, while Law's defense attorney Matthew Gourlay requested a 12-year sentence. If the prosecution's recommendation is accepted, credit for time served since Law's May 2023 arrest would leave him with just under 20 years in prison. Sentencing is due at a later date.

Crown Attorney Kathleen Doherty said many of the victims were young and struggling with mental distress. She described Law as a predator who took advantage of "vulnerable people." "What they needed was help. But what they found was Mr Law and his products for death," Doherty said.

Doherty also said Law's motives were financial. "If people could pay, he sold it to them. There's no discussion or inquiries or anything going on whereby Mr Law would have any ability to appreciate the circumstances of the individual he was dealing with," she said.

Gourlay countered the prosecution's account by arguing that Law was not responsible for the victims' struggles with mental illness. "Each of these incidents is its own unique tragedy," Gourlay said. "Mr Law may be a common denominator between them, but Mr Law was not the cause of the struggles and hardships that caused all these victims to seek out his products in the first place."

Gourlay also said Law held a "misguided belief" that he was helping his victims.

The hearing followed emotional testimony in September, when grieving family members and friends described the pain Law had caused. Those victim impact statements will be taken into account when the judge sentences Law.

Authorities said the former chef sold about 1,200 packages of toxic substances to recipients he met in online suicide forums in 40 countries. Roughly one-quarter of the packages were sent to the UK.
