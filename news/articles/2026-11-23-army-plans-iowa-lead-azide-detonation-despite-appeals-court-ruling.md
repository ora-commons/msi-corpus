## Federal officials warn falling temperatures could destabilize lead azide before Wednesday detonation

The U.S. Army plans to detonate 9,500 pounds of toxic lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, Iowa, on Wednesday. Government lawyers said in a Saturday court filing that the Army will carry out the operation without an environmental permit under an emergency munitions and explosives exemption under federal environmental law.

The Army said the detonation, originally planned for last week, was rescheduled to Wednesday to allow for optimal weather conditions. The operation would destroy degraded lead azide, an explosive used in military munitions, that the Army had previously delayed destroying after pushback from local residents and farmers.

The plan follows a lawsuit by the Iowa Environmental Council and the Sierra Club's Iowa chapter, which sought to stop the blasts. On Thursday, the Eighth U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals froze an EPA permit for the operation, and government lawyers' Saturday filing set out the exemption the Army would use to proceed.

The plant sits in the small city of Middletown, near the Illinois state line and roughly 160 miles southeast of Des Moines.

In the lawsuit, the Iowa Environmental Council and the Sierra Club's Iowa chapter argued the blasts would send a toxic plume of lead dust into the air that could drift up to 100 miles north. Lead exposure poses severe health risks, particularly to children and pregnant women, including potential damage to the brain and nervous system.

Government officials told the court that falling temperatures could destabilize the material and trigger an accidental explosion, and that the blast must occur within the next several days. The Justice Department said, "The Army must perform a controlled detonation to protect the public from the more significant threat from an uncontrolled explosion."

The Army told residents to avoid the area and urged those unable to evacuate to remain indoors, shut windows and doors, and turn off heating and cooling systems that draw in outside air. The advisories follow earlier delays by the Army after pushback from residents and farmers.

The Iowa Agriculture Department and Land Stewardship advised farmers near the site to move livestock, feed and equipment indoors or relocate them temporarily.

Wednesday's blast would proceed under the Army's emergency exemption even as the environmental groups' lawsuit challenges the EPA permit, and local residents continue to oppose the operation.


[MSI previously reported that the Army's late-Saturday court filing proposed an emergency exclusion and set Nov. 24 as the deadline](/articles/2026-11-22-army-plans-iowa-lead-azide-detonation-by-nov-24-despite-court-pause/). The Army has now set the detonation for Wednesday and identified the exemption it plans to use to proceed without the permit, even as the appeals court's freeze on the EPA permit is unchanged.
