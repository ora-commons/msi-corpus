## Scheduled data revisions proceed without regard to the political calendar

West Palm Beach, Fla. — President Donald Trump accused the Commerce Department on Thursday of purposely issuing incorrect economic-growth figures before the midterm elections and asked for an immediate investigation. The complaint followed a Wednesday release from the Commerce Department's Bureau of Economic Analysis that revised third-quarter gross domestic product to a 4.3% annual rate, up from a prior estimate of 3.4%.

AP reported that the Bureau of Economic Analysis routinely updates economic data to improve accuracy, that GDP figures, monthly jobs reports and other data are regularly revised, and that those updates occur on a pre-scheduled basis without regard to the political calendar.

Trump wrote on social media that the revision was a "massive, and purposeful, error." He linked the complaint to the poor performance of Republicans in the Nov. 3 midterm elections, which gave Democrats control of both houses of Congress.

"It was just revealed that the REAL Numbers on the Economy, published before the Election, were FAKE, and a correction was issued yesterday by the people in the Government Agency that does these numbers," Trump posted. "Why wasn’t the correct number issued before the Election? I am asking for an immediate investigation of this situation."

The Commerce Department did not immediately respond to a request for comment. The government was largely closed Thursday for the Thanksgiving holiday.

AP said the complaint was the latest instance of Trump questioning the integrity of economic data he views as unfavorable to him, without providing evidence that the revisions were politically motivated. Trump has repeatedly sought to undermine trust in economic data that fails to match his claims of a historic boom, AP reported. The pattern threatens the credibility of figures that businesses, financial markets and consumers rely on when making investments.

In August 2025, Trump fired the head of the Bureau of Labor Statistics after a decidedly weak jobs report. The report showed that only 73,000 jobs were added in July 2025 and that 258,000 fewer jobs were added in May and June than previously reported. Trump called the jobs report "phony" and "rigged."

Trump has claimed that he deserves credit for powering stronger economic growth, but voters do not share his view that the economy is strong. The AP Voter Poll, described as an extensive survey of the electorate, found that 76% of midterm voters described economic conditions as "not so good" or "poor." About two-thirds of voters said Trump's own policies had fueled inflation.

The same federal agency also reported Wednesday that the personal consumption expenditures price index, a key measure of inflation, accelerated to an annual pace of 3.8% in October. Trump's social media post did not mention the report.
