## Draft text omits fossil fuels; summit warns of five years of stalled progress

Negotiators at the United Nations climate talks in Antalya, Turkey, planned to work through Saturday after the two-week COP31 summit was expected to close Friday. Their aim was a final agreement addressing global warming, but hopes were slim that additional discussions would produce concrete plans to drastically slash fossil-fuel use.

A draft of the final agreement drew strong condemnation from activists and some countries because it did not mention fossil fuels. The document was meant to address how the world will deal with climate change that is contributing to devastating heat waves, floods and other disasters worldwide.

COP31 was held in Antalya, a Mediterranean coastal city in Turkey. The summit included a warning that global progress has stalled for the past five years.

That warning came as disasters grew more devastating and deadly, including this year's heat waves in Europe and flooding in Nepal.

Experts cited in the summit coverage warned that averting the most catastrophic effects of warming requires urgent and deep cuts to emissions, mainly from the use of coal, oil and gas.

The world was already on track to go beyond the 2015 Paris Agreement goal of limiting warming to 1.5 degrees Celsius (2.7 degrees Fahrenheit) above pre-industrial temperatures.

Countries most vulnerable to climate change, including island nations whose lands are battered by rising seas, said wealthy nations that caused the climate crisis must contribute more to a United Nations fund. The fund is intended to help those countries recover from disasters and adapt to a warmer world.

Many of the world's top-polluting nations, including the United States, did not participate directly in this year's talks. The additional discussions were intended to produce a final agreement from a draft that did not mention fossil fuels.
