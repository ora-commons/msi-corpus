## Chutkan weighs longer relief as separate lawsuits expand the case

U.S. District Judge Tanya Chutkan extended by 14 days on Wednesday a temporary restraining order that blocks the Trump administration from breaking ground on a 250-foot memorial arch in the nation's capital. The order had been due to expire Thursday. The extension keeps President Donald Trump's construction project on hold until Dec. 3 or until Chutkan rules on a pending request for a more permanent preliminary injunction.

Chutkan's decision followed her Nov. 5 order, which she issued shortly after a National Capital Planning Commission hearing ended with the Trump-heavy commission giving the arch its final approval in an 8-4 vote. Dozens of witnesses opposed the construction at that hearing. Chutkan granted a request from the plaintiffs in the lawsuits challenging the project, saying nothing had significantly changed in the litigation during the two weeks since she issued the emergency order, and she maintained the status quo while she considers the request for longer relief.

Nicolas Sansone, an attorney with the Public Citizen Litigation Group representing the plaintiffs in the original lawsuit, said the plaintiffs were “gratified to see that the judge recognized that the circumstances that required emergency relief two weeks ago haven’t changed.” He said: “We’re hopeful the additional two-week pause will give the court the opportunity to assess our arguments in favor of a longer-term preliminary injunction that will block construction of the arch while the lawsuit proceeds.”

In an emailed statement, White House spokesman Davis Ingle said “An activist district court has again blocked the construction of the long-awaited Military Triumphal Arch” and that “The judiciary should not be abused to politicize a structure designed to unite our nation.” Ingle said the administration will continue making its case for the project and is confident it will be “fully vindicated as this litigation proceeds.”

The administration said work could begin as soon as the approval was in hand, the Interior Department issued a final decision authorizing construction and the National Park Service published the proper notification.

The original plaintiffs in the District of Columbia case — three Vietnam veterans and a retired architectural historian — had asked Chutkan to prohibit the administration and the National Park Service “from taking any steps to construct, or to direct or facilitate construction of, any structure in Memorial Circle, absent congressional authorization, during the pendency of this litigation.” Critics, including the plaintiffs, argue the administration must seek congressional approval for the arch, and they say the finished structure would disrupt the sightlines connecting historic landmarks in the capital. The administration has argued that Congress decades ago cleared the way for a monument at Memorial Circle.

Separate groups have filed suits asking that the project not go forward. The District of Columbia and Virginia filed suit on Oct. 31, and Chutkan consolidated that case with the original. The Kennedy family filed a separate lawsuit on Nov. 10, citing the impact the 250-foot (76-meter) structure would have on family members buried at Arlington National Cemetery. The Arlington County Board filed suit on Oct. 8 in federal court in the Eastern District of Virginia, challenging the speed with which the project was being undertaken and the harm it would cause to the county, which sits immediately adjacent to the proposed site.

Associated Press reporting described the arch as one of several projects around Washington that Trump has undertaken “as he remakes the city,” from renovating one of its storied public golf courses to “threatening to demolish the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts.” The report said those projects could soon face greater scrutiny after the Democrats take control of Congress, and many of those plans are already being challenged in courts.

In the golf course case, the federal government filed a notice of appeal on Thursday to a 14-day temporary restraining order issued Nov. 14.

For the arch, the schedule now runs to Dec. 3, when the extended order would lapse unless Chutkan rules on the pending request for a preliminary injunction. The plaintiffs' attorneys say they want that injunction to block construction for the duration of the lawsuit; the administration has said it will continue making its case for the project.
