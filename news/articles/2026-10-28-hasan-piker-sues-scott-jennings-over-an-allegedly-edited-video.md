## Hasan Piker sues Scott Jennings over an allegedly edited video

## The complaint says the 42-second clip omitted context and attribution

Political commentator and Twitch streamer Hasan Piker, who is based in California, sued Scott Jennings on Tuesday in the US district court for the District of Columbia, alleging that Jennings used an edited video to portray him as advocating the lynching of Black people. Piker seeks $72.5 million and has demanded a jury trial. Jennings, a political commentator for CNN who hosts the independent podcast the Scott Jennings Show, has not publicly commented on the lawsuit.

The defamation complaint divides the $72.5 million demand into $27.5 million in compensatory damages and $45 million in punitive damages. It alleges that Jennings deliberately twisted Piker’s remarks to make them appear to be Piker’s own opinions. Piker’s filing says the dispute concerns both a podcast episode and a later social media post. “He falsely told his listeners that Mr Piker had a ‘plan of returning our country to lynchings’. And then he falsely told his social media followers that Mr Piker ‘says the USA should resume lynchings against black people’,” the document said.

At issue is a 42-second video that, according to the complaint, circulated on conservative social media accounts as a mash-up of Piker’s comments on different subjects. Jennings played the clip on his podcast on 11 August. Piker says the video removed signs that he was quoting and mocking another commentator rather than endorsing the position attributed to him.

The underlying Twitch episode took place in August 2024, according to the lawsuit. It centered on Piker reacting to one of two Black commentators who said “woke” district attorneys deserved blame for policies supposedly harming Black Americans. Piker’s filing says he was plainly ridiculing that position.

Piker’s summary, as quoted in the court document, was: “He said, ‘We need more Klansmen, okay? Can we go back to a better time when lynchings were happening? This is what I like as a Black man,’”

The complaint describes the 42-second clip as “cropped, shortened, and rearranged” and says it included a “sarcastic mocking” of the commentator’s position. According to Piker’s filing, the video omitted the words “he said” along with contextual clues that Piker was ridiculing the commentator.

The filing says Jennings added a caption to the clip and presented it as a direct quotation from Piker: “Let’s go back to a better time when lynchings were happening.” The complaint says the original broadcast did not contain that sentence and that neither did the manipulated video Jennings aired. It alleges that Jennings converted a sarcastic question, expressly attributed to and directed toward another person, into a false first-person statement about Piker’s beliefs.

In an 11 August post on X, Jennings wrote, referring to Piker’s support for Abdul El-Sayed, the Michigan Democratic candidate for a US Senate seat: “Abdul El-Sayed’s best pal and campaign partner says the USA should resume lynchings against black people. Listen for yourself and understand what the socialists have in store for the black community.”

The complaint also alleged that Jennings was publicly known at the time to be in the running to become Donald Trump’s new White House press secretary. It said that provided “an additional reason to attack a prominent left-wing commentator whom the President himself had criticized.”

The filing asks a jury to decide the case. Jennings has not publicly commented on the lawsuit, and The Guardian said it contacted him for comment.
