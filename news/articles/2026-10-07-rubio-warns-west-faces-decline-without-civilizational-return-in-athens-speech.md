## Athens address opens Rubio's three-country NATO allies tour

Standing before the Acropolis in Athens on Wednesday, Rubio swept through the history of ancient Greece to the rise of American industry in a speech that portrayed western civilisation at risk of decline. The Secretary of State characterized the world as being at "an inflection point." He argued that while nations "need not abandon their distinct identities in order to recognise their common interests," they must rebuild institutions and alliances to confront shared threats together.

"We believe that Europe can awaken from its long slumber and rekindle the fire that was here millennia ago," Rubio said. He called for a return to "the classical understanding of politics and statecraft that the Trump administration hopes to reclaim - reclaim not just for our country, but for the whole of our civilisation."

Rubio touched briefly on specific geopolitical dangers, citing "radical mullahs" in Iran and suicide bombers among the threats the West must confront. Most of his speech, however, focused on a broader threat, with Rubio describing the current period as a crossroads.

"The course we take will determine whether the West maintains its place at the head of the world, or resigns itself to a future of atrophy, and servitude, and decline," Rubio said. "The next decade will write the story of the next century."

Rubio said renewed national pride was something the ancient Greeks had embraced but that the West had abandoned because "we felt embarrassed that our achievements set us apart from the rest of the world." He argued that the word "nationalism" had become "a dirty word" and accused the West of turning against its own heritage.

"So we tore down our monuments. We condemned our own history, and in too many places, we taught our children to be ashamed of the countries they would inherit," Rubio said. "In the process, we opened our borders and outsourced our industry and allowed our militaries to wither and to decay."

The address comes as President Donald Trump has sought to carry out an "America first" agenda during both his terms in office and taken a more confrontational stance toward traditional allies and in longstanding trade relationships. Six months before the Athens speech, Trump again threatened to pull the United States out of NATO, an alliance he has repeatedly criticised while pressing member countries to increase their defence contributions.

Rubio, often mentioned as a possible 2028 presidential candidate, made the Athens visit as part of his three-country tour of NATO allies that includes stops in Iceland and Portugal.
