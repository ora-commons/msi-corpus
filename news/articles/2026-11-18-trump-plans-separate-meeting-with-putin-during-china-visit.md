## The planned session would be their first since Alaska talks last year

President Donald Trump plans to meet Russian President Vladimir Putin separately on Thursday in Shenzhen, China, a U.S. official told The Associated Press. The official spoke on condition of anonymity because the plans had not been public, and the exact timing was unclear. The meeting is in addition to Trump's previously announced separate meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping and a historic three-way meeting with Xi and Putin.

The planned session would be the first time Trump and Putin have held talks since their discussions in Alaska last year failed to broker a deal to ease Russia's war in Ukraine. Trump has frequently emphasized his rapport with Putin as relations between the United States and Ukraine appeared to have cratered recently. Trump rejected new Russia sanctions while Kyiv was undermined by disruptions in satellite support for its drones during the war.

Trump said the joint meeting with Xi and Putin would address limiting nuclear weapons and ending the war in Ukraine, which began in 2022 with Russia's invasion. Analysts were not expecting substantive breakthroughs despite the unprecedented nature of the meeting.

Before the trip, Trump's aides told reporters that the war in Ukraine would not be on the agenda. But on Tuesday, Trump said the deaths of tens of thousands of Russian troops every month had created an opening to resolve the conflict. "I think it's very close to getting it done," the Republican president said. "We're going to have a good meeting on that."

Trump landed in China on Wednesday seeking to reinforce America's status as the world's leading technology power, but technical outages at home left his administration scrambling to calm the U.S. public. The problems put the administration on the defensive at a moment when Trump wanted to project strength and certainty before the meeting with Xi and Putin.

As he began the Asia trip, Trump was initially nonchalant about outages that left people in the United States worried about accessing email, checking bank accounts or safely flying on an airplane. "It's fine," Trump said Tuesday from the Philippine capital of Manila, suggesting it was all just a consequence of being a nation that is "big and powerful."

The outages involving Microsoft, the Federal Aviation Administration, JPMorgan Chase and the National Security Agency's communications network overshadowed the visit. They raised the specter that artificial intelligence can disrupt daily life as newly identified cyber vulnerabilities require software to be upgraded continually to close security holes, a process known as patching that was supposed to improve how the technology runs.

At the start of the second day of Trump's trip, U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio stepped in to reassure Americans on Trump's behalf. Rubio told reporters there was "zero" evidence that the outages were connected or tied to rival nations, cyber hackers or criminals. "Sometimes when you are going in to fix your system because you've identified a vulnerability, it creates a short-term disruption," said Rubio, who is also Trump's national security adviser.

Xi paid Trump a special honor by greeting him on the tarmac after Air Force One arrived in Shenzhen, reciprocating a gesture by Trump during Xi's state visit to Washington in September. Trump and Xi greeted each other on Wednesday at the foot of the airplane's stairs in a handshake that became a hug as dancers waved flowers and flags. When Putin arrived earlier that day, Xi did not greet him at the airport despite their long-standing friendship.

Trump signaled that he wanted to take an improvised approach during his time in Shenzhen, the skyscraper-filled technology hub hosting world leaders for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. The Associated Press described the improvised approach as a bet that his unscripted brand of diplomacy, based on personal relationships, can create some kind of legacy to reshape world affairs during his final two years in the White House.

As leaders were introduced Wednesday night before an APEC group photo, Putin approached Trump. They shook hands and traded greetings. The White House said Trump was scheduled earlier Wednesday to meet separately with Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum and Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney before the official APEC greeting ceremony. Trump had previously voiced disapproval of the U.S. trade pact with Canada and Mexico that was negotiated during his first term.

Before landing in Shenzhen, Trump had lunch in Manila with President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. at the Malacañang Presidential Palace. Marcos noted that he had sent fried chicken from the Philippine chain Jollibee to the U.S. delegation for a late-night snack Tuesday and complimented Trump for "working very hard." Trump praised the U.S. bond with the Philippines and the palace's interior decoration. "Our people share common history, common values, common interests, common architectural tastes," Trump said. "I love what you've done here."

Trump also had a short reunion with Imelda Marcos, the Philippines' former first lady and the mother of the current president. She had known Trump for decades through the New York City party scene and was famed for her lavish shoe collection. After meeting Marcos Jr., Trump said the two nations had reached a trade deal Wednesday, though he had also said last year that an agreement had been reached.

The Associated Press said the prospect of Trump meeting two of America's largest geopolitical rivals was a source of unease because Trump has been less welcoming of historic allies such as Canada and NATO partners in Europe. Trump's tariffs, intended to curb China's trade surplus, did little to stop the progress of the world's dominant manufacturer, which for the second straight year was expected to export $1 trillion more in goods than it imported.

Even without the digital outages in the United States, questions about how to handle AI loomed over the summit. The technology was viewed as key to unlocking faster economic growth, but its rapid development posed national security risks and could evolve in ways that put AI beyond human control.

Trump's close technology-industry ally Jensen Huang was recently removed as CEO of Nvidia after he admitted to an affair with a woman alleged to be an agent of the Chinese government. Beijing denied that she was a spy, but the affair raised concerns about how China has been able to access Nvidia computer chips to develop its own AI models competing with those in the United States. Those technology questions were already looming over the summit as Trump prepared for the planned meetings with Putin and Xi in Shenzhen. Associated Press writers Darlene Superville in Washington and Jim Gomez in Manila, Philippines, contributed to this report.
