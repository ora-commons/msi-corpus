## New accord covers youth experience scheme, food checks and emissions trading

Andy Burnham and European Union leaders agreed a reset deal in Brussels on Friday covering British food exports, emissions trading and a youth experience scheme. Burnham said the UK will discuss options for even closer ties with the EU, while the Treasury believes the deal could boost the UK economy by up to £17bn a year. Legislation to implement the agreement is expected in the UK Parliament next week.

Burnham arrived in Brussels to finalise the latest stage of the government's post-Brexit "reset" of relations with the EU, which originally began under former prime minister Sir Keir Starmer. He called the agreement a "good day" for Britain and Europe.

Under the agreements announced on Friday, most checks on British food exports will be scrapped in return for the UK following some EU regulations from 2028. The UK government believes the changes will cut costs for businesses and could bring prices down for shoppers, while critics argue the change would make the UK a "rule taker" from Brussels rather than a rule maker.

The new youth experience scheme will allow up to 100,000 people aged 18 to 31 from the EU to travel, work and study in the UK for up to three years, and the same number of young Britons will be allowed into Europe. The two sides want opportunities under the annual scheme to be available to young people by no later than January 2030.

Tuition fees remain unsettled. A further six months is available to discuss a deal on fees, which could prove to be a potential stumbling block. There have been calls from the European side for fees to be reduced for EU students at British universities, and the joint statement says any agreement would also cover UK students in the EU. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said she was "confident" a solution would be found, with Burnham adding: "Me too."

The UK-EU statement also said talks on linking the EU and UK emissions trading systems have concluded, and that the two sides aim to apply the agreement from 2028. On immigration, the UK and EU committed to work closely to tackle migrant smuggling and human trafficking, and said they expect their law enforcement agencies to share more data. Burnham said "significant progress" had been made to help "disrupt the gangs who are making those dangerous crossings across the English Channel".

Burnham cited Treasury analysis suggesting the deal "will add up to 0.5%" to UK gross domestic product, which he described as a "£17bn boost to the British economy". Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch said Burnham had "plucked that figure out of the air" and that "the only modelling I've seen that even talks about that figure says it's actually 0.0%". Badenoch said the deal "isn't fair" because UK farmers would have to follow rules made in Brussels and more young people would come to the UK to "compete with our young people who are struggling to get jobs".

Reform leader Nigel Farage, a leading figure in the 2016 campaign to get Britain out of the EU, called Friday a "very good day for the EU", adding: "A good day to try and humiliate the one member state that actually had the courage to say, 'We don't need to be part of you, we can govern our own country'." Farage called the deal a "betrayal of the British people" and, in a social media post, added: "Like every prime minister before him, Burnham is a total fraud. Only Reform will stop the endless betrayal of the British people."

Liberal Democrat leader Sir Ed Davey said there needed to be more than "tweaks" to the UK's relationship to the EU. "A youth mobility scheme is a big step forward, but it's nowhere near enough. We need a whole new deal that actually works for Britain, and fast."

Burnham argued that polling suggests the UK public "want closer ties" with the EU and that the deal "delivers that". He added: "Beyond that we do need to consider what is the right long-term relationship. Incremental steps only take us so far." His government will publish a 10-year plan next month, setting out ambitions for reindustrialisation, growth in "all parts of Britain" and opportunities for young people. Burnham said those ambitions are "connected to our long-term relationship with our nearest partner".

Over the coming months before the next UK-EU summit, Burnham said both sides will "seek to conduct an informal brainstorming process" to look at what those options are. Von der Leyen, who called the summit a "turning point" for the EU-UK relationship, said "we are open for brainstorming".

Burnham said: "What we are announcing today is probably the limit of what we can achieve in this Parliament, given what needs to be done to implement it." He added: "But we want to inform the Parliament after this one, so that decisions can be taken about that long-term relationship."

Burnham reaffirmed Labour's 2024 general election manifesto pledge that there would be "no return to the single market, the customs union, or freedom of movement". He said the "British people will be the boss of what happens next", with the next election needing to be held by August 2029.

European Council President António Costa said the EU remains "open to have the closest possible relationship" with the UK. The agreement settles the immediate parts of the reset, while tuition fees and Britain's long-term relationship with the EU remain unresolved.
