## Peru's first Black culture minister on forgiveness and the pope's apology

Susana Baca had promised to let the music speak for itself and turned down every interview request that followed the invitation to sing for Pope Leo XIV. Breaking that silence in an exclusive written interview with The Associated Press, she said she had feared the moment could be trivialized when it in fact marked a turning point for her and for Peru's Afro-Peruvian community.

Baca, one of the most widely known Afro-Peruvian artists in the world, is 82 and a Latin Grammy winner. As Peru's first Black culture minister, she remains a leader in the campaign to end the discrimination that for a long time has turned Indigenous and Black Peruvians into second-class citizens. In the interview she reflected on Leo's message, on what it means for Peruvians, and on the music she chose to sing to him. She also reflected on the significance of Leo's historic apology for the Catholic Church's role in facilitating slavery and on his visit to a slavery memorial in Peru recognized by UNESCO, given that Leo's own family tree includes enslaved people and slave owners. Her answers were edited for brevity and clarity.

Asked how the visit was for her, Baca said: "El Papa en sí, para mí ha sido una revelación." She said she found in his gaze as a good man a greater sense of meaning in his ecclesial commitment, and that he draws closer to his religious base, where the people grow.

She said the pope carries in his words the faith of a church renewed in its social dimension, to highlight Catholicism in its most elemental principles. "Trae en su decir la fe de una iglesia renovada en lo social para resaltar el catolicismo en sus principios elementales: la lucha y el compromiso por los hombres y mujeres más vulnerables," she said.

On the musical side of the visit, Baca said: "El Papa, con su humildad, es el que le abre las puertas a la música y a la comunicación con los jóvenes." She compared the visit to the celebration, in every culture, of the return of the prodigal son, saying that here the pope is the son who comes to visit them, and they celebrate him with music and dances and flavors for the palate. She said that can only happen when the bond between the people and the pope is one of family.

Asked about the lyrics of "Canción de Fe," Baca said the poem and song she sang before the pope has its foundations in her heart. She said she believes and thinks that singing to the pope is to accompany him in his saying, in his preaching of faith that he manifests. She described "Canción de Fe" as a song of hope at a difficult moment for Peru, "con políticos de poco nivel y poca calidad moral," as she put it.

She also sang "Si me quitaran totalmente todo," which she called "un poema muy intenso," and she sang it in solidarity with those who suffer in a violent world. She recorded it on her album "Espíritu vivo," recorded in New York on Sept. 11, 2001. She said she managed to move people living through moments of great desperation and war because the poem joins life and death at the same time. She said she feels that "esto es Gaza de hoy" — that pain, which she said is the same as today's in the Middle East and cannot be silenced.

Asked how she felt when the pope denounced the "sufrimiento y la humillación" suffered by enslaved African people at the Santuario de Santo Toribio de Mogrovejo, venerated for having denounced abuses against Indigenous people and enslaved persons by Spanish colonizers, Baca said: "Me sentí muy bien y feliz."

She said the pope's act of asking forgiveness, and the reflection of placing himself later — but valuably — on the historical matter of Santo Toribio de Mogrovejo in its place, was very important to her. She said it is a gesture that the Afro-Peruvian community not only thanks but feels finally takes up that dark topic of history with a clear vision: light, forgiveness, and the recognition that people can fail but can also remedy their errors.

Asked how she sees the Afro-Peruvian community today, Baca said: "Yo creo que una de las cosas más importantes en la historia de los afroperuanos es que no nos quedamos sumergidos a pesar de nuestra historia de dolor. Es un pueblo afro que aprendió a perdonar."

She said that is perhaps what joins her in conscience with the pope. She said she feels the pope asks in all his homilies and sayings for the importance of asking forgiveness and forgiving, and that the injustice of many moments in history is less painful when people have the capacity to forgive and to be forgiven. "Finalmente es el perdón. Y eso es la fe," she said.

Baca said she feels this identifies her singing, which seeks a reflective song, the same notion of faith that the pope has, and a historical process that belongs to the Afro community.

She also said Peru is a people with a beautiful diversity, a point the pope also made in his discourse, and that its human richness is condensed in the presence of Afro-Peruvians, the presence of Indigenous peoples and of Amazonian peoples.

Asked finally what message she would like to share with the world — especially with young people — after the pope's visit, Baca said the message is very clear. First for young people, she said, in the sense that young people understand the pope's words as part of their language today, of rebellion, of telling a truth that was covered up in the management of the previous, previous pope.

She said that before Francis the Church was distant from young people and did not represent a feeling of future, and that this pope has put all his saying in a context of youth. She said she believes the pope does not remain in the conservative discourse of the old Church but in a young, vital, day-to-day context. "Yo siento que las palabras del Papa son un abrazo para los jóvenes," she said.
