## Flynn's daughters read letters in court pleading with him to admit guilt

A Tipp City, Ohio man who once appeared on the television talent competition American Idol was sentenced Monday to life in prison without the possibility of parole for the February murder of his wife, a 37-year-old teacher, in their home. The sentence imposed by Judge Jeannine Pratt came a week after a jury convicted Caleb Flynn, 40, after a short deliberation and eight days of testimony in which the panel rejected his claim that an intruder broke into the home and shot his wife as she slept.

Flynn was also found guilty of eight additional charges, including aggravated murder and tampering with evidence. The sentencing hearing included victim impact statements from Ashley Flynn's sister, Kayla Keyt, and from the couple's two daughters, ages 9 and 11, who were sleeping in the house at the time of the shooting.

According to testimony, Flynn fatally shot his wife in February before staging their home in Tipp City to appear as if someone had broken in through the garage. Flynn claimed he was asleep when he heard gunshots and found his wife dead in bed after discovering the garage door open. Jurors rejected that account after reviewing evidence that included police body camera footage and activity on Flynn's iPhone and Apple Watch from around the time of the murder, when he claimed to have been sleeping.

During the sentencing, letters from Flynn's two daughters were read aloud in court. One daughter wrote: "Dear Daddy, I don't even know where to start. How could you do such a thing?" She also wrote: "Mommy was the perfect person... And then you chose to kill the only mother I ever had." The other daughter addressed the letter "Dear Caleb," rather than as her father, and asked him to "tell everyone what you did it and receive your outcome." "You made me feel like an idiot thinking I could trust you," she said, adding that she continues to have faith in God and that she will never visit her father in prison.

Ashley Flynn's sister, Kayla Keyt, read a victim impact statement saying the murder had caused his daughters to question their Christian faith and had "shattered a family who trusted you."

Flynn, a former music pastor, briefly appeared on American Idol in 2013, saying at the time that he loved his wife "more than anything." At Monday's hearing, he read a statement expressing love for his family, apologizing for unspecified "decisions" that he made, and again claiming that he did not kill his wife. "I made vows to Ashley on our wedding day. I failed those vows," he said. He sobbed loudly as the letters from his children were read.

Judge Jeannine Pratt called Flynn an "extreme" risk to the public as she imposed her sentence.

The prosecution's case relied heavily on more than 100,000 text messages Flynn exchanged with a former mistress, Alleigha Botner, whom he met while she was an intern at his church. Botner read aloud in court some of the messages, including one in which Flynn wrote: "I was up until 4am last night crying, literally thinking of ways to kill her and not go to jail." In another message, he wrote: "I just want her dead and gone."

Prosecutors also played AI-generated songs they said Flynn had made for Botner, and they presented evidence from police body camera footage and from Flynn's iPhone and Apple Watch around the time of the murder.

The prosecution called dozens of witnesses, including Botner. Flynn did not testify during the trial, and his defense team called no witnesses.

His lawyer, Patrick Mulligan, has said he plans to file a notice of appeal, telling reporters: "There's a lack of evidence on prior calculation and design." Mulligan argued that evidence of infidelity was not evidence of murder and questioned why Flynn was being accused of tampering with evidence because his gun could not be found. The gun, which had been kept in the console of his truck, has been missing since investigators arrived at the scene and found the truck's center console open.

After the September verdict, Prosecutor Paul Watkins read a statement from Ashley Flynn's family in court. "Ashley loved Jesus with her whole heart," the statement said. "She loved people. Most of all, she loved being a Mum."
