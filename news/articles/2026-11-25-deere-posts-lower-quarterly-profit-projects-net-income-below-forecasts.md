## Production and precision agriculture sales fell 15% to $4.02 billion in the quarter

Deere reported fiscal fourth-quarter profit of $1.05 billion, or $3.89 a share, on Wednesday, down from $1.07 billion, or $3.93 a share, a year earlier, as sales in the company's large agricultural equipment business declined. Net sales came in at $10.51 billion, above the $10.38 billion analysts expected, and including other revenues the company's top line fell 2% to $12.12 billion.

On a per-share basis, the results fell short of expectations. Analysts polled by FactSet expected earnings of $4.03 a share. Deere's shares fell 3.04%.

The pressure came from the company's largest business unit. Sales in Deere's production and precision agriculture division, which includes large farm tractors, crop harvesters and other farm production equipment, slid 15% to $4.02 billion during the quarter.

The other two divisions moved the other way. Construction and forestry sales gained 12%, and small agriculture and turf sales rose 10%.

For the year ahead, Deere projected net income of $4.5 billion to $5.25 billion, below the $5.91 billion Wall Street projects for the year.

That projected range includes an estimated one-time, non-cash pension settlement charge of $250 million to $400 million, related to a lump-sum payment option for certain U.S. salaried retirees.

Segment guidance for the current fiscal year calls for production and precision agriculture sales to grow 5% to 15%. Deere guided construction and forestry sales to be flat to up 10%, and expects small agriculture and turf sales to be about flat.

Chief Executive John May said the company is seeing early signs of improvement in large agriculture, led by North America. He said the company's outlook reflects a wider range of outcomes given continued uncertainty in key end markets.

The guidance sets up a contrast with the quarter just reported. The two divisions that grew in the quarter, construction and forestry at 12% and small agriculture and turf at 10%, carry the flattest outlook for the year, while the division whose sales fell 15% is the one guided to growth of 5% to 15%.

Deere's results pair a revenue figure above analyst estimates with per-share earnings below them, a full-year net income projection below what Wall Street expects, and a management outlook that May described as covering a wider range of outcomes in end markets where uncertainty persists.
