## Newsletter also covers Gates's planned AI-risk push as Anthropic IPO nears

YouTube has held discussions with publishers in recent months about developing an audiobook feature, potentially bringing the Google-owned video platform into competition with Spotify and Amazon's Audible, the Wall Street Journal reported Wednesday. The video platform has weighed bundling the product into its ad-free YouTube Premium service, which starts at $15.99 a month and includes access to music and videos, people familiar with the matter told the Journal's Alexandra Bruell and Anne Steele. It isn't clear whether YouTube would charge for access to individual books.

The offering could be another way to make YouTube's ecosystem richer and reduce subscriber churn, per the Journal. Embracing audiobooks might also give YouTube's massive creator network a new format to experiment with and cross-promote directly to viewers on the platform where many of them are strongest. Publishing executives see a possible route to new revenue — and an alternative to the unauthorized audio versions of their books that now appear on YouTube.
