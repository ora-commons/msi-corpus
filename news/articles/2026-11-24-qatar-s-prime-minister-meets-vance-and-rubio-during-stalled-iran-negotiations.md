## State Department says Rubio urged continued coordination with Qatar's mediation efforts

Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio met separately on Monday with Qatar's prime minister, Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim Al Thani, during a week in which stalled negotiations to end the U.S. conflict with Iran left the Gulf state pressing its mediation role. Vance held talks with the prime minister at the White House, according to a person familiar with the meeting. Rubio met the prime minister for a U.S.-Qatar Strategic Dialogue that includes Iran among its regional security topics.

A person familiar with Vance's meeting said the vice president met the Qatari prime minister at the White House. The person, who spoke on condition of anonymity because of the sensitivity of the discussions, would not give details of the meeting. Vance's office did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The Qatari prime minister was in Washington for a biannual meeting of the U.S.-Qatar Strategic Dialogue with Rubio. The dialogue encompasses a broad range of regional security issues, including Iran, as well as relations between Washington and Doha.

After the Rubio meeting, the State Department said Rubio had “expressed appreciation for Qatar’s global mediation efforts and stressed the importance of continued close coordination to deter threats and promote stability and security across the Middle East.” Rubio posted on X that “We’re working together to advance shared economic and security priorities and strengthen our partnership to promote a more stable and secure Middle East.” A joint statement said the strategic dialogue involved discussions of “shared policy priorities” ranging from the Middle East to Africa and Latin America, but it did not reveal any new policy directions or priorities.

The meetings came with the nearly nine-month-old conflict producing sporadic bursts of military action on both sides rather than a resumption of major operations, which President Donald Trump has said he sees no need for. The U.S. has maintained a naval blockade on Iranian shipping in the Strait of Hormuz, a crucial waterway for the global energy trade. Trump has left much of the world guessing about his next moves, and fears have grown among U.S. allies in the region about the possible resumption of all-out war.

At a White House news conference Friday, Trump said Tehran wants an agreement to end hostilities but not one that is “acceptable” to him, adding that “we want to make a real deal, not a fake deal.”

Mediators have been pushing proposals that would aim to lift disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz in exchange for limited sanctions relief for Iran and pave the way for a resumption in broader negotiations to include Tehran's nuclear program. Those elements remained the stated terms of the mediation effort as Vance and Rubio met Qatar's prime minister.

The Vance meeting also came after Trump told the vice president that the administration did not need a deal with Iran and instructed him to focus on fraud and crime in Democratic-led cities following the Republican Party's losses in this month's midterm elections. Vance had been the chief negotiator on a brief ceasefire agreement and has blamed voter unhappiness with the Iran war for the Republican losses.

Trump had also met Qatar's prime minister earlier this month at the White House and afterward declared that the Iran war “militarily is effectively over.”

Neither meeting, as publicly described, produced a new policy direction on the conflict. Details of Vance's discussion were not disclosed, and the joint statement on the strategic dialogue said only that shared priorities were discussed. The disputes mediators have been trying to bridge — shipping disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, sanctions relief for Iran and negotiations on its nuclear program — remained unresolved in the public accounts after the talks.
