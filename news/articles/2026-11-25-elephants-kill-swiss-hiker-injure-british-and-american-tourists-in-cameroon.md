## Park staff and military carried the victim's body to a Limbe mortuary

A herd of elephants attacked a group of six tourists and their local guides in Cameroon's Mount Cameroon national park on 19 November, killing Swiss insurance executive Alain Throo, based in London, and injuring British and American hikers, according to media reports.

Media reports said the six tourists were four British, one Swiss and one American. They were hiking near the village of Bakingili, in the south of the park, on a path used by elephants to access a nearby lake, when they encountered the herd of about 15 animals. Mount Cameroon is the highest mountain in west and central Africa, at more than 4,000 metres.

The elephants attacked the group. Throo was reportedly gored, one of the guides was tossed into a ditch on top of him, and the other hikers hid in trees.

One of the British female hikers told Cameroonian media that Throo had a black eye and was initially responsive, complaining about an injury to his arm. The outlets said they had feared he was bleeding internally.

In a video posted by CBS Radio Buea FM, an unidentified Cameroonian man said Throo told him the elephant attacked him, adding: "It smashed him."

One of the British tourists, who said he had hidden in a tree stump for two hours, recalled the moment the elephants attacked the group: "I saw these elephants almost next to me. And I thought, if I go down this path as well, I'm going to get hit. So I just moved to the opposite direction. They ran down the path, I ran and hid in a bush. And I heard some screaming."

Throo later died of his injuries. His body was carried out of the park by a combined team of park staff and the military to a mortuary in the town of Limbe on 21 November. The American hiker was treated at the same hospital and later discharged, according to local media.

Sean McGovern, the UK chief executive of the insurer Axa XL, where Throo had worked for 13 years, confirmed the death, saying Throo was a "valued member of our team, respected colleague and friend whose contribution, professionalism and warmth touched many people."

A spokesperson for Britain's Foreign Office said: "We offered assistance after a number of British nationals were injured in Cameroon."

Cameroonian media reported that clashes between elephants and local people had been happening for the past 10 years.

The attack came against a shrinking forest elephant population. The African forest elephant was declared critically endangered by the International Union for Conservation of Nature in 2021. The World Wildlife Fund says about 195,000 are left in the wild, less than 15% of their population 50 years ago. Just 5% of forest elephants, which are smaller than their savannah-based counterparts, are found outside central Africa. In Cameroon, about 6,000 forest elephants live, according to the NGO Global Conservation, which said the species was at risk from poaching syndicates.
