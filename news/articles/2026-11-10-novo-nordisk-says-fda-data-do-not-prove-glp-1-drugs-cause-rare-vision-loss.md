## FDA review of trials found 15 probable NAION cases, 10 among GLP-1 patients

Novo Nordisk said the data behind a U.S. Food and Drug Administration review of GLP-1 medications do not establish a cause-and-effect relationship with a rare eye condition that can cause sudden, painless and permanent vision loss. A company spokesperson made the statement to The Guardian in response to the FDA's announcement on Monday, November 9, that a review of 126 clinical trials found a potential increased risk of nonarteritic anterior ischemic optic neuropathy, or NAION, among patients taking the medications.

The agency's review covered 126 trials involving more than 134,200 patients. The FDA identified 15 probable NAION cases: 10 among patients taking GLP-1 medications, five among patients receiving placebo and none among patients who were on glucose-lowering medications that were not GLP-1 drugs. The agency found a NAION incidence rate of 6.4 cases per 100,000 person-years among GLP-1 patients, compared with 3.8 cases among patients on placebo or alternative glucose-lowering medications.

The FDA said, "NAION cases have been reported for all currently marketed GLP-1 RA products," and concluded that there is a potential increased risk of rare NAION events in patients taking GLP-1 receptor agonists. "Based on these findings, FDA is requiring that application holders add information about the risk for NAION to the Warnings and Precautions section of labeling of all GLP-1 RAs," the agency said. The requirement covers Novo Nordisk's Ozempic and Wegovy, Eli Lilly's Mounjaro and Zepbound and Sanofi's Soliqua.

The Novo Nordisk spokesperson said the company "continues to believe the data do not establish a cause-and-effect relationship and we remain confident in the benefit-risk profile of our GLP-1 medicines." The statement also said, "Patient safety is a top priority for Novo, and we continue to monitor the safety of our medicines, review new research and work with health authorities around the world."

The FDA began assessing the potential connection after a 2024 study published in the journal JAMA Ophthalmology found an increased risk of NAION among patients using GLP-1 medications. Despite identifying several limitations of the study, the agency said it "determined that further assessment of the potential increased risk for NAION was warranted."

NAION is a rare condition involving a sudden reduction of blood flow to the optic nerve, resulting in acute, painless, decreased vision in one eye, including permanent vision loss, the FDA warned. Common risk factors include diabetes, smoking, hypertension and hypercholesterolemia. The agency said NAION is the most common cause of acute optic nerve injury in individuals over 50 years old, with an annual average of 2.3 to 10.2 cases out of 100,000 people above that age. There is for now no treatment to reverse vision loss from NAION.

The Guardian reported that it had reached out to Eli Lilly and Sanofi for additional comment. Eli Lilly had previously said it is aware of publications on the risk of NAION among GLP-1 patients.

GLP-1 medications are mainly used by people with type 2 diabetes managing their blood sugar, or glucose, levels, and some are also used to help treat obesity. Some people have been known to seek access to the drugs for cosmetic reasons rather than legitimate medical ones.

The FDA's announcement came amid warnings from the Netherlands-based non-profit Access to Medicine Foundation that the pharmaceutical focus on GLP-1 weight-loss drugs risks putting global insulin supply at risk.
