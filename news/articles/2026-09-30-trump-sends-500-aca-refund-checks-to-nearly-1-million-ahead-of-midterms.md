## Trump sends $500 ACA refund checks to nearly 1 million ahead of midterms

**Subtype:** fact

- President Trump began mailing $500 checks to nearly one million people who paid what his administration called excessive fees under the Affordable Care Act.
- A letter signed by Trump accompanies each check, touting his record on lowering healthcare costs and promoting the TrumpRx drug program.
- An administration official said the primary recipients are enrollees who did not receive subsidized coverage and who bought plans through the federal marketplace in 30 states.
- The distribution arrives less than a month before midterm elections, with Republicans defending their House and Senate majorities amid voter concern about rising costs.

## White House frames payments as reimbursement for excessive ACA marketplace fees

The Wall Street Journal's Natalie Andrews reported Wednesday that the administration first announced the payments earlier in September. On the day the letters went out, Trump posted a video from the Oval Office saying "our Administration is doing the right thing and giving the money back to the people who were wrongly ripped off."

The accompanying letter, signed by Trump, says: "Since returning to office, I have fought to lower Healthcare Costs, increase Accountability, and Put Patients First." The letter also promotes the TrumpRx drug program, which the administration says offers some medications at lower prices through deals it negotiated with pharmaceutical companies.

The checks go to people who purchased ACA plans in 30 states that rely on the federal insurance marketplace to sell coverage. An administration official told the Journal that the primary recipients are those who did not receive subsidized healthcare plans — enrollees who paid full price for their marketplace coverage and bore the full cost of fees the administration now characterizes as excessive.

The payments arrive about a month before voters head to the polls for the midterm elections. The Journal reported that Republicans are working to defend their majorities in the House and Senate this fall while facing voters increasingly concerned about rising costs.

Trump has pushed for a way to send checks to Americans since his term began. At the Dallas Republican convention in early September, he pledged $5,000 payments to American adults if Republicans won the midterms. The Journal noted that he has made similar promises in the past that did not materialize, including an earlier-term proposal to give "20% of the DOGE savings to American citizens" as the government worked to cut waste.
