## Letters signal planned probes after Democrats gain subpoena power

On Wednesday, Rep. Robert Garcia, the top Democrat on the House Oversight Committee and the lawmaker expected to lead it when Democrats take control, sent letters directing members of President Donald Trump’s family and other administration officials to preserve documents. The action is an early step toward oversight investigations that are expected to begin with the new Congress in January.

Garcia said in a statement, “Oversight Democrats are fighting to put an end to this Administration’s self-dealing, misconduct, and abuse,” and added, “These investigations will lay the groundwork for legislation which helps all Americans. This is just the beginning.”

Recipients included Eric Trump, Donald Trump Jr., Vice President JD Vance, White House chief of staff Stephen Miller, Attorney General Todd Blanche and Jared Kushner, the president’s son-in-law. Garcia also sent notices to former Ambassador to Greece Kimberly Guilfoyle and former Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem.

The family letters to Eric Trump, Donald Trump Jr. and Kushner seek preservation of documents related to alleged conflicts of interest and their business dealings. The notices to Noem and Guilfoyle concern separate allegations of misconduct and financial conflicts.

The letters to Vance and Blanche focus on the administration’s handling of files related to Epstein. They also address allegations by Democrats that White House officials sought to conceal information and manage the political fallout from the investigation.

Garcia’s preservation notices do not compel recipients to turn over documents. They instead signal the investigations Democrats intend to pursue once they control the House Oversight Committee and its subpoena power in January.

Before the midterms, Garcia outlined areas Democrats would scrutinize, including the administration’s handling of the Epstein investigation, Immigration and Customs Enforcement operations and potential financial conflicts involving Trump’s family. As favorable election results rolled in for Democrats on the night of the midterms, Garcia posted a two-word warning on social media: “Lawyer up.”

The White House has increasingly warned Garcia and other Democrats that their planned investigations into the Trump administration may open them up to investigations from the Justice Department. Last week, White House counsel Will Scharf said in a letter to Garcia that “threats to executive branch officials designed to deter them from public service, or process designed merely to abuse or instill fear in the recipient may cross the line into blatant criminality.”

Congress’s investigative powers are well established and inherent in its constitutional authority as a check on the other branches of government. Modern U.S. history is filled with congressional probes and their aftermath, from the Watergate investigation to the 9/11 attacks to Republicans’ more recent look into President Joe Biden’s family. The preservation notices are an early step toward the investigations but do not themselves compel document production.
