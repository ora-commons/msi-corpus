## Redacted court brief alleges crimes predating Omar's congressional service

Federal prosecutors are seeking evidence dating to 1980 in an investigation of Rep. Ilhan Omar, according to court records unsealed Monday evening in Washington. The 169-page document, which contains multiple court records, was released on the order of a federal judge after more than a dozen news organizations and a conservative activist group argued that the information should be made public. The heavily redacted filings are the documents federal authorities used to justify an October search of Omar's home in Washington, D.C.

Much of the substance of the investigation is not in the release. Details about what prosecutors are searching for and what they have found were blacked out, and the search warrant itself was not included; a judge previously agreed to keep it confidential for now.

One court brief states the government's position: prosecutors believe Omar has "committed [redacted] crimes over decades—beginning long before she was ever in Congress." The filing provides no additional details about the alleged offenses.

A brief in support of the search warrant says prosecutors are looking for evidence from "1980 to the present." The congresswoman, who was born in the early 1980s, is listed in the documents as "Ilhan Nur Said Elmi a/k/a Ilhan Abdullahi Omar."

The sequence the records describe begins with an application for a search warrant of Omar's home on Oct. 2 and the judge's grant of it a week later. Federal agents arrived at the house around dawn on Oct. 12, when Omar was sleeping. One document says the search included "papers, records, photographs and any other physical objects" relating to a person or thing that is blacked out, along with computer thumb drives, external hard drives, computers and cellphones registered to or used by Omar.

The Wall Street Journal previously reported that items taken from Omar's home included her cellphone, notebooks, personal computers and her passport. Only the congresswoman and her husband were home at the time of the search.

The records describe a broader inquiry beyond the October search. The Wall Street Journal reported in October, citing U.S. officials and other people familiar with the probe, that the investigation is aimed at building a case to strip Omar of her U.S. citizenship and ultimately deport her. Authorities have issued more than a dozen subpoenas, including to some of Omar's relatives, seeking testimony before a Minnesota grand jury, the Journal reported last month. The investigation is also looking at her family's immigration paperwork, according to a person familiar with those proceedings.

Allegations of immigration fraud have surrounded Omar since she first ran for public office in 2016, when she won a seat in the Minnesota House. She has repeatedly denied allegations that her former husband, Ahmed Nur Said Elmi, is her brother whom she married as part of committing immigration fraud.

A large portion of the released document relates to the arguments prosecutors made in support of searching a current member of Congress. That argument turns on the Constitution's speech and debate clause, which protects members of Congress from being questioned outside the legislative branch about their speech.

The Wall Street Journal reported that Trump and conservatives have repeatedly attacked Omar and that she has faced GOP investigations. The Journal said a welfare-fraud scandal in Minnesota tied to Omar's fellow Somali immigrants had intensified the political attacks. It also said Trump has used Omar to rally the GOP's anti-immigration and anti-Muslim wings, noting that he has mentioned her at least 20 times in social-media posts this year and routinely brings her up in cabinet meetings and appearances.

Omar is a Democrat who was elected earlier this month to a fifth term from a deeply blue district that includes Minneapolis. Born in Mogadishu, Somalia, she arrived in the U.S. in the 1990s and initially settled in the suburbs of Washington, D.C., before her family moved to Minnesota as she entered high school.

Omar has said the investigation is politically motivated. A spokeswoman for the Minnesota congresswoman didn't immediately respond to a message seeking comment.
