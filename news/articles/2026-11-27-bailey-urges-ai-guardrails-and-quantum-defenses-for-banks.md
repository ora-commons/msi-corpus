## UK bank tax hike could hit borrowers and savers

Bank of England governor Andrew Bailey warned in The Guardian's live coverage that a UK bank tax hike could hit borrowers and savers. The same coverage detailed his call for guardrails on increasingly powerful AI models and his warning that quantum computing could undermine the encryption protecting essential financial transactions.

Bailey said in comments to Politico that lawmakers, not regulators, would need to set the guardrails for artificial intelligence. His comments paired the growing power of AI models with the need for public trust and explanations of what the systems do, how they operate and what is being done.

Bailey used a comparison about rearing children to describe the balance he wanted: “It’s a bit like rearing children. You have to give them the chance to do things, but you also have to set some sort of boundaries as it were.” The analogy gave AI systems room to act while requiring limits.

He tied public trust to visibility into the technology: “The public needs to trust this technology. There needs to be greater transparency, explanation of what the things do, how they do it, what we’re doing.”

His proposed path was stronger government understanding and coordination, with regulation potentially following. “It’s sensible to come at it from that angle and say, we’ve got to have stronger government understanding, stronger government coordination on this stuff. Regulation may follow it,” Bailey said.

At a press conference the previous day, Bailey said policymakers were worried about “open weight” AI, described in The Guardian's report as open source models, many of which were being developed in China.

The Bank of England also raised fears for the first time about emerging quantum computing capabilities potentially undermining the encryption technology that protects essential financial transactions.

“It could have powers that go beyond what we’re seeing with frontier AI and have substantial power to undermine cryptography,” Bailey said. He added that it was crucial, and possible, for banks to start building their defenses against quantum risks even while the technology remained in its nascent stage.
