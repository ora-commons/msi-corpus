## White House lawyer says administration is monitoring Democrats' planned probes

The Trump administration has told Elizabeth Warren, the senior Democratic senator expected to chair the powerful Senate banking committee, that she could face potential "significant legal consequences" if records related to investigations into Donald Trump are not preserved. The warning comes in a letter from US attorney Aaron Reitz of the southern district of Texas, which says Warren appears "eager to wield your future gavel like a tomahawk, hunting for the scalps of President Trump's private associates".

Reitz's letter to Warren is part of a broader set of warnings the administration has directed at Democratic lawmakers who have said they will investigate the Trump administration once the party holds the majority in Congress next year. Department of Justice officials have sent similar notices to other Democrats, and a top White House lawyer warned Robert Garcia, the congressman expected to lead the oversight committee of the House of Representatives, last week that the administration was "monitoring" Democrats' potential congressional investigations, claiming some of the inquiries could amount to criminal action. Reitz also sent a similar letter to Senator Richard Blumenthal, the top Democrat on the homeland security and governmental affairs subcommittee, over his letters to more than a dozen people who did not disclose donations for Trump's White House ballroom project.

Democrats have pressed for investigations of Trump, his administration and those close to him, accusing individuals of corruption, and after winning back the House and Senate in the 2026 midterms, Democrats poised to take top roles on congressional committees have signaled they will begin investigating some of those people.

Warren has been among the lawmakers preparing those moves. She and other lawmakers recently sent a letter to Meredith O'Rourke, a top Trump fundraiser, asking that she preserve documents related to potential funding for the White House ballroom, among other projects. While no formal investigation has been launched, notices to preserve records are typically a sign of an incoming probe.

Warren has publicly previewed the scrutiny she intends to bring. Earlier this month, she told CBS News's Face the Nation: "If you've been making your profits by cheating American families and scamming your investors, you better get ready for some investigations, serious ones." She also wrote in an op-ed for Time: "With astonishing brazenness, Trump has attempted to reengineer the federal government into a corruption machine designed to rake in profits for himself, his family, and his cronies."

The Guardian has asked Warren's office for comment.
