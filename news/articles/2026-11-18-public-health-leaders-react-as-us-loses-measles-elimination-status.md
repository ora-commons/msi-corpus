## Federal response draws criticism amid 5,400-plus measles cases

Public health leaders expressed grief and alarm after the Pan American Health Organization declared measles resurgent in the United States, Bolivia and Mexico on Tuesday, ending a 26-year US record of measles elimination after transmission continued for 12 months. The Americas had once been the only measles-free World Health Organization region in the world.

The announcement came during the worst US measles outbreak since 1991. State health authorities had reported more than 5,400 cases and 10 measles-associated deaths. Measles is considered one of the most infectious diseases known to medicine; it kills one to three unvaccinated people per 1,000 and hospitalizes up to one in five.

Dr Jon Andrus, chair of the PAHO advisory committee that recommended the United States lose its status, said, “There’s no question it was emotional, because we’ve been working on this initiative for decades.” Andrus said, “To see the US, once a global leader, lose its status is of course sad,” adding that it was also sad to see Mexican and Bolivian colleagues lose their status.

Measles-elimination status is a public health designation. It does not come with penalties or travel restrictions. Public health workers from local agencies to international offices rank vaccination among the most cost-effective ways to improve human life, alongside public sanitation and clean drinking water. “There’s a huge return on investment that is pretty amazing,” Andrus said.

Health Secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr. responded in a statement posted on X late Tuesday afternoon. He said the US “will stop measles outbreaks, protect American families, and work to regain US measles elimination status.” He added that he would “continue to recommend MMR vaccination as the most effective way to prevent measles” and that Department of Health and Human Services officials were working to close vaccination gaps and rebuild public trust.

Kennedy said officials would answer families’ questions honestly about vaccine benefits and risks and work with health care providers and community leaders to prevent measles, limit its spread and support timely treatment.

On Sunday, Republican Sen. Bill Cassidy of Louisiana, a critical swing-vote senator, called Kennedy’s tenure “a series of failures.” Democratic Sen. Raphael Warnock of Georgia criticized Kennedy for posting “AI slop” when the United States lost its status. The loss of elimination status had heightened calls for Kennedy to be fired.

The Guardian contrasted the loss with the administration’s response, saying Kennedy had spent years spreading misinformation about the MMR vaccine and downplaying the disease. HHS promoted an appearance with a Texas-based chef and posted a picture of Kennedy as James Bond that week. After what The Guardian described as devastating midterm losses, Trump appointed Stephen Miller, described as a “Maha advocate,” as White House chief of staff.

Dr Andrew D Racine, president of the American Academy of Pediatrics, said, “The measles virus is not harmless.” He said it can cause pneumonia, hospitalization, brain damage and death. “We have an extraordinary tool to prevent it: a safe, effective, inexpensive, and readily available vaccine,” Racine said.

Dr Richard Besser, president and CEO of the Robert Wood Johnson Foundation and a former acting director of the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, said, “As a pediatrician and a parent, I am heartbroken that we have reached this point.” He said medical providers and state, local and tribal health officials were trying to stop current outbreaks and prevent new ones, but charged that the federal government had “largely been missing in action” for the past two years. Besser said, “It is no surprise that the resurgence of measles has coincided with an administration that has inflicted significant damage to our healthcare system while spreading false claims about vaccines.”

Democratic Sen.-elect Dr Annie Andrews of South Carolina, one of several newly elected doctors and scientists and a critic of Kennedy, said, “We need to restore trust in public health, make sure every family has access to life-saving vaccines, and put the health and safety of our children ahead of our politics.”

Health experts also called on the United States to begin work on a PAHO action plan that could stop transmission and produce gains in vaccination. The proposal is a multi-year project. Brazil lost its measles-free status in 2019 and regained it five years later; on Tuesday, PAHO announced that Canada had stopped transmission for 60 days, a step toward regaining its measles-free status.

The announcement also came amid a wider global resurgence. Canada lost its measles-free status in 2025 and the UK did so this year. Andrus and his colleagues had reviewed data only from the first half of 2026, meaning the worst of the year’s outbreak was not before them, yet the announcement was widely predicted and had been leaked.

In Cleveland, Ohio, a nearby outbreak prompted the local public health director to tell residents to find decades-old vaccination certificates in case they were exposed and required a blood draw or quarantine. Public health experts said pockets of low vaccination rates can combust into outbreaks.

Epidemiologist and Yale researcher Gregg Gonsalves, who serves on the coordinating committee for advocacy group Defend Public Health, said, “Because measles is so contagious, it’s considered a bellwether, a signal of problems in efforts to control disease.” Gonsalves blamed Kennedy, saying, “RFK Jr has spent over a decade trying to destroy disease prevention, the last two as HHS secretary, and his public health malpractice is coming home to roost. Congress can no longer sit back and twiddle its thumbs.”
