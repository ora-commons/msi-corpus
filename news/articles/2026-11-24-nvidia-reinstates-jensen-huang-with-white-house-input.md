## Shares jump 32% as disclosure demands follow the leadership change

Jensen Huang was reinstated as Nvidia's chief executive on Monday, hours before he returned to the company's Silicon Valley office to a welcome from employees. The Guardian said the sequence leading to the return included a romantic affair with a suspected Chinese spy, a drastic plummet in the stock market and intervention by Donald Trump.

Employees rushed to greet him, clapping, hollering and chanting: “Jensen, Jensen, Jensen!” According to several verified videos posted to social media, Huang told employees, “I’m going to be a better CEO the second time around.”

Fuxiao Liu, a research scientist at Nvidia, posted on X: “Our Jesus @JensenHuang is back.” Nvidia AI researcher Jim Fan quoted Charles Dickens’s *A Tale of Two Cities*, saying: “It was the Winter of despair, it is the Spring of hope.”

Trump had reportedly dialed into Nvidia's Sunday board meeting to advocate for Huang's return, according to Semafor. Nvidia said it reached its decision with “input from senior levels of the United States government.”

White House director of communications Steven Cheung said, “President Trump was totally involved, and this is a great decision by the Nvidia board,” and added: “We have to protect our geniuses.” Trump also posted an AI image on social media recreating his own assassination attempt, with Huang at the center instead. The image depicted Huang with blood on his face and a raised fist while holding a bright red bra.

OpenAI's Sam Altman and SpaceX's Elon Musk cheered the news. Nvidia shares surged 32% on Monday after weeks of a downward spiral, adding more than $1tn to the company's market value.

Nvidia, which Huang co-founded, was described as the world's most valuable chip company and one of the world's most valuable businesses, worth about $4tn. The Guardian said the AI ecosystem relied on Nvidia. The chipmaker engineers advanced chips used to train AI models and power tech firms such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Google.

The Guardian reported that Huang remained subject to several government and internal investigations, shareholder lawsuits and accusations that he is a national security threat.

Harvey Jones, chair of Nvidia's board, said: “The board believes Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers.”

Tulane University business professor Peter Ricchiuti challenged the idea that Huang was the only person able to handle the role. Ricchiuti said, “I understand that he is ‘the face’ of the company, but that shouldn’t matter. It’s hard to argue that he is the only one who could handle the CEO role,” and added: “The graveyards are filled with irreplaceable people.”

Democratic Rhode Island Sen. Jack Reed, a member of the Senate Banking Committee, called for Huang to disclose all communications with Trump and for Nvidia's board to release a transcript of its call with the president.

Reed said, “Mr Huang’s abrupt reinstatement is troubling on many levels,” and continued: “This case raises serious issues about ethics, misconduct, corruption, national security, the integrity of our technology and military supply chains under the Trump administration’s watch.”

Reed also questioned “whether Nvidia itself is ‘too big to fail’ – or was Mr Huang too powerful to fire?”
