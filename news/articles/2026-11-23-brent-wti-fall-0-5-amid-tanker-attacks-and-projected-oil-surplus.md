## Preliminary seven-day Kpler data show Hormuz transits 42% below prewar levels despite recovering shipments

Oil prices edged lower Monday as traders weighed expectations of a global supply surplus against renewed attacks on shipping in the Middle East and concerns over disruptions to regional oil infrastructure. Farhan Rafid reported from Doha, Qatar, that Brent crude futures for January delivery fell 0.5% to $92.13 a barrel, while U.S. West Texas Intermediate crude futures declined 0.5% to $83.03 a barrel.

The International Energy Agency expects a global oil supply surplus of 1.7 million barrels a day in the fourth quarter, widening to 3.8 million barrels a day next year, as recovering production outpaces demand growth. Oil prices have traded in a narrow range in recent sessions, according to analysts at MUFG, as traders balanced expectations of rising global inventories against concern that geopolitical tensions in the Middle East could worsen.

Both benchmarks fell for a third consecutive week last week, with Brent losing around 3.4% and WTI declining 5.2%, as expectations of a sizable supply surplus next year continued to weigh on prices.

The U.K. Maritime Trade Operations reported several incidents involving tankers in the Strait of Hormuz over the weekend, including attacks on vessels near the coast of Oman. Despite the continuing attacks, oil shipments through the waterway have been recovering.

Preliminary data from ship-tracking firm Kpler showed that transits through the Strait of Hormuz remained 42% below prewar levels in the seven days through Nov. 21. The figures showed a recovery in traffic without a return to prewar shipping levels.

Separately, Saudi Arabia’s coalition forces intercepted and destroyed a drone launched by Houthi militants toward the kingdom’s southern region Sunday, according to the Saudi Press Agency.

Traders were also looking ahead to the Nov. 29 meeting of the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries and its allies. The producer group was expected to discuss its output policy for 2027.
