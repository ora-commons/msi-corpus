## Talbott helped translate Khrushchev memoirs before serving as deputy secretary

Strobe Talbott, a former Time magazine reporter who served as deputy secretary of state under President Bill Clinton and later headed the Brookings Institution, died Thursday at age 80. His son, Devin Talbott, confirmed that Talbott died peacefully after a long illness at his home in East Hampton, New York.

Talbott's career joined journalism, government service and the leadership of the Brookings Institution. At the State Department, he helped shape the Clinton administration's approach to Russia and other formerly Soviet countries before leading Brookings.

Talbott was educated at Yale and Oxford, where he shared a room with Clinton. While at the British university, after interning for Time in Moscow, the magazine asked him to help translate the leaked memoirs of Nikita Khrushchev. The assignment brought Talbott some notoriety as a young man and a job at the magazine. Khrushchev was the former first secretary of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union and was ousted in 1964.

Talbott then spent 21 years at Time, focusing on Soviet-American relations. According to his U.S. State Department biography, he held positions including Washington bureau chief and foreign-affairs columnist.

After his years at Time, Talbott received a call from the newly elected Clinton. He joined the U.S. State Department in 1994 and swiftly became deputy secretary of state. In that role, he was the architect of the Clinton administration's approach to Russia and other formerly Soviet countries. He also conducted negotiations between the United States and India after India's nuclear tests in 1998.

Afterward, Talbott served as president of Brookings from 2002 to 2017. During that time, he also chaired the U.S. State Department's Foreign Affairs Policy Board.

Talbott authored more than a dozen books. His last book was *Our Founders' Warning: The Age of Reason Meets the Age of Trump*, published in 2020.

Talbott was born in Dayton, Ohio, in 1946. He is survived by two sons, Devin and Adrian, and six grandchildren. His first wife, Brook Shearer, died in 2009, and he later married author Barbara Lazear Ascher.
