## Trump's lawyers will not depose BBC personnel until he sits for questioning

Donald Trump received a temporary delay before he must give a witness statement in his $10 billion defamation lawsuit against the BBC, after federal district court judge Jeffrey Kuntz approved the parties' joint motion on Tuesday. Kuntz also moved the discovery cutoff from December 18 to January 29 and ordered the sides to select the deposition date by Friday at noon.

The new schedule follows a late-October order from federal magistrate judge Enjoliqué Lett, who gave Trump a firm December 11 deadline to sit for a deposition by lawyers representing the BBC. Lett warned that Trump could face penalties up to dismissal of the case if he failed to comply. Trump had initially agreed to sit on October 16, but his team backed out, citing “matters of national importance.” The BBC then moved to compel Trump to sit for a deposition in a timely fashion.

In a joint motion filed Monday, the BBC agreed to Trump's proposal for a deposition between January 11 and January 22. Trump's representatives said the president was unavailable to testify this year “due to his schedule and the nature of his profession.” As that window approaches, the parties are to meet to select a date and identify a secure location for the deposition.

Kuntz wrote in his order, “It is Trump’s case, so he should expect to be deposed,” and added: “It appears he acknowledges that reality.” The judge set a trial date for May 3, 2027, in Fort Lauderdale, Florida.

Trump's lawyers have deposed seven defendant witnesses. Under an agreement between Trump and the BBC, Trump's lawyers will not depose Samir Shah, the BBC chair, or a former BBC director-general, nor any current or former BBC employee, until the president sits for questioning.

Trump's seven-hour-long deposition will be videotaped, but the recording will be available only to attorneys for the parties.

In addition to Trump, the BBC might also be able to depose his daughter Ivanka Trump, his son Donald Trump Jr and his son-in-law Jared Kushner. The parties wrote in their joint motion: “Defendant is currently conferring with President Trump and these nonparties regarding the subpoenas directed to them, given that these nonparties may be objecting to, or moving to quash, those subpoenas.”

In a ruling last week, Kuntz, a Trump appointee, refused the BBC's request to dismiss the case but threw out Trump's contention that the broadcaster violated Florida's Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act.

The lawsuit stems from a Panorama documentary aired by the BBC that spliced together two comments Trump made nearly an hour apart during a rally on January 6, 2021, before the US Capitol riot. The BBC has acknowledged that this specific edit was misleading, but Trump has falsely claimed that the broadcaster did not just edit his speech but used AI to insert words into his speech.

Kuntz addressed the edit in his ruling: “BBC tries to frame this editing as ordinary editing done to condense a 70-minute-long speech into 12 seconds,” the judge wrote. “But the court is not persuaded.”
