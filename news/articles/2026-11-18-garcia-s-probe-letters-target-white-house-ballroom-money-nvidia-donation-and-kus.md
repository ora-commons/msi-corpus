## Noem contracts, GEO Group demand and Epstein logs widen the targets of 12 letters

Rep. Robert Garcia (D., Calif.), who is set to be chairman of the Oversight Committee, sent 12 letters Wednesday to members of President Donald Trump's family and his administration. The letters provide an early roadmap of potential investigation targets for next year, and the most detailed allegations concern the White House ballroom project, the ethics of payments to Trump's son-in-law and the contracts and enforcement practices of the Department of Homeland Security.

In a letter to Catherine Townsend, chief executive of the Trust for the National Mall, Garcia accused the nonprofit of acting as a "middleman" for ballroom donations and put the ballroom price tag above $600 million in taxpayer and donor money. The letter singled out Nvidia, saying the semiconductor giant gave millions of dollars soon after winning approval from the administration to sell advanced chips to China. Nvidia has said its contribution was independent of business interests. Townsend did not immediately respond to a request for comment. The Supreme Court ruled in August that Trump could continue building the ballroom, despite never having received congressional approval.

Garcia also raised concerns that hefty fees paid to Jared Kushner, the president's son-in-law who also serves as a U.S. envoy overseas, are "disguised payoffs for policy changes." Kushner is an envoy on Middle East policy, Gaza, Iran, and Russia's war against Ukraine. A letter to former U.S. ambassador to Greece Kimberly Guilfoyle, who resigned in October, cited a Wall Street Journal report that Guilfoyle demanded $100,000 for access to senior administration officials prior to her confirmation. She has called the allegations false. Kushner and Guilfoyle didn't immediately respond to requests for comment.

Garcia sent separate letters to former DHS special government employee Corey Lewandowski, former DHS secretary Kristi Noem and Trump's immigration czar-turned-chief of staff Stephen Miller. Noem "allegedly awarded lucrative DHS contracts to political allies and donors," her letter says, referencing five airplanes purchased from a connected donor on her last day in the job. Noem has called the donor label misleading. Trump fired her in March. The letter to Lewandowski alleges he demanded money from GEO Group, a private-detention operator, pegged to the value of its DHS contracts. A spokesperson for Lewandowski said Garcia was attempting to build a misleading narrative that "will be proven false at the appropriate time and in the appropriate forum."

Democrats have alleged the Department of Homeland Security abused its power under Miller's direction, accusing him of pushing arrest quotas and ordering immigration sweeps without regard to criminal history. Noem and Miller didn't immediately respond to requests for comment.

Garcia's letters to Donald Trump Jr. and Eric Trump allege they cashed in on their father's presidency as co-founders of the family cryptocurrency venture World Liberty Financial. The letter to Donald Trump Jr. also cites his wedding, referencing a ProPublica report that a Russian oligarch close to Russian President Vladimir Putin helped foot the bill. Trump Jr.'s wife confirmed a gift, which President Trump said his son has since repaid. The brothers have denied any wrongdoing. A spokesman for World Liberty Financial called the probe a "baseless witchhunt" and said the company has complied with all "applicable U.S. laws and requirements."

The letters to Vice President JD Vance and former White House chief of staff Susie Wiles don't contain preservation language. Instead, they demand by Dec. 2 the date, location and attendance logs from every meeting administration officials held concerning Epstein, who died in 2019, and on his longtime associate Ghislaine Maxwell's prison sentence. Each says the recipient was among officials who allegedly colluded to "undermine public interest in the Epstein files and avoid scrutiny of President Trump." Letters to Attorney General Todd Blanche and prison officials cite what Garcia calls "extraordinary preferential treatment" Maxwell received at a minimum-security prison camp in Texas. Blanche has denied Maxwell received preferential treatment and defended her transfer to the camp, saying she faced threats to her life in federal custody.

The 12 letters present formal notices to preserve records and answer questions for pending congressional investigations. Democrats get subpoena power in January when the new Congress begins and they take over the majority. Democrats have promised an intense investigational agenda, largely handled by the Oversight and Judiciary committees, while the party is grappling with how to balance the probes with the affordability agenda they embraced in the campaign. "This is just the beginning," Garcia said.

White House counsel Will Scharf informed Democrats in a letter Wednesday that the administration wouldn't comply with post-election oversight demands from the minority party and said any requests wouldn't carry over into the next Congress. The White House had warned the lawmaker in a Nov. 9 letter that Democratic probes "may cross the line into blatant criminality" if they don't serve a legislative purpose. Lauren Bis, a spokeswoman for the White House, called the investigations a sham.
