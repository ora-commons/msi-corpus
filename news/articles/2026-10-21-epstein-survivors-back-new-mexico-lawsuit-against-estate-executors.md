## Complaint alleges exchange of benefits for furtherance and silence

Several Epstein abuse survivors signed a Wednesday letter supporting a New Mexico civil lawsuit against Jeffrey Epstein’s longtime attorney Darren Indyke and longtime accountant Richard Kahn in the U.S. District Court for the District of New Mexico. The letter said the case could expose what roles Indyke and Kahn played in Epstein’s operation, examine how they handled survivors’ claims and prevent rewards for their roles in Epstein’s organization. The survivors wrote, “Survivors deserve accountability and answers under oath. Epstein’s chosen confidants should not get the last word – or the money.”

New Mexico’s complaint alleges that “Epstein, with the help of his associates, trafficked, isolated, and abused survivors at alluring locations,” including Zorro Ranch in Stanley, New Mexico. It accuses Indyke and Kahn of “facilitating and concealing” Epstein’s crimes. The complaint alleges associates received generous salaries, access to elite social circles and lavish gifts, including designation as beneficiaries of Epstein’s multimillion-dollar estate, in exchange for furthering his criminal project and remaining silent about it.

The lawsuit announcement came amid a separate fight between New Mexico officials and the Justice Department over files Torrez is seeking for the state’s investigation into Epstein’s possible crimes, including at his ranch outside Santa Fe, where multiple survivors have said they were abused. Torrez sued the Justice Department in August, accusing the department and Attorney General Todd Blanche of “stonewalling” the investigation, which was reopened in February. Torrez sought unredacted Epstein-related files and said state prosecutors had faced “sustained resistance” from the department.

The records case remains ongoing. The Justice Department recently filed a motion to dismiss, arguing that “decisions about law-enforcement information sharing are committed to the agency’s discretion” and that turning over unredacted files would violate victims’ privacy.

At a Wednesday news conference about the filing, Epstein survivor Anouska De Georgiou thanked Torrez for “standing with survivors.” She said, “We have carried this burden for long enough. It is time for those responsible to answer for their part in it.”

Wednesday’s filing names Indyke and Kahn as defendants both individually and in their capacity as co-executors of Epstein’s estate. It also names the estate and the 1953 Trust, which holds the estate’s assets. New Mexico seeks a court order preventing Indyke and Kahn from receiving estate payments while the case proceeds.

The complaint alleges that both men profited personally from their relationships with Epstein. It says that from 2011 to 2019, Indyke received more than $16 million from Epstein and related entities, while Kahn received more than $10 million.

Torrez said Epstein “did not operate alone, and his death does not end the pursuit of accountability for the people who helped make his crimes possible.” He said, “The allegations in this lawsuit describe a network of people and financial arrangements that helped sustain and conceal the abuse of vulnerable girls and young women, including here in New Mexico.” Torrez also said, “Those who allegedly enabled this exploitation should not be allowed to profit from it while survivors continue to live with its consequences.” In the complaint, he wrote that “through this action, the State seeks to hold these individuals accountable to the fullest extent possible and to ensure that Epstein’s assets are distributed to the survivors of his horrific crimes.”

Indyke and Kahn rejected the allegations. An attorney for Indyke, speaking for both men, said they “emphatically reject the New Mexico Attorney General’s allegations that they were complicit in or in any way knowingly facilitated Mr Epstein’s sexual abuse or trafficking.” The statement said the conduct occurred, “by all accounts,” behind closed doors, in secluded locations and at times when neither man was present.

The statement added that “Not a single woman has ever accused Mr Indyke or Mr Kahn of committing sexual abuse or witnessing sexual abuse, nor claimed at any time that she or anyone else reported to them any allegation of Mr Epstein’s abuse.” It said the complaint did not allege otherwise. The attorney also said neither Torrez nor anyone in his office had asked to speak with them before filing what the statement called a “baseless lawsuit,” raising questions about the investigation’s thoroughness and the lawsuit’s motivations.

Zorro Ranch came under renewed scrutiny after the Justice Department released roughly 3 million Epstein-related files late last year and early this year. The Guardian reported in early February that the newly disclosed files contained thousands of references to Zorro Ranch and numerous photographs of men and young girls at the property. The Guardian also reported that several Epstein abuse survivors had previously given testimony about abuse they said they experienced there.

A Guardian investigation also found that, unlike Epstein’s other properties in Palm Beach, in New York and on his private Caribbean island, federal authorities apparently never searched the New Mexico ranch. Epstein died in a Manhattan jail in 2019 while awaiting trial on federal sex-trafficking charges. He bought the property in New Mexico in 1993, and his estate sold it in 2023.

In late February, Torrez announced that New Mexico would reopen its investigation into Epstein’s activities at the ranch. The initial investigation had been put on hold in 2019 at the request of federal prosecutors in New York, who were pursuing the Epstein sex-trafficking operation that led to his arrest. New Mexico authorities launched a search in March. Torrez has said he wants to establish what took place at Zorro Ranch, and state lawmakers established a “truth commission” to examine activities there.


As MSI previously reported, [Indyke and Kahn had told a House committee investigating Epstein that they had no knowledge of Epstein’s crimes during their employment](/articles/2026-03-20-epstein-ex-attorney-says-he-had-no-knowledge-of-abuse-in-house-testimony/). Wednesday’s civil complaint now alleges that they facilitated and concealed those crimes.
