## Existing-home sales fall to slowest annual pace in more than 16 years

The average U.S. 30-year fixed mortgage rate eased this week, falling to 7.21% from 7.28% last week, Freddie Mac said Wednesday. The decline was modest: the rate remained above 7% and near the 2026 high reached last month. A year earlier, the average stood at 6.23%.

Freddie Mac's average 15-year fixed mortgage rate also fell, to 6.54% from 6.66% last week. The 15-year rate was 5.51% a year earlier. Borrowers often seek 15-year fixed mortgages when refinancing a home loan.

The weekly 30-year average has remained above 7% since late September. It reached 7.4% on Oct. 15, the highest level in nearly three years. Wednesday's easing brought the measure below the recent peak but did not return it to the year-earlier level.

Elevated mortgage rates can add hundreds of dollars a month to borrowing costs, limiting homebuyers' purchasing power as affordability challenges continue to sideline many aspiring homeowners. For additional dollar context, FRED's [raw median sales price of houses sold](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MSPUS) was $393,300 as of Nov. 25, 2026. The price series does not calculate a buyer's monthly payment, but it provides context for the affordability pressure described in the rate data.

Mortgage rates are influenced by inflation, Federal Reserve policy and bond-market investors' expectations for the economy, among other factors. Their trajectory generally follows the 10-year Treasury yield, which lenders use to guide the pricing of home loans. The yield was at 4.81% at midday Wednesday.

Associated Press reporting linked the broader climb in mortgage rates to the U.S. war with Iran, which began in late February, saying the conflict pushed up oil prices and drove inflation higher. The rise in borrowing costs then weighed on home sales and deepened a national housing slump that dates to 2022, when mortgage rates began climbing from pandemic-era lows.

Previously occupied U.S. home sales were essentially flat last year, stuck at a 30-year low. Sales declined last month to their slowest annual pace in more than 16 years as mortgage rates surged, adding to the strain on the housing market.

Data pointing to accelerating inflation raised the odds that the Federal Reserve would hike its key interest rate at its next meeting in December. The central bank kept rates unchanged late last month after raising them in September. The Federal Reserve does not set mortgage rates, but bond investors watch its decisions closely, and those decisions can ultimately influence rates on home loans.

For homebuyers, Wednesday's move was a modest easing rather than a reversal: both the 30-year and 15-year averages remained above their year-earlier levels, and the 30-year rate remained above 7%. Inflation data, bond-market expectations and the Federal Reserve's December decision remained key factors shaping home-loan borrowing costs.
