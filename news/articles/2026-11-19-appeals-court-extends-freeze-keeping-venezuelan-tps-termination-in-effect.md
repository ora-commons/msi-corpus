## Trump administration asks Supreme Court to halt Venezuelan TPS restoration order

The U.S. 9th Circuit Court of Appeals extended its freeze on the restoration of Temporary Protected Status for hundreds of thousands of Venezuelan migrants late Wednesday, hours after the Trump administration asked the Supreme Court to halt a lower court's order. As a result, the administration remains free to detain and deport Venezuelans who previously held the status. Federal attorneys said some 1,800 Venezuelans who previously held TPS are in detention and about 330 are subject to deportation proceedings.

The freeze will remain in place until seven days after the appeals court hands down a full ruling, while the administration's emergency appeal is before the Supreme Court.

The sequence moved quickly. The 9th Circuit had yet to rule when the administration returned to the Supreme Court on Wednesday, arguing that a temporary pause in the case would soon expire and allow the restoration of deportation protections. Hours later, the appeals court extended its temporary freeze.

A California-based judge had ruled that the administration's move to end the Venezuelan protections was unlawful. The Supreme Court twice intervened with temporary emergency orders, allowing the administration to temporarily end the program for Venezuelans, and later sent the case back to an appeals court after upholding the end of TPS for Haitians in a different case.

Congress created the TPS program in 1990 to protect migrants from deportation to countries experiencing natural disasters or civil unrest, and designations are granted in periods of up to 18 months. The Trump administration argues that TPS is a temporary program and that Venezuela no longer meets the requirements for its renewal.

During President Donald Trump's second term, the Department of Homeland Security has terminated TPS for more than 1 million people from 13 countries, including Venezuela, Haiti, Honduras, Nicaragua, Afghanistan and Syria.

The Venezuelan case comes as the administration has maintained protections for nearly 200,000 Salvadorans and more than 100,000 people from Ukraine and Sudan, a shift in policy in recent months.

TPS was first granted to Venezuelans in March 2021, under Democratic President Joe Biden, and the designation was expanded in September 2023 to cover additional beneficiaries. About 600,000 Venezuelans have received the protection.

The administration's termination took effect last year, and Venezuelans' work authorizations became invalid when their protections ended. Thousands of Venezuelans faced deportation proceedings, detention and removal, and many others chose to leave the country voluntarily.

It remains unclear how many Venezuelans who previously held TPS are still living in the United States.

The litigation remains in motion. The appeals court has not issued its full ruling, the freeze will run for seven days beyond that decision, and the administration has asked the Supreme Court to intervene.
