## Prosecutors say Markle could face up to 40 years in prison

A federal grand jury in Los Angeles returned the indictment against Thomas Wayne Markle on Tuesday, accusing the estranged father of Meghan, Duchess of Sussex, of keeping child sexual abuse material. Prosecutors said the indictment includes images showing Markle with prepubescent children. A witness identified Markle in at least two photographs with a nude, prepubescent girl, according to the indictment.

If convicted, Markle could be sentenced to up to 40 years in prison, according to a statement shared by Bill Essayli, the top Los Angeles federal prosecutor. Markle is currently held in Florida, and Essayli's office said he will be transferred to Los Angeles in the coming weeks.

Markle was arrested in early November after federal officials said they found dozens of illicit images, including film negatives, slides and books depicting suspected child abuse, in a Los Angeles storage facility.

Court filings alleged Markle possessed similar material in the Philippines, where he has a home. Prosecutors said some of the lewd photographs of the children appear to have been taken there.

Separately, Brig. Gen. Allen Rae Co said in a written statement that Markle is under investigation in the Philippines for possession and intent to distribute illegal child abuse images.

Markle moved to the United States from the Philippines after suffering a stroke earlier this year that resulted in his left leg being amputated. He was arrested Nov. 6 at a senior living facility in Florida.

After initially being granted bond, Markle was detained again days later when he attempted to cut off his ankle monitor in the middle of the night.

The indictment came shortly after a judge ruled Markle mentally sound enough to stand trial, based on an analysis by an expert psychiatrist. The psychiatrist said he understands the charges and can assist his lawyer, two key points in any competency determination.

Markle previously denied any wrongdoing during a 90-minute competency interview in mid-November, according to the report filed by the psychiatrist who conducted the interview. The report quoted Markle as asking, “Why I am here. What have I done?” It also quoted him as saying, “I don’t have any child porn. I have no pictures of naked kids.”

The psychiatrist said Markle was “malingering,” or exaggerating, at times in his responses during the interview. The federal public defender representing Markle did not immediately respond to an emailed request for comment Tuesday afternoon.
