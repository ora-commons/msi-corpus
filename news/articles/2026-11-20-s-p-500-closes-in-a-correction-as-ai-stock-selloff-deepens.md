## Nasdaq turns negative for the year as chip shares plunge

An intense wave of selling in artificial intelligence shares pushed the S&P 500 down 3,7% on Friday and left the benchmark index in what professional investors call a correction, more than 10% below its record high of Oct. 6. The weekly loss was the S&P 500's worst in more than 18 months. The tech-heavy Nasdaq composite fell 5,3% and moved into negative territory for the year, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average, which carries less exposure to technology shares, fell 1,9%.

The last time the S&P 500 entered a correction was in March 2025, when the trade war waged by Donald Trump's administration shook markets. The weekly decline, the index's worst of its kind since April 2025, when the trade war intensified, capped a run of seven straight daily losses, the longest streak since the start of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020.

Chipmakers took the worst of the damage, as they have been among the big beneficiaries of the buildout of AI data centers. Advanced Micro Devices fell 13,3%, Micron Technology dropped 13,9% and Broadcom lost 12,2%. The PHLX Semiconductor index slid 10,7%.

Companies tied to AI deployment beyond chips also sank. Corning, the optical-products company, fell 10,9%, and power supplier Bloom Energy dropped 15,3%.

Nvidia, which many investors treat as the emblem of the AI boom, fell for a ninth day in the last 10 and closed 14% lower. The chip company's stock has been caught in market anxiety about the durability of the AI buildout and, more recently, has been pressed by questions about its future after the sudden departure of Jensen Huang, its former chief executive and co-founder. Since the scandal involving Huang erupted this month, Nvidia shares have fallen more than 30%.

Anthropic's market debut deflated a day after it listed, along with the rest of the technology sector. The maker of the Claude AI tool fell 8,5% to 88,91 dollars a share, still above its initial public offering price of 83 dollars. The offering was the largest IPO in history and at one point carried a value above 2 trillion dollars.

Friday's close left Anthropic's shares roughly 23% below the brief peak of 115,10 dollars a share set the previous day, a deeper early drawdown than Facebook, now Meta, suffered in the days after its May 2012 listing. Volatility after a technology listing is common. SpaceX, which previously held the title of largest IPO, rose nearly 50% in its first three days before falling below its offering price a month later.

AI shares carry high market valuations and an outsized influence on the direction of the market. They were responsible for Wall Street's big gains this year and now sit behind the market's sharp retreat. The bet on AI has been losing momentum in part over concern that the companies' large market values and heavy spending are not justified. Fears of a bubble inside the sector have been aggravated by the results of the recent midterm elections, in which candidates who advocate slowing the construction of data centers won at both the federal and state levels.

Michael Antonelli, a market strategist at Baird, said the whole idea that AI can keep spending unlimited amounts of money and sustain the stock market is ending at this very moment, and that is what the market is discounting now. He said he does not think that means the broader bull market that began in October 2022 has ended.

The selling in technology shares spread around the world. Japan's Nikkei and South Korea's Kospi each fell more than 5%, and European indexes also dropped sharply.

Outside technology, several companies advanced despite the downward pressure on the market. General Mills, the maker of Cheerios, rose 3,7%, and homebuilder Lennar gained 1%. The yield on the 10-year Treasury bond fell to 4,93% from 5,04%. By the close, the S&P 500 had lost 265,44 points on the day and the Dow had dropped 949,73 points.

Brent crude, the international benchmark, rose 0,6% to close at 92,57 dollars a barrel amid persistent tension while the U.S. war with Iran continues. Saudi Arabia reported Friday that rebels had fired more missiles and drones at the kingdom, days after Saudi Arabia launched a major air campaign in areas of Yemen controlled by the rebels.


Friday's retreat extends the AI-led selloff MSI reported on Oct. 22, when [AI-related shares dragged U.S. indexes lower](/articles/2026-10-22-ai-stock-selloff-drags-global-indexes-lower-as-brent-reaches-113-17/) as investors weighed the cost of financing data centers and additional AI chips.
