## Negotiators remain far apart on territory, security guarantees and Europe's role

Russia and Ukraine agreed Wednesday to carry out a large exchange of prisoners of war, both sides said, as two days of U.S.-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi ended without a major breakthrough toward ending Moscow's nearly five-year-old full-scale invasion.

It was the first meeting between the two delegations in nine months. The sides last sat down in February over Russia's invasion, which analysts and humanitarian organizations say has killed hundreds of thousands of soldiers and thousands of civilians. Hopes for significant progress had been low: the countries have swapped prisoners from time to time during the war but have remained far apart on the terms of a peace settlement. Wednesday's session ran just under two hours, after a four-hour session the day before.

"One of the positives is we agreed that we're going to have the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners, which we're going to finalize the numbers tomorrow," said Jared Kushner, U.S. President Donald Trump's son-in-law, after the talks ended.

Vladimir Medinsky, head of the Russian delegation, and Rustem Umerov, who led Kyiv's contingent, confirmed the planned exchange, though Medinsky noted the agreement was "in principle." Alexander Zorin, a deputy chief of the Russian General Staff's information department who attended the talks, told Russian state TV it would be one of the biggest such swaps since 1,000 soldiers from each side returned home last year. The exchange could take place within the next two or three weeks, he said.

Kushner said he and U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff will travel to Moscow on Thursday to meet with President Vladimir Putin, then fly to Dublin, Ireland, to meet Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. U.S. officials say they are determined to revive Washington's peace effort, which so far has failed to deliver significant results. The main hurdles remain postwar security guarantees for both countries, the fate of Russian-occupied Ukrainian territory and what role European countries will play in an eventual settlement.

Zelenskyy called the proposed prisoner exchange a positive step but said Ukraine will not give in to demands that it cede the territory Russia has occupied in exchange for peace.

Europe's place in the process remains unresolved. Umerov said Kyiv's delegation was expected to meet later Wednesday with representatives of European governments, the European Union and NATO who were in the Emirati capital but did not attend the talks. Moscow does not want European countries, which are concerned about Russia's conduct and broader intentions on the continent, involved in the direct negotiations, and Kushner said one goal of the Moscow meeting is to try to get Europeans added to the talks.

In Dublin, Zelenskyy said it was positive that security advisers and senior officials from European governments, the EU and NATO were present in Abu Dhabi even though they were not part of the negotiations. He said there had been a "change in temperature," with Washington warming to the idea of a European role. "Ending this war without Europe's participation would be unjust, and it would be impossible," Zelenskyy said through an interpreter.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said European representatives could eventually join the peace talks but added that their current statements "don't indicate any desire on their part to contribute to movement along the path toward peace." Moscow complains that Europe is financing and supplying weaponry to Ukraine.

Kushner said both Ukraine and Russia need clarity on postwar security arrangements, and that European countries, the EU and NATO must be involved in those discussions to build consensus on next steps. Ukraine wants to ensure Moscow cannot invade again, and EU officials say any discussions on Europe's security arrangements could start only after a ceasefire. Many in Europe view including Russia in those plans as a concession to Putin, who has described NATO's eastward expansion as a top security threat to his country; Moscow has demanded that the alliance pull its troops back from Russia's borders.

Russia's security concerns were discussed at the talks, Kushner said, adding that Moscow was told it needs to provide different de-escalation proposals acceptable to Europe. "The reality is, is there's no trust right now between the sides. And that's to be expected after a conflict like this," he said. In a post on X, he said the prisoner exchange "will reunite families, build trust and create momentum toward peace."

Medinsky said he felt the talks helped Moscow and Kyiv edge closer to peace, and Kremlin foreign affairs adviser Yuri Ushakov described the sessions as "substantive and quite useful."

The context around the negotiations has changed since the sides' February meeting in Geneva. The Trump administration must now reckon with Democrats taking control of the U.S. Congress in January following the midterm elections, and they could demand more support for Ukraine. Russia's wartime economy is under heavy strain, and Ukraine is reeling from devastating aerial attacks on its power grid.

Fighting continues along the front line, where little territory has changed hands this year, with both countries striking each other with long-range missiles and drones. Three people died and 11 were injured after Russia launched a daytime attack on a market in the southern Odesa region, local authorities said. Residential buildings were damaged, said Oleh Kiper, head of the regional military administration.

Overnight, Russia struck the Zaporizhzhia region in southern Ukraine and the eastern city of Kharkiv, wounding seven civilians and damaging property, Ukrainian officials said. Russia's Defense Ministry said it downed 627 Ukrainian drones overnight, 150 of them over the region around St. Petersburg. Regional Gov. Alexander Drozdenko said the drones started a fire at the port of Ust-Luga, a main facility for Russian oil exports, injuring three workers.

Separately, a 49-year-old Indonesian man was reportedly captured on the battlefield in the southern Zaporizhzhia region after saying he was unknowingly recruited to fight for the Russian military. Yvonne Mewengkang, a spokesperson for the Indonesian Ministry of Foreign Affairs, said the country's embassy in Kyiv was seeking access to the man, who is in Ukrainian custody. According to Ukraine's "I Want to Live" project, which offers Russian troops a way to surrender, the man was sent to occupied Donetsk with no military training and then to the Zaporizhzhia region, where his unit was attacked and he was captured. He said he had signed what he believed to be a contract for a year of construction work in Moscow, the group said on Telegram. Ukrainian and Russian officials had no immediate comment.
