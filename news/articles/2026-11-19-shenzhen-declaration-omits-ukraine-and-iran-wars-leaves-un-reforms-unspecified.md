## Three sides backed ICC immunity and set nuclear-risk and oil-supply goals

Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin held the first-ever sit-down of the leaders of the United States, China and Russia in Shenzhen on Thursday during the APEC summit, according to Associated Press. The talks lasted over an hour. Their "peace declaration" called for reforms to the United Nations, increased oil supplies and reduced nuclear risks, but it did not specify the type and extent of the UN reforms. The statement did not mention Russia's war in Ukraine or the U.S.-led war in Iran.

AP said the post-meeting readouts "reflected more symbolism than substance." Post-meeting reports said the three sides also agreed that state officials should enjoy immunity from prosecution, opposing what they called "jurisdiction abuse" by the International Criminal Court.

Trump said Iran and Russia's war against Ukraine were on the agenda. AP reported that China and Russia "carry enormous sway with Tehran and also have been accused by the U.S. of supporting the Islamic Republic."

Trump reiterated that he and Xi agreed Iran needed to fully reopen the Strait of Hormuz. AP described the strait as a "critical waterway for world energy supplies" that had become a "chokepoint since the start of the war." Trump said he and Xi also agreed Iran could not obtain a nuclear weapon. AP reported there were no signs that Trump had secured agreements from Xi or Putin to help solve the Iran conflict.

The leaders' broadly worded agreement did not address China's insistence that it has the right to recover the democratically governed island of Taiwan, possibly by force. China considers Taiwan a domestic issue.

Ruby Osman, a senior policy adviser at London's Tony Blair Institute for Global Change, said China likes to position itself as "a different kind of superpower" from the United States and Russia — "one that doesn't get involved in wars and advocates for smaller states." She said, "Beijing's priority was steering the meeting away from specifics," and added, "Beijing wanted to be seen as the adult in the room."

AP's assessment was that "Xi added to China's already burgeoning diplomatic stature merely by hosting the event." AP said the three-way talks on the sidelines of the APEC summit and Putin's earlier meetings with Trump and Xi offered a "much-coveted chance" to "signal that he heads a superpower on par with the United States and China." AP noted that Putin last attended an APEC summit in 2017. It described his presence as an "honored guest" and Russia's status as a "top global power," at least at the Shenzhen gathering, as marking "a diplomatic breakthrough."

Trump and Putin found common ground in their opposition to the ICC. The court has an arrest warrant for Putin for his alleged war crimes in Ukraine, while the United States opposes an ICC arrest warrant against former Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

Just before the summit, Russia sent a pair of nuclear-capable Tu-95 bombers on a visit to Vietnam. AP described the visit as "a reminder of its nuclear might." As Putin arrived in China, Russian television focused on an officer in his retinue carrying a briefcase with the codes for Moscow's nuclear forces.

After the Shenzhen talks, Trump dialed into a Berlin meeting that included U.S. envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and more than 40 European leaders supporting Ukraine's war effort. Witkoff said Trump told the gathering that he had spoken with Xi and Putin about finding a peaceful end to the Ukraine war.

According to China's Xinhua official news agency, Xi described the talks with Trump and Putin as "positive and fruitful" and said the three nations had "aligned views on safeguarding world peace." Xinhua also reported that the leaders agreed major powers should take the "legitimate security concerns of all countries" seriously and believe differences should be resolved through "negotiation and cooperation." Xi was quoted as saying, "As long as dialogue does not stop, doors remain open, there is hope for peace." He added, "The seeds planted at today's trilateral meeting will surely take root, sprout, blossom and bear fruit."

Trump called the gathering "really making history" and told Xi and Putin the three were "shaking things up" by ditching the "old school" way adversaries engage. According to AP, "The White House set low expectations for the talks." Trump described the meeting as only the beginning and a chance to get the conversation rolling on solving "some of the world's thorniest issues." During a one-on-one meeting before the three-way session, Trump told Putin, "A lot of wonderful things can come out of this, or at least start."

AP described the "carefully crafted event" as a "welcome break" for Trump after a couple of "rotten weeks" for his presidency. AP said his Republican Party had lost the midterm elections, widespread technology outages had "spooked Americans," and Iran had escalated attacks on U.S. allies.

At the start of the three-way talks, Putin said the three nations were "united not only by our status as victors in World War II, but, most importantly, by our shared responsibility for maintaining international peace and security." He noted that the countries were among the founders of the United Nations and added, "we bear responsibility for preserving and strengthening the system of international law." Putin also said, "Our nations share many common interests regarding the resolution of regional conflicts, including, of course, the conflict in Ukraine." Russia's war in Ukraine featured in his bilateral meetings with Trump and Xi, but Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said it was not the main topic of the three-way talks.

During their talks on Wednesday, Putin addressed Xi as "dear friend." On Thursday, Putin "smiled contentedly" as he shook hands with Trump. Trump called him "my friend" and described their "great relationship for many years." The Kremlin released video of Xi escorting the Russian president to his car at the end of the talks and the two men shaking hands. As they parted, Putin told Xi, "I don't think anyone even expected things to go so well." Aamer Madhani reported from Washington and Kanis Leung reported from Hong Kong.
