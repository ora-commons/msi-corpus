## Imperial Brands' announced job cuts could sum to 4,600 as AI use expands

- Imperial Brands is expanding its use of software and AI as cigarette volumes decline.

## Revenue fell 1.5% and profit before tax fell 12.1%

Imperial Brands is reducing a global workforce of about 25,000 through a restructuring that will remove 2,100 roles globally this year and another 2,100 to 2,500 by the end of 2030. Those two announced components could sum to 4,600 job cuts.

The program includes the closure of Imperial Brands’ Langenhagen factory in Germany and the sale of another factory in Taiwan, which have led to 700 job losses. Imperial Brands is also holding consultations about further reductions in several countries. The current cuts affect a small number of jobs in the UK.

Chief executive Lukas Paravicini said the reductions were part of Imperial Brands’ “transformation,” with at least £320m of annual savings targeted by 2030. “We continue to perform and transform,” he said. “We want to become more agile, more data-led, more consumer facing and more efficient.”

Imperial Brands is also increasing its use of technology as it restructures. Paravicini added: “We’re fusing the organisation with more technologies”, including new software and AI.

As cigarette volumes decline, Imperial Brands is shifting toward newer products, including vapes, heated tobacco and oral nicotine. Its brands include Davidoff cigarettes, John Player Special, Golden Virginia, Blu vapes and Rizla.

Imperial Brands faces tax increases in Germany and a ban in the UK on advertising vaping and nicotine products from next June. Paravicini said the company had experienced regulation in other countries and that Blu vape products were growing at double digits in the UK.

“We support regulation on vapes, it will put more clarity in the market,” Paravicini said.

For the year ended September 30, Imperial Brands reported total revenues of £31.7bn, down 1.5%. Profit before tax fell 12.1% to £2.7bn.

Tobacco net revenue grew 1.7% in key markets. Imperial Brands said a 5.1% increase in prices offset a 3.4% drop in volumes.

Earlier this year, British American Tobacco said it would cut about a fifth of its 47,000-strong workforce this year, as the cigarette maker looked for ways to push down costs and become more “technology enabled”. BAT, which is one of the biggest tobacco groups in the world, said it would cut 5,500 jobs by the end of the year and outsource a further 3,500, affecting a total of 9,000 employees.
