## Friday Brussels talks cover food, youth visas and carbon trading

Andy Burnham said he will set out next month the options for reshaping the UK's long-term relationship with the EU. However, it is understood he will not indicate any preference at this stage about which of the long-term options he could back in future. Burnham will travel to a Brussels meeting with the EU on Friday, where both sides hope to unveil agreements on food, youth visas and re-linking British industry to the EU's carbon trading regime. BBC News political reporter Kate Whannel and political correspondent Damian Grammaticas wrote the report.

Burnham described the trip as part of an ongoing negotiation. Speaking to Sky News, he said: "I'm in a negotiation about improving the Brexit deal - that's what I'm doing this week. We'll see how it goes. And then we will consider whether there are options to further the relationship with the EU."

Burnham said the options would be set out "alongside" the 10-year plan for the economy that he has promised to unveil before Parliament breaks for Christmas. Downing Street had been facing pressure to specify the timing after Burnham previously said he would do so "around" a summit taking place in Brussels that week.

In his first major foreign policy speech on Monday night, Burnham said: "After the summit, we will initiate a discussion with our EU partners about options for our long-term relationship". He added: "This will take time, and we will present those options and our assessment of them to inform decisions in the next Parliament." In September, Burnham told the Labour Party conference he would use the period around the summit to lay out what he considered the options for Britain's long-term relationship with European partners.

Asked on Tuesday whether Burnham was "hitting the brakes" and pushing an announcement further back, a No 10 spokesman said: "I don't accept that characterisation." Burnham rejected the idea that he was stepping back, arguing he was "doing exactly what I said." He added: "I will set out next month what the next phase looks like, and that is exactly what I said at my conference speech."

Burnham has previously suggested he could commit Labour to pursuing a much closer future relationship with Europe, including asking to rejoin the EU as a full member. He has ruled out major changes before the next election. Labour's 2024 manifesto promised not to return to the EU single market, customs union or freedom of movement, which allows EU citizens to live and work elsewhere in the bloc without a visa.

Negotiations for the agreements to be unveiled on Friday began under Burnham's predecessor, Sir Keir Starmer. Burnham has indicated he believes the UK's Brexit deal needs to be revised further.

Burnham dismissed a suggestion from former prime minister Boris Johnson that Burnham would be wasting his premiership by reopening arguments about Brexit. Johnson told the Daily Telegraph: "It is madness to escalate the Brexit issue as your political priority." He added: "He will impale himself and his party on this point, and he needs to move off it because he won't win – because the reality is that people are not fools."

Responding to Johnson's comments, Burnham said: "I just don't think he's an authority on these matters." He added: "I'm afraid I'm not going to listen to Boris Johnson on this particular matter. He burned bridges and created a lot of bad feeling, and consequently, Britain suffered as a result of that."

Reform UK leader Nigel Farage MP said Burnham was "turning his back" on his constituents in Makerfield, where two-thirds of residents voted for Brexit in the 2016 referendum. Farage said the UK had not taken the full advantage of Brexit and called the idea of tying the country back to single market rules overseen by the European Court of Justice "nothing less than a democratic outrage."
