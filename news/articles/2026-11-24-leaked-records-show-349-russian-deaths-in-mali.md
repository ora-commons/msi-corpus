## Kidal fell after an April assault that killed 43 Russians

Leaked Russian state death records list 349 Russian deaths in Mali between September 2023 and September 2026, according to a Guardian analysis published on 24 November 2026. Cyber Partisans and Silent Crow obtained the documents, which consisted of death certificates held by Rosstat, Russia’s state statistics agency. The Guardian said most of the listed deaths could be linked to known battles with rebel groups.

The death certificates show the scale of Russian losses under the military support Moscow has provided Mali’s ruling junta, a role initially operated by Wagner and later handed to the Africa Corps. They also trace a sharp reversal around Kidal: a coordinated rebel assault drove Russian and Malian forces from the city in April, and Malian troops with the Africa Corps returned in October.

The attack on Kidal, a remote desert city nestled among the rocky hills of northern Mali, began before dawn on 25 April. Russian fighters were caught off guard. Within the first hour, checkpoints on the outskirts had fallen to insurgents from the al-Qaida-linked JNIM and the Tuareg-led Azawad Liberation Front, who pushed toward the police station, where fierce fighting erupted.

A former member of Russia’s Africa Corps who was stationed in Mali and spoke on condition of anonymity recalled: “Everything was happening at once. Explosions were going off all around us, people were shouting” before adding, “Nobody knew where the enemy was coming from.”

Russian-led forces and their Malian allies faced the coordinated surprise assault. The rebels used suicide car bombs and drones to break through the city’s defences.

Rosstat’s records show 43 Russians died on 25 April, including six in Kidal and one in nearby Aguelhok. Other deaths were recorded in Bamako and in Gao, where rebels shot down a Russian military helicopter that day. By the following morning, Russian forces had abandoned Kidal, which Russian mercenaries had helped capture in November 2023 and Moscow had since presented as a showcase of its growing influence in Africa.

Despite the Russian presence, Mali’s security situation has continued to deteriorate. Wagner first arrived in late 2021, a year after a military coup toppled President Ibrahim Boubacar Keïta. French forces were ordered to leave as relations with Mali’s military government deteriorated, bringing to an end France’s decade-long counter-terrorism operation and leaving Russia as the main foreign protector of Mali’s military government.

Wagner handed its operations in Mali to the Africa Corps in June 2025, two years after Yevgeny Prigozhin led a failed mutiny on Moscow that discredited the group in the Kremlin’s eyes. By that summer, British intelligence estimated about 2,000 Africa Corps personnel were deployed in the country. The Africa Corps, closely overseen by Russian military intelligence, currently provides backing to several African governments, including Mali’s ruling junta, in return for payment or contracts granting access to natural resources. The deployment in Mali has become Russia’s largest military deployment in Africa.

In late July 2024, Russian and Malian forces fought Tuareg separatists for several days near Tinzaouaten, on the Algerian border. Wagner issued a rare statement admitting losses, including the death of a commander, but gave no total. Estimates from Russian sources ranged from about 20 to more than 80 dead. Rosstat’s records list 70 Russians who died on 27 July 2024, all but one of them in the Kidal region. That made the battle of Tinzaouaten the deadliest single day for Russian forces in Africa since Wagner mercenaries first deployed to the continent in 2017.

The records are probably an undercount because registration often lags. They list no Russian deaths in Mali in March 2026, although at least three Africa Corps fighters were reported killed in an ambush near Nampala that month. The Guardian said the toll may have been driven up by Ukraine’s extension of its war against Russia into the Sahel. Two people with knowledge of the operations said Kyiv was assisting Tuareg rebels there, and CNN reported that about 15 Ukrainian specialists had been deployed to Mali to help local rebel forces fight Russian troops.

The leaked data also shows Russian military activity elsewhere in Africa. In the Central African Republic, where Wagner fighters guard President Faustin-Archange Touadéra, the records account for 30 deaths, although not all could be linked to known incidents; three men died on the night drones struck Wagner camps in Kaga-Bandoro in December 2023. In Libya, Russian forces back the eastern commander Khalifa Haftar and use his airbases as a supply route for deployments in the Sahel. Some recorded deaths were probably linked to a helicopter crash at the Maaten al-Sarra base in February 2026. On Sunday, Ukrainian drones sank a Russian warship off the coast of Libya, though it was unclear whether there were casualties.

In Niger, where the Africa Corps moved in after the military junta expelled French and US troops, a Russian fighter died two days after Islamic State militants attacked Niamey airport in January. The death was not previously reported.

The Guardian reported that Moscow had in recent months stepped up its effort to show it was back in control in Mali. On 6 October, Malian troops and the Africa Corps recaptured Kidal after the army called the operation an air and ground campaign. In a report from this month, November 2026, the US-based Conflict Insights Group found that the Africa Corps had sharply increased its use of drones in Mali, including Lancet suicide drones, amid an intensifying air campaign. The group wrote that the drone campaign had also targeted civilians, boosting support for the rebels. “Civilian deaths in the intensified air campaign, which the Africa Corps leads, are likely to aid insurgent recruitment,” the group said.

Two Western officials who travelled to Mali and were shown the data said the death toll was higher than previously estimated. One official, speaking on condition of anonymity because the official was not authorised to comment publicly, said: “That’s over 10% of Russia’s forces killed in action.”

A Western diplomat who works in the region said the late-April attacks had punctured the image of Russian military invincibility that Moscow and the junta had cultivated among Malians. “Russia still wields considerable influence in Mali, but patience with its military performance is wearing thin.” Moscow does not publish its losses in Africa, and its foreign ministry did not respond to a request for comment.
