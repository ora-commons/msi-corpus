## White House puts the wartime reunification total at 43 children

First lady Melania Trump helped return a seventh group of Ukrainian and Russian children to their families, reuniting nine children and raising the total number of children returned to relatives to 43, the White House announced Wednesday. Melania Trump said the reunification took place with the "full cooperation" of Russian and Ukrainian leaders.

The latest return is part of a series of interventions the first lady has made in Russia's war in Ukraine, which began nearly five years ago. President Donald Trump had vowed to broker a quick end to the conflict but has said it has been harder than he expected.

In a statement shared first with The Associated Press, Melania Trump said, "Children in this region of the world, like children everywhere, cannot possibly understand why geography and government have put them into the horrors of war." She said, "As adults, it is our moral imperative to elevate our children above the divisions that power war in order to restore the simple joys of life, immediately."

Helping children in conflict zones has become one of the first lady's signature issues during her husband's second term. Earlier this year, she hosted a U.N. Security Council meeting on the issue.

The effort began in August 2025, when Melania Trump had Republican President Donald Trump hand-deliver a letter calling for peace in Ukraine to Russian President Vladimir Putin during a summit in Alaska. After a year, she had helped nearly three dozen children return to relatives after they had been separated because of the war, and she has said she has a "direct channel" with the leaders of the warring countries.

In October, Melania Trump helped negotiate a six-day ceasefire for the Russian-occupied city of Oleshky in southern Ukraine. Earlier this month, she hosted Russian and Ukrainian officials together at the White House to mark that milestone.

The local ceasefire in the war-divided region of Kherson in southern Ukraine was in effect from Oct. 27 to Nov. 1 and amounted to the most significant truce since Russia launched its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022.

During the pause in the war, some 800 people were able to leave Oleshky while food, water and other humanitarian aid were delivered to help those who stayed.

No additional information about the nine children was made available. The latest return brought the White House's announced total to 43 as the war in Ukraine continued nearly five years after Russia launched its full-scale invasion in February 2022.
