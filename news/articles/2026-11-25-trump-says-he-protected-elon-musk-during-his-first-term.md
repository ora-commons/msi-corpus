## Trump says he protected Elon Musk during his first term

**Subtype:** fact

- President Donald Trump claimed in a Monday CNBC interview that his first-term intervention protected Elon Musk.
- Trump did not specify how he protected Musk or identify the “tough incident” behind the claim.
- Musk had some tangles with the Securities and Exchange Commission during Trump's first term.
- Trump said, “we have to protect our brilliant people because we don’t have enough.”
- The Wall Street Journal reported that Trump’s intervention on behalf of Nvidia Chief Executive Jensen Huang has drawn objections from Republicans and Democrats in Congress.
- Huang is scheduled for at least two congressional appearances next week.

## Huang heads to Congress for at least two appearances next week

Trump made the claim while intervening on behalf of Nvidia Chief Executive Jensen Huang. He offered no details about what protection he provided to Musk or what the “certain incident” involved. His comments added Musk as a claimed precedent for his defense of Huang.

Trump said, “I protected Elon in my first term, very big. Elon was protected.” He then praised Musk, saying, “I talk about it, and I’m proud of Elon. I love Elon. I think he’s great.” He described Musk as “80% supergenius, 20% he has other problems,” and said, “You wouldn’t have an Elon if it wasn’t for me.”

Trump also said, “we have to protect our brilliant people because we don’t have enough.” The line linked his defense of Huang with his claimed protection of Musk and presented both cases as part of a broader rationale for protecting scarce business talent.

The Wall Street Journal noted that Musk had some “tangles with the Securities and Exchange Commission” during Trump’s first term. The outlet said it was hard to imagine that a Trump intervention in those matters might have saved Musk’s entire career.

The Wall Street Journal noted, perhaps coincidentally, that Jay Clayton was the SEC chairman during Trump’s first term, is now Trump’s director of national intelligence and has played a role in the Huang matter.

Trump’s intervention on Huang’s behalf has drawn objections from Republicans and Democrats in Congress, The Wall Street Journal reported. Huang is due before Congress next week for at least two appearances. Unless things change, the report said, he will be met by “the sound and the fury of Capitol Hill.” The appearances could test how far Trump’s protection extends.

The Wall Street Journal said Trump’s explanation sounded similar to his account in a tape-recorded 2020 interview with Bob Woodward about Saudi Arabia’s Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman. Trump told Woodward, “I saved his a—.” When Woodward asked whose “a—” was saved, Trump replied, “MBS.” He said Congress was “coming down on him very strongly,” but he was “able to get Congress to leave him alone.”


Trump’s new claim extends [MSI’s Nov. 24 report on his defense of Huang](/articles/2026-11-24-trump-says-the-country-needs-more-geniuses-as-nvidia-reinstates-huang/) by identifying Musk as another technology executive he says he protected. The new account leaves the specific action in the Musk case unexplained while placing Trump’s support for Huang before congressional scrutiny.
