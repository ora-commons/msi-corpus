## Concertgoers describe choking; show-over announcement came almost an hour later

The North West Ambulance Service said more than 80 people were assessed at Co-op Live in Manchester, England, after an unknown irritant was released in the crowd during a Don Toliver concert, with some reporting eye and respiratory symptoms. Authorities said over 80 people were treated by medics, and one person was taken to a hospital. The ambulance service said, "decontamination is being carried out as a precaution" at the venue.

Footage shared on social media appeared to show concertgoers coughing and covering their mouths as they left the arena. Ben Stevenson, who was in the standing area, told the Manchester Evening News he "started coughing and (my) eyes were kind of itchy."

Stevenson said Toliver left the stage and the venue lights came on, but it was not until almost an hour later that an announcement said, "due to an unforeseen issue, the show is over, and please make your way out at your own pace."

Joey Howe, another concertgoer, told BBC Radio Manchester there was a "horrible smell" and people rushed out of the venue. "People were coming through in wheelchairs, choking. It was really distressing," he said.

More than 12,000 fans were evacuated from the concert, authorities said Monday. Greater Manchester Police said officers were called around 10 p.m. (2200 GMT) Sunday to the arena after reports of people being affected by a substance initially believed to be tear gas. The force declared a "major incident," which allows different emergency services to coordinate their response.

Police Assistant Chief Constable Steph Parker said: "Anyone who has left the event and since become unwell is advised to attend their local hospital." She added: "We will now begin to fully understand what this substance was and how this incident came about."

Co-op Live said in a statement that venue bosses were working with police to identify the cause of the incident. The arena, which opened in 2024, is about 2 miles (3.2 kilometers) from Manchester Arena, where 22 people were killed when a bomber blew himself up during an Ariana Grande concert in 2017.

Tom Wells, a spokesman for Prime Minister Andy Burnham, said the incident was "deeply worrying" and added that "many families will understandably be anxious this morning." He urged people to let police conduct a full investigation.

American rapper Don Toliver is a Grammy Award-nominated trap and R&B rapper-singer who features on the soundtrack to the new "Grand Theft Auto" game. He was scheduled to perform at London's O2 Arena on Monday and Wembley Arena on Tuesday as part of his Nitrous tour.
