## Indictment alleges at least $510 million in servers diverted to China

Jay Puri, who recently stepped aside as Nvidia's head of global sales, married Cenly Chen, who was Super Micro's chief growth officer, on March 7 in Big Sur, Calif., after the two had dated for years while their companies did business, according to current and former co-workers and marriage records. The Wall Street Journal reported that FBI agents recently interviewed a person familiar with Super Micro and sought information about senior executives, including Chen, according to the person who was questioned. Chen said she had not been contacted by federal law enforcement. Super Micro was not named as a defendant and said it was a victim of the alleged scheme.

Puri, 72, joined Nvidia in 2005 after two decades at Sun Microsystems and roles at other technology companies. While dating Chen, he headed Nvidia's global sales organization, which was responsible for deciding how the latest chips were allocated among customers. In July, Puri posted on LinkedIn that he was stepping back from the role but would remain a senior adviser to Nvidia.

Chen, 50, had spent almost two decades at Super Micro before leaving the San Jose, Calif., company. She joined in 2008 and climbed the sales ranks after graduating from Fudan University in China and working at U.S. telecommunications and technology companies. As chief growth officer, she oversaw the sales team handling some of Super Micro's largest customers.

At Nvidia's annual conference in March, Chen discussed the companies' close ties. “We have the next generation of AI factories based on Nvidia’s technology,” she said in a video posted on Super Micro's YouTube channel. Weeks after the conference, Chen left Super Micro and was named CEO of cloud-services company ChronoScale, which was spun out of Applied Digital, another Nvidia partner.

Chen regularly dealt with Puri and accompanied Super Micro's chief executive when he visited Nvidia's offices and pressed Nvidia to supply chips for Super Micro customers, employees at both companies said. During Super Micro sales meetings, Chen encouraged team members to approach her with Nvidia-related questions, serving as the go-to contact. People who worked with Puri and Chen described their relationship as an open secret. They were photographed together at the San Francisco Opera's annual masked gala in 2024, posing and smiling arm in arm. A few weeks after their marriage and the Super Micro indictment was unsealed, the couple was photographed together in April at a California Academy of Sciences gala with other Nvidia executives and their spouses.

Chen declined to discuss her personal life, saying it had no bearing on her professional life. “I never had to rely on personal relationships for professional gain,” she wrote in an email. She also said she dealt with Super Micro's customers, that customers decided which chips they bought, and that she was not involved in selecting or procuring the chips.

Super Micro said Chen did not manage the company's overall relationship with Nvidia or its procurement of Nvidia GPUs. The company said chip allocations were determined through a process involving multiple departments and that its relationship with Nvidia spanned dozens of teams, functions and projects. Nvidia and Puri did not respond to requests for comment.

The Justice Department is investigating Super Micro over allegations of a conspiracy to smuggle servers with high-end Nvidia chips into China. On March 19, the department unsealed charges against Super Micro co-founder Yih-Shyan “Wally” Liaw, a Super Micro sales manager and a third-party broker. The indictment accused the men of conspiring to smuggle more than $500 million worth of servers containing high-end Nvidia chips into China.

The indictment alleges that Liaw and the two other defendants directed an unidentified company in Southeast Asia to place orders for about $2.5 billion worth of Super Micro servers in 2024 and 2025, then diverted at least $510 million worth of those servers to China. Federal prosecutors in the Southern District of New York brought the case, and Jay Clayton, now President Trump's director of national intelligence, signed the indictment. Liaw has pleaded not guilty and resigned from Super Micro's board in March. The third-party broker pleaded guilty as part of an agreement with prosecutors, according to court filings. The third defendant, a Super Micro sales manager based in Taiwan, has not appeared in court, and it could not be determined whether the manager has an attorney. Attorneys for Liaw and the broker declined to comment.

Super Micro, which has annual sales of around $40 billion, makes data-center servers loaded with chips from Nvidia, Intel and other suppliers. The company said it was cooperating with authorities and had cut ties with the individuals who were charged. In August, Super Micro said a review of customer transactions led by independent board members found no evidence that any current member of senior management knew of the alleged diversion. The company said its compliance personnel had acted in good faith to mitigate the risk of products being diverted to restricted locations.

The relationship was brought into public focus by a shareholder lawsuit alleging that Chen played a role in Super Micro's acquisition of Nvidia chips. Short seller Muddy Waters also highlighted the couple's ties in a research report earlier in the month, and The Information had previously reported the relationship.

Nvidia has faced disclosures that Chief Executive Officer Jensen Huang had an extramarital affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at Megaspeed International. Some U.S. officials suspected that Alice Huang had worked with Chinese intelligence, though she no longer worked at Megaspeed and could not be reached for comment. The Wall Street Journal reported that both Megaspeed and Super Micro were dealing with Justice Department investigations into allegations of chip smuggling into China. Megaspeed said it was compliant and had hired outside counsel to investigate the allegations.

The report also said Huang resigned from Nvidia but returned this week after President Trump intervened with Nvidia's board and U.S. officials relayed that he did not pose a national security risk. Upon his return, Huang disclosed additional affairs to the board and told staff he had violated Nvidia's code of conduct. Huang and Puri cultivated a network of dozens of partners, including Megaspeed in Asia and Super Micro in California, that could feed China's need for AI data centers. The network set up data centers in places such as Malaysia that customers including Alibaba Group could access remotely.

Wes Cummins, CEO of Applied Digital and chairman of ChronoScale, said Chen had been instrumental in helping Applied Digital establish its cloud business while at Super Micro. She “went to bat” with Nvidia on behalf of Applied Digital and Super Micro's other customers, Cummins said, while praising her as highly competent. He acknowledged that her relationship with Puri created the appearance of a conflict of interest but said he was unaware that Super Micro had received any special treatment from Nvidia.

“Salespeople for servers want to get those delivered to their customers,” Cummins said. He said Nvidia takes conflicts of interest seriously and that Chen's relationship with Puri had prevented ChronoScale and Applied Digital from taking advantage of some Nvidia assistance to customers because of the disclosed conflict of interest. Super Micro was not named as a defendant, and Chen was not named in the indictment. Super Micro said in August that a review of customer transactions led by independent board members found no evidence that any current member of Super Micro's senior management team knew of the alleged scheme.
