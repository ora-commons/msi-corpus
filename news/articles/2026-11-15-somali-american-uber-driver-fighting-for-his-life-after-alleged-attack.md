## Hopkins police took a male suspect into custody early Saturday

A Somali-American Uber driver is fighting for his life after a passenger allegedly choked him from behind, seized control of his car and used it to assault him early Saturday in Hopkins, a suburb south-west of Minneapolis. Mohamed Yusuf's family says the attack "appears to be a hate crime."

The Hopkins police department said officers responded to a report of a suspicious vehicle at 1.20am on Saturday. "Officers located an adult male suspect nearby and took him into custody," the police department stated. The department noted that preliminary information indicates the incident involved an Uber ride share interaction and that the investigation remains ongoing.

The account of the assault itself comes from Yusuf's relatives, who spoke to the Somali Brief, a digital news site run by two Somali American journalists. Yusuf's cousin, Abdinasir Ali, told the outlet that the attack took place after a passenger asked Yusuf's name and then said he did not like the name Mohamed.

The passenger allegedly then choked the driver from behind, took control of the car and used it to assault the driver. "He was run over multiple times with his own vehicle," Yusuf's son, Mustafa Osman, told the Somali Brief.

Osman said the badly injured driver was later found a mile down the road, in a ditch, with broken legs and ribs, two collapsed lungs and a spinal injury. A GoFundMe appeal created on behalf of Yusuf and his family states that he is now in a coma, fighting for his life.

The Hopkins police department has not released a charging decision, and its statement described the investigation as ongoing.

One member of Yusuf's family, speaking to the Somali Brief at the hospital, called the assault "a pure hate crime" and said "our politicians created this hostile environment." The family member was not identified in the Guardian's report.

The alleged assault comes amid heated political rhetoric about Somali Americans in Minnesota. The Guardian reported that Donald Trump made claims about Somali Americans in Minnesota a central focus of his midterm campaign rallies, and that the president's claims have been echoed by Minnesota Republicans.

Tom Emmer, the House majority whip from Minnesota, recently said on a conservative podcast: "I've got basically the Somali civil war that's come to the state of Minnesota."

Omar Fateh, a state senator in Minnesota, called for an investigation in a statement on social media. "Mohamed Yusuf should be home safe with his family, not fighting for his life after an attack fueled by hatred towards his Somali identity," Fateh said. "We need answers and an immediate and transparent investigation."

SEIU Local 26, a local labor union that organizes rideshare drivers, said in a social media post: "We are shocked, angered and saddened by the news of the alleged attack that happened this weekend on Mohamed, a Minnesotan and Uber driver."

The Minnesota chapter of the Council on American-Islamic Relations, community leaders, elected officials and ride-share driver groups were planning a press conference on Monday at the Hennepin County Medical Center to call for justice.

Uber said it had acted against the passenger. "We are shocked and horrified by the details of this senseless attack," a spokesperson for Uber said in an email. "We banned the rider from the Uber platform, and we are keeping the driver and his loved ones in our thoughts. We stand ready to assist law enforcement with their investigation."

A GoFundMe created on behalf of Yusuf and his family had already raised over $140,000. "As a result of this senseless assault, he suffered severe injuries and is now in a coma, fighting for his life," the family wrote in the appeal for financial support.
