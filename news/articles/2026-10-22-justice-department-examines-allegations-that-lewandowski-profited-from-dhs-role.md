## Contract evidence anchors inquiry; earlier report covered foreign hiring requests

The Justice Department is examining allegations that Corey Lewandowski profited from his top position at the Department of Homeland Security, according to people familiar with the matter. People familiar with the matter said the review is based on a referral from the DHS inspector general, which shared its findings about Lewandowski’s tenure in recent days.

Investigators with the DHS inspector general’s office found evidence that Lewandowski may have been involved in improperly awarding government contracts, The Wall Street Journal reported in July. The office, the agency’s internal watchdog, had been weighing a criminal referral to the Justice Department for months.

Investigators looked into many of the contracts the department signed during the tenure of then-Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem, and department officials had been surprised at how involved Lewandowski was in the contracting process, some of the people said. People who reviewed those documents or had knowledge of the approvals said Lewandowski personally signed specific contracts despite not technically serving as a full-time U.S. government official.

He was working in an unpaid capacity as a “Special Government Employee,” a designation in federal ethics law meant to bring outside officials with particular expertise into the government on a temporary and part-time basis, without forcing them to relinquish outside work or investments.

Lewandowski or close associates signed off on nearly all department spending decisions for amounts exceeding $100,000, The Wall Street Journal reported. That authority gave him visibility into the array of contractors DHS worked with and the ability to hold the contracts up.

Lewandowski served as the top adviser to then-Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem until the two were ousted from DHS in March after revelations about infighting at the agency and their spending decisions.

The Wall Street Journal described the review as the first publicly known instance of Trump’s second-term Justice Department scrutinizing someone close to the president.

In earlier reporting, The Wall Street Journal said Lewandowski appeared to try to use his influence for personal financial gain while at DHS, including asking several countries in the Middle East to hire him on the side to assist with visa issues and other matters.

Current Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin has received briefings on the inquiry, according to people familiar with the matter.

Lewandowski previously denied seeking foreign contracts while in government or playing a role in approving DHS contracts. A representative for Lewandowski said the Justice Department has not contacted him regarding any investigation. “He denies any wrongdoing when he was proudly serving his country in a voluntary capacity,” the representative said. The DHS inspector general’s office said it does not discuss specific investigative matters or confirm or deny the existence of an investigation.

The Wall Street Journal reported that since Trump took office last year, Justice Department officials have largely focused on trying to build cases against many of “Trump’s political adversaries and other favored targets” and have dropped cases involving people with ties to the administration.

More broadly, the Justice Department has curtailed efforts to pursue cases of fraud and corruption against public officials, amid criticism from Trump and other administration officials who said such prosecutions were politically motivated. A unit focused on such investigations is down to two prosecutors after roughly two dozen others left the department or transferred to other roles.

The Wall Street Journal reported that Lewandowski served as Trump’s first campaign manager in 2016 and has maintained a close relationship over the years with the president, who valued Lewandowski’s loyalty.

The newspaper previously reported that Lewandowski told people close to him that he expected the president would pardon him if a criminal case were brought. A lawyer for Lewandowski said Lewandowski did not discuss or suggest that he would seek or need a presidential pardon.
