## Third-country data centers face customer checks and China-specific restrictions

NPR reported that the Treasury Department and National Security Council wrote and directed the rule Commerce announced. NPR reported that Commerce has a role in promoting overseas sales, while Treasury has favored tighter restrictions on China. The controversy involving Nvidia drew attention to that difference.

The rule requires data centers in third countries to police the customers they serve and subjects data centers doing business with China to additional restrictions. It also limits advanced chip sales to Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand, which are seen at high risk of having advanced chips diverted to China.

NPR reported that the federal government already prohibits the sale of the most advanced AI chips to China, which the administration regards as both an economic and security rival. Critics said the existing controls were too lax because data centers in places such as Malaysia could obtain advanced chips and make their computing power available remotely to Chinese users. NPR said the new rule was designed to close that gap by stopping China from gaining remote access to the most powerful chips.

The controls came under scrutiny after Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang stepped down last week. News reports tied Huang to an affair with a woman suspected of spying for China. China denied the allegations, and NPR did not independently confirm reports tying Huang to a woman suspected of spying for China.

The woman formerly worked for Megaspeed, a Nvidia customer that bought many advanced chips for its operating data centers in Malaysia. Megaspeed said the woman left the company over a year ago and insisted that the company follows all existing regulations.

Some administration officials worried that the existing regulations were not strong enough and had allowed China to use the third-country workaround. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said on CNBC last week that he and others wanted to cut off Megaspeed's access to chips to prevent that computing power from being used remotely by China.

Bessent said, “Any leakage is very frustrating because it hurts our great companies, and the AI race gives the Chinese an advantage.”

President Donald Trump said on November 16 that the Commerce and Treasury secretaries held very different views about how to deal with China. In an archived recording, he said: “One view is you don't want to insult China. We have a very good relationship with China, and you don't want to insult them. And the other view is just go, you know, down and dirty and do what you have to do. Both - can't say either is wrong or right. We'll let you know.”

Both the Commerce secretary and the Treasury secretary are accompanying Trump on his trip to Asia this week. NPR said the trip should give them time to discuss the differences on Air Force One.
