## Kreiss-Tomkins finished first in August's Alaska primary

Republican U.S. Sen. Lisa Murkowski endorsed Democrat Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins a week before Alaska's gubernatorial election, and three former governors — Democrat Tony Knowles, independent Bill Walker and Republican Frank Murkowski, the senator's father — also backed him. The Associated Press called the race Friday for Kreiss-Tomkins, a former state lawmaker, over three Republicans, including one endorsed by President Donald Trump.

Murkowski, who has at times been at odds with her party, including Trump, said Kreiss-Tomkins "understands that governing requires listening to people with different perspectives and finding common ground to solve the problems all of us face."

Knowles, the state's last Democratic governor, left office in 2002 after serving back-to-back terms. Walker served from 2014 to 2018.

Kreiss-Tomkins, often referred to as JKT, completed a political comeback three years after leaving the state House. He traveled throughout Alaska to build his coalition and focused on affordability, advocated for full funding of public schools and called for all oil and gas producers to pay corporate income tax. He also sought a moratorium on new data centers until state lawmakers pass regulations for their development.

He said Alaska had become "broken" under eight years of outgoing Republican Gov. Mike Dunleavy and needed a change. He cited among other issues a state ferry system that he said has been left in shambles, with routes to many communities cut drastically. The ferry is a key mode of transportation for southeast Alaska communities not connected by roads, some of which also have limited air service.

Kreiss-Tomkins, 37, emphasized a willingness to work across party lines and said he wanted a Cabinet with Republicans, Democrats and independents. "If they're competent, if they're service-minded, if they're entrepreneurial, if they're problem-solvers, you're on the team. Game on," he said during a candidate forum last month. "Let's go, and let's get Alaska back on track." He also said compromise "is a virtue, not a dirty word," and said he saw the mission of the new governor as working with lawmakers to resolve longstanding problems in the state.

Kreiss-Tomkins finished first in August's open primary, followed by a second Democrat, Tom Begich, who withdrew from the race. Begich's campaign at the time said it had concluded that the "strongest path to victory is to bring Democratic voters, donors and resources together behind one candidate." Wilson finished third in the primary.

Many Republicans saw ranking as their party's best shot of retaining the governor's seat. It was a message that conflicted with that of Trump, who has consistently complained about Alaska's unusual voting method, including during a rally in Alaska in October.

Kreiss-Tomkins defeated Republicans Bernadette Wilson, Dave Bronson and Treg Taylor. Trump had endorsed Wilson, a business owner, and criticized the other Republican candidates for staying in the race. Bronson is a former mayor of Anchorage, Alaska's largest city, while Taylor was a state attorney general under Dunleavy.

Bronson congratulated Kreiss-Tomkins on social media and wished him success. A message seeking comment from Wilson's campaign was not immediately returned. His campaign did not immediately respond to requests for comment after The Associated Press called the race.

In a statement declaring victory earlier Friday, Kreiss-Tomkins said voters wanted change and "a government that works across party lines to move Alaska forward. Our administration will not waste a single day getting to work to solve problems for Alaska." He said his campaign had more than 10,000 donors.

Kreiss-Tomkins's victory makes him the first Democrat elected Alaska governor since 1998 and part of a national midterm elections wave that gave the party sweeping wins in state governments across the country. Democrats will control the governor's office in 30 states, including several they flipped after years of Republican control in places such as Iowa, Georgia, Ohio and Texas. In Juneau, the result ends eight years of Republican rule under Dunleavy. Kreiss-Tomkins will be sworn in Dec. 7 with Zac Johnson, an independent, as his lieutenant governor.
