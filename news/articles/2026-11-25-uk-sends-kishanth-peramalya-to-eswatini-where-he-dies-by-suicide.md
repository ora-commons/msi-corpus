## Eswatini government says the temporary arrangement did not become a long-term agreement

The UK sent Kishanth Peramalya to Eswatini after denying the Sri Lankan asylum seeker entry because of his criminal convictions, The Guardian reported. Peramalya, who had no connection to the southern African country, died by suicide there on 13 September. Eswatini is landlocked by South Africa and Mozambique and was formerly known as Swaziland.

Peramalya was among a group that set sail from India hoping to reach Canada and claim asylum. The Royal Navy rescued the boat after it got into difficulty in October 2021 and brought it to Diego Garcia in the remote British Indian Ocean Territory, which hosts a US-UK military base.

In November 2024, after a UN report about conditions in the camp, the UK allowed 61 migrants to enter from Diego Garcia. Then-home secretary Yvette Cooper denied Peramalya entry because he had four counts of sexual assault, one count of assault occasioning actual bodily harm and one count of arson. UK courts upheld Cooper's decision.

Peramalya had been granted protection from being returned to Sri Lanka after he said Sri Lankan military personnel tortured and sexually abused him as a teenager. In late March, the Maldives said it would take him for 30 days, according to the BBC. Peramalya was then transferred to Eswatini.

The BBC reported that a draft agreement between the UK and Eswatini governments would have committed Downing Street to covering Peramalya's resettlement costs for three years. The BBC also said the UK was finalizing arrangements for Peramalya to live in Eswatini permanently. The Guardian said it had not seen the draft deal.

Thabile Mdluli, a spokesperson for Eswatini's government, said: “The initial arrangement was temporary in nature and intended to safeguard Mr Peramalya’s safety while the government of the United Kingdom worked towards a long-term solution regarding his circumstances.” Mdluli said no formal agreement was reached over Peramalya's long-term residency status with the UK and declined to comment further on the specific arrangements of his stay or the circumstances of his death.

Mdluli said Eswatini had not received any other similar request from the UK government. Mdluli said the Eswatini government remained committed to ensuring agreements with external partners safeguard national interest and the safety and security of Emaswati, the Swazi people.

A UK government spokesperson said keeping the British public safe would always be the first priority. The spokesperson said the government would continue to take steps to ensure what it called “high harm foreign criminals” could not enter the UK and would continue working with Eswatini authorities in the case.

A UK High Court judgment from February 2025 said Peramalya had post-traumatic stress disorder and had attempted suicide multiple times on Diego Garcia.

Peramalya's family told the BBC he was initially housed alone in an isolated guesthouse in Eswatini. His sister, speaking from India, said, “My brother did not want to go there at all.” She added, “He couldn’t manage being there alone, he was very lonely.”

Peramalya called his sister four times a day and initially attended weekly psychiatric appointments, which became daily as his mental health deteriorated, his family told the BBC. Someone was brought in to prepare his food. Relatives said they were not aware of his convictions for sexual offences or any plan to make his Eswatini transfer permanent.

Separately, Eswatini had accepted dozens of third-country deportees from the US since July 2025. In November 2025, the Eswatini government confirmed it had received $5.1m in exchange for taking up to 160 migrants from the US.
