## Lawsuit alleges new panels diluted stories of nine enslaved people

Two Philadelphia history groups sued the Trump administration on Monday in federal court in Pennsylvania over changes to the President’s House/Slavery Memorial at the site of George Washington’s home. Avenging the Ancestors Coalition and the Black Journey: African American Walking Tour of Philadelphia characterize the changes as a “whitewashed depiction” of slavery.

The lawsuit names the Interior Department and Secretary Doug Burgum, as well as the National Park Service and Acting Director Jessica Bowron. It challenges changes made earlier this year to an exhibit that tells the story of nine enslaved people living in the home with George and Martha Washington during the 1790s, when Philadelphia was briefly the national capital.

Avenging the Ancestors Coalition says the changes violate the Equal Protection component of the Fifth Amendment and the Administrative Procedure Act. The act, in part, authorizes a court to set aside agency action that is arbitrary, capricious, an abuse of discretion or does not follow the law.

Glass panels installed at the exhibit in 2010 originally presented the lives of the nine enslaved people. The Trump administration began removing those panels earlier this year. Philadelphia filed a lawsuit, and a lower court forced the federal government to stop in February while the city appealed the changes.

A federal appeals court panel reversed that order on July 3 and ruled that the work could continue. According to the lawsuit, the National Park Service later began installing interpretive panels for a new exhibit at the President’s House site.

The complaint says the new panels “dilute and de-emphasize” the original meaning of the exhibit. The original display described hardships faced by the nine enslaved people, including physical and sexual violence, which the lawsuit says were watered down or completely omitted in the replacement exhibit.

The complaint also states: “Moreover, the original glass panels that were removed have not been replaced or restored, and the motion-activated videos remain turned off.”

Black Journey: African American Walking Tour of Philadelphia asks the federal court to return the memorial to its original condition and to permanently restrain the Trump administration from enforcing or implementing the orders that led to this year’s changes.

The lawsuit comes amid an administration effort against diversity, equity and inclusion initiatives throughout the federal government, including in historic and cultural institutions, education, medicine and private businesses. An executive order signed by Donald Trump in 2025 called for federally owned or controlled historic sites not to display information to “disparage Americans past or living” and to focus on the “greatness of the achievements and progress of the American people.”

The National Park Service and the Interior Department did not immediately respond to requests for comment on Tuesday. The Interior Department said this summer that the new “panels are full of historical context and highlight the momentous events that took place in the President’s House and the other sites at Independence National Historical Park.”

“They acknowledge the evils of slavery, including its injustices and hypocrisies, and, by telling the stories of the nine slaves that Washington kept in the President’s House, remind us of their essential humanity,” the statement said.


As [MSI previously reported](/articles/2026-05-22-ona-judge-escape-from-slavery-marked-with-rallies-and-new-memorials-in-pa-and-n-), advocates held a rally at the President’s House site after slavery-related panels were removed and restoration work was paused during an appeal. The new complaint advances that dispute by seeking the memorial’s original condition and a permanent order against the directives behind this year’s changes.
