## Judge rejects trafficking and labour allegations while flagging possible statute-of-limitations issues

Spain’s Audiencia Nacional is investigating singer Julio Iglesias over allegations that he sexually abused and physically assaulted two female employees at his properties in the Dominican Republic and the Bahamas. Judge Antonio Piña said a detailed account and preliminary evidence were sufficient to initiate proceedings, while saying the decision did not imply an evaluative judgment about the complainant’s credibility. Iglesias has denied the allegations and said he would defend himself against what he described as “a serious affront.”

The two complainants are a domestic worker and a physical therapist who were employed at Iglesias’s mansions in the Dominican Republic and the Bahamas. They allege that in 2021 he subjected them “to inappropriate touching, insults and humiliation … in an atmosphere of control and constant harassment.” In a statement released Wednesday, the Audiencia Nacional said it was investigating the women’s complaint, which accuses Iglesias of “offences against sexual freedom, as well as offences involving bodily harm and degrading treatment.”

Piña’s reasons focused in part on a photograph submitted by one of the complainants. “A photograph of the complainant shows a bruise on one of her arms, consistent with her having been gripped forcefully,” Piña said. “In the same photograph, two marks are visible on one of her thighs – marks that could correspond to a human bite – which the complaint states are the result of her being forced to participate in sexual acts.”

Piña said the detailed account, considered with the preliminary evidence, was sufficient to initiate proceedings. He said that conclusion did not imply an evaluative judgment about the complainant’s credibility. His decision did not accept every theory advanced by the women: the judge rejected their allegations of human trafficking and labour offences, and noted that some of the alleged offences may have expired under the statute of limitations. Piña called the two women to give evidence in person on 3 February next year.

The investigation comes almost 11 months after Spanish prosecutors shelved the women’s complaints, arguing that Spanish courts had no jurisdiction because the alleged offences took place outside Spain. Women’s Link Worldwide, which represents the women, said it had successfully argued that the Audiencia Nacional had jurisdiction and that Iglesias’s Spanish nationality provided sufficient grounds for the case to be tried in Spain. The NGO filed a new criminal complaint on the women’s behalf on 3 September.

The Guardian reported that the allegations emerged in January this year after a three-year joint investigation by the Spanish news site elDiario.es and the Spanish-language TV network Univision Noticias. That investigation gathered testimony from 15 former employees who worked for Iglesias between the late 1990s and 2023.

A photograph by Paras Griffin/Getty Images showed Iglesias performing in Duluth, Georgia, in December 2019. The Guardian reported that Iglesias, now 83, has sold more than 300m records during a six-decade career. He has denied all the allegations. In a statement posted on Instagram in January, Iglesias wrote: “With great regret, I am responding to the allegations from two people who used to work in my house. I have never abused, coerced or disrespected any woman. These accusations are absolutely false and pain me deeply.”

Women’s Link said the case showed the need for states to ensure access to justice. “Everyone has the right to have justice systems investigate facts when there are indications of alleged human rights violations,” said Jovana Ríos Cisneros, the NGO’s executive director. “The Spanish state has an obligation to guarantee equal access to justice, regardless of who the accused person is.”

As of the court’s announcement, the proceeding remains at the investigative stage. Piña has not resolved the underlying abuse allegations, and the two women are scheduled to give evidence in person on 3 February next year.
