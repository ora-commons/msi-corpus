## All three are unopposed for historic leadership firsts

House Democrats are scheduled to elect their leadership slate Wednesday in closed-door meetings. Hakeem Jeffries, Rep. Katherine Clark of Massachusetts and Rep. Pete Aguilar of California are running unopposed for Democratic leader, majority leader and majority whip, respectively.

If the slate is elected, Clark would become the first woman majority leader and Aguilar the first Latino majority whip. Jeffries would then face a separate roll call of the full House in January to become speaker, which would make him the first Black American in the office. The new Congress convenes Jan. 3, 2027.

For dozens of members-elect, Wednesday’s vote will be the first of their careers, and they will not be sworn into office until Jan. 3, 2027. If reelected as Democratic leader, Jeffries will still need the full House’s vote in January for the speakership.

The leadership choice comes as House Democrats prepare to govern with a new majority after voters ousted Republicans from power. The Associated Press described the party as restive and eager for a fight. It described the result as voters ousting Republicans in repudiation of President Donald Trump and the party in power. Calls and social media posts have been flooding the offices of Democrats and newly elected members, demanding a fresh start. Protesters heckled Jeffries last week during a Democratic conference in Denver.

On Tuesday, Jeffries was meeting privately with the separate caucuses that make up House Democrats, including the Congressional Progressive Caucus, the New Democrat Coalition and the fiscally conservative Blue Dogs, among others. He has been personally reaching out to every House Democrat and says he is taking nothing for granted.

“I embrace those conversations, and we’re going to continue to do that over the next 24 hours,” he said.

Rep.-elect Brad Lander of New York said he would support Jeffries when he launched his congressional campaign. Rep.-elect Randy Villegas of California said he was supporting whoever would help him best deliver for his Central Valley district. Returning Rep. Emily Randall of Washington said she heard questions about House leadership during town halls in her district and had been watching direct messages stream into her Instagram feed about the election, prompting her to start returning calls.

One of Randall’s constituents asked whether Bernie Sanders could become House speaker. “People have messaged me asking if Bernie Sanders could be the speaker of the House,” Randall said. “Technically yes, and it would be probably difficult.” The Associated Press noted that the House can elect anyone as speaker, while Sanders already has a day job in the U.S. Senate and is not likely to be chosen for the post. On another call, Randall said she listened to the constituent’s concerns and then said, “Truthfully, not like blowing smoke, whoever is our speaker — and I think it is going to be Hakeem Jeffries, and I am supporting Hakeem Jeffries for speaker — I think we will accomplish a lot of the things that he was asking us to.”

Lawmakers pointed to the livestream in which Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez explained the speaker’s role. “There is one candidate in this race: Hakeem Jeffries,” Ocasio-Cortez said at the Capitol. “I think that everyone is far more concerned what that leadership means, what is it promising.”

Jeffries, Clark and Aguilar have risen together as a team, forming the first new generation of leaders of the post-Pelosi era and preparing for their first time as leaders in the majority. Rep. Nancy Pelosi decided to step aside from leadership in 2022 after the party’s electoral defeat handed Republicans the majority. Pelosi, the No. 2 Democrat Rep. Steny Hoyer of Maryland and Rep. James Clyburn of South Carolina had often moved together, rising and then stepping back from their long-held positions.

A similar contest is underway in the Senate, where Senate Leader Chuck Schumer faces a challenge from Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland. Returning Rep. Yassamin Ansari of Arizona said the House and Senate situations “are quite different.” She described Jeffries as a collaborative leader who is listening to ideas from progressive Caucus members like her and others. “His job is to reflect all of us, not just a faction,” Ansari said. Ansari said, “It is more productive to focus on preparing on what we’re going to do in the majority, and what we need and want to get done in those first 100 days.”

Rep. Shomari Figures of Alabama, who pulled off what the AP described as a stunning victory in a redrawn district favoring the Republican candidate, said he would support Jeffries. “It’s not even like a complex question,” he said about Jeffries, who he said “led us through what is likely the most tumultuous two-year tenure of any presidency in the history of this nation.” “I’m not going to not stand by him right here on the precipice of history,” he said.

The immediate choice is limited to three unopposed candidates, while the broader debate is over how the leadership should govern in the new majority. The new Congress convenes Jan. 3, 2027, and Jeffries will still face the full House in January.
