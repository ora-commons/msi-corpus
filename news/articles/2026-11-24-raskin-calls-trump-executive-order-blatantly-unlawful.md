## Raskin says Juul clearance came four months after 1789 invested

Rep. Jamie Raskin, the ranking member of the House Judiciary Committee and its incoming chair, called a Trump administration executive order issued Friday “blatantly unlawful,” saying public contractors have a “duty and a right to speak truthfully when Congress calls.” The Associated Press reported that the Trump administration has taken steps to push back on the Democrats’ oversight efforts. Those steps include the order, which directs federal contractors and subcontractors to consult the administration about inquiries they receive from Congress and withhold any communications the White House considers privileged.

The order could complicate compliance with Raskin’s investigation for at least one of the companies, Hadrian Automation, which is a federal contractor.

Raskin sent the letters Monday evening to defense contractor Hadrian Automation, vape maker Juul Labs, prediction market platform Polymarket and the online firearms seller GrabAGun. The Maryland congressman requested that each company send him two years’ worth of communications with 1789 Capital and other select documents, saying the materials would help him investigate whether Donald Trump Jr. used insider information to orchestrate or anticipate government actions that boosted the companies and his own finances.

In letters obtained by The Associated Press, Raskin wrote that 1789 Capital had suspiciously “serendipitous timing” when it invested in the four companies. He outlined how federal policies or contracts benefited their businesses.

Raskin wrote that Juul Labs received Food and Drug Administration clearance to keep its electronic cigarette products on the market in July 2025, four months after 1789 Capital had taken a stake in the company.

He also wrote that Hadrian Automation received nearly $1 billion in U.S. Navy and Army contracts in March, its first-ever federal contracts, after bringing 1789 Capital on as an investor in 2025.

Raskin wrote that GrabAGun got help from 1789 Capital to go public and put Trump Jr. on its board before the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives issued a proposed rule to ease the process of shipping guns to people’s homes.

The congressman also raised questions about the Justice Department and the Commodity Futures Trading Commission closing investigations into Polymarket in July 2025, after 1789 Capital had already invested in the prediction market platform.

The investigation was opened over the summer, in August. 1789 Capital is based in Palm Beach, Florida, was founded in 2022, and Trump Jr. was announced as a partner in November 2024. Raskin said 1789 Capital “suddenly struck investment gold” after the firm named the president’s son as a partner in 2024.

Raskin is one of the lawmakers who will spearhead Democrats’ oversight of the Trump administration when they take control of Congress in January. He has said that uncovering information about bribery and financial corruption will be among his top priorities.

Donald Trump Jr. has repeatedly rejected accusations that he has exploited government access for profit. At an Axios event last week, he acknowledged that he understood people’s corruption concerns but said they were inaccurate, noting that he had been an investor for decades. “I can see what they are saying. It doesn’t make it so,” he said. “The notion that I should somehow sit in a corner, suck my thumb and wait, you know, eight years — in this case 12 years — till we’re out of the political game, it’s not real.”

Trump Jr. and 1789 Capital have denied wrongdoing. In a September letter to Raskin, the firm’s lawyers defended its investment thesis as predating the Trump administration and said the firm “welcomes legitimate congressional oversight” but “has no intention of being held hostage to political gamesmanship.”

On Monday, 1789 Capital attorney Bill Burck said the September letter explained flaws in Raskin’s understanding, but Raskin “never bothered to reply.” “Facts are facts and Congressman Raskin has them wrong here, but persists in his tall tales against 1789,” Burck said. “These are personal attacks untethered to actual evidence.”

GrabAGun said Raskin’s letter “is a political document, and its allegations are baseless.” The company said the ATF’s review of its regulations was well known across the firearms industry, the agency published numerous proposed rules for public comment, and GrabAGun’s only input came through public comments on several proposals.

Juul Labs said it was reviewing its letter closely. “As always, we intend to cooperate with any Congressional effort to understand our company, our commitment to combating underage vaping, and our mission to help adult smokers of combustible cigarettes transition completely away from smoking,” Juul said in an emailed response. Hadrian Automation and Polymarket did not immediately respond to requests for comment.
