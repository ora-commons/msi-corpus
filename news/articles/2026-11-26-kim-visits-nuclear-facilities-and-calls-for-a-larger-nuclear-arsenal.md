## Pyongyang did not publicly answer Trump’s overture as U.S. denuclearization demands continued

North Korean leader Kim Jong Un inspected facilities that produce nuclear material and called for building more nuclear weapons, the Korean Central News Agency reported Thursday. The tour came days after President Donald Trump sought a meeting during a recent visit to Asia, and Pyongyang did not publicly respond to the overture.

KCNA said Thursday that Kim inspected two nuclear-material production factories, one newly inaugurated and the other being expanded. Kim also visited a nuclear-powered equipment production base and a nuclear research institute on Wednesday. North Korea did not disclose where the two material-production factories were located, and some photographs from the visit were blurred.

KCNA photos showed Kim and senior military officials walking between dense rows of what appeared to be centrifuges used to produce weapons-grade uranium and touring halls displaying what were believed to be warheads. North Korea has disclosed a uranium enrichment site a few times since showing one at its main Yongbyon nuclear complex to visiting American scholars in 2010. In June, when Pyongyang disclosed parts of an enrichment site while reporting on Kim’s visit there, outside experts assessed it was likely an additional uranium enrichment facility at Yongbyon.

Kim said the inspection was part of efforts to strengthen North Korea’s defense against “variable nuclear threats approaching the Korean Peninsula moment by moment.” He accused the United States of inciting its regional allies to obtain nuclear-powered submarines and of committing to share its nuclear weapons with them, KCNA said.

Kim said, according to KCNA, “The validity and substantiality of our possession of nuclear weapons and the trend of bolstering up its nuclear capability in quality and quantity are an irreversible reality.” He described the deployment of low-yield battlefield nuclear weapons across multiple domains for various missions as “crucial and urgent,” given North Korea’s “inevitable reality facing the border with the most dangerous enemy state,” a reference to South Korea. North Korea has in recent years focused on developing such tactical nuclear weapons amid heightened animosity with South Korea.

The Associated Press reported that experts believed Kim was likely trying to show off an evolving arsenal, described as the backbone of his authoritarian rule, and reaffirm his rejection of future talks on dismantling the program. Moon Seong Mook, an analyst for the Seoul-based Korea Research Institute for National Strategy, said, “Trump has talked about a willingness to meet Kim Jong Un, but the basic U.S. position that North Korea must denuclearize remains unchanged. So Kim Jong Un wants to say 'Look at how powerful we are. Don't talk about denuclearization if you want to meet us,'”

Moon said the visit also was likely meant to check how state objectives on the nuclear program had progressed this year and what future goals needed to be set before the year-end Workers’ Party conference.

South Korea’s Defense Ministry said it takes Kim’s comments on battlefield nuclear weapons seriously and added that Seoul maintains a firm military readiness against Pyongyang’s nuclear threats under an alliance with the United States. South Korea’s Joint Chiefs of Staff declined to share an analysis of the North Korean nuclear facilities reported by KCNA, calling it confidential information for South Korea and the United States.

During a visit to China last week for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, Trump reiterated his eagerness to restore diplomacy with Kim. A Trump-Kim meeting did not take place. In an interview with the New York Post, Trump dismissed the suggestion that Kim had rebuffed his outreach, saying, “No, he didn't ignore it. You know, we had many leaders there … There'll be a time when we meet … It was just not really the right time. It was a very busy two-day period.”

Trump also said he could revive diplomacy at any time by restarting the exchange of letters maintained during his first term, although he acknowledged that the leaders had not corresponded “recently.” Kim previously said he could return to talks if the United States dropped its “hostile policy” and respected what he called the North’s status as a nuclear state. The Associated Press reported that experts viewed Kim’s aim as winning international recognition for that status so North Korea could demand the lifting of international sanctions.

Last week, International Atomic Energy Agency Director General Rafael Grossi assessed that North Korea had been expanding the original uranium enrichment facility at Yongbyon, calling the move “a cause for serious concern.” Nuclear weapons can be built using either highly enriched uranium or weapons-grade plutonium, and North Korea has facilities to produce both at Yongbyon.

Last year, South Korean Unification Minister Chung Dong-young said North Korea was operating four uranium enrichment facilities and that they were running every day. The public record thus combines continued operation, an expansion assessed by Grossi and Kim’s renewed demand for more weapons, although the newest factories’ locations were not disclosed.


The tour KCNA reported Thursday and Kim’s renewed call for more weapons advanced [Kim’s June order for an accelerated nuclear buildup](/articles/2026-06-23-kim-jong-un-orders-accelerated-nuclear-buildup-calls-south-korea-most-hostile-st/), carrying that directive into a public inspection of production facilities and another declaration that North Korea’s nuclear status is irreversible.
