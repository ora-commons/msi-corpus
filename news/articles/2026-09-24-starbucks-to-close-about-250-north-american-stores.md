## Starbucks to close about 250 North American stores

**Subtype:** fact

- Starbucks will close about 250 underperforming North American stores under CEO Brian Niccol's "Back to Starbucks" initiative, roughly 1% of the chain's 18,000 locations.
- Chief Operating Officer Mike Grams disclosed the closures Sept. 24 in a letter to employees.
- The move marks the second major wave of Starbucks closures since Niccol became CEO two years ago.
- A regulatory filing indicates most of the affected stores will close before the end of fiscal 2026.
- The restructuring is expected to cost about $300 million, including $200 million for breaking leases and severance and $100 million from the disposal of company-owned restaurant assets.

## Restructuring to cost $300 million; workers offered transfers or severance

The closures follow a North American coffeehouse portfolio review that identified locations where the company could not deliver a consistent experience for customers and partners or could not reach acceptable financial performance, according to Grams's letter.

"We have carefully reviewed our North America coffeehouse portfolio and identified locations where we do not believe we can consistently deliver the experience we want for customers and partners or where we don't see a path to acceptable financial performance," Grams wrote.

The company has not yet specified which locations will close. A regulatory filing indicates most of the affected stores will be shuttered before the end of fiscal 2026.

Grams wrote that employees at affected stores will be offered transfers where possible, with others receiving severance support.

The total restructuring cost is expected to reach about $300 million. Of that sum, roughly $200 million will go toward breaking leases and paying severance benefits to workers, while the remaining $100 million will come from the disposal of restaurant assets owned by the company.

The announcement marks the second major wave of Starbucks closures since Niccol became CEO two years ago, continuing the "Back to Starbucks" improvement initiative announced under his leadership.
