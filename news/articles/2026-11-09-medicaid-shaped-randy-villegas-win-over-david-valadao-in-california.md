## Villegas pledged Medicare for All after the Central Valley upset

Randy Villegas defeated Republican congressman David Valadao in California's redrawn Central Valley congressional district, where 6 out of 10 people were on Medicaid, according to an NPR report by Jackie Fortiér for KFF Health News. The race was called days earlier than expected, and health care was at the center of the political upset. After the win, Villegas said he would be a Day 1 co-sponsor of Medicare for All.

The Central Valley is known for agriculture and oil fields and for sending Valadao to Congress. NPR said the congressman had survived challenges for years by distancing himself from President Trump. This time, the report said Medicaid made a big difference in a district where 6 out of 10 people relied on the program.

David McCuan, a political science professor at Sonoma State University, said Valadao "has had more political lives than a cat." McCuan said Democrats had tried for years to flip the inland district with the same kind of candidate. "Democrats have typically had someone who's been much more moderate, kind of a blue dog Democrat, a chamber of commerce-type Democrat, and they actually haven't fared well," he said. Villegas was different: a professor and school board member endorsed by Senator Bernie Sanders.

Jeffrey Cummins, a political scientist at Fresno State, said the result was unexpected in its margin. "I was definitely surprised at the margin of victory. I mean, that is pretty overwhelming," he said. Cummins also said the redrawn congressional map put more Democrats in the district, giving Villegas an advantage.

Cummins described a "perfect storm against Valadao" that combined the national political environment, President Trump's popularity, high inflation and Valadao's votes in Congress. NPR said Valadao's vote for the Republican budget bill became central to the campaign. The report said the law would cut Medicaid spending by more than $900 billion over the next few years, even though 6 out of 10 people in the district were on Medicaid.

At an October debate in Bakersfield, Villegas said Valadao had publicly promised not to cut Medicaid and then was the deciding vote for the budget law. Villegas said the measure would provide $1.1 trillion in tax cuts to the wealthiest Americans while taking health care from Valley families.

The report said Medicaid was the deciding factor for Bakersfield resident Ulises Hernandez, who spoke before the election. "I have Medicaid. I rely on it for wheelchair repair. And without it, I would be stuck at home without anyone to help me," he said. Hernandez voted for Villegas. "I hope and pray that he does what he says he's going to do and help us," he said.

The day after the election, Villegas told reporters he would pursue Medicare for All, a pledge to bring universal health care to every American. He tied the proposal to prescription drug and health care costs across the border. "I'll be a Day 1 co-sponsor of Medicare for All because people in my district will drive seven to eight hours across the border to get cheaper prescription drugs, cheaper healthcare, and that shouldn't be happening in the richest country in the history of the world," he said.

The report said Central Valley voters would watch whether Villegas could help the new Democratic majority in Congress reverse deep Medicaid cuts and "reward their pivot to the left." Villegas enters Congress with a Day 1 Medicare for All co-sponsorship pledge and a district where 6 out of 10 residents rely on Medicaid.
