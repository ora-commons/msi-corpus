## Milburn's under-25s benefit cut would save £3.6bn

The final report of the personal independence payment review, co-led by social security minister Stephen Timms, is expected to be published as soon as Tuesday and will call for the benefit to be overhauled rather than scrapped for any age group, the Guardian reported.

The review is understood to be very similar to a draft set of recommendations that emerged in September, which called for a series of changes, particularly to the system of assessment, to take better account of conditions that vary over time. An interim report by Timms' committee described the benefit, which is intended to help disabled people meet the extra costs of disability, as widely valued but "no longer fit for purpose".

Last week's report by Alan Milburn into worklessness among young people took a different course. It called for PIP to be scrapped for adults under 25 and replaced by an "additional cost allowance", part of a wider network of job-based help for that age group.

Government officials have pushed back on the idea of a clash between the two reviews, saying Milburn's review was commissioned after the Timms process had begun, and that about 90% of the PIP caseload involved people over 25.

With Timms' recommendations understood to be quite broad, Burnham and his ministers will need to make a series of decisions about what comes next, on a deeply sensitive subject. The report will include short-term overhauls, within the current spending round, and longer-term ones. Many of the specifics for the latter would need to be decided by ministers before they legislate next year.

Keir Starmer was forced to abandon planned changes in 2025 after a major rebellion by Labour MPs. Those plans were based around £5bn in cuts, and the Timms review — framed by ministers as a groundbreaking "co-produced" policy initiative — was seen as an attempt to restore trust among disabled people after they collapsed.

The review's steering group is co-chaired by Timms and the disability rights advocates Clenton Farquharson and Sharon Brennan, with most of its 12 members having lived experience of disability or long-term health conditions.

There are reportedly worries among some members of the Timms review board over the scale of Milburn's proposals on PIP, which would cut £3.6bn from spending on young people. "Some members are really concerned," one source told the Guardian.

The interim report in July and further emerging findings in September confirmed a consensus view that PIP was unfit for purpose, and that a major overhaul was needed to make the benefit fair, humane and efficient. It raised the prospect that some claimants could in future receive "in kind" awards such as services, aids or training alongside basic cash awards.

The interim findings underlined that cash payments were "the foundation" of a future reformed disability benefit, that it would be non-means tested, and that any "reformed benefit will be designed with disabled people and those with long-term health conditions, and tested to make sure it works before it is rolled out". There was no mention of scrapping PIP for under-25s, or for any other age group.

Until recently, most disability charities had been optimistic that the Timms review would lead to much-needed changes and improve the lives of the 4 million adults who claim the benefit. Its remit did not include the costs of PIP or potential cuts in spending.

But the timing and scope of the Milburn review, which ministers have conspicuously welcomed, has dented that confidence. There was widespread shock among disability campaigners and charities at Milburn's recommendations to scrap PIP and universal credit health benefit payments for under-25s as part of efforts to boost jobs and training for young people not in education, employment or training.

Burnham faces pressure from the Conservatives to reduce the overall cost of working-age benefits. He has said this is his plan but has pushed back against the idea of what he called "crude cuts", saying these often just created costs elsewhere.


The Timms committee's course answers a question left open by earlier reporting: MSI reported on 17 November that [Alan Milburn's review would describe Neet numbers as an economic and moral crisis](/articles/2026-11-17-milburn-review-to-call-neet-numbers-an-economic-and-moral-crisis/) and recommend that young people lose access to the additional health-related benefits claimed by people who cannot work. The overhaul recommended this week keeps PIP open to every age group, leaving that proposal unresolved.
