## Jim Jordan reportedly seeks House Republicans' top post

On November 4, 2026, The Guardian reported, citing multiple reports, that Mike Johnson, the Republican speaker of the House, has said he is not planning to pursue the role of minority leader once Republicans lose their majority in the chamber.

The Guardian said Johnson's decision effectively yields the position of highest-ranking Republican in the House to whichever member of the party can claim it. The development followed the Republicans' midterm defeat and their loss of the chamber's majority.

The report surfaced moments after Fox News reported on Wednesday, citing two people familiar with the matter, that Jim Jordan, the Ohio Republican who chairs the House Judiciary Committee, was planning an effort to oust Johnson and take the minority leader position.

Jordan has pursued the House Republicans' top job before. After Kevin McCarthy, the previous Republican speaker, lost the speakership in 2023, Jordan sought the title but failed to win enough votes from members of the chamber.

The current situation differs from that earlier bid in a specific way: the incumbent speaker has said he will not compete, while Jordan is making another attempt at the top job. The account did not describe either development as completing a leadership selection.

Jordan's move was described as a plan rather than a completed selection, and the report did not say he had secured the position. The leadership contest therefore remained unsettled after Johnson's reported decision.

Jordan's 2023 bid provides context for the current challenge because it shows that his previous effort ended without enough votes from House members. The new reporting does not state whether his support has changed.

For now, the available account leaves the identity of the next highest-ranking House Republican unresolved. Johnson reportedly is not seeking the role, while Jordan reportedly is trying to claim it.
