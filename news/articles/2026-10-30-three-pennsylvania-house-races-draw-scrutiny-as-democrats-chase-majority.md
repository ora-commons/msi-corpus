## Three Pennsylvania House races draw scrutiny as Democrats chase majority

**Subtype:** fact

- Three Republican-held Pennsylvania districts — the 7th, 8th and 10th — are being closely watched as Democrats need to win three more House seats than they did in 2024 to take back the majority.
- President Donald Trump outran the Republican House winners in all three districts in 2024, a warning sign the Associated Press reports could complicate Republican turnout with his name off the ballot.
- Republican Rep. Ryan Mackenzie and Democrat Bob Brooks had each raised about $5.3 million as of mid-October in the 7th District.
- Democrat Janelle Stelson had raised $9.2 million to Republican Rep. Scott Perry's $6.8 million as of mid-October in the 10th District.
- Pennsylvania requires an automatic recount in statewide races decided by a margin of 0.5% or less, and polls close at 8 p.m. ET.

## Polls close at 8 p.m. ET as counties report absentee votes

Pennsylvania is one of several states whose House races could decide whether Democrats take back power in the chamber, according to the Associated Press. Democrats need to win three more seats in the midterm elections than they did in 2024 to take the majority from Republicans.

Conditions have worsened for Republicans seeking office since the start of President Donald Trump's second term, the AP reported. The wire service cited his approval rating, which it said has plunged, and pointed to his tariff policy, his immigration crackdown and his decision to launch a war in Iran as squeezing Americans' finances and threatening to alienate his supporters.

In the three key Pennsylvania districts — the 7th, 8th and 10th — Trump outperformed the Republican House winners in the 2024 election. The AP described that gap as a warning sign that without his name on the ballot, the incumbents seeking reelection may struggle to get the Republican turnout necessary to stay in office.

In 2024, Trump won the 7th Congressional District, in the Lehigh Valley, by a margin of just 3 percentage points. Republican Rep. Ryan Mackenzie, who is running for a second term, and Democrat Bob Brooks, a former firefighter and union leader, are now facing off in the district. Mackenzie ousted the Democratic incumbent, Susan Wild, in 2024, winning that election by one percentage point.

Brooks and Mackenzie had raised nearly the same amounts — about $5.3 million each — as of mid-October, and outside groups have poured millions more into the race.

The race in the 8th Congressional District, in the state's northeast, is also being closely watched. Rep. Rob Bresnahan, the Republican incumbent, faces Paige Cognetti, the Democratic mayor of Scranton. Bresnahan flipped the seat in 2024, winning by about 2 percentage points, while Trump won the same district by a much more comfortable margin of nearly 9 points.

Cognetti has made eradicating corruption central to her campaign and has attacked her opponent's past congressional stock trades. Bresnahan has campaigned on lowering taxes and costs and increasing border security.

The 10th Congressional District sits in the south-central part of the state and includes Harrisburg, the state capital. Scott Perry, the Republican incumbent endorsed by Trump, is up for a rematch of the 2024 election against Democrat Janelle Stelson. Perry won that election by just over 1 percentage point, while Trump won the district by a larger margin of over 5 percentage points.

Some Republicans have been highlighting their differences with the president to appeal to alienated voters, but the AP reported that this may be harder for Perry, a former Freedom Caucus chair who has aligned himself with the president for years. Perry was involved in Trump's attempt to overturn the results of the 2020 election, which Trump lost. Perry was not charged with a crime, but his cellphone was seized as part of the FBI's investigation into the matter.

Stelson, a former television news anchor, has campaigned as a moderate alternative to Perry, pitching herself as a candidate with a bipartisan focus committed to reducing the cost of living. As of mid-October, Stelson had raised $9.2 million, while Perry had raised $6.8 million, and millions more in outside spending have gone into the race.

In the governor's race, Democratic incumbent Josh Shapiro is seeking a second term. He faces Republican state treasurer Stacy Garrity, but polls suggest he is ahead of his opponent. Shapiro is considered a possible contender for the 2028 Democratic presidential nomination.

The AP will provide vote results and declare winners in races for governor, U.S. House, state Senate and state House. About 5.4 million votes were cast in the 2022 race for the U.S. Senate in Pennsylvania, and about 23% of votes in that race were cast early or by absentee ballot.

Counties are expected to release reports for early and absentee votes at varied times over the course of election night, according to the AP. Some counties are expected to release results for absentee and early votes in the first report of the night, and almost all are expected to have released all or almost all votes of all types by the last vote report of the night.

In 2024, the first results were reported starting at 8 p.m. ET on Nov. 5 as polls closed, according to the AP. The state reached 50% of votes reported at 10:12 p.m. ET and 90% of votes reported at 12:48 a.m. ET on Nov. 6, 2024.

The AP does not make projections and will declare a winner only when it determines there is no scenario that would allow a trailing candidate to close the gap. If a race has not been called, the AP will continue to cover newsworthy developments such as candidate concessions or declarations of victory, and will make clear that it has not yet declared a winner.

Pennsylvania requires an automatic recount for statewide races with a vote margin of 0.5% or less, and voters may also request a recount. The AP may declare a winner in a race subject to a recount if it can determine the lead is too large for a recount or legal challenge to change the outcome.
