## Mao Ning urges deeper exchanges on peace and stability

At a regular briefing in Beijing on Monday, China's Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said the planned three-way nuclear talks among President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin would be of "groundbreaking significance." She said China aimed to have in-depth exchanges about safeguarding world peace and promoting stability.

Mao also said: "Under the current circumstances, the three countries shoulder an important responsibility to work together in addressing global challenges."

The remarks came as Trump prepared to depart for Asia on Monday on a trip focused on economic deals and the historic trilateral meeting. The itinerary begins in Manila and continues to Shenzhen, where Trump was to attend the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. At the end of the trip, Trump was expected to meet Xi and Putin for their first-ever three-way talks, with nuclear stockpiles on the agenda.

Trump and U.S. officials had indicated that the meeting would focus on nuclear weapons and that the topics would not include Russia's war in Ukraine, now in its fifth year. Trump had recently played up chances for a settlement to that conflict.

The trip comes as the administration adjusts to a new political reality in Washington after the November midterm election, in which Trump's party lost both chambers of Congress. Trump had remained largely out of public sight for the past several days, even as a nationwide ground stop snarled air traffic and the Republican administration faced tough questions about its handling of the situation.

A video caption in the Associated Press report described the FAA ground stop as rare and attributed it to "extraordinary circumstances." The Associated Press said the trip gave Trump an opportunity to change the narrative from his domestic woes, including the air-travel problems and the midterm-election results.

In the Philippines, Trump was scheduled to meet Southeast Asian leaders who, according to the Associated Press, look to the United States for help countering Beijing's regional conduct. The White House said Trump planned to sign a trade deal with Thailand during the Manila stop.

The Associated Press also reported that the Nvidia controversy was certain to hang over Trump's visit to China, despite efforts by administration officials to downplay the president's close ties to Jensen Huang. Huang resigned as Nvidia's CEO last week after admitting to an affair with an alleged Chinese government agent. Beijing said it was not involved.

U.S. officials who briefed reporters anonymously about the trip under ground rules set by the White House said Trump had no plans to raise the issue with Xi. Huang had accompanied Trump on Air Force One during the president's last trip to China in May and had since been a sporadic presence around Trump at the White House while speaking with him frequently. Trump had argued that America needed to preserve its leadership in artificial intelligence and had warned of dire consequences if China won that competition.

Scrutiny of the administration's actions involving Nvidia was growing, including the Commerce Department's delay in placing restrictions on Nvidia chip exports to a company led by the alleged Chinese government agent with whom Huang had the affair. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, asked about the matter in a Fox News Channel interview that aired Sunday with anchor Larry Kudlow, said he would "leave the internal workings of the Commerce Department to the Commerce Department."

Bessent said: "I think what's more important here, Larry, is to examine whether Nvidia had a culture of compliance around the U.S. export restrictions ... and at what level?" He added: "I think we're going to see answers." Bessent also said: "Jensen Huang is an incredible innovator, but that does not abrogate their duties of compliance."

An Associated Press video caption said Bessent accused Lutnick of failing to prevent China from obtaining Nvidia chips.

The three-way meeting was also expected to put the ongoing U.S. war with Iran in the spotlight. The Associated Press described Iran as a country that has often allied with China and Russia and said the war had been widely blamed for the Republican Party's steep midterm losses. Trump had tried to play down the chance he would order a resumption of major strikes against Iran while also saying he did not see the need for talks.

Elon Musk, who heads SpaceX and Tesla, was among the travelers on the Asia trip with Trump. The Associated Press reported that Musk is shaping up to be a top investigative target of the incoming Democratic majority in Congress.
