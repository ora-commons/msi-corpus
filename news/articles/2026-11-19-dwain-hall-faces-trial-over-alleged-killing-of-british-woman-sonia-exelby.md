## Prosecutors outline airport pickup, messages and physical evidence

Dwain Hall, an auto repair and tow truck driver, is on trial in Ocala, Florida, on charges that he kidnapped and murdered British woman Sonia Exelby in October 2025. Hall has pleaded not guilty, and opening statements this week put the prosecution's account of a journey from England to Florida against the defense's challenge to the evidence.

Exelby, of Portsmouth, England, disappeared from her home, prompting an international search and investigation. US investigators found her body in a shallow grave in Marion Oaks on 17 October 2025. Prosecutors allege Hall lured her to the United States, killed her and buried her there.

The prosecution's account focused on Exelby's online posts and mental health. Investigators, reporting after Hall's arrest in November 2025, said Exelby had traveled to Florida after posting online about wanting someone to end her life. Hall's arrest affidavit said that Exelby had previously expressed suicidal thoughts and investigators alleged that Hall knew about her struggles and used that vulnerability to bring her to Florida, the Ocala Starbanner reported.

Authorities also said Exelby boarded a flight to the US seemingly with an intention “to be possibly sexually abused, tortured, and murdered by unknown individuals living in the United States”.

During opening statements, assistant state attorney Amy Berndt said Exelby's parents struggled with mental illness and addiction and that Exelby also had mental illness and twice attempted suicide. Berndt said Exelby visited a website depicting graphic sexual abuse of women and that Hall was among several men who contacted her.

Berndt characterized Exelby's search for contact as an attempt to find someone to substitute for self-harm. Berndt said Exelby “was trying to find some substitute” to the self-harm. “Unfortunately, she found another outlet.” Berndt said Exelby was “trying to find someone who would kill her”. “Unfortunately there was no shortage of depraved men willing to kill Sonia in the most torturous and vile ways possible,” Berndt added.

Prosecutors said Hall picked up Exelby at Gainesville airport in central Florida and drove her to an Airbnb rental. Investigators later found evidence there that they said connected him to her disappearance and death, including transactions on her bank cards and a video of Exelby on Hall's cellphone.

The arrest affidavit said Exelby told a friend she felt scared and trapped. The message quoted her writing: “I’m sorry, he keeps taking my phone, he doesn’t trust me with it,” and “He made it clear there was no way out unless I shoot him.”

Prosecutors said evidence linking Hall to Exelby's death includes a shovel with Exelby's blood on it, cellphone data and a knife recovered from a friend of Hall. Jurors were also expected to see photographs and surveillance video of Hall purchasing rope, a shovel, deodorant and other items at a local Walmart store.

Hall denied killing Exelby but acknowledged in a police interview that he had contacted her and that the two had been intimate. Defense attorney Junior Barrett said in opening statements that his client was not a murderer and asked jurors to consider the facts, witness testimony and circumstances. “We believe that when you listen to all the facts and evidence in this case, you will arrive at the decision that Dwain Hall did not murder,” Barrett said.

After Exelby disappeared, her former boyfriend, Stevie Hunt, contacted the authorities, the court heard. Hunt described Exelby as a kind, beautiful and loving woman, while her sister talked about their difficult childhood. Hunt said Exelby experienced mood swings that sometimes lasted for days and had been in therapy for more than a decade.

The case continues.
