## Stevens found failings in Thorpe's care during his final four months

Amanda, the widow of former England Test cricketer and coach Graham Thorpe, has lodged a personal injury claim at the high court against Surrey and Borders Partnership NHS foundation trust, the NHS body involved in his care, The Telegraph reported. The legal step follows an inquest at Surrey coroner's court in Woking, held last year, that found the trust culpable of "failings" and "shortcomings" in the provision of his mental health care.

Thorpe, who played 100 Tests for England, died aged 55 in Surrey in August 2024. The crowd paid tribute to him during the first Test match between England and Sri Lanka at Old Trafford that month.

The inquest heard that he had been diagnosed with anxiety and depression and had tried to kill himself in 2022. It also heard that he had "spiralled into depression" after losing his job as a batting coach in 2022, at the conclusion of the Ashes series in Australia.

Surrey coroner's court was told there had been a gap of more than four months in Thorpe being seen by the local NHS trust before his death, despite a "crisis" call in June 2024 in which he told his care coordinator he had "given up". Thorpe was offered a follow-up appointment by the community mental health team, but no home visit was made.

Jonathan Stevens, the assistant coroner, said at the inquest: "In my judgment, someone should have been going to see him to properly monitor and assess him, to understand his care needs. Saying 'come and see me in two weeks' was not an appropriate response to the crisis."

Stevens added: "I don't accept that when Graham was constantly asking his wife to help end his life, which was a new presentation that hadn't been seen before, he was not at that moment in crisis. There were shortcomings in the care that should have been provided to Graham in the last four months of his life. There were failings in the provision of his care."

The assistant coroner did not conclude that those shortcomings were negligent. "I cannot say whether it would have made any difference or not," he said.

Amanda told the inquest that her husband was constantly asking for help to end his life and was sure nothing would work. She also described his desire to go to Switzerland to end his life, although he also told his care coordinator that he had "no immediate plans" to act on his suicidal thoughts. A coroner recorded a verdict of suicide.

In response, a statement from Surrey and Borders Partnership NHS trust read: "We understand there is an intention to commence proceedings and cannot comment further at this stage." The statement did not address the inquest's findings, and the report included no further response from either side.
