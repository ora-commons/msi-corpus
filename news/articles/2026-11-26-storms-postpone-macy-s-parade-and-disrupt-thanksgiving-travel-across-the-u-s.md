## Emergency crews rescue motorists as high tides flood East Coast roads

A strengthening storm along the Eastern Seaboard and coastal flooding on both coasts disrupted Thanksgiving travel across much of the United States on Thursday. Heavy rain, strong winds and rising water closed roads, complicated air travel and prompted Macy's to postpone its 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade until Saturday.

Macy's said in a statement that it was postponing the event until Saturday "in an abundance of caution" after consulting with city officials. The strengthening storm had raised worries about how the parade's massive balloons would fare between Manhattan's skyscrapers.

The National Weather Service said the storm was expected to hamper air travel at eastern U.S. airports from Thanksgiving Day into Friday morning, with reduced visibility, greater turbulence, strong winds and aircraft icing. Wind gusts were expected to hit 50 mph (80 kph) or higher along the Interstate 95 corridor, and the weather service posted wind advisories from northern Georgia to most of the mid-Atlantic to southern New England. The disruption came on the holiday itself, though the days leading up to Thanksgiving are typically far busier on the roads and in the airports than the holiday itself.

All of New Jersey was under a state of emergency declared Wednesday night by Gov. Mikie Sherrill, and parts of New York were under a state of emergency following a declaration by Gov. Kathy Hochul.

As the high tide rolled in Thursday morning, the storm was flooding coastal villages and cities from North Carolina through New Jersey. Roads were closed, and emergency workers were rescuing people from cars stranded in water.

The nearly 18-mile (29-kilometer) Chesapeake Bay Bridge-Tunnel in Virginia was closed for several hours Thursday morning because of high winds. NC 12, the only highway connecting Hatteras Island on the North Carolina Outer Banks to the mainland, also closed after seawater breached the dunes again and covered the two-lane highway. Crews did not know when it might reopen.

Winter storm warnings and watches were posted for parts of Missouri and Illinois, with up to 13 inches (33 centimeters) of snow expected around St. Louis by Thursday afternoon.

On the West Coast, king tides continued surging, and coastal flood warnings were issued from Southern California to the San Francisco area and along the Oregon and Washington coasts. King tides occur when the gravitational pulls of the moon and sun align to raise water levels. The report attributed this year's amplified effects to the ongoing super El Niño, which has warmed the ocean and led to an increased volume of seawater.

A video showed a massive wave tearing down a portion of the L-shaped pier in Pacifica, California, on Wednesday and slamming the structure into the ocean. Officials had closed the pier in the summer after finding cracks in the structure.

North of San Francisco, Marin County officials issued an evacuation warning for residents of beachfront properties in Stinson Beach, saying large waves expected through the afternoon could send water and debris into roads and homes. The warning was lifted later Wednesday, but authorities advised people to stay away from the beach as large waves continued to strike the area.


[Forecast high winds had already threatened the parade's giant balloons](/articles/2026-11-25-nor-easter-threatens-macy-s-thanksgiving-parade-balloons-with-high-winds/), and [Macy's had moved the start to 8:30 a.m. Saturday after postponing Thursday's event over extreme weather](/articles/2026-11-25-macy-s-sets-100th-parade-start-at-8-30-a-m-saturday-amid-balloon-preparations/); by Thursday morning, the storm had closed highways and was complicating air travel along the East Coast.
