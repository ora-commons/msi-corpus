## Hubbard's win gives Democrats 11th seat in North Carolina delegation

U.S. Rep. Virginia Foxx wrote on Sunday that she was conceding her seat to Democratic challenger Chuck Hubbard, closing out a contest that a recount had already decided by a margin of 330 votes.

"The voters of the Fifth Congressional District exercised their Constitutional rights and made their voices heard," Foxx wrote in the statement conceding the race.

The 83-year-old Republican said she was saddened not to emerge victorious but continues to have faith in the election process.

"The peaceful transition of elected office from one person to another is one of the true hallmarks of our system of government, and I am proud to be a citizen of a nation that prizes fair and free elections," Foxx said.

Hubbard said the congresswoman conceded graciously after the results of the recount made it clear that she could not prevail. Foxx had a Sunday deadline to request a partial hand recount. The machine recount completed Saturday netted her only one vote, leaving Hubbard 330 votes ahead.

"I deeply appreciate her commitment to a smooth transition of the office and constituent casework," Hubbard said on social media. "We may not always agree, but we both love North Carolina and the people of the Fifth District."

Hubbard's win officially gives Democrats an 11th seat in the North Carolina congressional delegation, with three Republicans — a large switch from the current delegation of 10 Republicans and four Democrats.

North Carolina's 5th District covers the northwestern part of the state. It stretches from Caldwell and Watauga counties in the west to Rockingham and Guilford counties in the east.

Foxx has held the seat since 2005. A staunch ally of President Donald Trump, she served most recently as chairwoman of the House Committee on Rules.

As a member of the U.S. House Oversight and Reform Committee, Foxx notably skipped the vast majority of closed-door impeachment depositions against Trump in his first term. In October 2023, she drew headlines for telling a reporter to "shut up" after the reporter asked House Speaker nominee Mike Johnson about attempts to overturn the results of the 2020 presidential election.

Foxx served two stints as chair of the House Committee on Education and the Workforce and used the role to push back against the Biden-Harris administration's student loan forgiveness program. She joined her Republican colleagues in promising to protect women's sports from those who would equate "perceived sex with biological sex."
