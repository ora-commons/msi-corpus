## Weeks of unpaid training and seasonal hours complicate hiring

The number of U.S. school bus drivers fell 5% from 2025 to 2026, a loss of nearly 10,200 workers, according to an Economic Policy Institute analysis. The decline is the largest single-year drop since 2022 for the workforce that transports an estimated 26 million children every day, making school busing the largest mass transit program in the country.

The total number of school bus drivers is down 15%, or nearly 34,000, from 2019 levels, the institute found. According to the analysis, drivers continue to experience low pay, poor working conditions and school budget shortfalls.

Shortages have forced school districts to cut and consolidate bus routes. Eighty percent of school districts in the U.S. have been affected by driver shortages, and 73% experienced shortages from budget shortfalls, according to a 2025 report from the Associated Press-NORC Center for Public Affairs Research.

Higher diesel costs amid the war with Iran and Russia’s war on Ukraine have added to those budget problems, costing school districts an estimated $1.1bn more from November 2025 to October 2026 than in the previous year.

Wages for school bus drivers dropped 0.7% in September 2026 from September 2025 after accounting for inflation, while pay for other bus drivers remained steady, the report said. Median hourly pay for school bus drivers was $22.49 in 2026, compared with $23.89 for all bus drivers. School bus drivers comprise 39.4% of all bus drivers.

Entry into the job begins with a stretch of unpaid work, Dawn Freedenburg said. The 16-year school bus driver in Chicago, Illinois, said new drivers must obtain a commercial drivers license, undergo extensive training and pass background checks before they can drive, a process that requires weeks of unpaid work. “It isn’t easy for most people to put that much time into a new job without being paid, especially in this economy,” she said.

Once hired, the hours are often limited. Wages typically range from $19 to $27 an hour, most drivers do not average 40 hours a week during the school year, and they receive no pay during school breaks, Freedenburg said. Few contractors or school districts provide additional benefits such as health insurance. Her district guarantees five hours a day for 174 school days a year, while route assignments can place her work between 5.45am and 5pm. Field trips can bring extra work, but Freedenburg said there are typically more drivers wanting them than trips available.

Peter Sandler began driving school buses in Connecticut in 2020, after retiring in 2016. Many drivers are retirees, he said, a factor that contributes to high turnover in the field. When shortages hit, his former contractor consolidated bus routes and increased the number of children per bus. “That made buses filled to capacity which makes the driver’s life a nightmare,” Sandler said. “Every parent should be forced to see how their children act when they ride the school bus.”

With only 5.5 hours of paid work a day and little work when school is out of session, Sandler said it is economically unfeasible to make ends meet as a school bus driver. “If you add in summer work and taking charter work, its difficult to get past $40,000 annually,” he said. He noted that a one-bedroom apartment would set him back $1,600 to $1,700 a month in the cheapest communities and over $2,000 a month in better areas.

Drivers also cited student behavior since the Covid-19 pandemic as a working condition that has worsened. School bus drivers often find themselves between school administrators and parents when they try to address behavioral issues, they said.

The shortage is not affecting every district the same way. Antonia Namnath, a school bus driver in the Beaverton school district in Oregon, said the driver shortage is not an issue in her district because drivers there are paid good wages, with starting wages of $26 to $33 an hour and annual increases tied to a union contract with the Oregon School Employees Association, OSEA Chapter 48. “We get a good wage, incredible benefits, health benefits, vision, dental, everything,” she said. “The drivers have to be greatly compensated for the responsibility they’re taking on, which is to transport children safely from point A to point B, and manage the students in a safe way.”

Melissa Fay France, a school bus driver outside Kalamazoo, Michigan, said the lack of medical insurance and low pay often make leaving the profession an attractive option. France said wages need to match the responsibility of the job and the cost of maintaining a CDL. “I am a great driver, both on the road and for the kids. I know my worth and the schools aren’t living up to their end,” France said.

The result is a school transportation system carrying an estimated 26 million children daily with a workforce 15% smaller than in 2019, and districts responding to shortages by consolidating routes and, in some cases, loading more children onto each bus.
