## Huang's Capitol Hill testimony is scheduled for next week

President Trump said he helped broker Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia, while members of Congress were not so sure about Nvidia's board's assessment. Nvidia's board said it consulted with senior levels of the U.S. government and was confident that Huang did not pose a national security risk. In the PBS News discussion, Tamara Keith of NPR said Trump called CNBC for something like 45 minutes to describe his role and said he encouraged Nvidia's board both directly and indirectly. Keith said, "So the president's fingerprints are all over this." Keith's comments placed Trump's public account alongside the board's statement that it had consulted with senior levels of the U.S. government.

PBS News host Amna Nawaz, citing earlier reporting by Liz Landers, said members of Congress were not so sure about Nvidia's board's confidence that Huang did not pose a national security risk. Landers's reporting said some Democrats had concerns and identified Republicans with concerns including Senators John Curtis of Utah, Pete Ricketts of Nebraska, Kevin Cramer of North Dakota and Tom Cotton of Arkansas.

Amy Walter of The Cook Political Report said she expected much more pointed questions from both Democrats and Republicans about what Nvidia has or has not allegedly done in connection with chips it was selling overseas. Walter's comments focused on the questions Huang is expected to face when he testifies.

Walter tied the episode to what she described as an entire 2026 campaign driven by frustration among voters about the idea that there are people in the country who were seen as elites or as able to rise above everyone else, were not held to the same standards as everybody else, and received special compensation and dispensation from government. She said the episode presented Trump as viewing Jensen Huang as "too big to fail" at a time when many Americans were worried about their own well-being and day-to-day living. Walter argued that elite circles were more focused on stock holdings and personal consequences than on what the episode meant for the public broadly.

Keith said the phrase "too big to fail" was significant and that it was exactly what Trump was saying.
