## Andrews will meet with Schumer and Van Hollen before deciding her vote

South Carolina Sen.-elect Annie Andrews said Tuesday that she is undecided on whether to support New York Sen. Chuck Schumer or Maryland Sen. Chris Van Hollen for Senate Democratic majority leader, after repeatedly saying she would not back Schumer. Andrews said she is meeting both candidates before the closed caucus vote set for Dec. 1.

The leadership contest follows midterm elections that put Democrats back in charge of the upper chamber. Schumer, who has led Senate Democrats for a decade, is being challenged by Van Hollen. Andrews's victory was among at least eight Democratic flips in the U.S. Senate, with a ninth possibility in Alaska, where the race has not been called.

Andrews tied her undecided vote to her campaign push for generational change. “On the campaign trail, I really stressed that I think it’s time for generational change, and I stand by that statement,” she told reporters on a virtual call Tuesday.

Her reasoning centered on the incoming Senate class. “The way I assess the situation as it is right now is that the generational change is represented by this incredibly dynamic incoming freshman class of senators, and the generational change goal is something we’ll achieve, you know, as we move forward,” she said.

Before the election, Andrews's position was more direct. On Oct. 29, during an interview with Fox News anchor Bret Baier, the 46-year-old Democrat said: “No, I think it’s really clear that both parties are ready for a new generation of leadership.” Baier asked again two days after the election, on Nov. 5, and Andrews said: “I still very much believe that it’s time for a generational change on both sides of the aisle.”

News From The States reported that Schumer, 75, is considered likely to keep control of the caucus. Schumer previously served as majority leader from 2021 to 2025, while Van Hollen, 67, is challenging him.

Schumer has served in Congress for 46 years. New York voters first elected him to the House in 1980 and to the Senate in 1998. Van Hollen has served for 24 years, first winning a House seat in 2002 and a Senate seat in 2016.

Although Van Hollen is not part of the new generation of senators, his challenge is backed by prominent progressives. They include Vermont Sen. Bernie Sanders, an independent who identifies as a democratic socialist and caucuses with Democrats, and Michigan Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed. News From The States identified El-Sayed as the first Muslim elected to the U.S. Senate.

Andrews said the freshman senators will break the “norms of the way the Senate functions” to deliver for Americans. She also tied her own election to a broader demand for change: “It’s clear to me that Americans are ready for change. I think my election is a big shining symbol of that fact, so I continue to believe we need generational change. I continue to believe that we need to act with urgency,” she said.

Andrews said she was optimistic that Democrats would “get to work to solve problems” after the leadership election, regardless of who won. She said the freshmen were eager to “deliver on the promises we made on the campaign trail” and added: “I think we’re going to push as hard as we can to get the job done” once the majority leader is chosen.

The freshman class will be sworn in Jan. 3. Andrews defeated U.S. Sen. Darline Graham, who was appointed in July to fulfill the remaining term of her late brother, Lindsey Graham. Andrews is the first Democrat elected to the U.S. Senate from South Carolina since Fritz Hollings' last re-election in 1998.

Conversations were ongoing, Andrews said, about keeping on some Graham staffers. Her decision remains unsettled ahead of the closed caucus on Dec. 1.
