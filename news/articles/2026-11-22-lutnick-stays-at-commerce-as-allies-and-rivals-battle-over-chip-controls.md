## Trump keeps a longtime friend despite Nvidia export friction

Lutnick told associates "I'm not going anywhere" shortly after Trump announced that Wiles, his chief of staff, was leaving her post, according to people familiar with his comments. Wiles had kept the commerce secretary on a tight leash and wanted a heads up before he talked publicly about policy issues, those people said. With her departure, he seemed liberated.

Lutnick has battled with key players in the Trump administration yet has proven remarkably difficult to dislodge, because of the president's personal fondness for his longtime friend, officials and allies say.

Last week he was momentarily in the hot seat again, when Bessent blamed him for slow-walking chip restrictions at the heart of the Nvidia scandal. By Monday, the president was more conciliatory. "If you didn't delay it, if you didn't do something, you could have a blow up with China," Trump said as he prepared to go to China, with both Bessent and Lutnick.

Both New Yorkers, Lutnick and Trump have been friends for more than 20 years and share a similar brash style. While other economic officials often try to hem in Trump's impulses, Lutnick often enables them, said Sen. Thom Tillis, the retiring North Carolina Republican who has often clashed with Trump.

"One thing that I find both admirable and vexing about the president is his loyalty to past friends," Tillis said, adding that he thought Lutnick "needs to be replaced with somebody who can perform better for the president."

Sen. Tom Cotton of Arkansas, who chairs the Senate intelligence panel, also criticized Lutnick in an interview with The Wall Street Journal, saying he repeatedly ignored warnings that U.S. artificial-intelligence chips were being smuggled to China.

Trump was close to ousting Lutnick earlier this year after the commerce secretary acknowledged he had visited convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein at his private island, according to people familiar with the matter. But Lutnick, a billionaire and former chairman of Wall Street firm Cantor Fitzgerald, made a $25 million donation to Trump's presidential library, and Trump stopped talking about replacing him, the people said. The donation was earlier reported in the book "Regime Change."

Lutnick sometimes meets Trump at the White House for off-the-books meetings after staff have gone home for the night, according to officials familiar with the matter. Trump sometimes gives Lutnick directives at these one-on-one meetings, unbeknown to the president's other top advisers, the officials said. The president has also told aides he likes keeping Lutnick around as a foil and values his fight in policy debates, according to people familiar with the comments.

While Lutnick's relationship with Wiles has been strained for months, he has had a good relationship with the new chief of staff, Stephen Miller, according to people familiar with the matter. During the transition, Miller stayed at Lutnick's house in Palm Beach, Fla., and Lutnick has continued to seek counsel from the new chief, the people said.

A White House official said the president maintains complete confidence in Lutnick. Trump praised him at a press conference at the White House on Friday, saying he believes he is doing a "very good job" as commerce secretary. Aides and allies caution that Trump could always change his mind on Lutnick, despite their friendship; the president has previously attacked longtime allies if he feels they have wronged him.

In one sign of Lutnick's shifting influence, White House officials had privately told allies that, in response to the Nvidia issues, they planned to change export control policies and put national security adviser Marco Rubio in charge of the process, according to people familiar with the matter. When asked about it, a White House official denied Rubio would take the role over from the Commerce Department.

The tensions surrounding Lutnick have stemmed in part from concerns that his family business poses a conflict of interest. Lutnick handed ownership of Cantor Fitzgerald to his adult sons after he took office. Some of the president's senior advisers have looked at whether Lutnick's moves as commerce secretary benefit the firm's investments, an issue the president has himself raised at times.

Earlier this year, Trump fielded complaints from advisers alleging Lutnick's sons faced potential conflicts of interest linked to the secretary, and told others he didn't like what he was hearing, according to people familiar with the comments. Cantor has denied any conflicts of interest.

Companies have also sought Cantor's help in reaching the administration, according to people familiar with the requests. In response, Cantor has suggested to those companies that they hire a government relations firm, a spokesman for Cantor confirmed. Cantor has continued to employ lobbyists allied with Trump and his family; this year it paid more than $300,000 to Miller Strategies, a lobbying shop run by Trump ally and fundraiser Jeffrey Miller, according to disclosure records.

Lutnick's actions as commerce secretary continued to confuse other officials. When White House officials asked him to crack down on shipments of U.S. artificial-intelligence chips to Southeast Asia, he repeatedly told top White House officials that he wasn't sure whether Trump was on board. Eventually, Trump had to tell him to do the export controls himself, after tense meetings with Bessent, Wiles and a variety of other administration officials. Some White House and administration officials suspected Lutnick was holding up the controls for personal gain.

Cantor Fitzgerald has done deals involving AI companies operating in Southeast Asia — including participating in a $1 billion equity offering for Singapore-based data-center developer Bitdeer — that stand to benefit from a loose approach to chip export rules.

Democrats and some Republicans have said they would investigate Lutnick, Cantor and the secretary's decisions on chip export controls as well as other decisions tied to Nvidia.

"It appears China has been circumventing our export controls for advanced chips, and it appears the Department of Commerce is either incompetent, or they knew about it, but were allowing it to happen," said Louisiana Republican and Trump ally Sen. John Kennedy. "Those are the appearances; we need to get the facts."

In preparation, Cantor has hired new outside legal advisers to handle the investigations, according to people familiar with the matter.

After Bessent's interview last week on CNBC criticizing Lutnick's agency, some Commerce officials were convinced that Trump would see the Treasury secretary's comments and fire Lutnick, a person familiar with the matter said. The tensions between the two secretaries have continued to simmer. In the Philippines, Bessent and Lutnick were spotted in the same room ignoring each other during a signing event. When Lutnick was directed to a seat next to Bessent, the Treasury secretary ignored him and turned to speak to Rubio. But as Trump went down a receiving line of his cabinet secretaries in the Philippines, he paused to speak with Lutnick and Miller, who smiled and laughed together.

The secretary enjoys some support from key Republicans in the Senate. Lutnick has brought "energy, passion and creativity" to his role, said Texas Republican Sen. Ted Cruz who, like many other lawmakers, declined to comment on the dispute between the commerce and Treasury secretaries. "I'm going to stay out of that particular pissing match," Cruz said.
