## Prosecutors and defense offer sharply different accounts of Huawei’s conduct

The racketeering conspiracy trial of Huawei Technologies approached its end Tuesday in Brooklyn federal court after a two-month-long trial, with prosecutors and the defense offering sharply different accounts of the Chinese telecommunications giant’s conduct. Assistant U.S. Attorney Meredith Arfa told jurors that Huawei encouraged workers to lie, cheat and steal while helping Iran spy on its citizens. Defense attorney Brian Heberlig said federal prosecutors had decided Huawei was a criminal enterprise and built their case from that premise. The jury was expected to begin deliberations sometime Wednesday.

Arfa said Huawei had for two decades encouraged a workforce of over 200,000 workers in 170 countries to lie, cheat and steal “for a competitive advantage to grow and protect its business.” She said the company committed crimes targeting American companies, the U.S. financial system and the American government while providing Iran with equipment to spy on its citizens.

Heberlig said federal prosecutors had decided Huawei was a criminal enterprise and built the case from that premise. “They began with guilt and worked backwards,” he said. “When the evidence didn’t fit, they moved the goalposts.”

He credited Huawei’s growth to its success in engineering, investment, innovation and competition. He said prosecutors built the case in part by focusing on wrongdoing by a few employees who were fired after their misdeeds were discovered. Prosecutors sometimes blamed the company for gaining access to competitors’ intellectual property when the materials were already public, he said. “You can’t steal a secret that isn’t secret,” Heberlig said.

Huawei is the world’s largest supplier of gear for wireless telecommunications networks, but the company is not allowed to sell equipment to U.S. carriers over fears it poses a security risk. Other Western allies, including Canada and Britain, have also blacklisted Huawei.

The Chinese company makes phones and other consumer electronics but has been cut off from other lines of business. More recently, Huawei has expanded into chipmaking as the boom in artificial intelligence fuels demand for microprocessors.

During President Donald Trump’s first term, his administration raised national security concerns about Huawei and began lobbying Western allies against including the company’s products in their next-generation high-speed wireless networks. The United States announced the criminal charges in early 2019 and has since expanded the allegations.

Chinese officials said the United States had engaged in “economic bullying” and improperly used national security as a pretext for “oppressing Chinese companies.”

The jury was expected to begin deliberations sometime Wednesday after both sides presented their closing arguments.
