## Over 300,000 Virginians face Medicaid enrollment risk next year

Virginia lawmakers are preparing for the 2027 legislative session with two questions: how federal Medicaid changes will affect state spending and whether Congress will restore expired Affordable Care Act subsidies. Because Virginia uses a two-year budget cycle, they will begin earmarking spending allocations for the next two years this January.

H.R. 1, the reconciliation bill Congress passed last summer, contains forthcoming changes to Medicaid eligibility. Healthcare leaders and researchers predicted the nation's uninsured population would surge after the bill's passage. Starting January 2027, Medicaid enrollees will face an additional verification process each year and are expected to meet work or volunteer-hours criteria unless they have certain medical conditions. More than 5,000 lawful immigrants were pushed off Medicaid this fall as the changes rolled out.

A state report found 13 rural hospitals could be subject to closure or service cuts, and multiple Virginia hospitals have already trimmed services and cited H.R. 1 as a factor. Sen. Barbara Favola, D-Arlington, who chairs the chamber's Education and Health Committee, said, “The requirements were intended to kick people off the rolls,” and added, “The way you stop the bleed is by keeping more people insured, not less.”

Healthcare affordability and access defined the congressional midterm elections. Democratic candidates in Virginia and across the country built their campaigns around H.R. 1 and restoring expired ACA subsidies, helping their party take control of both chambers. “I think the American people sent a pretty darn strong message,” Favola said.

Favola, who also serves on the Senate's finance committee and attended a briefing on the state of Medicaid at the chamber's recent retreat, noted uncertainty over how soon Congress can act and whether President Donald Trump will sign what it sends. State lawmakers must forecast carefully as they enter the next budget cycle. A presentation at the retreat showed continued growth in Medicaid expenditures because of rising healthcare costs and the program's expansion.

Virginia's total healthcare spending increased from $9.4 billion in fiscal year 2017 to $28.5 billion in 2026. The report attributed that increase to the state's 2018 vote to expand Medicaid, which widened eligibility for thousands of Virginians. The expansion enabled people without employer-provided insurance who made too little to comfortably purchase an ACA plan to qualify for Medicaid coverage.

Over 300,000 people in the Medicaid expansion population could be at risk of dropping out next year because of H.R. 1. Medicaid is the largest source of federal funds to states, and the report said that status prompted President Donald Trump and Republican majorities in Congress to cut spending for the program and boost it elsewhere. Medicaid spending in Virginia is expected to reach 24% of the general fund budget by 2028. That general fund draws taxes from individuals, corporations and sales and use taxes.

H.R. 1 will also change hospital funding mechanisms, affecting hospital revenue and hospitals' ability to contribute to state funding responsibilities for the expanded Medicaid population. Favola said President Donald Trump could become a sticking point if Congress tried to reverse H.R. 1's Medicaid impact. She noted Democratic gains in GOP-leaning states with rural populations that rely on Medicaid.

In remarks late this summer, U.S. Sen. Tim Kaine, D-Virginia, and U.S. Rep. Jennifer McClellan, D-Richmond, suggested voters could compel more lawmakers to act on healthcare access and possibly reverse the H.R. 1 changes.

Favola said the more likely near-term congressional action was to restore expired ACA subsidies that had helped low-income families afford insurance. The subsidy fight was at the center of a government shutdown last October and spurred 17 Republicans nationwide to side with Democratic colleagues on extending the subsidies. The votes fell short at the time, but Democrats have since clinched nearly 260 seats and the party's largest majority in over 30 years.

Virginia has invested $150 million in a state-level substitute for the ACA subsidies. Eligible families can use the state program as open enrollment is under way for the next year.
