## Disney employment practices and “The View” remain under FCC investigation

U.S. District Judge Loren AliKhan granted ABC a preliminary injunction on Wednesday, directing the Federal Communications Commission to stop its early review of eight ABC television station licenses. AliKhan said ABC was likely to prevail on its claim that the FCC was considering revoking the licenses as retaliation for programming critical of President Trump.

ABC sued the FCC in August in U.S. District Court, requesting a temporary restraining order and preliminary injunction to halt the agency's license-renewal process. AliKhan granted the preliminary injunction on Wednesday.

The FCC said it launched the review to determine whether Disney's diversity, equity and inclusion initiatives violated the agency's prohibition on unlawful discrimination. An early review can put a broadcaster at risk of losing its licenses.

ABC argued that the FCC was seeking to punish it for content the administration did not like and that the review violated its First Amendment rights. On that claim, AliKhan wrote that ABC was “likely to succeed on their First Amendment claim.” She also said the FCC's explanation that its actions were motivated by the DEI probe was not “credible on the record before the court.”

In her ruling, AliKhan said FCC Chairman Brendan Carr “has taken President Trump's rhetoric as his marching orders.” AliKhan also noted the timing of the FCC's action: the agency launched the early review on April 28, a day after President Trump criticized remarks by Kimmel.

President Trump had disparaged ABC programming, particularly the late-night show “Jimmy Kimmel Live!,” which frequently makes fun of him. He also had suggested that ABC should lose its broadcast licenses for not carrying his address on election integrity last July.

“This is not a close case,” AliKhan wrote. The ruling focused on ABC's likelihood of success on its challenge to the early license review.

AliKhan's order did not stop two separate FCC investigations: Disney's employment practices and “The View.” AliKhan also declined ABC's request for a broader injunction barring the FCC from threatening the company over editorial decisions.

In the same ruling, AliKhan said: “No one doubts that the Commission may enforce the law in the manner it sees fit. But in doing so, Defendants may not retaliate against or punish Plaintiffs and other broadcast license holders for disfavored speech.”

The Wall Street Journal reported that ABC's morning talk show “The View” often criticizes Trump. The FCC's investigation of “The View” concerns its equal-time rules, which require stations that have political candidates on non-news shows during elections to provide similar airtime to their opponents. The agency launched the inquiry to determine whether the program had violated those rules.

An ABC spokesman said in a statement, “Today's decision is an important victory for the First Amendment,” and added, “We thank the court for its careful consideration.”

Democratic FCC Commissioner Anna Gomez, a critic of Carr's actions, welcomed the ruling. Gomez said, “This decision shows that this FCC is a paper tiger whose threats crumble when challenged by those willing to defend their rights.” The FCC did not immediately respond to a request for comment.


As MSI reported in [ABC's August lawsuit](/articles/2026-08-18-disney-s-abc-sues-fcc-over-license-challenges-the-view-probe/), ABC asked a federal court to halt the FCC's early license review and challenge the agency's inquiry into “The View.” The judge's order grants ABC preliminary relief on the license-review claim; the separate “The View” investigation continues, and the court declined ABC's request for broader relief.
