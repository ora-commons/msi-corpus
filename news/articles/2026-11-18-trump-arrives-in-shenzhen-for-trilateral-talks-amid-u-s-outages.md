## Rubio says no evidence ties disruptions to rivals, hackers or criminals

President Donald Trump arrived in Shenzhen, China, on Wednesday for a first-ever trilateral meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin. The trip was intended to reinforce the United States' status as the world's leading technology power and project strength and certainty ahead of the potentially complicated meeting, but a series of technical outages at home left the administration scrambling to calm the American public. Shenzhen, a skyscraper-filled technology hub, was hosting world leaders for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit.

The outages left people in the United States worried about accessing their email, checking bank accounts or safely flying on an airplane. The affected services and agencies included Microsoft, the Federal Aviation Administration, JPMorgan Chase and the National Security Agency's communications network. From Manila on Tuesday, Trump was initially nonchalant, saying, "It's fine," and suggesting the outages followed from the country being "big and powerful." By the second day of his trip, however, the disruptions had overshadowed the visit and challenged the image he sought to project.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who also serves as Trump's national security adviser, stepped in to reassure Americans on Trump's behalf. He said there was "zero" evidence that the outages were connected or tied to rival nations, cyber hackers or criminals. Rubio also described the disruption that can accompany system repairs: "Sometimes when you are going in to fix your system because you've identified a vulnerability, it creates a short-term disruption."

The Federal Aviation Administration separately attributed Friday's ground stop to an "equipment issue" during a telecommunications network upgrade.

Xi greeted Trump on the tarmac after Air Force One arrived in Shenzhen, reciprocating the gesture Trump made during Xi's state visit to Washington in September. At the foot of the airplane's stairs, the two leaders shook hands before embracing as a group of dancers waved flowers and flags. Putin had arrived earlier Wednesday, and Xi did not greet him at the airport.

Trump had signaled that he wanted an improvised approach during his Shenzhen stay. The Associated Press described his unscripted, relationship-centered diplomacy as a bet that it could create a legacy to reshape world affairs during his final two years in the White House.

On Thursday, Trump said he planned to discuss with Xi and Putin limiting nuclear weapons and ending the war in Ukraine, which began in 2022 with Russia's invasion. Analysts were not expecting substantive breakthroughs despite the unprecedented nature of the meeting. Before the trip, Trump's aides had told reporters that Ukraine would not be on the agenda. Trump said on Tuesday that the deaths of tens of thousands of Russian troops every month had created an opening to resolve the conflict. "I think it's very close to getting it done," Trump said. "We're going to have a good meeting on that."

Trump also raised the possibility of a private meeting with Putin and a separate sit-down with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un. Rubio clarified Wednesday that Trump was "serious" about meeting Kim as a way to resolve tensions with North Korea, but he suggested no meeting was currently scheduled for Thursday.

Even without the outages, questions about how to handle artificial intelligence were expected to loom over the summit. The Associated Press reported that the technology was viewed as key to unlocking faster economic growth, while its rapid development posed national security risks and could evolve in ways that put AI beyond human control. The outages also raised questions about whether AI could disrupt daily life. Separately, newly identified cyber vulnerabilities require software to be constantly upgraded to fix flaws or other security holes, known as patching, which is intended to improve how the technology runs.

Trump's tariffs, intended to curb China's trade surplus, had done little to stop the progress of the world's dominant manufacturer, which for the second straight year was expected to have exported $1 trillion more goods than it imported.

Jensen Huang was recently removed as the CEO of Nvidia after he admitted to an affair with a woman alleged to be an agent of the Chinese government. Beijing denied that she was a spy. The Associated Press reported that the affair raised concerns about how China has been able to access Nvidia computer chips to develop its own AI models that are competing with those in the United States.

When asked why the United States had not been more aggressive in cutting off China's access to advanced computer chips, Trump said there were differences of opinion within his administration. "We have a very good relationship with China, and you don't want to insult them," he said. Trump said he did not plan to raise Taiwan during the summit. The self-governing island is claimed by China and is the leading manufacturer of advanced computer chips, including chips designed by U.S. companies. Trump nevertheless expected Xi to raise Taiwan's sovereignty, saying, "I'm sure he'll raise it," and, "I won't have to raise it."

Before landing in Shenzhen, Trump had lunch in Manila with Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. at the Malacañang Presidential Palace. Marcos had sent fried chicken from the Philippine chain Jollibee to the U.S. delegation for a late-night snack Tuesday and complimented Trump for "working very hard." Trump praised the U.S. bond with the Philippines and the palace's interior decoration, saying, "Our people share common history, common values, common interests, common architectural tastes," and, "I love what you've done here."

After the meeting, Trump said the two countries had reached a trade deal on Wednesday, although he had also said an agreement had been reached the previous year. No details were provided, but the White House said the agreement would involve Manila buying more energy and farm products. The U.S. goods trade deficit with the Philippines had risen under Trump, climbing from $4.9 billion in 2024 to $12.8 billion during the first nine months of this year, according to the Census Bureau.

Trump also had a short reunion with Imelda Marcos, the former first lady and mother of the Philippines' current president. She was known for her lavish shoe collection and had known Trump for decades through the New York City party scene. During the 1986 "People Power" uprising, her husband, Ferdinand Marcos, was ousted as the Philippines' strongman leader. Trump called the 97-year-old "always the most beautiful," and she appeared to hand him a gift, according to a video posted by the White House.
