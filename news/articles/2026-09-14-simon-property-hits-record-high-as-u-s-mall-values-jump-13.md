## Malls now outperform every other commercial property type

The Wall Street Journal reported Monday that U.S. mall property values rose 13% over the past year, more than double the gain for the overall commercial real estate market. The reversal runs against years of widely reported decline in brick-and-mortar shopping centers, which the Journal called "a surprising twist for commercial real estate."

The shift was reported by the Journal's Kate King in the newspaper's 10-Point newsletter. In its lead item, the paper wrote that "for years, the demise of the American mall felt like an open-and-shut case" before the recent turnaround.

The Journal attributed the reversal to resilient consumer spending combined with operators' "smart pivots into luxury retail, dining and entertainment," which the paper said had helped bring shoppers back. The Journal reported that malls are now "outperforming every other property type" in commercial real estate.

Simon Property, the U.S.'s largest mall owner according to the Journal, is hitting record stock highs on the property-value gains. The Journal described the long-beleaguered mall business as "having a moment."
