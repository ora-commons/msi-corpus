## Raid in Sinaloa used U.S. intelligence, Defense Ministry says

Mexican security forces captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, a drug kingpin who was on the FBI's 10 most-wanted list, after a fierce gunfight, the Defense Ministry said Saturday.

The ministry said members of the army's special forces and other elite groups seized Meza Flores, known as "Chapo Isidro." The Wall Street Journal reported that the capture dealt a heavy blow to one of Mexico's most powerful drug-trafficking organizations.

Meza Flores controlled key smuggling routes along the U.S.-Mexico border. He led an organized crime group that held much of the border state of Sonora in northwestern Mexico, across from Arizona, and he operated along routes used for fentanyl and other narcotics.

The Defense Ministry said the military operation was carried out with U.S. intelligence. According to the ministry, special forces conducted a "stealth and surprise" operation on Friday night, descending on the city of Guasave in northern Sinaloa state.

Guasave sits in the cradle of Mexico's drug-trafficking industry, a region the Journal described as now ravaged by turf wars.

Mexican soldiers established security perimeters to surround Meza Flores and his gunmen, who responded with heavy gunfire and explosives.

Video recordings posted on social media showed residents scrambling for cover as gunmen torched vehicles to block roads, the Journal reported. Baseball games were suspended as military helicopters flew overhead, firing high-powered machine guns.

Meza Flores was captured inside a house and transported to Mexico City.

The Journal described him as a low-profile operator who mostly shunned the violent methods of his rivals in Sinaloa.

The operation places the capture of a federally listed most-wanted fugitive alongside a U.S. intelligence contribution, in a border state whose smuggling corridors feed narcotics flows north into Arizona and beyond.
