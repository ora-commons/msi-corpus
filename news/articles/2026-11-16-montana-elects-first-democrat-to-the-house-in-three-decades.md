## Montana elects first Democrat to the House in three decades

**Subtype:** fact

- Montana's 1st Congressional District elected Sam Forstag, a 32-year-old former smokejumper, on November 3, ending a three-decade stretch without a Democratic House member.
- The district had not elected a Democrat in 30 years, and no Democrat had won a Montana statewide race in 10 years.
- Forstag campaigned against President Trump on public lands and federal jobs while also criticizing Democrats, saying he made barely 19 bucks an hour jumping into wildfires during the Biden administration.

## Analyst says too early to call Forstag's win a rural model

Montana has been solid Trump country since he was first elected president in 2016, and until the evening of November 3, it seemed like Democrats in the state could not figure out how to respond to his popularity. That changed as Forstag declared victory before a boisterous crowd of mostly young voters in the liberal college town of Missoula.

"We can again be a state that bends the course of this country's history for the better. The only way we fix this is we send working-class people to Washington so that we can go fight for ourselves," Forstag told the crowd.

Montana's 1st Congressional District stretches from Glacier National Park to Yellowstone. It has not elected a Democrat for three decades, and it has been 10 years since any Democrat in the state won a statewide race. The last Democrat to win the district was the late Montana congressman Pat Williams, the father of Whitney Williams, who watched Forstag's victory as a sign that Democrats should not write off rural America.

"Democrats didn't do a good enough job with rural America, there's no question. But I think that that's changing, and I think it's changing pretty dramatically, and we can see it in these numbers tonight," Whitney Williams said.

Forstag did not shy away from attacking President Trump, casting him as a corrupt billionaire gutting public lands protections and public services. But he also criticized Democrats, saying he made barely 19 bucks an hour jumping out of airplanes into wildfires during the Biden administration. The day after the election, Forstag, in jeans and a Carhartt jacket, stood in Missoula's freezing fog for what he said was his first-ever official press conference.

"Quite frankly, I don't care what party you're in. I just care that you're going to be out there working for working people," Forstag said. He said working people too many times to count.

In the huge district, people drive pickups to work and are fuming over high gas prices, and the national parks and federal lands agencies cut back under Trump's DOGE did not sit well in tourism-dependent Montana. Lee Banville, the Montana Public Radio political analyst, said Forstag narrowed his message deliberately.

"He really has kind of laser-focused in on this message of, you know, it's not about turning Montana blue. It's about turning Montana blue collar," Banville said.

Banville said it is too early to tell whether Forstag will serve as a model for rural Democrats or whether he simply ran at the right time. The former smokejumper was active on social media, working in the woods and criticizing Trump for cutting hundreds of federal jobs in the state. Banville compared Forstag's social-media appeal to that of Talarico and Ossoff.

"In some ways, you know, kind of like Talarico, kind of like Ossoff, he's kind of made for TikTok. He's very quotable. He's running his campaign both as a broad effort but also as a real soundbite machine," Banville said.

David Bedey, a Republican state legislator, said there was not as much enthusiasm for Forstag's opponent, conservative radio host Aaron Flint. Bedey said Trump's policies like tariffs and the Iran war may have alienated some Montanans who voted for him in 2024.

"I think it was largely the moment, but I'm going to give Forstag credit. He ran a great campaign. It's amazing how many people are fed up with both parties," Bedey said.

Before Trump, Montana was famous for split-ticket voters. Whitney Williams said Forstag's win is a sign that pattern is possible again.

"Montanans like voting for people who are authentic and honest. And they're working people. And I think that we Democrats have fielded extraordinary candidates around this country," Williams said.

Voters in the district say those candidates must now actually deliver for working people, many of whom appear to have soured on the president and his MAGA movement. Forstag is currently on Capitol Hill for freshman orientation.
