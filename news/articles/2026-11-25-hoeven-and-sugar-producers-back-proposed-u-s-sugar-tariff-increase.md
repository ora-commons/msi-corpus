## Written comments are due Dec. 30; hearing is set for Jan. 14

The federal government is seeking to protect U.S. sugar producers from what it calls unfair competition from Brazil and other countries. On Wednesday, U.S. Sen. John Hoeven, R-N.D., held a press conference at the headquarters of American Crystal Sugar in Moorhead to highlight plans for an additional tariff on sugar imports. Greer said the proposed increase is aimed at Brazil but will apply to other countries.

The Red River Valley of North Dakota and Minnesota is a prime sugar beet growing region. Hoeven said the domestic industry needs to be protected from foreign dumping of sugar into the U.S. market or consumers will be affected.

“If these unfair trade practices wipe out our industry, the consumer is disadvantaged because then we’re at the mercy of whatever price they want to charge,” Hoeven said of sugar-exporting countries.

Written comments on the policy are due Dec. 30. The trade agency will hold a hearing about the proposed tariff modification Jan. 14.

Hoeven cited a study by the North Dakota State University Agricultural Risk Policy Center. The study found that sugar imports going over quotas resulted in an estimated loss of up to $1.8 billion for the U.S. sugar industry last year.

Hoeven was joined by representatives of American Crystal Sugar and Minn-Dak Farmers Cooperative, which is based in Wahpeton, North Dakota. The gathering included American Crystal Sugar President and CEO Tom Astrup and Minn-Dak President and CEO Rick Kasper.

“We will urge USTR to take this opportunity to update the sugar tariffs, thereby ensuring that foreign producers are not enriched at the expense of American farmers and workers,” Minn-Dak President and CEO Rick Kasper said.

In May, 112 members of Congress, including all three members of the North Dakota delegation, sent a letter to the trade agency calling for action on sugar imports.
