## Transportation secretary says fiber swap on old wiring overloaded network

President Donald Trump on Monday blamed old technology and his Democratic predecessor, Joe Biden, for the nationwide grounding of all planes last week, speaking outside the White House ahead of his trip to Asia.

"What you saw on Friday was very, very old equipment that had a heart attack," Trump said. He added: "The Biden administration hooked up a lot of copper into glass and copper."

The grounding on Friday followed a router issue in the aging air traffic control system. Trump had not spoken publicly or commented on the flight grounding until Sunday, when he posted on social media criticizing Biden and asking for more money to cover the cost of system improvements.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy, who made the decision Friday to ground the planes, also pressed Congress for billions more dollars to upgrade a system he said had been neglected for decades.

Duffy said the problem began when a federal contractor was replacing a section of old copper wire with new fiber wire, causing the system to overload.

The Federal Aviation Administration said in a statement that "excessive data" was flooding the system "at a rate of 1,000 connection attempts every 4 to 5 seconds." The agency said that deluge created instability with "aircraft communications and radar feeds."

Duffy said Sunday that billions of dollars are needed to build new architecture for the network, and said companies — including AT&T, Verizon and Lumen — are ready to deploy it as soon as the money is there.

Congress has dedicated significant sums to upgrading the nation's air transportation system in recent years. Under Biden, Congress approved a massive infrastructure bill that included $25 billion over five years for airport and air traffic control infrastructure improvements, much of which went to airport projects like new runways, taxiways and updated terminals.

Under Trump, a Republican-led Congress approved $12.5 billion in 2025 to modernize the FAA's air traffic system. The Trump administration said the money would provide critical updates to outdated radar systems and traffic control centers, some dating back to the 1960s. Republicans used the same bill to allocate $170 billion for border security and immigration enforcement.

Trump's comments and congressional Democrats' criticism underscore political divisions that could widen when Democrats take control of Congress in January, with scrutiny of his administration accelerating.
