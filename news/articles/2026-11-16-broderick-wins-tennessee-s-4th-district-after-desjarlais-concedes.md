## Democrats' only U.S. House win in Tennessee this cycle

Democrat Victoria Broderick defeated eight-term Republican U.S. Rep. Scott DesJarlais in Tennessee's 4th Congressional District, giving Democrats their only U.S. House win in Tennessee during this election cycle. Almost two weeks after polls closed in the Nov. 3 midterm elections, DesJarlais conceded Monday afternoon after provisional ballots counted in Nashville showed Broderick expanding her narrow lead.

After Election Day, Broderick held a 285-vote lead over DesJarlais, with around 700 provisional ballots outstanding, leading to a drawn-out process to count them. The Associated Press had not officially declared Broderick the winner as of 1:15 p.m. Monday.

"I am incredibly grateful to the people of Tennessee's 4th District for putting their faith in me," Broderick said in a statement. "It's an honor to have earned your trust, and I don't take the responsibility of representing our communities lightly."

DesJarlais offered his support to the incoming representative. "I have personally reached out to Ms. Broderick to offer my congratulations and support on this new undertaking," he said in a statement. "I am proud of the standard our office has set in terms of representing the values, interests, and needs of our district."

Broderick addressed voters who backed her opponent. "Whether you voted for me or not, I want you to know that I will be your representative," Broderick said in her statement. "I look forward to listening and doing the work you've entrusted me to do. Thank you for giving me this opportunity. I'm ready to get to work."

Broderick is a tech support worker and member of the Tennessee Democratic Party executive committee who campaigned on affordability. She described herself as "a working-class mom from rural Tennessee" and said she was in the race "because we deserve better than being treated like an afterthought." She lives in Lincoln County with her husband and two sons. Broderick defeated four other candidates in the August Democratic primary, including Metro Nashville Councilmember Mike Cortese.

The District 4 race flew below the radar, as other Tennessee congressional races gained more national attention following a redrawing of the state's U.S. House map in the spring. National organizations, including the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee and a PAC funded by Illinois Gov. J.B. Pritzker, poured millions of dollars into candidates running in Tennessee's 5th and 9th congressional districts, while Broderick raised less than $100,000. Democratic candidates in the 5th and 9th narrowly lost their races.

Erin Covey, the head of the Cook Political Report's U.S. House ratings, said President Donald Trump won the 4th district by 24% in 2024, making Broderick's win the largest congressional district flip of the 2026 cycle. Broderick, the former chair of the Lincoln County Democratic Party, ran against DesJarlais in 2024, earning 27% of the vote to DesJarlais's 70%. But the district was drawn differently from the one she ultimately won.

During the May redistricting, Republicans redrew the state's U.S. House map in hopes of flipping the last Democratic-held seat in Memphis. Republicans held an unusual mid-cycle redistricting in the wake of the U.S. Supreme Court ruling in Louisiana v. Callais that scrapped key portions of the 1965 Voting Rights Act, chiefly one requiring states with a history of racially motivated voter suppression and discrimination to carve out majority-minority congressional districts. The new U.S. House map moved southeast Nashville into the 4th district and combined it with all of Rutherford County.

Over 80% of Broderick's total votes came from Davidson and Rutherford counties. In the Nov. 3 election, DesJarlais won nine of the district's 11 counties, which are largely rural. Broderick beat DesJarlais in Rutherford County by a few hundred votes and ran up the vote tally in Nashville-Davidson County, defeating DesJarlais by a nearly three-to-one margin.

DesJarlais, a physician, was elected to Congress in 2010 as part of the Tea Party wave, defeating incumbent Democrat Lincoln Davis. He maintained his seat despite close elections, including the 2014 Republican primary in which he defeated then-state Sen. Jim Tracy by 38 votes.

Allegations of spousal abuse trailed DesJarlais in his first campaign and later, reports he pressured one of his patients, with whom he was having an affair, to have an abortion after he impregnated her. He was a member of the U.S. House Freedom Caucus and received an A-plus rating from Susan B. Anthony Pro-Life America, an anti-abortion group.
