## Kennedy faces an impeachment call as US loses measles elimination status

**Subtype:** fact

- The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention reported more than 7,700 US measles cases since January 2025.

- The Pan American Health Organization revoked US measles elimination status on November 17.

## CDC announced $100 million for vaccination and outbreak response

Judith Levine, a frequent Guardian contributor, made the case in a November 19, 2026, opinion column that Health and Human Services Secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr should be impeached over the country’s measles resurgence. Levine wrote that at least 11 Americans had died since January 2025, the month before Kennedy became secretary, and that the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention had counted more than 7,700 cases. She placed Kennedy at the center of what she described as a federal misinformation campaign about vaccination, linking his policies and public statements to a sliding vaccination rate.

Levine said one of Friday’s victims was a child in Wisconsin, where a baby died of measles the week before, and that Pennsylvania had not disclosed the age of the other person. The column said outbreaks in 47 states had pushed the numbers of illnesses and deaths to levels unseen since 1991. It noted that the United States had eliminated measles a quarter-century earlier and that the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) had held the country’s elimination status since 2000.

On November 17, PAHO revoked that status. Before the revocation, the column said, only a few dozen to a few hundred cases had been reported annually since the mid-1990s, and small outbreaks had been contained within the year, the standard for gaining and keeping the status. KFF Health News said, “measles has re-established regular transmission in the country.”

Levine wrote that most of those sickened, hospitalized and killed were under 20 years old, with an alarming percentage not yet five. She said 112 pregnant women had been hospitalized with measles complications over the past two years, two had lost their pregnancies and a dozen babies had been born infected. Measles is highly contagious and vaccine-preventable, the column said, and can kill through pneumonia, leave a person deaf or permanently damage the immune system.

Her explanation centered on a sliding vaccination rate and growing suspicion of vaccines, which she said had circulated for decades but gained renewed force through Kennedy and Trump. She cited Kennedy’s statements that WiFi causes “leaky brain” and that chemicals in water make children transgender, and said he amplified the false claim that vaccinations cause autism. Citing Reuters, the column described Kennedy as “the nation’s leading anti-vaccine activist.” Levine said almost all recent measles victims were unvaccinated.

Levine said the virus started spreading in Texas in January 2025 and that Kennedy had not mobilized the CDC’s rumor-quelling mechanisms or enlisted the American Academy of Pediatrics, ordinarily the government’s partner in guiding frightened families through outbreaks. She noted that Kennedy disputed being labeled “anti-vaccine,” endorsed the measles, mumps and rubella vaccination as “effective” and then said it was “not safety tested.” The column said the combined vaccine was proven safe and effective.

According to Levine, Kennedy fired all 17 members of the CDC Advisory Committee on Immunizations in June 2025 and staffed the reconstituted committee with vaccine skeptics. In January, after a presidential executive order, the CDC released a plan to revise childhood immunization guidelines, which Levine said would complicate and loosen them. “At a time when parents, pediatricians and the public are looking for clear guidance and accurate information, this ill-considered decision will sow further chaos and confusion and erode confidence in immunizations,” the AAP president, Dr Andrew D Racine, said.

The AAP sued. In March 2026, a federal judge annulled all votes taken by the reconstituted committee and ordered restoration of the pre-Kennedy guidelines. Levine said Trump issued another executive order that summer, introducing the committee’s “gold standard” recommendations. Trump was most focused on separating the measles, mumps and rubella vaccine into individual shots, according to the column, and claimed the three-in-one product was “quite lethal”; Levine described the claim as false. Experts said spacing out the immunizations would endanger children, while drugmakers said the product could not be divided.

Levine also said Kennedy had sown distrust in state health departments, long the source of data for federal agencies. After Pennsylvania reported the deaths of two babies too young to be vaccinated, Kennedy posted without evidence that one of them “may … have been altogether fabricated.” In September, he ordered CDC director Erica Schwartz to remove the two deaths from publicly accessible data. In late October, Schwartz declined to sign off on an urgent health alert to clinicians encountering measles-related symptoms in pregnant patients that they had not seen in decades, or ever.

Levine said CDC higher-ups had also postponed other vaccine-related reports cleared for publication by expert readers. One was a study of infant mortality from whooping cough, or pertussis, which found that the mothers of most of the babies who died did not receive the vaccine during pregnancy that protects infants, who are most susceptible to severe pertussis. “We don’t have data on this part. The experience is limited,” a Canadian obstetrician told the Washington Post. “When I’m dealing with this situation, I’m dealing with not just Jane who’s pregnant, but the baby or babies inside her womb.”

After PAHO withdrew the country’s elimination status, the CDC announced a $100 million investment to regain it. Its statement said: “We will encourage MMR vaccination, expand access to vaccines, and help communities close gaps in protection. We will equip state, local, tribal, and territorial health departments with tools and technical support to identify communities at increased risk, engage trusted partners, detect cases quickly, and respond effectively to outbreaks.”

The chair of an independent U.S. advisory committee to PAHO said this week: “We’ve gotten comfortable letting children be stopped by a deadly disease and die from it.” Levine wrote that it was hard to imagine the plan represented an epiphany for Kennedy after his earlier actions.

The column also cited an April 2025 appearance in Indianapolis, when more than 800 Americans had contracted measles and three had died. Kennedy praised his staff and said the CDC had “done a very good job containing the measles outbreak.” The column reached back to the Covid-19 epidemic, when Axios asked Trump to comment on 1,000 people dying each day from suffocation and organ failure caused by the virus. Trump replied, “It is what it is.”

Levine used those episodes to argue that the Trump administration had become indifferent to death and that Kennedy bore central responsibility for the public-health consequences of his actions. Her conclusion was that HHS did not want the public to know that vaccines save lives and that refusing them imperils lives, especially those of the most vulnerable.
