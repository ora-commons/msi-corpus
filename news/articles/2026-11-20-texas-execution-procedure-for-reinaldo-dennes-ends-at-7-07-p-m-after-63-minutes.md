## Technicians find usable veins in Dennes’s left foot and neck

The procedure was completed at 7:07 p.m., 63 minutes after Dennes was strapped to the gurney. One minute later, after a physician examined him, Dennes was pronounced dead at 7:08 p.m. at the penitentiary in Huntsville.

Texas Department of Criminal Justice spokeswoman Amanda Hernandez said execution technicians made repeated unsuccessful attempts to find suitable veins. Hernandez could not say how many attempts were made. Technicians eventually found a vein in Dennes’s left foot and another on the left side of his neck that accepted the IV needles, she said.

Dennes’s feet were covered by a sheet, and only the right side of his neck was visible to witnesses. Hernandez said the injection sites were in line with the prison system’s procedures. Alternate sites had been used in previous executions, although the need to do so was relatively rare.

Journalists and observers reported delays of more than 30 minutes before they could enter the death chamber, where Dennes’s final statement was to be read and the execution was to take place. When admitted, Dennes was said to appear docile, with his eyes closed and his head pointed toward the ceiling.

One observer of the execution said the effects of the lethal injection were like nothing the observer had ever seen before. The reporter in attendance said Dennes’s reaction to the drug was also like nothing the reporter had ever seen. The Guardian said Dennes’s reaction once the injection was administered shocked those in attendance.

The Associated Press reported that Dennes did not appear dishevelled and showed no sign of struggle. Before the lethal injection was administered, Dennes reportedly told the warden overseeing the procedure, “Go.” As soon as the procedure began, his demeanor was said to have changed. Dennes exclaimed, “The pain!” and “Go! Go!”

For nearly three minutes, Dennes repeatedly cried out: “Take me father! … Take me into your arms! … Take me father!” He occasionally interjected his cries with obscenities, a grunt and then groans.

Dennes said his right leg was “killing me,” likening the sensation to a cramp. “I don’t think this is normal,” he said at one point.

In previous executions, prisoners have been known to report that the lethal drug was hot or cold, that they could smell or taste it, or that it hurt, as The Guardian noted.

Dennes had been sentenced to death after his conviction for the 1996 murder of Houston diamond dealer Janos Szucs. Before the injection was administered, the warden asked whether he had a final statement. Dennes spoke about being “extremely blessed” by the love of “the father” and said he hoped his family and loved ones “get to know this great gift and great grace of God the father.”

Hernandez also suggested Dennes may have been faking complaints about pain. She said Dennes had complained about pain from areas where no injections were attempted.
