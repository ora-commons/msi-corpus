## Any Democratic legislation would need Trump's signature under divided government

Democrats took power across the country in the midterms, and the swing voters who could be decisive in the next election are focused on what their new representatives do next. Asked about their hopes and fears after the election, Swing Shift voters told NPR again and again that they want the incoming Democratic Congress to focus on the issues that affect their daily lives, and they fear more of the same broken politics. John, who backed Republicans in the midterms, said he still wants Democrats to tackle the cost of living; Michelle, who also chose Republicans, said she hopes the politicians voted in will live up to their promises and deliver change; and Wally, who swung back to Democrats, said voting is not the end of the game and that Democrats must be held to some sort of tangible change. Jason, who voted for Roy Cooper for Senate, wants the incoming Democratic Congress to focus on the cost of living and steer clear of impeachment.

NPR has agreed not to use the full names of the Swing Shift voters so they can speak freely about politics without fear of personal or professional repercussions. Some of the accounts reached the newsroom as voice memos. Theresa, a voter from Pennsylvania, sent hers days after the midterms; Wally in Georgia sent his at 9:56 PM on election night.

Theresa voted for President Biden in 2020, President Trump in 2024 and split her ticket in the midterms. Like many voters, her top issue this year was affordability, and she said she is eager for the cost of living to come down. "I just don't remember it ever being as bad in our time," Theresa said. "And I'm just hoping we get some relief and we get some relief soon." She also wants an end to the standoff she expects. "I am hoping that we don't experience a bunch of gridlock and a lot of whining and crying and just political infighting for the next two years," she said. "I'm hoping we don't have that. I fear that we will have that."

Wally voted for Trump in 2024 but swung back to Democrats in the midterms. He had initially supported Trump in hopes that a Trump presidency would shake up the political system, but that did not happen, and he says the second Trump term has been exhausting. As he sees it, the decisive Democratic wins signal that the country is ready to move on. "I believe that this is a lot of Trump's doing and pushback on his style of politics and how he governs," he said. "Trump has been shooting himself in the foot for many months now," Wally said. "But voting isn't the end of the game here. We have to hold Democrats to some sort of tangible change and not just promises about what they want to do."

Jason from North Carolina split his ticket, voting for Democrat Roy Cooper for Senate and the Republican incumbent for the House. His own congressman was defeated by someone nearly unknown to him. "My congressman was defeated this time by someone that no one's really ever heard of," said Jason, sounding almost puzzled by the blue wave that crashed on North Carolina. Jason wants the incoming Democratic Congress to focus on the cost of living and steer clear of impeachment. "My fear is that the Democrats now are going to spend the next 2 years not focusing on any of the things that they say Trump didn't do," said Jason.

Michelle in Michigan voted for Biden in 2020 but chose Republicans in 2024 and again in the midterms. "This is the first time I think ever since I've been voting that not one of the people that I voted for won their election," Michelle said. She said she understands why so many people voted for Democrats: Americans are tired, she said, of high gas prices, the cost of living and not being able to afford their groceries. They voted for change. "I will choose to hope because that's really all we can do is hope," said Michelle. "Hope that the politicians that were voted in will live up to the promises they made and that change will truly be made."

John from Pennsylvania said it felt as though his state went from purple to blue overnight. "I'm just very surprised," John said. "Not surprised that Democrats had a good night, but they had a very good night. Absolutely amazed that they won in Texas and places that just seemed like they were really red." John voted for Biden in 2020 and Trump in 2024, and in the midterms he stuck with Republicans despite concerns about the cost of living and the war with Iran. Even so, he wants the incoming Democratic Congress working on affordability. "Over these next 2 years I hope they are concerned with and concentrate on the affordability issue," John said. "I hope they work with President Trump to try to lower prices. I just hope it's not two years of investigations and impeachment hearings."

Gerald in Georgia ran through the list of states where Democrats won Senate races in disbelief. "Come on man, Texas turned blue," Gerald said. "South Carolina turned blue, Ohio, Kansas. Like, this was a referendum of epic proportions." A Biden-to-Trump voter from Georgia who said he has gone all in on MAGA, Gerald is already looking ahead to a future without Trump leading Republicans. "The Donald Trump era for all intents and purposes is coming to an end in 2028 for certain, if not before that," Gerald said.

Across these accounts, Swing Shift voters said again and again that they want the incoming Democratic Congress to focus on the issues that affect their daily lives, and they fear more of the same broken politics; Wally framed the bar as tangible change rather than promises. That hope now meets an institutional limit. Anything Democrats pass in a divided government would have to be signed by President Trump, and the president has been inconsistent in recent days about whether he wants cooperation or combat with the new Congress. John, who backed Republicans in the midterms, said he hopes Democrats work with the president to try to lower prices.

The voters themselves disagree about what comes next. Michelle said she hopes for change and chooses to hope; John, while surprised by the scale of the Democratic wins, backed Republicans in the midterms; Gerald said the Trump era is ending. The thread they returned to was the same: Swing Shift voters said again and again that they hope the incoming Democratic Congress will focus on the issues that affect their daily lives, though they fear more of the same broken politics, as Democrats prepare to take power in Congress.
