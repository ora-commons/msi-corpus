## Incoming Democratic senators decline to back Schumer as majority leader

**Subtype:** fact

- Incoming Democratic senators-elect are declining to support Chuck Schumer as Senate majority leader, though Schumer says he will be re-elected to the post.
- Annie Andrews of South Carolina told Fox News's Bret Baier she believes it is time for generational change on both sides of the aisle.
- Adam Hamilton of Kansas told CNN on the campaign trail he would not support Schumer as the party's leader, and Abdul El-Sayed of Michigan declined to back him in a CNN interview.
- James Talarico of Texas, Juliana Stratton of Illinois, Chris Pappas of New Hampshire, Peggy Flanagan of Minnesota and Troy Jackson of Maine have also declined to support Schumer.
- No other Senate Democrat had officially entered the race for leader as of Friday afternoon, and Schumer, 75, has led Senate Democrats since 2017.

## Schumer says he will be re-elected; no rival has formally entered

Annie Andrews, the Democratic senator-elect from South Carolina, told Fox News's Bret Baier on Thursday that the Democratic party needs new leadership. Asked whether she would support Schumer's leadership bid, she replied: "I still very much believe it's time for generational change on both sides of the aisle."

Adam Hamilton, the senator-elect from Kansas, told CNN on the campaign trail that he would not support Schumer as the party's leader. Hamilton, the first Democrat to win a Kansas Senate seat in 94 years, had pitched himself as independent from the Democratic party.

Abdul El-Sayed, the senator-elect from Michigan, again declined to support Schumer as the majority leader on CNN on Thursday and said he wanted to consider other options.

Other incoming Democrats have also declined to support Schumer, including James Talarico, the senator-elect from Texas; Juliana Stratton, the senator-elect of Illinois; Chris Pappas, the senator-elect of New Hampshire; Peggy Flanagan, the senator-elect of Minnesota; and Troy Jackson, the senator-elect of Maine.

Democratic voters have indicated they want newer and younger leadership in the party. Over the last two years, progressive Democrats have scored major victories in local and state elections.

Those results have led some lawmakers to push for similar changes in decade-old leadership. Schumer, 75, has served as the Democrats' leader in the Senate since 2017.

Schumer did not appear worried about his bid for Senate majority leader, telling reporters on Wednesday: "I'll be re-elected as majority leader."

Schumer likely has enough votes within his party to become the next majority leader, and he was running uncontested as of Friday afternoon — no other Senate Democrat had officially thrown their hat into the ring.

But the longtime Democrat has also encouraged members of his party to rally around one another. "The issue is not me … the issue is focusing on the American people and what they want," Schumer told reporters.

After securing a comfortable majority in both the House and Senate, Democrats will head into a new Congress with the ability to push back on Donald Trump's administration and pass legislation they promised to voters.
