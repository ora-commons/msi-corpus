## Prejean calls Pike's failed execution attempt 'the essence of cruelty'

**Subtype:** fact

- Helen Prejean, a Catholic nun who is perhaps the best-known advocate for the abolition of the death penalty in America, told the Guardian that Tennessee's failed execution of Christa Pike was "the essence of cruelty."
- Christa Pike, 50, is conscious and speaking at a hospital after surviving Tennessee's attempt to execute her last Wednesday, her attorneys said Tuesday.
- Pike's attorneys said her recovery is "medically unprecedented" and her prognosis "unclear," with at least a long recovery expected; she remains handcuffed and shackled to her hospital bed despite severe damage to her arms.
- Tennessee Corrections Commissioner Frank Strada resigned after the procedure; Pike's case was the fifth botched execution under his leadership across two states since 2022.

## Attorneys call Pike's recovery 'medically unprecedented'

"Against all odds, Christa Pike is conscious, speaking and continues to receive critical medical care," attorneys for Pike said in a statement Tuesday. "Despite the severe damage she endured to her arms, she is still handcuffed and shackled to the bed. Her recovery is medically unprecedented. Her prognosis remains unclear but, at a minimum, we expect a long recovery."

The statement was the first indication that Pike, 50, has improved since an ambulance carried her away from a Nashville prison last Wednesday, when she survived two doses of pentobarbital as Tennessee officials attempted to put her to death in a case that shocked many in America and created headlines around the world. She was set to be the first woman executed in Tennessee in more than 200 years.

Her attorneys said they were grateful for the first responders and the medical team at the hospital "who treated Christa like a human and have provided exceptional care to her." They added that Pike was "still handcuffed and shackled to the bed."

Witnesses to the attempt described hearing Pike choking and struggling for an hour after she received the first injection. She was then hospitalized after a second dose failed to kill her; in the hospital she was put on a ventilator and had been in critical condition.

The failed procedure drew widespread condemnation across the political spectrum and among advocates for banning the death penalty. Tennessee's prison chief, Frank Strada, resigned in the aftermath; Pike's case was the fifth botched execution under his leadership across two states since 2022.

In the wake of the botched execution attempt, Helen Prejean, a Catholic nun who is perhaps the best-known advocate for the abolition of the death penalty in America, told the Guardian: "This is the essence of cruelty. Take a conscious, imaginative human being, give her a date for her death, have her anticipate it over and over again, and then take her out and try and kill her."

"She was rendered defenceless as they deliberately attempted to kill her – what greater mental torture for a human being is there?" Prejean added.

Pike was given a death sentence for the torture and killing of 19-year-old Colleen Slemmer, a fellow student at the Knoxville Job Corps, in 1995. Pike was 18 at the time. Her then-boyfriend and co-defendant, Tadaryl Shipp, was 17 and ineligible for the death penalty; he received a sentence of life imprisonment.

The Associated Press contributed to the Guardian's reporting.
