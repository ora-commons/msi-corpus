## Broderick's win raises Democrats to 259 House seats

Victoria Broderick was elected to the U.S. House from Tennessee on Election Day, narrowly defeating Republican Rep. Scott DesJarlais. The Associated Press called the race on Tuesday, putting Broderick ahead of DesJarlais.

The AP's count had Broderick ahead by fewer than 400 votes out of about 196,000, a margin of 0.18 percentage points.

Broderick's upset victory further padded Democrats' House majority, which now stands at 259 seats, the largest since 1992. One race remained too early to call, while two others were set for runoffs next month.

The Tennessee contest was a rematch between Broderick and DesJarlais, a physician first elected to the House in 2010. DesJarlais defeated Broderick with 70% of the vote in 2024.

Republican state lawmakers redrew the state's congressional boundaries, narrowing the GOP's overwhelming advantage in the district. The redraw made Broderick's district slightly more competitive. The lawmakers' effort to pick up a Democratic-held Memphis seat was successful.

Minnesota's 1st District was the last uncalled House contest. Democratic challenger Jake Johnson led Republican Rep. Brad Finstad by 0.1 points, and a recount was scheduled to begin Nov. 30.

Two Louisiana races will be decided in a December runoff.


DesJarlais conceded on Nov. 16 before the Associated Press officially called the race, as [MSI reported at the time](/articles/2026-11-16-broderick-wins-tennessee-s-4th-district-after-desjarlais-concedes/). Tuesday's call put Broderick's lead at fewer than 400 votes.
