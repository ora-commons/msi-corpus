## Proposed chip restrictions would cover Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Singapore and Brazil

Nvidia said Tuesday that it had formed the National Security and Export Control Oversight Committee following the resignation of founder Jensen Huang the previous week. The board committee will oversee national security policies, compliance with export controls and risks related to global trade.

Director Suzanne Nora Johnson will chair the committee, which will meet at least quarterly. Its work will cover company policies governing national security and the controls Nvidia must follow when shipping chips.

The Wall Street Journal reported that Huang resigned after an affair with a woman whom U.S. officials suspect was working for Chinese intelligence. Huang's departure left Colette Kress serving as interim chief executive.

The committee announcement also came as the Commerce Department proposed a rule to tighten restrictions on shipments of chips from Nvidia and other U.S. companies. The proposal would add Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, Singapore and Brazil to a list of countries subject to export restrictions.

Under the proposal, companies would need Commerce Department licenses to ship chips to those countries, although U.S. technology companies could qualify for exceptions. The stated aim is to prevent Chinese customers from remotely accessing data-center capacity in those countries.

Nvidia's national security and global trade compliance teams will report directly to the committee and Kress. The arrangement places export-control compliance and national-security policy within a recurring board review process while the Commerce Department proposal would set licensing requirements for shipments to the listed countries, with possible exceptions for U.S. technology companies.

“We are committed to working constructively with government and industry partners to help establish and advance a robust framework that uses every tool at our disposal to safeguard national security while supporting continued technological innovation,” Nvidia board Chairman Harvey Jones said.
