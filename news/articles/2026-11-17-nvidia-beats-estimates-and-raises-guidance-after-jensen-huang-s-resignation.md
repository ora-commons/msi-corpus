## SEC investigates Nvidia amid export-control and succession questions

Nvidia reported another quarter of record-breaking sales, with revenue of $115.8 billion for the quarter ended Oct. 25. The figure was 6.3% above the $108.9 billion predicted by analysts polled by FactSet and more than double revenue from the year-earlier quarter. Nvidia also reported net income of $65.3 billion, which beat analyst expectations.

Revenue from data centers, Nvidia’s most important segment, reached $107.6 billion, an increase of 110% from a year earlier. Nvidia said the growth was driven by the rollout of its new Vera Rubin servers.

Nvidia forecast $130 billion in revenue for the current quarter, about 4% above Wall Street estimates. On Tuesday’s investor call, interim CEO Colette Kress said the company was raising its forecast revenue growth for fiscal 2028 from 70% to 75%. Nvidia shares rose more than 2% in aftermarket trading.

The results came as investors focused on Nvidia’s performance without Jensen Huang. The Wall Street Journal reported that the longtime CEO resigned Thursday following revelations of affairs with two women, one an employee and another whom U.S. officials suspect is a Chinese spy.

Huang said in a statement Tuesday that he would be “honored” to return as CEO if the board saw fit. On the investor call, Nvidia Chairman Harvey C. Jones said the board was not considering reinstating Huang. Jones said the board voted unanimously to accept Huang’s resignation and that the company would not discuss the circumstances of his departure on the call.

Huang’s departure leaves questions about what will happen to hundreds of billions of dollars in financing deals Nvidia has struck with partners and customers in recent years. At the end of the fiscal third quarter, Nvidia reported nearly $110 billion in marketable and non-marketable securities on hand, with stakes in privately held companies larger than they were a quarter earlier.

Nvidia’s future spending commitments also increased. The chipmaker reported $390 billion committed toward future supply and capacity, up from $279 billion a quarter earlier. Nvidia said the increase was “primarily related to the multiyear procurement of memory supply.”

Legal and regulatory risks remain tied to the export of restricted chips. Brian Smedley, chief investment officer of hedge fund Cynosure Strategies, said: “There are a lot of questions about succession, and also legal and regulatory risk for the firm, especially surrounding some of the deals Jensen pursued to sell restricted chips to intermediaries in Asia.”

The Wall Street Journal reported Tuesday that the Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia. The focus could not immediately be learned, but the report said it included whether the company’s disclosures misled investors.

Smedley said: “Investors have already priced in a much higher risk premium.” He added: “It’s the ouster of Jensen and the uncertainty that creates about strategic vision and execution that’s weighing on the stock.”

Market sentiment toward Nvidia and other artificial-intelligence companies had begun to sour even before Huang’s resignation. Fears about AI safety grew while scrutiny increased over what critics described as “circular financing,” which involved Nvidia providing financial guarantees, equity investments and other support to buyers of its chips.

Nvidia shares fell 30% in the previous month. The company’s market capitalization was above $5 trillion as recently as early October but stood at $3.7 trillion, costing Nvidia the title of the world’s most valuable public company to Apple.

Attention also shifted to how Nvidia accounted for sales of advanced AI processors in Asia. Huang’s admission of an affair with Alice Huang, a former executive at the Singaporean data-center company Megaspeed whom U.S. officials believe is a Chinese intelligence asset, brought renewed attention to whether billions of dollars in chip shipments to Singapore may have ended up in China. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent highlighted the issue, which is subject to federal investigations, in interviews this week.

Early Tuesday, Nvidia’s board said it had formed a new committee to oversee the company’s national-security policies and compliance with U.S. export controls. President Trump spoke out in support of Huang, while congressional Democrats called for a full investigation of Nvidia’s exports to Asia and the Commerce Department’s oversight of the shipments.

Huang and Nvidia General Counsel Tim Teter agreed to brief three House of Representatives committees in early December, lawmakers said Tuesday.
