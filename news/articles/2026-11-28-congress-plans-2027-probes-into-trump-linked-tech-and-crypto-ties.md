## White House lawyers threaten Democrats over planned investigations

Democratic lawmakers who took control of Congress in the midterms are preparing House and Senate investigations in 2027 into Donald Trump's business relationships with major donors and allies, according to Democratic lawmakers and scholars. Musk, Huang and Zhao are expected to face scrutiny as lawmakers seek to determine how federal contracts, tax breaks, regulatory changes and other government actions benefited their companies, Trump or his family.

The investigations are still in planning stages. Sen. Richard Blumenthal of Connecticut, who will lead the permanent subcommittee on investigations, and Sen. Elizabeth Warren of Massachusetts, who will head the banking committee, are likely to continue examining technology and crypto leaders. Rep. Robert Garcia of California will lead the House oversight committee, while Rep. Jamie Raskin of Maryland is poised to run the House judiciary committee.

Raskin said in an email that planned investigations would examine foreign investors in World Liberty Financial and Trump's pardon of Zhao. A firm backed by the UAE's national security adviser reportedly agreed to pay $500m for a 49% stake in World Liberty four days before Trump took office in 2025, according to Raskin. He said that by May, the Trump administration had announced a deal giving the UAE access to advanced American AI chips. Congress would examine whether business with the president's family helped Zhao receive his pardon or helped the UAE obtain access to the chips.

Raskin also said congressional scrutiny would cover donor funding for Trump's ballroom project, including companies run by technology executives who sat behind him at his inauguration. Separately, Forbes reported in September that Trump's wealth had increased by $2.7bn since his 2024 reelection, in good measure because of crypto ventures and overseas deals. Trump's sons, Eric and Donald Jr, have also made sizable gains in technology, crypto and other areas since the 2024 win and are expected to face inquiries into whether their Oval Office ties benefited them.

Musk spent almost $300m in 2024 to help Trump win and about $450m in the midterms in mostly failed efforts to assist GOP candidates. Before the elections, Musk wrote on X: “Those who pick fights with me should understand that I will find out who is really pulling the strings and come for them.”

A fall report from the nonpartisan Democracy Defenders Fund said Musk firms secured $6bn in government contracts while he was leading Trump's Doge drive to cut federal spending and another $10bn after he left the post. The report also counted 25 federal agencies or departments that took actions benefiting or potentially benefiting Musk's businesses.

The report estimated that Musk's net worth grew from approximately $419bn to between $900bn and $1tn after he left Doge in May 2025, more than doubling. Its central finding was that the government ethics framework allowed the world's richest person, a major political donor and former government official, to earn billions from federal contracts while his companies received favorable treatment across government.

Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive after reports of an affair with an alleged Chinese spy, prompting fears of Chinese access to Nvidia's valuable microchips. Eleven days after Huang quit, Nvidia announced his return as chief executive amid clear pressure from Trump and other administration officials. The move fueled bipartisan concern and calls for hearings next month. Public Citizen reported that Huang gave $25m in 2025 to finance Trump's ballroom venture.

Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington said Trump's 2026 financial disclosures showed Nvidia stock trading over 100 times since he returned to office, with a combined transaction value between $27m and $97m. Trump said all his stocks are managed solely by independent outside advisers and that he has no role in trading. He has repeatedly denied conflicts of interest or quid pro quos as president.

According to press reports, Trump and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick ignored national security intelligence warnings and allowed Nvidia to sell advanced chips to the Singapore-based datacenter firm Megaspeed. Until August 2025, Megaspeed was run by Alice Huang, whom the reports described as an alleged Chinese spy and who had had an affair with Jensen Huang. The reports said she was in touch with China's spy agency for months while her firm sold some advanced chips to datacenters in southeast Asia with China ties, and some of the chips were suspected of being smuggled to China. The account fueled bipartisan criticism and internal administration dissent.

Several major crypto companies have profited from the administration's changes to crypto oversight, including Binance. In a complex deal last year, Binance helped World Liberty Financial, which launched in the fall of 2024, secure a $2bn infusion from an Abu Dhabi-backed state investment fund. Trump reported earning over $1.4bn from World Liberty and other crypto ventures in 2025, about two-thirds of the $2.2bn in earnings shown by his 2025 financial disclosures. Trump told reporters: “You know why I’m profiting? Because the stock market’s going up. Everybody’s profiting.”

Zhao pleaded guilty in 2023 to anti-money-laundering and sanctions violations, and Binance agreed to pay $4.3bn in fines and restitution to the government. Zhao reportedly owns 90% of Binance stock, served a four-month sentence and was pardoned by Trump late last year. Since beginning his second term, Trump has issued executive orders easing cryptocurrency regulations and oversight by the Securities and Exchange Commission, departing from the approach under Joe Biden after promising the change in a mid-2024 talk at a major bitcoin conference. Crypto moguls and allied groups also poured tens of millions into the midterms, largely trying to defeat Democrats.

White House and Justice Department lawyers sent letters in November to key congressional Democrats, warning they could face scrutiny and perhaps criminal charges if they investigated some of Trump's big-money patrons and their federal contracts. Attorney General Todd Blanche said on CNN in early November that Democrats had “done nothing but threaten everything that’s happening in this administration” and suggested that members of Congress might face criminal investigations. White House Counsel Will Scharf told Garcia in a letter that oversight investigations “may cross the line into blatant criminality” and accused him of intending to “transgress” congressional power.

Garcia called Blanche's warning a threat. On CNN, he said: “He’s threatening our committee. He’s trying to stop us from doing real oversight.” On November 11, Garcia wrote that: “It appears Donald Trump has something to hide. Let’s be clear: Congress’s authority comes from article I of the constitution, not from the permission of the White House.” Raskin rejected the administration's attacks and said Congress would continue its oversight work regardless.

Several scholars and former prosecutors characterized the legal warnings as intimidation tactics that would not affect the planned investigations and would not stand up in court. Former federal prosecutor Barbara McQuade, who teaches law at the University of Michigan, said: “These threats of criminal prosecution strike me as part of the old strategy that the best defense is a good offense.” Cornell economist Eswar Prasad said Trump, his family and those in his inner circle had their personal wealth and projects funded by technology leaders whose own wealth and power had been boosted by administration policies. Former Federal Election Commission general counsel Larry Noble said: “They just learned that democracy is not some computer game or theoretical idea. It’s voters exercising their power in real time, and the voters were not happy.”
