## Kress inherits an 'avalanche' of chip-supply commitments and open probes

The Wall Street Journal's account of Nvidia's leadership handoff traces back to a summer meeting in Jensen Huang's office. Huang summoned his top finance executives to his office this summer. The AI economy, backstopped by Nvidia's increasingly outrageous bets, was teetering, and Huang wanted to make sure his finance team was getting it right, the Journal reported.

During that meeting, Huang tore into the woman who is now taking over for him. Colette Kress was "a deer in the headlights," Huang said. He described her as a "reporting CFO" who couldn't help him define strategy as an "avalanche" of chip-supply commitments loomed, the Journal reported.

Kress was named interim chief executive Thursday after Huang resigned in the wake of two extramarital affairs. She must now steer Nvidia through that avalanche — and a bigger one — while the company faces federal and internal probes and searches for a permanent CEO, the Journal reported.

Huang's exit makes Nvidia the most valuable company to abruptly lose a leader so instrumental to its fortunes, the Journal reported, though it is hardly the first. Apple lost founder-and-rescuer Steve Jobs to cancer. McDonald's abruptly fired Steve Easterbrook, alleging an improper relationship with a subordinate. Nissan dropped Carlos Ghosn as chairman in 2018, just after police arrested him in Tokyo on allegations of financial misconduct and underreporting his income. The danger of losing a seemingly indispensable leader is known as key-person risk in investor parlance.

The gap Huang identified in that summer meeting was strategic: he said Kress couldn't help him define strategy as the chip-supply commitments piled up. Kress now leads Nvidia through those commitments and its investigations while the search for a permanent chief executive continues.
