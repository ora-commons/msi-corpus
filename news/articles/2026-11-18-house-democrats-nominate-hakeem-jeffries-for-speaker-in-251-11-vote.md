## A January 3 floor vote would make Jeffries the first Black speaker

House Democrats on Wednesday nominated House Minority Leader Rep. Hakeem Jeffries of New York to serve as speaker of the House when Democrats retake the majority in the new year, voting 251 to 11 in a closed-door meeting at which the New York Democrat ran uncontested. Jeffries still needs a simple majority of member votes in a floor vote on January 3 to claim the post. NPR described the outcome as widely expected; if Jeffries succeeds, he would make history as the first Black lawmaker to serve as speaker.

The Wednesday vote formally opens the selection process for speaker, which the House is scheduled to conduct at the start of the new Congress on January 3. NPR reported that Jeffries ran uncontested and won the closed-door vote by a tally of 251 to 11.

Jeffries has led House Democrats since 2023. He needs only a simple majority of member votes in a Congress where Democrats are poised to control at least 257 seats, with several remaining races still to be decided.

The Democratic selection came just over a week after House Republicans chose Rep. Jim Jordan of Ohio to be the conference's minority leader, and after Senate Republicans tapped South Dakota's John Thune for a second term as party leader.

The size of the majority awaiting Jeffries contrasts with what awaited House Republicans four years ago, when they regained the House majority with extremely narrow margins. GOP leaders then struggled to unite the conference around a speaker and were forced to negotiate with a handful of holdouts on the party's right flank to reach consensus; after multiple candidates failed to secure a majority, the party coalesced around the now-outgoing Speaker Mike Johnson, R-La.

Jeffries will still have to navigate intraparty divides, though the size of the Democratic majority affords him room to weather some dissent. Some members on the party's left-most flank argue Democrats should prioritize advancing a more progressive agenda next year, and they have been lobbying for votes on Medicare for All and legislation that would stop the transfer of offensive weapons to Israel.

Because Democrats are set to hold double-digit margins in the chamber — an edge propelled by the success of more moderate candidates in districts President Trump carried in 2024 — NPR reported that it is unclear how much leverage the left wing of the party will have in the speaker's vote.

Divides inside the party may prove more pronounced in the Senate, where some Democrats are more publicly questioning whether current Minority Leader Chuck Schumer of New York is best suited to deliver on voter demands for change. Schumer faces a challenge from Maryland's Chris Van Hollen, who announced his candidacy last week and is pitching himself as an alternative to the status quo, echoing much of the progressive populist messaging that several successful Senate candidates ran on this cycle.

Like Schumer, Van Hollen has served in Congress for decades. Schumer, the top Democrat in the Senate for nearly a decade, contends that he still has the votes he needs to be elected majority leader, but Van Hollen's challenge comes as a handful of current and incoming members have declined to back Schumer for the job.

Both sides still have time to shore up votes, with Senate leadership elections set for early December, ahead of the House floor vote on January 3.
