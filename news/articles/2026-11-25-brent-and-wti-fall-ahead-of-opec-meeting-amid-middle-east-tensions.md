## ANZ says limited spare capacity could fuel renewed price gains

Front-month Brent crude futures fell 1.7% to $89.87 a barrel Wednesday, while front-month U.S. West Texas Intermediate declined 1.9% to $80.47. The moves came as traders weighed expectations of a global supply surplus ahead of Sunday's meeting of the Organization of the Petroleum Exporting Countries and its allies, with renewed Middle East hostilities keeping shipping and supply risks in view.

Both benchmarks settled 2.3% higher Tuesday but remained under pressure this month. Recovering Middle Eastern oil exports and expectations of excess supply next year have eased concerns about global oil availability.

Recovering production and uncertainty over demand growth clouded the supply outlook for next year as Sunday's meeting approached. Against that surplus outlook, analysts at ANZ Research said the oil market remains under considerable strain despite recent improvements in inventories and the availability of refined products.

Limited spare production capacity and depleted global inventories leave the market vulnerable to renewed price increases if further supply disruptions occur, the ANZ analysts said. That conditional risk remained in view even as Middle Eastern production and exports continued to recover.

Renewed hostilities between Saudi Arabia and Houthi militants in Yemen have raised concerns about the security of Red Sea shipping routes. Those routes have become increasingly important for Saudi oil exports amid disruptions around the Strait of Hormuz.

The Saudi-led coalition said Wednesday that it had targeted operational capabilities at Yemen's Hodeidah port, according to the Saudi Press Agency. The strikes followed an earlier evacuation warning issued by the coalition.

Saudi Arabia has relied on its East-West pipeline and ports along its western coast to maintain exports amid regional disruptions, Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman said Tuesday following approval of the kingdom's 2027 budget.

Expectations of potential negotiations between the U.S. and Iran have helped ease some immediate concerns over Middle Eastern oil supplies, analysts at ING said Tuesday. Significant differences between Washington and Tehran leave prices vulnerable to setbacks in the negotiations, they said.


In [MSI's Nov. 23 report](/articles/2026-11-23-brent-wti-fall-0-5-amid-tanker-attacks-and-projected-oil-surplus/), Brent and WTI had fallen 0.5% as traders weighed a projected supply surplus against tanker attacks in the Strait of Hormuz. By Wednesday, both benchmarks had declined more sharply, Sunday's OPEC+ meeting was approaching, and renewed fighting involving Saudi Arabia and Houthi militants added another shipping concern.
