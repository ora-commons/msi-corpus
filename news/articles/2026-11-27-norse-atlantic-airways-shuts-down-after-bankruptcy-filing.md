## Jet fuel costs doubled as the Iran war weakened demand

Norse Atlantic Airways filed for bankruptcy on Friday and canceled all future flights, ending the Norwegian low-cost, long-haul carrier after fuel costs rose during the Iran war. The company told passengers not to go to airports and said customer service was no longer available, while its board concluded operations were no longer viable.

The collapse came a day after Norse put its flights on hold and warned that its finances were critical. On Friday, the airline posted a message telling customers, including some passengers away from home, not to go to airports.

Norse operated low-cost, long-haul flights around the world, including from London Gatwick and the United States to Europe and Asia. The cancellations affected Gatwick and Manchester flights to Thailand due to begin next month, along with London flights to South Africa and the United States.

The Guardian reported that Norse's board concluded operations were no longer viable after a year in which airlines worldwide were hit by spiraling oil prices that doubled the cost of jet fuel and dampened consumer demand, after the Iran war began in February. Rival airlines predicted surging air fares and the collapse of weaker carriers.

Eivind Roald, the chief executive of Norse, said in a statement to the Oslo stock exchange: “The board of Norse Atlantic Airways AS has made the difficult decision to file for bankruptcy despite extensive efforts from the company and the group to find a solution.”

He added: “We are deeply sorry for the impact this has on our passengers, employees and partners.”

Roald said the owning group and Norse's UK subsidiary, which was set up to comply with aviation rules after Brexit, were “working to find a solution for the remaining part of the business to continue operations.”

However, Norse canceled all UK flights and halted ticket sales. It advised passengers to contact travel agents, flight bookers, insurers or credit card companies. Passengers already traveling were told they were on their own and should ask airport information desks.

In a message on its website, Norse said: “We know this will disrupt travel plans and may leave some passengers away from home. Customer service for both airlines is no longer available by phone, email, chat or social media.”

On Thursday morning, Norse had announced that it was on the brink of collapse. Friday morning, it reported that aircraft lessors had served termination notices for the 12 planes in its fleet.

Founded in 2021, Norse sought to emulate Norwegian Air's model for low-cost, long-haul service. Norwegian Air's attempt to establish that market ended with the pandemic, but Norse briefly revived transatlantic routes using the same plane type, the Boeing 787 Dreamliner.

Norse eventually encountered similar financial turbulence. This summer, the airline told investors that it was exploring a sale or merger and embarked on a $50m (£38m) cost-cutting programme that included shedding jobs and its Norwegian headquarters.


As MSI reported the previous day, [Norse Atlantic Airways had suspended all flights and warned that bankruptcy could follow without short-term financing](/articles/2026-11-26-norse-atlantic-airways-suspends-all-flights-warns-of-bankruptcy/). Friday's filing ended that contingency, while the airline canceled every future flight.
