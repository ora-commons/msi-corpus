## Her rise challenged the state's “three men in a room” politics

New York state Senate Majority Leader Andrea Stewart-Cousins, the first woman to lead the chamber, died Friday at 76, her office said. Senate Democratic spokesman Mike Murphy announced the death Friday but did not disclose the cause or location. Stewart-Cousins had been reelected by her constituents on Nov. 3 and chosen by fellow Democrats in mid-November to retain the majority leader position.

Murphy described Stewart-Cousins as “a groundbreaking leader who shattered one of New York’s highest glass ceilings.” Gov. Kathy Hochul, a fellow Democrat, said she was heartbroken by the death of a legislator she admired “more than she could have ever known.” Hochul also said: “She never forgot where she came from, who she was fighting for or the doors she could open for those coming behind her.”

A state senator since 2006, Stewart-Cousins led Senate Democrats from 2012. She became majority leader in 2019 after her party won control of the chamber, making her the first woman and first Black woman to hold the post. She promised to “give New Yorkers the progressive leadership they have been demanding.”

Her ascent also shook up the state’s clubby politics, where the budget and other key bills were routinely hammered out by the governor and the Senate and Assembly leaders, a group known as the so-called “three men in a room.” Stewart-Cousins told The Associated Press at the time: “I have not been in the room.” She added: “So that will be a difference right there.”

Stewart-Cousins used her role to advance liberal priorities that had gone nowhere when Republicans controlled the Senate, including the legalization of marijuana and stronger gun control laws. She also led the effort to secure voter approval of a state constitutional amendment that supporters billed as protecting abortion rights.

Her agenda also prioritized passing voting-rights laws and a slate of police accountability measures that gained momentum after the 2020 police killing of George Floyd in Minneapolis. Stewart-Cousins called it “a moment of reckoning” and commended the advocacy of Valerie Bell and Gwen Carr, whose sons — Sean Bell and Eric Garner — were killed by New York City police. The Rev. Al Sharpton recalled in a Friday statement: “Andrea used her authority in the state Senate to help turn what those mothers had fought for into law.”

Sharpton said he was stunned by Stewart-Cousins's death and noted that she had been a guest on “PoliticsNation,” his show on MS NOW, the previous week.

In a notable 2021 moment, Stewart-Cousins became the first senior New York Democrat to call for three-term Democratic Gov. Andrew Cuomo to resign amid mounting sexual harassment allegations and a separate controversy over his administration’s count of COVID-19 deaths among nursing home residents. Cuomo denied the allegations, initially insisted he would not and should not step down, and ultimately did.

Cuomo said Friday that the two “did not agree on everything” but that he was proud of legislation they worked together to pass. He said Stewart-Cousins “stood up for her members and fought for what she believed was right, without hesitation and always with dignity, grace and class.”

Stewart-Cousins was a resident of Yonkers, a city just north of the New York City border. She was a teacher, sales and marketing professional, Yonkers city official and Westchester County legislator before she was elected to the state Senate in 2006.

Her husband, Thomas Cousins, died in 2007. She is survived by their children, Kevin, Steven and Candice, and her brother, Robert Stewart Jr.
