## Putin gains standing as the three leaders reveal competing priorities

Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin ended their three-way summit in Shenzhen on Nov. 19, 2026, without announcing concrete progress toward ending the war in Ukraine. The leaders issued a declaration pledging cooperation on nuclear security, energy supplies and other issues, but announced no concrete progress toward ending the war or reshaping Europe's security order. The appearance of Putin alongside two of the world's most powerful leaders nearly five years after his invasion of Ukraine made him a pariah across much of the West was itself a major diplomatic coup, The Wall Street Journal reported. The meeting also showed the limits of what Putin's relationships with Trump and Xi could accomplish, with the three powers pursuing their own interests and divided over major geopolitical questions.

The declaration set out the cooperation the leaders announced. Trump, Xi and Putin pledged cooperation on nuclear security, energy supplies and other issues, but announced neither concrete progress toward ending the Ukraine war nor movement toward a new European security order. The latter was a longstanding Russian objective, and the absence of a major policy win left Putin without the larger diplomatic victory he had sought.

Putin had pushed for the three-way meeting for months and used it to press his case on Ukraine and a new security architecture. Moscow wants that architecture to roll back some aspects of the post-Cold War order, including NATO's eastward expansion. The summit therefore gave Putin a venue to advance one of his central aims while appearing alongside Trump and Xi.

The report described Russia as the world's biggest nuclear power but one whose economic strength and influence abroad have declined since the fall of the Soviet Union. It said Putin launched the war in Ukraine partly to reverse that erosion of Russian influence and restore the country's standing among the world's great powers.

On Thursday, Trump met separately with Putin and greeted the Russian leader as “my friend,” while praising their longstanding relationship. “I think a lot of wonderful things can come out of this, or at least start,” Trump told reporters before the two men sat down for roughly 40 minutes of talks.

Trump and Putin discussed Ukraine and bilateral relations, according to Kremlin foreign-policy adviser Yuri Ushakov. Ushakov said Putin emerged in an “excellent mood,” but also acknowledged that neither side had made any particularly important statements. He described the bilateral as largely preparatory for the three-way summit.

European and Ukrainian officials have objected to being excluded from negotiations over their own security, and Xi Jinping has shown little appetite for discussing Ukraine's future without European participation. The contrast in priorities was visible in Shenzhen: Putin explicitly mentioned Ukraine at the opening of Thursday's talks, while Xi spoke more broadly about cooperation among major powers.

Those positions did not change in the summit declaration. Andrew Weiss, the James Family Chair and vice president for studies at the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace, said, “Putin is once again pushing ideas about European security that were nonstarters on the eve of the full-scale invasion five years ago.” He added: “The Russian leader’s hope is that Trump is desperate enough for a foreign policy ‘win’ that he latches on to at least some of them and rams them down Ukraine’s and Europe’s throat.”

After the three-way summit, Trump dialed into a meeting of European leaders on support for Ukraine, according to Jared Kushner, his envoy and son-in-law, who attended. Kushner said Trump had delivered a strong message to his counterparts about his desire for peace. “But I believe he also came away believing that peace is achievable,” Kushner told reporters afterward.

Any agreement on the global order would have been difficult. The report said Russia has watched both the U.S. and China encroach on Moscow's influence, with Beijing chipping away at Russian influence in Central Asia and NATO moving eastward. Despite Trump's personal ties to Putin, he has presided over efforts to expand American influence in Russia's former Soviet backyard. Neither side appeared willing to cede influence.

The competing priorities were also visible in the summit's diplomatic choreography. Xi personally greeted Trump at the airport Wednesday with an embrace and a flyover by jet fighters, while Putin was received by Cai Qi, a senior Chinese official and close Xi confidant. Xi separately praised Russia's “rock-solid” ties with China during talks with Putin. Xi's warm reception for Trump reflected Beijing's determination to manage relations with Washington, its biggest strategic rival and an economic power far larger than Russia. China has provided Moscow with a crucial economic lifeline since its invasion of Ukraine, but the meeting showed Beijing pursuing its own priorities rather than simply following Putin's diplomatic agenda.

For Putin, the absence of a major policy win did not erase the political value of the summit. The highly publicized gathering received extensive coverage inside Russia and demonstrated his ability to turn a weak hand into a tactical success. Images of Putin sitting alongside Trump and Xi, along with Trump's public embrace of the Russian leader, offered the Kremlin a potent symbol of Russia's continued relevance in global affairs. Hanna Notte, Eurasia director at the James Martin Center for Nonproliferation Studies and author of a recent book on Russia's foreign and security policy, said, “The very appearance of the leaders together affirms Russia’s great-power status on par with the U.S. and China.”

Those tactical gains came against enormous battlefield losses. Russia has suffered enormous losses this year, and some analysts estimated that the total number of Russian war dead since 2022 could now be close to 600,000. At the same time, the Kremlin has persistently courted Trump, holding talks with White House envoys Steve Witkoff and Kushner and offering economic incentives and investment opportunities in Russia.

Abbas Gallyamov, a political analyst and former speechwriter for Putin, said, “Putin’s comeback and the possibility of this meeting at all is almost exclusively thanks to Trump because it’s hard to keep someone an outcast when the president of the strongest country in the world is treating you like an equal.” The report said economic growth has stalled and Putin's popularity has weakened, analysts say, and soaring military spending has strained public finances, forcing some regions to cut payments to families of dead soldiers. “Under Putin now, Russia is becoming closer to the Soviet Union, lots of tactical gains while it’s strategically in decline,” he said.
