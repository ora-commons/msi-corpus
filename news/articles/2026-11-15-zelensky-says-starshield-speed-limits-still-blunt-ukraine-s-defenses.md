## Ukraine's winter energy protection depends on lifting restrictions, Zelensky says

President Volodymyr Zelensky said on Sunday that a crucial US satellite internet service was still operating too slowly and was affecting Ukraine's ability to stop Russian attacks. "We are counting on these restrictions being lifted. The protection of lives and Ukraine's energy infrastructure ahead of winter directly depends on this," he said. The immediate operational cost, Zelensky said, is visible against drones aimed at the power grid: Ukraine was struggling to intercept Russian jet-powered drones targeting electricity substations because "restrictions on the operating speed of Starshield terminals along the front line and in the temporarily occupied territories are still there." "Our interceptor drones lose connection with their operators at a speed of 161km/h (100mph) and simply cannot catch up with Russian jet-powered 'shaheds'," he posted, referring to the drones that follow an Iranian design.

Starshield is a military-grade Starlink service owned by Elon Musk and provided to Ukraine by the US government, and the dispute over its operating speed has persisted for weeks. Zelensky said he had asked officials "to work as closely as possible" with the US government and Musk's company. "It is important to be on the right side of history," he said. Ukraine relies on the normal Starlink service for communications on the battlefield, while Starshield handles the interception of missiles and drones in Ukraine's airspace.

The speed restrictions were first identified in October and produced conflicting public accounts from Musk and Trump. Musk said the US government was responsible because Starshield was controlled by them, but Trump denied being responsible and said he had told the billionaire owner to "put it back". The service appeared to return to normal after a week, after which Ukraine said the restrictions were still being implemented. The connection problems unfolded alongside a tense diplomatic stand-off between Zelensky and Trump over Ukrainian strikes on oil infrastructure within Russia. Trump said those strikes on Russian oil refineries had pushed global prices higher; Kyiv says the refineries are crucial to stemming the main revenue source for Moscow's war effort.

Ukraine has also faced a shortage of US-made Patriot interceptors used to defend against Russian missiles, a situation that Russia has exploited to intensify strikes on Ukrainian targets.

## Russia strikes energy and railway infrastructure

Overnight and into Sunday morning, Russian strikes hit energy infrastructure in Zaporizhzhia and the Sumy, Odesa, Zhytomyr and Kirovohrad regions, Ukraine said. The attacks also struck railway infrastructure in the Dnipro, Kharkiv and Zaporizhzhia regions. Scores of people are said to have been hurt in those attacks, as well as two in the Donetsk and Kyiv regions.

Two people were killed in a strike on an infrastructure facility in Mena, in Chernihiv Region, governor Vyacheslav Chaus said. Energy facilities across the region were also hit multiple times, he added on Telegram.

## Ukraine strikes Nizhnekamsk and other Russian targets

Ukraine said its forces struck a petrochemical plant and an oil refinery in Nizhnekamsk, a central Russian city. "On the night of November 15, units of Ukraine's Defence Forces reached Nizhnekamsk in Tatarstan," the military said on Telegram. "Two enterprises that feed the Russian war with raw materials and fuel came under attack."

Other Ukrainian strikes killed one person in Taganrog, an industrial port city near the border, regional governor Yuri Slyusar said. In the occupied Luhansk region, a lorry driver and an elderly woman were also killed, Leonid Pasechnik, the regional leader installed by Moscow, said.

## Estonia closes Russian border crossings from 1 December

Estonia announced it will shut its border crossing points with Russia from 1 December following a suspected sabotage attack on a defence company. Eight people were detained over an attempted arson attack on drone maker Threod Systems. Estonian authorities say the suspects had been ordered by Russia's GRU military intelligence service to carry out the attack, and Russia has not commented. "Russia must understand that hostile actions against Estonia come with consequences," Foreign Minister Margus Tsahkna said.
