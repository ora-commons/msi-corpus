## Thumann says license decisions should not be based solely on dissatisfaction with programming

At a contentious Senate confirmation hearing in Washington on November 10, 2026, Danielle Thumann, Donald Trump's nominee for a Federal Communications Commission seat, faced repeated questions from Democrats about the FCC's actions involving ABC and late-night host Jimmy Kimmel. Thumann is senior counsel to FCC Chairman Brendan Carr. Ben Ray Luján, a senator of New Mexico, said the commission's actions would face close scrutiny when Democrats take control of the Senate in January. The agency's recent actions have been criticized as hostile to press freedom.

Luján asked Thumann repeatedly whether she agreed with actions taken by Carr's FCC, including a nearly unprecedented order from late April that forced ABC to apply early to renew its local station licenses. The order came immediately after President Donald Trump and first lady Melania Trump criticized Jimmy Kimmel, one of the network's biggest stars. Carr was also accused of pressuring ABC affiliates to drop Kimmel's show the previous fall.

“Do you support Chairman Carr’s illegal campaign to remove Jimmy Kimmel from the nation’s airways?” Luján asked.

Thumann responded: “I have not worked with the chairman on any media-related issues, and I’m not familiar with all of the details regarding the investigation of ABC or the early license renewal of their licenses.” She added: “And I haven’t been involved in any of those conversations regarding any of their practices or regarding Jimmy Kimmel.”

Luján pressed Thumann to identify an issue on which she had disagreed with Carr, but she did not name one. He also asked whether it was appropriate for the FCC to call up Disney's broadcast license for early renewal.

Several of Luján's Democratic colleagues also questioned Thumann about the FCC's actions toward television networks. Ed Markey, a Massachusetts senator, pressed her on what he called Chairman Carr's FCC “censorship agenda” and asked: “Ms Thumann, does the FCC have the authority to punish a broadcaster because the president disagrees with its news coverage?”

After her response, Markey said: “Again, this should be a straightforward constitutional limitation, because the FCC cannot punish a broadcaster simply because the president doesn’t like his coverage. Yet Chairman Carr, your boss right now, has threatened to do exactly that.” Markey accused Thumann of choosing to “evade” his questions and said anyone seeking a seat on the FCC must be willing to stand up to threats and defend the First Amendment. He told her that she was not willing to do so at the hearing.

Senator Tammy Duckworth also asked whether Thumann would yield to political pressure to punish disfavored networks. Thumann said the commission has to deal with complaints against networks it regulates. She also acknowledged that “editorial decision-making is protected by the first amendment for broadcasters.”

Thumann added: “I don’t think that a decision [about a station’s license] should be based purely on someone’s discontent with the content that’s being aired.”

Several Senate Democrats said it was inappropriate for the commerce committee to hold the hearing. They argued that if Republicans were nominating another member of their party to the commission, they should follow past precedent and nominate a Democrat as well. The FCC currently has one commissioner nominated by a Democratic president, Anna M Gomez, but her term has already technically expired.

At the close of the hearing, Luján warned Carr that he would be in the Senate once a month, if not more, and in the House of Representatives, answering questions without the protection of the gavel. He also said Carr would have to answer subpoenas so lawmakers could get to the bottom of “the nonsense and the cover-ups that have been taking place.”

Thumann's answers did not give Luján and Markey the positions they sought on the FCC's ABC actions and on any disagreement with Carr. She said she was not familiar with all of the details of the ABC investigation or its early license renewal, had not worked with Carr on media issues and had not participated in discussions about ABC practices or Kimmel. She also did not identify a disagreement with Carr. Her acknowledgment of First Amendment protections and her statement against license decisions based solely on content displeasure offered the clearest statement of her position.

With Republicans controlling both the Senate and House, Thumann was expected to win approval. The unresolved questions were her knowledge of the FCC's ABC action, any disagreement with Carr and whether she would stand up to political pressure. The hearing left the commission's late-April license order and the accusations surrounding Kimmel at the center of Democratic oversight.
