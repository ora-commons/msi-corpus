## Waltz says intelligence reports suggest the four men survived

El Salvador President Nayib Bukele launched a military combat operation in Haiti on Wednesday to rescue four Salvadoran aviation technicians kidnapped by criminal gangs. The four men were from Freedom Task Force II, deployed as part of the international Gang Suppression Force, a multinational security mission operating in Haiti.

The operation followed an attack Sunday in the Santo area of Croix-des-Bouquets, on the outskirts of Haiti's capital, Port-au-Prince. According to security reports, the technicians were returning from a food-supply trip when heavily armed gang members opened fire and attacked their armored vehicle with explosives.

The Salvadoran personnel managed to escape the vehicle before it was engulfed in flames. Contact with the four men was lost after they were forced to flee on foot through gang-controlled territory.

In a post on X dated October 21, 2026, Bukele said the hostages were not combat personnel but aviation technicians whose sole mission in Haiti was to coordinate medical evacuations and humanitarian assistance. He said the four men were "cowardly kidnapped" while returning from a local supermarket where they had gone to buy food.

"That is why today we launched combat operations to rescue them. We have only one objective: to bring our four compatriots home safe and sound," Bukele wrote.

Bukele also issued a direct ultimatum to Haiti's gang leaders and gang members holding the four Salvadorans hostage. "Today's attack is nothing compared with what will come if you do not hand over all four alive. We have already eliminated the gangs in El Salvador, and we can eliminate you, too," he warned, referring to his administration's actions against criminal gangs.

In Haitian Creole, Bukele addressed both gang leaders and gang members holding the four Salvadorans hostage. He wrote that the mission included only pilots and technical personnel, whose principal work was medical evacuation and saving lives rather than wage war. Bukele warned the gang leaders and gang members not to dismiss his warning as baseless, said they still had time to avoid the threatened consequences, and demanded that all four men be returned alive and unharmed. Bukele wrote that everything would return as it had before the kidnapping and that the decision was in their hands.

Bukele said the current operation was only an initial phase and warned that he could order a much larger military intervention if necessary.

The kidnapping prompted an international response at United Nations headquarters in New York. U.S. Ambassador to the United Nations Mike Waltz condemned the kidnapping and reaffirmed Washington's full support for rescue efforts by Salvadoran forces and their international partners. Speaking before the Security Council, Waltz said intelligence reports suggested the Salvadorans had survived and pledged to help bring them home.

Waltz warned the criminal gangs operating in Port-au-Prince that they were making a "terrible mistake" if they believed such attacks would intimidate or force the international security mission to withdraw.

On Wednesday afternoon, Haiti's government issued a social media statement describing the rescue of the Salvadoran personnel as "an absolute priority for state authorities."

A press communiqué from the Primature de la République d'Haïti, dated October 21, 2026, was titled "Le Gouvernement coordonne la réponse de l'État face à l'enlèvement des soldats salvadoriens." The Haitian government said that, "In the face of the seriousness of this act, the state reaffirms its authority and determination. Security forces are firmly continuing their operations to secure the release of the kidnapped soldiers and protect human lives." A photograph accompanying the statement showed Haitian officials meeting to coordinate the government's response.


The kidnappings directly involve personnel from the multinational force.
