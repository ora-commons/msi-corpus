## People within 20 miles told to remain indoors for Wednesday blast

The U.S. Army released new maps projecting that a toxic lead-dust plume from the planned detonation at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown would move southwest through southeastern Iowa and into northern Missouri. The controlled operation, involving 68 barrels — five tons — of lead azide, is scheduled for 10 a.m. central time Wednesday. People within 20 miles were told to remain indoors with windows closed, while some within five miles southwest of the plant may be evacuated.

A federal appeals court cleared the way for the detonation after the Army changed the scheduled date three times following an outpouring of public concern. Environmentalists sued to stop the operation, arguing that permits for the planned explosion had not been issued correctly. An attorney for the environmentalists said the legal challenge concerns the validity of the Environmental Protection Agency permit, not the Army’s decision to move forward under the emergency provision.

The Army says it needs to destroy the stockpile to prevent a possible chain-reaction explosion that would be more dangerous. Acting Secretary of the Army Adam Telle said there was no reason for the public to panic. He said various government entities had reached a “unanimous judgment” that the Army “should proceed with an extensively mitigated, carefully controlled detonation.”

“We are proceeding with the controlled removal in full coordination with all state, local and federal authorities, because the safety of Iowa’s citizens comes first,” Telle said.

The Army said it had set up additional “blast barriers” around the bunkers where the lead azide will be detonated. It also said it would apply ballistic gels and foams to absorb the lead dust and douse the explosive materials with thousands of gallons of water.

An emergency notification sent to cellphones on Tuesday confirmed the 10 a.m. schedule and warned that some people within five miles southwest of the plant may be evacuated. The Army’s “imminent threat alert” told those up to 20 miles away to remain indoors with windows closed “to avoid airborne lead dust, noise and vibration.” The Des Moines Register reported that evacuated residents would be notified by phone once they could return home.

Protective measures recommended by the Army and the Iowa Department of Health and Human Services include closing all windows and exterior doors; turning off or placing heating, ventilation and air conditioning systems in recirculation mode; avoiding unnecessary travel; and cleaning indoor and outdoor surfaces that children may contact.

Donald Trump intervened in the decision, saying on social media that, in addition to the court’s go-ahead, he wanted Rob Sand, Iowa’s governor-elect, to approve the detonation. Sand, a Democrat who does not take office until January, said the decision was fully up to the president’s administration.

Outgoing Iowa governor Kim Reynolds said she supports the planned detonation after speaking with military leaders, including U.S. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. “I trust the army will do everything in its power to protect the people who could be affected,” Reynolds said.

Telle said the Army’s goal was to keep as much of the material as possible at the plant to prevent it from traveling. The new maps show a southwest-moving plume extending through southeastern Iowa and into northern Missouri.


The Army’s decision to proceed follows [Trump’s Nov. 23 order to await approval from the 8th Circuit and Rob Sand](/articles/2026-11-23-trump-orders-army-to-await-agreement-from-8th-circuit-and-rob-sand/) and [the request by environmental groups for another pause](/articles/2026-11-23-environmental-groups-seek-another-pause-before-iowa-lead-azide-detonation/); a federal appeals court has now cleared the way.
