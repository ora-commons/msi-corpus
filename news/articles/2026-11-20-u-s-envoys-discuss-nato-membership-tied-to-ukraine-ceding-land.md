## European negotiators viewed Moscow's non-paper demands as opening bids

During closed-door peace talks over the past few months, President Trump's son-in-law and special envoy Jared Kushner discussed the possibility of full NATO membership for Ukraine if it ceded territory to Russia, according to people familiar with the matter. According to people involved in the talks, the idea also arose in several recent conversations involving Kushner and fellow U.S. envoy Steve Witkoff with European officials. One European leader said the proposal came up in a phone call as early as August.

Most recently, Kushner discussed the possibility with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky and European officials at a Dublin military airfield last week, according to people familiar with the discussion. At the airfield, the U.S. envoys also met with Zelensky, his adviser Rustem Umerov and selected European national-security advisers. The proposal also surfaced in at least one meeting last week in the United Arab Emirates, where Kushner and Witkoff met with Ukrainians, Europeans and Russians in meetings aimed at negotiating a deal to end the war, according to people familiar with those discussions.

People involved in the negotiations said the U.S. raised two possible scenarios in recent talks. One would require Ukraine to give up the territory it still controls in the Donbas region of eastern Ukraine, where the two sides have battled for much of the war, in exchange for full NATO membership. The other would freeze the conflict along the front line, leave part of the Donbas in Ukrainian hands and provide security guarantees against a future Russian assault through an international treaty with the U.S. and potentially European allies.

A U.S. official said negotiators were discussing many ideas, but both Ukraine and Russia would need to agree to any final deal. It remained unclear how seriously the U.S. mediators were entertaining the membership proposal or whether Trump had changed his view. If the U.S. backed the idea, it could expose tensions within the alliance; Germany has long opposed Kyiv's membership, and NATO can expand only by consensus.

The Wall Street Journal reported that Moscow has fought for years to prevent Ukraine from joining the 32-member North Atlantic Treaty Organization, and that Trump himself ruled out membership early last year as unacceptable to Russia. The report also said Trump and previous U.S. administrations had resisted offering membership, citing fears that doing so could draw the West into a confrontation with Moscow; some European leaders shared that concern.

During talks in Abu Dhabi last week, Russian negotiators explicitly demanded that Ukraine abandon any ambition to join NATO, according to people familiar with the talks. A paper distributed to negotiators and seen by The Wall Street Journal said Moscow also demanded that Ukraine remove its NATO aspirations from its constitution. Russian negotiators additionally insisted that NATO commit not to station troops or military equipment in Ukraine and that Western arms deliveries to Kyiv cease.

The document was known as a non-paper because it contained unofficial negotiating positions. European negotiators viewed the demands as opening bids rather than a final settlement blueprint.

The document also called for NATO to roll back its military infrastructure in Eastern Europe to 1997 levels, the year the alliance and Russia established a cooperation forum. It called for the removal of missiles capable of reaching Russia from countries across Europe and the Caucasus, along with the withdrawal of offers by France and Britain to extend nuclear protection to other European nations.

In keeping with agreements made with Russia, NATO has not built significant military infrastructure in eastern member states, with exceptions including radar systems in Poland and Romania and a base for a German unit that will be permanently stationed in Lithuania. The Lithuanian move responded to Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine.

On Thursday, Kushner said the Russian government had gotten “comfortable” with NATO-like security guarantees for Ukraine. He and Witkoff met with countries supporting Ukraine in Berlin on Thursday to discuss future funding and security guarantees for Kyiv. “There will be huge deterrence and massive consequences if it’s ever violated,” Kushner said, adding that Trump was open to providing such guarantees. “You make it so good that hopefully you don’t have to use it because the deterrence is so strong,” he said.

Trump, who was returning from a summit with Russian President Vladimir Putin and Chinese leader Xi Jinping, called Kushner's cellphone from Air Force One. According to two people present, Trump said Putin was tired of war and willing to settle. Speaking over the phone with European leaders, including Zelensky, who were gathered with Kushner, Trump said they could save a lot of money supporting Ukraine if they reached a peace deal with Putin.

After the Berlin meeting, French President Emmanuel Macron said it was time to discuss NATO membership for Ukraine. The issue of advancing Ukraine's NATO bid came up in the Berlin talks, but the German hosts were unwilling to pursue it, according to people who attended the meeting. German Chancellor Friedrich Merz, who is 70, told aides he expected Ukraine would not join NATO during his lifetime.

Last week, Zelensky said Kyiv was still discussing security guarantees with the U.S. and Europe and that any deal would need approval from the Ukrainian people. He said U.S. negotiators described the guarantees as bilateral agreements, “similar to what Israel has.” Zelensky also acknowledged that the U.S. had raised the idea of Ukraine's NATO membership. “The Americans raised a question: What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?” he told journalists.

After nearly five years of war, Russia still had not taken the whole Donbas. Ukraine feared ceding the territory it controlled there would help the Kremlin stage future offensives. “I have said repeatedly: Ukraine is not retreating and is not giving up its land to terrorists,” Zelensky said.
