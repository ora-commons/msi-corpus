## Air travel, banking and Microsoft 365 suffered recent disruptions

Apple acknowledged the failures on its system status page, which listed Apple Music, the App Store and AppleCare as suffering full outages and reported that the iCloud sign-in interface was also having issues. Downdetector.com separately showed a spike in user complaints about Apple Pay.

Apple said in an emailed statement: “Many of our systems are back up and running after an internal update caused some outages this morning,” and “We’re working to get the remaining systems online as soon as possible.”

Marriott International, the largest U.S. hotel operator, also had technology issues, according to users posting on Reddit, and Downdetector.com reported an outage. Marriott said in an emailed statement that it had “resolved a technical issue” that affected sign-in capabilities on Marriott.com and the Marriott Bonvoy app. The company added that a company review found no sign the problem “was cybersecurity-related.”

The latest incidents followed disruptions to several other widely used services. Last week, a global Microsoft outage disrupted email and other workplace tools, plunging business communication and everyday services into disarray worldwide. Microsoft 365 services, including Outlook, Teams and the AI assistant Copilot, were interrupted. Microsoft said there was “no indication” malicious activity caused the disruptions, specifically noting on Friday that the outage was not related to a cyberattack or artificial intelligence agents.

Days earlier, a malfunction in the Federal Aviation Administration's network prompted a nationwide ground stop. The disruption delayed hundreds of flights and stranded hundreds of thousands of travelers across the country.

On Monday, a system outage at United Airlines lasted more than 90 minutes, forcing the carrier to delay departing flights. Chicago-based United said there was no indication its outage was cybersecurity-related.

Also on Monday, thousands of customers reported difficulty accessing online accounts at several major U.S. banks and the money transfer service Zelle after a technical issue at JPMorgan Chase caused ripple effects throughout the financial system. Chase said the technical problems were not the result of a cyberattack and on Tuesday confirmed that the bank's systems were operating normally.

Each company described a different technical circumstance. Apple identified an internal update as the cause of some outages, while Marriott cited a technical issue affecting sign-in. Chase said its technical problems were not the result of a cyberattack. United said there was no indication its outage was cybersecurity-related, and Microsoft said there was no indication malicious activity caused its disruptions.

At the latest updates, Marriott had resolved its sign-in problem and Chase said its systems were operating normally. Apple said many systems were running but continued working to restore the rest without giving a firm completion time. Across the recent sequence, disruptions reached consumer services, airline departures, online banking and workplace tools.
