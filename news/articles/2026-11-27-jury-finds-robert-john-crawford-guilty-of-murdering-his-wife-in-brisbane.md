## Prosecutors told the jury the death was staged as a lawnmower accident

A Queensland supreme court jury found Robert John Crawford, a former RAAF pilot, guilty on Friday of murdering his wife Frances Crawford, having accepted the prosecution's account that her death was staged to resemble a fatal lawnmower accident. Gasps and sobs could be heard in Brisbane supreme court on Friday after the jury returned a guilty verdict after deliberating since 10am on Tuesday. Robert Crawford did not visually react to the verdict.

Frances Crawford's body was found by paramedics at 4am at the bottom of a retaining wall near an overturned ride-on lawnmower. Her husband was attempting to resuscitate her. Crawford was accused of murdering Frances Crawford by strangulation at the couple's property west of Brisbane, in the early hours of 30 July 2024, or late on 29 July.

Over three-and-a-half weeks, jurors heard evidence that included police forensic analysis of Frances Crawford's injuries, texts between the couple and a UK forensic physician called by the defence. The jury also visited the Crawfords' hilltop home, surrounded by bushland in the Lockyer Valley.

Crown prosecutor Chris Cook put the staging to the jury as a deliberate plan to make the death look like a tragic accident. "It was Mr Crawford who caused Mrs Crawford's body to be at the bottom of the retaining wall of the property," Cook said. "It was Mr Crawford who also moved the lawnmower over the retaining wall." Prosecutors described Crawford as a "master manipulator" who set out to make his wife's death appear accidental.

Prosecutors told the jury that Crawford was motivated to kill his wife because he stood to lose financially in a divorce settlement brought on by his serial infidelity. The former pilot faced the loss of his beloved $2m home and his early retirement plans if his wife followed through with divorce plans, jurors were told, and he was described to them as a "love rat". "He knew he stood to lose out," Cook said. "That's why he was telling his lovers all about it."

Crawford's defence attacked the case against him as full of gaps filled in with speculation and wrongful suspicion. "It is one of the weakest circumstantial cases you will ever see in a criminal court," Robert Crawford's barrister Saul Holt KC said. The defence said Crawford had engaged in multiple infidelities and lied about them but was trying to repair his marriage before his wife suffered an accident. Holt said Crawford had been busy with work calls and had let his wife move the lawnmower away from automated lawn sprinklers.

The prosecution said Frances Crawford would not have gone outside in her pyjamas in zero-degree weather to move an almost 250kg lawnmower that her children had never seen her use.

Robert Crawford, who was on bail throughout the trial, awaits sentencing.
