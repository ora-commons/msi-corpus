## Rubio and Guilfoyle embraced on Athens tarmac day before story published

Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Vice President JD Vance offered different reactions Thursday to a Wall Street Journal article about U.S. Ambassador to Greece Kimberly Guilfoyle.

The day before the WSJ article published, Rubio and Guilfoyle embraced on the tarmac in Greece, according to the WSJ Politics newsletter written by Damian Paletta, the Journal's Washington coverage chief. Paletta described the underlying Journal article as "explosive."

As ambassador, Guilfoyle is a key figure in Rubio's diplomatic realm — and, in Paletta's framing, potentially a liability.

Rubio, Paletta wrote, snapped at the press when asked about the article Thursday. "I don't know if it's true or not true," he told reporters in Greece. "You guys somehow walk around thinking that because some source told you something and you reported it, it's the gospel. It's not the gospel. It's not the gospel for me."

Vance, asked in Washington, was more circumspect. A reporter posed a hypothetical: "Do you have any reaction to a U.S. official, in this case a U.S. ambassador, asking someone to pay off debt in exchange to access to other government officials?" Vance responded: "Well, with that hypothetical…obviously I don't love that, but I don't know exactly who you are talking about."

He said he wanted to wait for more information before commenting further. The Journal noted that Vance's answer "was circumspect, but it definitely wasn't 'No comment.'"

Paletta wrote that either Rubio or Vance could end up as the 2028 Republican presidential candidate. Guilfoyle had been engaged to Donald Trump Jr. until a messy breakup, so there is a bit of drama all over the place. If she is ousted or forced to resign, Paletta wrote, there will be plenty of finger-pointing. "Maybe the finger-pointing has already begun," he added.
