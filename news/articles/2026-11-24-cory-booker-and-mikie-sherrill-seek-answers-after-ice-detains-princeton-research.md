## Steve Sigmund faults ICE tactics as Kusakawa demands Xie's rights protected

Democratic Sen. Cory Booker of New Jersey said in a statement that his office contacted ICE and did not receive a "satisfactory response" about why Eva Yi Xie was detained.

Democratic Gov. Mikie Sherrill directed her administration to coordinate with federal lawmakers and the state of Virginia to get more information. Sherrill's spokesperson Steve Sigmund said in an email, "While ICE claims to go after the worst of the worst, time and again the record shows they do not — and their tactics are making New Jerseyans less safe."

Gisela Perez Kusakawa, executive director of the Asian American Scholar Forum, demanded that Xie's rights be protected. "Due process and the rule of law build the trust that lets scientists, researchers, and students feel safe pursuing their studies and work here," she said. "That trust is what attracts the world's best scientific talents to the United States, and it is the foundation for our country's next generation of scientific leaders."

ICE detained Xie, a third-year doctoral student at Princeton University and Chinese neuroscience researcher, at Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport on Thursday as she tried to fly back to New Jersey from a conference in the nation's capital, adviser Carlos Brody said in a social media post. Xie is in the custody of immigration authorities, marking another instance of federal enforcement at airports.

The Department of Homeland Security, which oversees ICE, said Xie overstayed her visa and did not have authorization to study in the United States. The agency said she came to the United States on a temporary visa on March 16, 2016, and was required to depart by September 15, 2016. DHS said Xie "chose to violate our nation's laws by refusing to depart by the required date of September 15, 2016."

Lawyer Kerry Doyle said Xie came to the United States at age 14 with her family from China and has had an asylum application pending for years. Doyle said Xie has permission to live, work and study in the United States while her case is pending. Doyle said Xie is being held at ICE's Caroline Detention Center in Virginia.

DHS said "a pending application does not confer legal status." The agency also said, "Xie will receive due process and remain in ICE custody pending her removal proceedings."

Rodney Priestley, dean of Princeton University's graduate school, said in a statement, "News of Eva's detention is distressing for me and many members of the Princeton University community." He added, "We hope Eva will soon be able to resume her studies at Princeton."

On her professional website, Xie said her research at the Princeton Neuroscience Institute "seeks to understand the neural basis of decision-making." She also said she graduated from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 2024.

ICE has ramped up detentions at airports since the summer, especially targeting people the agency says have overstayed their visas.


Two days after Xie was taken into custody in Washington, masked immigration officers detained a father and daughter on an airport jet bridge in Seattle, as [MSI previously reported](/articles/2026-11-22-ice-airport-arrest-in-seattle-disrupted-family-s-baptism-trip-lawyer-says/).
