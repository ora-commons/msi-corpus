## NTSB says stuck rudder sent tanker into New Orleans cruise ship

**Subtype:** fact

- NTSB investigators said a steering failure locked the Atlantic Asphalt tanker's rudder in a starboard position, sending the vessel into the docked American Heritage cruise ship in New Orleans on Friday.
- The tanker struck the cruise ship two minutes after the rudder became stuck, NTSB accident investigator Bart Barnum said at a Sunday briefing in New Orleans.
- The cruise ship capsized 22 minutes after the impact, NTSB Member John DeLeeuw said.
- Officials said 32 people were taken to the hospital after the crash, none with critical injuries, and the city of New Orleans said six remained hospitalized as of Sunday.

## Six people remained hospitalized after Friday's river collision

National Transportation Safety Board officials said Sunday that a steering problem left the Atlantic Asphalt tanker's rudder stuck to starboard — the right — which sent the roughly 520-foot (160-meter) vessel veering toward the east bank of the Mississippi River, where it hit the American Heritage cruise ship two minutes later.

The briefing in New Orleans provided the first official word on what caused Friday's crash. NTSB officials said it is not yet clear what caused the steering failure.

"It could be many different things," NTSB accident investigator Bart Barnum said. "That is something that we're investigating very closely."

The tanker had a crew of 19. The cruise ship, about 300 feet (91 meters) long, carried 140 passengers and 50 crew, NTSB officials said.

As the tanker approached the east bank, the ship's data recorder captured audio sounds "consistent" with the ship's whistle going off, along with warnings and requests for assistance broadcast over the vessel's marine radio, Barnum said. He said the data also included commands to deploy the vessel anchors and emergency ship handling orders.

Officials said 32 people were taken to the hospital, including two police officers who were thrown into the river when the boat rolled, but none of the injuries were critical. As of Sunday, six people remained hospitalized, the city of New Orleans said in a statement.

DeLeeuw said the cruise ship capsized 22 minutes after the collision. He praised what he called "the excellent response" from federal, state, local and private vessels that helped rescue people immediately after the crash, and urged people with photos or video of the crash to submit them to the NTSB. He said the investigation is expected to result in a preliminary report within 30 days.

Hours after the crash, the Board of Commissioners of the Port of New Orleans filed a federal lawsuit saying that the tanker caused at least $1 million in damage to its terminal. A judge issued a warrant Friday giving federal officials control of the ship for now.
