## Oil rises to $91.11 as Middle East tensions persist despite supply recovery

Asian shares rebounded Thursday, led by artificial-intelligence-related stocks, after Wall Street pared its sharp losses Wednesday. U.S. markets traded ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday.

Nvidia, one of Wall Street’s biggest drivers, fell 3.9% as AI shares lost steam for a second day after Jensen Huang’s comeback as CEO. On Wednesday, S&P Global Ratings warned that it could downgrade Nvidia’s credit rating over concerns surrounding Nvidia’s management and governance.

Investors had already been growing cautious over the AI trade, even before Nvidia’s latest decline. The future of AI development and companies’ profitability had come into question after industry calls for an AI slowdown over safety concerns. The U.S. midterm elections also raised doubts about the pace of AI infrastructure buildouts.

Japan’s Nikkei 225 climbed 1.3% to 57,768.92 after falling 2.8% the previous day. OpenAI investor SoftBank Group jumped 9%, memory maker Kioxia Holdings surged 12.4% and chip-equipment maker Tokyo Electron gained 1.5%.

South Korea’s benchmark Kospi index added 1.2% to 4,755.29 after a 6.2% drop Wednesday. Samsung Electronics gained 1.6%, while memory-chipmaker SK Hynix rose 3.7%.

Taiwan’s Taiex was 2.6% higher, while shares of TSMC, or Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co., gained 0.2%. Hong Kong’s Hang Seng index rose 1.9% to 22,373.81, while the Shanghai Composite slipped less than 0.1% to 3,475.46.

Australia’s S&P/ASX 200 gained 0.9% to 8,767.20, while India’s Sensex edged up 0.1%.

On Wall Street, the S&P 500 lost 0.3% after falling as much as 3.2% earlier in the session. The technology-heavy Nasdaq Composite closed 0.6% lower after dropping as much as 4.6%, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.3%. Broadcom, which had fallen sharply earlier, ended 0.1% lower. Anthropic, which began public trading last week, recovered from an early drop and finished 1.1% higher, though it remained below its $83 per-share initial public offering price. [FRED’s Nov. 26 observation](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NASDAQCOM) put the Nasdaq Composite Index at 23,585.8.

Investors were also monitoring the effects of the latest U.S. tariff increases planned for China, Japan, the European Union and other trading partners. The increases could add to inflationary impacts and higher consumer prices.

In the bond market, the 10-year Treasury yield was little changed at around 4.86%. A separate [FRED observation for Nov. 26](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS10) put the 10-year Treasury yield at 4.91%.

Oil prices were up early Thursday as tensions in the Middle East persisted, even as global oil supply continued to recover. Brent crude, the international standard, gained 0.5% to $91.11 a barrel; it had traded at approximately $72 a barrel before the war began in late February. Benchmark U.S. crude rose 0.8% to $84.33 a barrel.

The U.S. dollar fell to 159.12 Japanese yen from 159.16 yen. The euro traded at $1.0961, down from $1.0974.

The market’s broader picture remained unsettled after the rebound: AI-related equities stayed volatile, planned tariffs could add to inflation, and oil prices remained elevated as Middle East tensions persisted and global supply recovered.

AP journalists Damian J. Troise, Alex Veiga and Christopher Rugaber contributed to this report.
