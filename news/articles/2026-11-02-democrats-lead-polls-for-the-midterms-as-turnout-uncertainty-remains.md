## Republicans hold a cash advantage as Senate races remain close

Democratic candidates enter the November 3 US midterm elections with substantial leads in public-opinion polling. BBC News reported on November 2 that the polls place Democrats ahead in well over the 218 seats needed to take control of the House of Representatives, while Democratic candidates are tied or narrowly ahead in key US Senate races. A recent New York Times poll had South Carolina's Senate contest tied even though Donald Trump won the state by 18 percentage points in 2024. CNN surveys put Democrats ahead in Texas, Kansas and Maine.

Those favorable numbers came against a political backdrop BBC News described as “a historically unpopular Republican president,” an American public unhappy with rising food and bill costs, and months of favorable polls. The outlet said those conditions pointed to a good night for Democrats. Republicans nevertheless begin the final stretch with structural advantages that could limit the size of any Democratic gains.

Clifford Young, president of Ipsos Public Affairs, which conducts political and market research surveys, said polling has become more difficult in recent years because Americans have abandoned landlines, screen calls and shown less willingness to participate. “Conservatives, especially those on the far right, are much more anti-establishment by nature,” Young said. “And they don't participate in anything. They hate the system. That's the challenge.”

Young said that if Republican voters were being systematically undercounted and pollsters did not account for that in their voting models, the data could skew toward Democrats. BBC News said this was one reason it has become trickier to predict what the electorate will look like.

Polling models try to capture shifting fundamentals, and a well-designed poll is built to anticipate issues that may make it less than reliable, BBC News reported. Individual polls have a built-in margin of error, a statistical level of uncertainty based on sample size compared with the population. Taken as an aggregate, polling averages can provide a more accurate prediction than individual surveys. BBC News said even the kind of polling lean seen in recent presidential elections would suggest Democrats are likely to have a good night on November 3.

BBC News noted that recent US political history includes many cases in which public-opinion polls were off the mark, including polls conducted only days before an election. Trump outperformed predictions on his way to White House victories in 2016 and 2024. His narrow defeat in 2020 came after most surveys indicated a more comfortable win for Democrat Joe Biden. Earlier in 2026, polls indicated a double-digit victory for Democrat Abdul El-Sayed in Michigan's Democratic Senate primary, but he won by 1%. In Wisconsin's Democratic primary for governor, a candidate with a polling lead approaching 20 points lost by a fraction of a percent.

BBC News said recent midterm general-election polls have proven more accurate in recent years in part because Trump — and his unique ability to draw disaffected Americans to the polls — was not on the ballot. In 2022, polls suggested Democrats would defy conventional expectations and avoid an electoral wipe-out despite Biden's low approval numbers. The party lost the House by slim margins but maintained control of the US Senate. Young said this year's primary surprises should not be taken as evidence that general-election polls are off the mark, because nominating contests have historically been volatile and voters are more likely to decide late.

BBC News noted that it is common for the president's party to have a bad night in the midterms. In 2010, Republicans flipped more than 60 House seats and made gains in the Senate as the economy struggled to recover from the financial crash and conservatives feared President Barack Obama's healthcare reforms. Polls predicted a Republican defeat, though not on that scale. The roles are reversed this year, with Democrats in opposition and the economy on voters' minds.

BBC News said several factors could limit how well Democrats perform relative to their polling position and Trump's low approval rating. Much of the territory in play was won easily by Trump in 2024, making it a home game for Republicans. Changes to House election boundaries over the years have meant fewer seats switch between parties. BBC News attributed the reduction in swing voters to the country's deeper polarisation and reported that some experts describe the electorate as calcified. Republicans also have a cash advantage worth hundreds of millions of dollars for attack advertising in the final weeks.

One explanation for Trump's previous overperformance, BBC News said, is that many of his supporters were not regular voters but flocked to the polls in higher-than-expected numbers. The outlet identified that turnout unpredictability as one of the main contributing factors to notable recent polling misses, even though the margins in most reputable polls were off by only a few percentage points. In close races, those few points can separate victory from defeat, an important consideration for key Senate contests within narrow margins.

Polling inaccuracies can run in both directions. In state and special elections last year, Democrats outperformed most surveys because their voters turned out in unexpectedly larger numbers. BBC News said Trump's rallies in the final campaign days in states he won comfortably in 2024 would have lifted Democratic hopes that Republican internal polling shows conservative candidates are in trouble.

Voters can still change their minds through election day, and undecided voters remain a key segment in many races. Late shifts can alter a race's dynamics, particularly when undecided voters break in the same direction. BBC News said some uncertainty will always remain and that managing it is part of the art that accompanies the science. Young said pollsters have improved their work, but uncertainty cannot be eliminated. “I think we're doing a better job,” he said. “But part of the problem we always have is not just with the pollsters. Part of it is that we're dealing with humans. Humans are not easy.” The current averages favor Democrats, but the Senate contests, late decisions and turnout patterns leave the forecast vulnerable to change.
