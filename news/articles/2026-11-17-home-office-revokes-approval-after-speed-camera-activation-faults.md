## National Highways ends contract with US firm Verra Mobility after 2,650 wrongful activations

The Home Office has revoked the approval that allows affected camera footage from National Highways roads to be used as evidence in legal proceedings after activation faults led to 2,650 wrongful camera activations between 2021 and the end of 2025. US firm Verra Mobility operates the cameras, but National Highways said it would now end its contract with the company. Roads minister Justin Madders said the system would not be fully fixed until sometime next year.

National Highways, which runs England's motorways, discovered issues with how speed cameras were activated last year, affecting 154 of 400 cameras. Following an investigation, it detected further faults. BBC business correspondent Marc Ashdown reported that the wider accuracy problems may have allowed thousands of drivers to slip through the cracks and escape fines.

In December 2025, National Highways blamed an "anomaly" in how variable-speed cameras interacted with speed-limit signs on some A roads and motorways. For example, on a road where the speed limit increases, a driver may see a sign showing 60mph while the camera recording it may still be working on the basis of a previous 40mph speed limit.

The fault affected all variable-speed cameras on smart motorways, a section of the A14 between Huntingdon and Cambridge and the A1 approach junction to the A14.

The fault led to 2,650 wrongful speed-camera activations between 2021 and the end of last year. National Highways said the incidents represented fewer than two each day, compared with more than six million activations of speed cameras on the affected roads over the same period.

The BBC has contacted Verra Mobility for comment. National Highways has been contacted for comment.

Roads minister Justin Madders told the BBC: "We can't take chances with this." He added: "If we're going to prosecute people, we need to be confident the system is working as it should. That's why we've taken the action we have."

Madders said the enforcement of speed limits continues through a range of tactics, including patrols by National Highways traffic officers, which have been stepped up.
