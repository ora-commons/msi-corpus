## Homan said Omar was under investigation for unspecified immigration violations

Congresswoman Nancy Mace of South Carolina unsuccessfully sought a subpoena for Rep. Ilhan Omar's immigration records earlier this year. In September, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin told a conservative podcaster that "something is going to happen" and made allegations of marital fraud against Omar. Separately, White House border tsar Tom Homan told Fox News that Omar was being investigated for unspecified immigration violations.

Omar denied the allegations and said, "I will say it again so it's as clear as possible: I'm not at all worried about these investigations, because they are complete shams." Rep. Ilhan Omar, a Minnesota Democrat and outspoken critic of President Donald Trump, moved to the US from Somalia in 1995 and became a naturalized citizen in 2000. Republicans had scrutinized her immigration records in the past.

Last month, President Donald Trump referred to the search of Omar's home, saying: "I don't know what they're doing with it, but I can tell you this: she's a disaster." The raid's timing — 22 days before the November midterm elections — raised concerns from Omar's fellow Democrats. The Justice Department has a longstanding policy of refraining from most enforcement action that is "likely to raise an issue or the perception" it is being brought for the purpose of affecting any election or to disadvantage a candidate.

A federal court has unsealed 169 heavily redacted pages of court documents related to the October search, despite an attempt by prosecutors to block the release. The released documents show a back-and-forth between a judge and federal prosecutors over executing the warrant and making the documents public.

The government applied for a warrant to search Omar's Washington DC home for evidence of crimes "involving Ilhan Nur Said Elmi a/k/a Ilhan Abdullahi Omar, and occurring between 1980 to the present." The pages show investigators looking for potential criminal violations involving Omar going back to 1980, while the specific alleged offenses remain redacted.

Prosecutors said they were pursuing evidence of crimes that allegedly happened decades ago and said the inquiry had nothing to do with Omar's work in Congress. Omar has not been charged with a crime and has denied wrongdoing.

After the October search, immigration agents offered limited details, saying it related to an ongoing federal criminal investigation. On social media, Omar described the search as "an attempt to target political enemies and weaponize federal law enforcement."
