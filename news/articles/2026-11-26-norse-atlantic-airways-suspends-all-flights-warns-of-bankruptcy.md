## Cost cuts and a possible sale preceded Norse Atlantic’s shutdown

Norse Atlantic Airways suspended all flights on Thursday and said it would file for bankruptcy if it could not secure short-term financing urgently. The Norwegian long-haul carrier operates around the world, including from London Gatwick and the United States to Europe and Asia. It described its finances as “critical” a day earlier.

In a statement to the Oslo stock exchange, Norse said: “If a short-term financing solution is not secured urgently, it is the board’s intention to file for bankruptcy.”

The company said it was still looking for ways to secure short-term financing and was in discussion with investors regarding a possible takeover. It warned: “There can be no assurance that a viable solution will be found.”

The Guardian reported that Norse, like many airlines worldwide, was reeling from surging jet fuel prices. Costs had risen sharply since the US-Israel war on Iran began in February, and the increase was also hitting consumer confidence.

Norse’s suspension affected London Gatwick services to Orlando, Bangkok and Cape Town. A winter service from Manchester to Thailand was due to start operating next month.

Founded in 2021, Norse entered the market after Norwegian Air abandoned its attempt to fly budget long-haul routes. The airline revived transatlantic routes with a fleet of 12 Boeing 787 Dreamliners and many former Norwegian staff.

Before the shutdown, Norse said it had explored a potential sale or merger. Its Project Falcon cost-cutting programme aimed to save $50 million (£38 million), including about 75 job cuts.

Norse had also moved its headquarters from Norway to cheaper offices in Latvia. Founder and chief executive Bjørn Tore Larsen left the airline.

Norse said it “sincerely apologises to its passengers for the inconvenience caused by this situation.”
