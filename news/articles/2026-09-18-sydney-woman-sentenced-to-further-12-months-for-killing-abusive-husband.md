## Sydney woman sentenced to further 12 months for killing abusive husband

**Subtype:** fact

- Nirmeen Noufl, 55, was sentenced Friday in the New South Wales Supreme Court to a further 12 months in prison after pleading guilty in August to killing her husband Mamdouh in excessive self-defence while being choked and assaulted at their Sydney home.
- Crown prosecutor Mark Hay told the court that the disposal of Mamdouh Noufl's body in garbage bags across south-west Sydney meant the defendant's account of the killing could not be independently tested.

- Mamdouh Noufl's body was never found; Nirmeen Noufl, who has been in custody since October 2024, will be eligible for parole in October 2027.

## Body never recovered; murder charge could not be pursued

Nirmeen Noufl appeared in the NSW Supreme Court on Friday for sentencing in the killing of her husband, Mamdouh, also known as Emad, inside their Greenacre home in south-west Sydney. She had pleaded guilty in August to manslaughter by way of excessive self-defence, a lesser charge that prosecutors accepted after she had originally been charged with murder at her arrest in October 2024. According to The Guardian's reporting on the sentencing, Noufl had lied to police multiple times during the cover-up of the killing and dismemberment.

Crown prosecutor Mark Hay told the court that the disposal of Mamdouh Noufl's body meant the 55-year-old defendant's version of events had to be accepted and that a murder charge could not be pursued. "Disposal of the body in this case was as thorough as it was brutal," Justice Peter Hamill said at sentencing. He added: "The conduct after the killing is remarkable and chilling … and is also relevant to issues such as remorse."

The judge found that an extremely traumatic childhood and severe domestic violence mitigated the seriousness of the killing, but he said the dismemberment remained important to the case. Justice Peter Hamill described the relationship as "gravely abusive," featuring violence, psychological manipulation and coercive control. A key factor in sentencing was the sustained history of abuse she suffered at her husband's hand; the attack on the night of the killing was the culmination of that pattern.

According to the agreed facts, Mamdouh Noufl held conservative Islamic views and was controlling of his wife and daughters. Days before his death, he told Nirmeen he had met a woman in Egypt whom he planned to marry, but he refused her request for a divorce. When he returned home later that day, the two argued, and he assaulted her, choking her as he pinned her to the ground, according to what she later told psychiatrists.

In a statement to a confidante after the killing, Noufl said: "All my life … he just treated me like shit, spat at me. I had enough." She then used a saw to dismember her husband's body and disposed of the remains in multiple garbage bags across south-west Sydney. His body was never found, and her claim that she stabbed him only once in the chest could not be tested.

The judge indicated that members of Mamdouh Noufl's family would probably be disappointed with the sentence. "I hope all of you understand … there will seem to be a gap between the loss you have suffered forever and the sentence I impose," he said. Noufl wiped away tears in court as Hamill outlined the circumstances of extreme violence and abuse in her childhood and marriage.

One of Noufl's daughters told the court she would immediately uproot her life abroad and remain in Australia to help her mother attend psychiatric appointments once she is released. "If she were to be released tomorrow, I would cancel my flight," the daughter, who cannot be identified for legal reasons, said.

Nirmeen Noufl will be eligible for parole in October 2027.
