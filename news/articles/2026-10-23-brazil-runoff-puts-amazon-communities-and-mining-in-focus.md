## Mining hopes in Roraima meet Indigenous determination to resist

The Guardian's Today in Focus episode reports two strands of expectation ahead of Sunday's Brazilian presidential runoff: voters, preachers and politicians in Roraima hope Flávio Bolsonaro will bring back mining, while Indigenous activists are determined to fight back even if the Bolsonaros take power again. The episode opens by saying that for the Amazon and the communities who live there, the stakes in the runoff could scarcely be higher, and it follows what is at stake for the Amazon rainforest and the communities living there.

The episode says Roraima, the country's most northern state, heavily backed Flávio Bolsonaro in the first round. Phillips found the voters, preachers and politicians he met hopeful that Flávio — whom the Guardian's episode calls 'the far-right candidate' — would bring back the mining his father, Jair Bolsonaro, encouraged when in power. The episode says that administration oversaw the felling of 11m acres (4.5m hectares) of rainforest.

The episode also reports on Indigenous activists determined to fight back even if the Bolsonaros take power again, a resolve that contrasts with the mining hopes recorded among the voters, preachers and politicians Phillips met.
