## Transport Workers Union plans court challenge over humane horse retirement law

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani signed a law Thursday ending Central Park's horse-carriage industry by June 2028, according to The Wall Street Journal. The law requires roughly 100 horses to be retired humanely and bars their sale for slaughter or their use in another horse-drawn carriage business. Transport Workers Union Local 100, which represents the drivers, said it plans to challenge the ban in court.

The law sets a deadline for ending a Central Park fixture that has been part of the city for more than 160 years. It also requires humane treatment and sale restrictions for roughly 100 horses while starting a city transition for drivers, stable hands and medallion owners. Animal-rights activists have long called the use of horses for carriage rides inhumane.

The New York City Council voted in October to ban the carriages. Mamdani, who has long supported ending horse-drawn carriage rides, signaled that he would support the bill. Before signing it, he held a hearing earlier this month to hear concerns from drivers and carriage medallion owners.

“Our city has changed and now it is time for our laws to change too,” Mamdani said.

“As I sign this bill into law, I do so with a deep sense of both gratitude and obligation toward the more than 200 New Yorkers who make up the horse-carriage industry today. I have the utmost respect for the work that carriage drivers and stable hands do and for the more than 160 years of contributions they have made to our city.”

Mamdani said the horse-carriage industry includes more than 200 New Yorkers. Opponents of the measure said it would leave roughly 170 drivers, owners and stable hands without jobs.

Frank Riccobono, the owner of NYC Horse Carriage Rides, criticized the law's treatment of workers. “This is not a humane bill for the humans of New York City that devoted their lives into this business,” Riccobono said. He followed his father—a Sicilian immigrant—into the horse-carriage business. Transport Workers Union Local 100, which represents the drivers, said it plans to challenge the ban in court.

About 100 horses pull the carriages, giving visitors tours of Manhattan's Central Park. The law requires the animals to be dealt with humanely after retirement and prohibits their sale for slaughter or for use in another horse-drawn carriage business.

Mamdani released a framework Tuesday to assist medallion owners, carriage drivers and stable hands while the industry winds down. New York City plans to offer workforce development training for drivers and stable hands.

The Mamdani administration will create a program to buy back medallions from owners. Transport Workers Union Local 100 said medallions can cost as much as $400,000. Mamdani said a full transition plan will be released in April.
