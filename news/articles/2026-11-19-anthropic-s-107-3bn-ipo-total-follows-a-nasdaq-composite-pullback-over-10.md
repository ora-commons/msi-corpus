## Anthropic ranks around 12th; Garry White calls the IPO a litmus test

Sky News calculated that Anthropic's IPO had secured a record $107.3 billion, including funds raised through a private sale of shares to technology firms. Anthropic was due to make its Nasdaq debut as technology stocks had pulled the Nasdaq Composite down by over 10% from its record high at the start of October. Garry White, chief investment commentator at wealth manager Raymond James, called the IPO a litmus test for current market sentiment toward the AI investment boom. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei and his fellow co-founders are expected to ring the Nasdaq opening bell. Anthropic posted a 2025 operating loss of more than $8 billion, while the IPO was expected to place Anthropic around 12th among the world's largest companies.

The Guardian reported that Anthropic, the frontier AI company behind the Claude chatbot, increased the share price to $83 from a previous guide of $70-$78. The public share sale alone was expected to raise $81.3 billion (£61.8 billion), exceeding the $75 billion raised by Elon Musk's SpaceX in June. Anthropic announced a “record-breaking sum” from the IPO the night before. The Guardian described the offering as the biggest initial public offering in history and a major test of market enthusiasm for the AI industry. It also reported a surge in demand from investors looking to buy a stake despite fears that AI could be a bubble.

CEO Dario Amodei and his fellow co-founders are expected to ring the Nasdaq opening bell at 9:30 a.m. New York time and 2:30 p.m. UK time. Anthropic was then due to make its Nasdaq debut under the symbol “ANT,” although its shares might not begin trading immediately after the bell.

The Guardian reported that investor unease related to Nvidia accompanied the drop in technology stocks, which pulled the Nasdaq Composite down by over 10% from its record high at the start of October.

The IPO was set to value Anthropic at about $1.5 trillion (£1.1 trillion), placing it around 12th on the list of the world's biggest companies.

Anthropic was loss-making. In 2025, Anthropic posted an operating loss of more than $8 billion, as expenses of nearly $13 billion outpaced revenue of $4.6 billion. Revenue accelerated in 2026, but Anthropic's challenge was to maintain that acceleration and turn it into profits.
