## NIH review finds positive but inconsistent glyphosate association with certain blood cancers

Missouri Circuit Court Judge Timothy Boyer approved Bayer’s $7.25 billion settlement Friday afternoon after nearly a decade of litigation. The agreement was designed to bring the bulk of the company’s current and future Roundup weedkiller cases to a close.

Released around the same time as the settlement decision, the National Institutes of Health study examined glyphosate, Roundup’s main ingredient. Requested by Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., the study did not run experiments or collect new data; it reviewed existing research.

The researchers found a positive but inconsistent association between glyphosate herbicides and certain blood cancers, particularly non-Hodgkin lymphoma. The authors said a link between cancer and glyphosate cannot be ruled out.

The U.S. government has not said there is any link, while the World Health Organization has deemed glyphosate a probable carcinogen. The Environmental Protection Agency is re-evaluating the carcinogenic risks of glyphosate and is expected to rule by early 2027.

An EPA spokesperson said the agency was working “hand in hand” with the Department of Health and Human Services, which oversees the NIH. “Every relevant, quality study gets a seat at the table, and this one is no exception,” an EPA spokesperson said.

Bayer’s glyphosate division spokeswoman said the NIH study had not undergone formal peer review, so its conclusions should be considered preliminary. The company said the EPA’s review of glyphosate is based on a large body of evidence and several long-term studies rather than a single review.

Bayer has long said glyphosate does not cause cancer and is safe to use. The company has described glyphosate as one of the most thoroughly studied herbicides in the world, with about 2,500 studies over half a century.

Roundup is the most popular weedkiller in the world, and roughly 300 million pounds of glyphosate are sprayed on U.S. crops each year. Although the herbicide is widely supported by farmers, most of the litigation has been brought by people who used it on residential lawns and gardens.

In his decision, Boyer called the deal “fair and adequate.” Bayer CEO Bill Anderson said the judge’s approval was “a decisive milestone in the company’s strategy to mitigate the risks of both pending and future Roundup litigation.” Analysts said the settlement materially reduces uncertainty surrounding Bayer’s glyphosate liabilities.

The agreement covers future plaintiffs, but objectors could pursue a lengthy appeals process that might take years to resolve. Tom Claps, managing director of legal and regulatory analysis at the Gordon Haskett advisory firm, said, “We are skeptical that an unpublished, non-peer-reviewed paper requested by HHS will, by itself, cause the EPA to change its existing position.” Claps also said that even if the EPA changes the Roundup label, Bayer has obtained an important layer of protection through the class settlement.

If the EPA changes the label, the decision could increase Bayer’s litigation exposure, Claps said. An EPA cancer warning could also bolster arguments from plaintiffs who opted out of Bayer’s settlement and are attempting to take individual cases to trial. Bayer has won most Roundup trials, including its most recent one in October.

The Supreme Court ruled in Bayer’s favor this summer, saying the company cannot be held liable for failing to warn about Roundup’s alleged risks when the federal regulator has repeatedly maintained glyphosate’s safety over the years. That ruling has helped Bayer fend off lawsuits from Roundup users alleging that the company failed to warn them about alleged cancer risks.

Make America Healthy Again advocates have long made opposing glyphosate a priority but have had little success restricting it. Kennedy, a longtime glyphosate critic, is also a former plaintiffs’ lawyer and was part of a legal team that won a 2018 Roundup verdict.

“With these findings, glyphosate meets every EPA criteria for a substance to be likely carcinogenic to humans,” said Calley Means, a Kennedy ally who until recently worked on health policy in the White House. “The EPA now has a decision.”

Bayer’s shares, which trade in Germany, were down slightly Monday. The stock had surged more than 70% over the previous 12 months but remained below its price when Bayer bought Roundup’s maker Monsanto for $63 billion in 2016.

The settlement and the NIH study are now moving through separate legal and regulatory tracks. The agreement is intended to close most current and future litigation, although objectors may appeal and plaintiffs who opted out remain in court. The EPA is expected to rule on glyphosate’s carcinogenic risks by early 2027, and a change in the Roundup label could increase Bayer’s exposure.
