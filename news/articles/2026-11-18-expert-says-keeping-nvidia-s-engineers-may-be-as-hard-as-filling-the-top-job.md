## Colette Kress serves as interim CEO after Nvidia board declines Huang's return

Nvidia's board said it does not intend to reinstate co-founder Jensen Huang as chief executive, and chief financial officer Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO after the company lost the man who built it into an AI powerhouse. Columbia Business School accounting professor Shivaram Rajgopal told NPR's Morning Edition that keeping the company's engineers may be as hard as filling the top job. He recommended pairing Kress with a technology leader, retaining key employees and sharing more information about ongoing investigations.

NPR reported that Huang resigned after admitting to being unfaithful to his wife, amid reports linking him to a woman whom U.S. intelligence officials say was recruited by China's spy agency. With Huang gone and his return ruled out, Nvidia now needs a permanent chief executive, and it has placed Kress in the role in the interim.

Retention, Rajgopal said, may be just as hard. "Many of them are independently wealthy, and they can easily go out and set up something else," he said of Nvidia's engineers. Their financial independence leaves them able to walk away and start something else, a risk the company faces alongside its leadership change.

Kress, Nvidia's chief financial officer for 13 years, handled the company's first earnings call since Huang's departure. Rajgopal said she did "as well as one could expect in the circumstances." He described her as "very good with numbers, very buttoned down, very precise," while saying her answers about strategy and employee retention were "a bit vague."

Rajgopal doubted that a finance chief alone can run a technology company on the same footing. "I don't know of many examples of tech companies that are being run successfully by finance people," he said. An outside hire, he said, may not be the answer either. "This is such a technically complex enterprise," he said, adding that even someone with a technology background could struggle to make an immediate impact.

His prescription for the transition had three parts: pair Kress with a technology leader, retain key employees and share more information about ongoing investigations.

He drew the disclosure point by comparison to Uber's handling of an investigation under former CEO Travis Kalanick, where the company released recommendations but not its findings. The NPR segment also noted that Uber was to pay $4.4 million to employees who were sexually harassed at work. "Give us the facts, not just recommendations," Rajgopal said.

The immediate questions for Nvidia are which executive will take the permanent role, what the company discloses about ongoing investigations and whether its engineers stay. Rajgopal addressed all three in an interview with NPR's Morning Edition. The radio interview was edited by Ally Schweitzer, and the digital version by Suzanne Nuyen.
