## Jeffries' 251-11 speaker vote included opposition from incoming progressives

House Democrats nominated Rep. Katherine Clark of Massachusetts on Wednesday to become House majority leader next year, a position that would make her the first woman to serve in the role. Clark, the current House minority whip, won the nomination by unanimous voice vote. Democrats also nominated Hakeem Jeffries for speaker and Pete Aguilar for majority whip.

Clark emphasized party unity in a Thursday interview with NPR's Morning Edition. She tied her optimism to the incoming caucus's diversity: "It was really looking at the diversity of that caucus, people who have been sent to Congress from all walks of life, that gives me the optimism and the energy that we can really deliver for the American people."

The caucus's growth also required a bigger room for Wednesday's gathering, Clark said. She described the newly expanded caucus as a source of optimism and energy.

The incoming leadership faces the challenge of lowering living costs while investigating alleged corruption in the Trump administration. Asked whether Democrats could accomplish both, Clark answered, "Of course we can," adding, "That's our responsibility."

Clark also called President Trump's description of himself as "Mr. Veto" a "stunning statement." She said she hoped for bipartisan cooperation on healthcare.

The party nevertheless showed internal divisions over the leadership slate. Jeffries won the speaker nomination 251-11, with opposition from incoming progressives including Darializa Avila Chevalier of New York and Adam Hamawy of New Jersey. Those lawmakers have pressed for Medicare for All, immigration enforcement reforms and an end to the war in Gaza.

Clark and Jeffries also differed over U.S. aid to Israel. In July, Clark voted for a Republican amendment that would have cut the aid, while Jeffries opposed it.

Clark was asked whether the 11 votes against Jeffries could complicate Democrats' agenda. She replied, "We are having conversations with every part of our new caucus, and they are united behind making life better for working people." Her response came despite the disagreement over aid to Israel and concerns among other Democrats about Jeffries' leadership.

The agenda Clark outlined included lowering living costs and investigating alleged corruption in the Trump administration. She said both were responsibilities for Democrats, while her broader argument was that the enlarged caucus could work together despite the divisions revealed during the nominations.
