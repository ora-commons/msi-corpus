## Disney+ without-ads pricing has more than tripled since its 2019 launch

U.S. viewers are moving toward less expensive entertainment as Netflix, Disney+, HBO Max and other premium streaming services raise prices over several years, including monthly increases to lower-priced ad-supported tiers. The Wall Street Journal, citing S&P Global and Antenna data, reported that a growing share of premium subscriptions now comes from ad-supported plans.

The price of Disney+ without ads has more than tripled since its 2019 launch. Netflix's comparable price has grown more than 50% over the same period, while overall inflation has been about 32%. The increases have exceeded inflation and helped streaming services generate revenue growth.

Those gains pleased investors who stopped rewarding subscriber growth at all costs and began scrutinizing the profitability of the services. As prices continued to rise, more consumers gravitated toward cheaper ad-supported tiers and free viewing options.

Brendan Brady, vice president of strategy for data firm Antenna, said higher prices were only part of the shift. The growing availability of live sports on streaming services and the volume of available content also pushed users toward ad-supported plans.

“There’s so much TV out there that, in an effort to potentially stack services, users may opt for those lower-priced offers,” Brady said.

Some services generate less money per user on average from ad-based subscription versions than from premium tiers, according to industry analysts. Streamers are trying to increase their advertising revenue to make up the difference.

Customers who choose cheaper plans also tend to be less loyal, according to Antenna data. That leaves streamers spending marketing dollars to recruit new subscribers or win back people who left. Brady said part of the cancellation-rate difference is due to longtime premium Netflix subscribers, who tend to be more loyal.

In recent years, media companies have increasingly relied on bundles to keep cancellation rates in check.

Recent data show that the number of premium subscriptions per household has plateaued, according to S&P Global, a signal that the industry's rapid subscription-growth period is coming to an end.

Google-owned YouTube has gained ground as consumers turn toward cheaper and free viewing options. Its nearly limitless catalog of free content has powered the service's continued ascent.

Together, the shifts leave streamers balancing higher prices against ad-supported tiers that for some services generate less money per user, cheaper-plan customers who tend to be less loyal, and a premium-subscription market that is no longer expanding quickly. For viewers, the available choices now range from premium subscriptions to ad-supported plans, bundled offerings and free services.
