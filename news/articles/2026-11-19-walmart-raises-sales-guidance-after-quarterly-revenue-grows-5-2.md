## Sales and profits grow in e-commerce, advertising and newer businesses

On Thursday, Walmart reported 5.2% growth in total revenue to $188.8 billion for the three months ended Oct. 31. The retailer said e-commerce, advertising and other newer businesses drove the increase, and raised its full-year net-sales growth guidance to 4.9% to 5.2% even as U.S. store comparable sales fell, Chief Financial Officer John David Rainey said. Market data showed Walmart’s WMT shares down 1.04%.

Walmart did not break out store-specific or e-commerce sales results. Rainey said the store comparable-sales figure was less meaningful than it had been because stores help fulfill a large chunk of e-commerce sales. “Stores and, I would argue, our delivery networks are actually becoming digital assets,” Rainey said. “If you just focus on the in-store comp, that really misses what’s going on in our business.”

Walmart’s U.S. comparable sales, covering stores and digital channels operating at least 12 months, grew 2.9% in the three months ended Oct. 30. That was slightly higher than the 2.6% growth reported in the previous quarter, Walmart’s slowest growth in six years.

Based on its sales performance, Walmart raised its full-year net-sales growth forecast to 4.9% to 5.2%, up from a previous range of 4% to 5%. The company now expects full-year earnings per share of $2.84 to $2.89, up from $2.80 to $2.87.

For the quarter, Walmart’s adjusted earnings per share grew 4.8% to $0.65, above analysts’ expectations for $0.64.

Walmart began as a discount retailer in 1962, and the bulk of the chain’s sales and profits still comes from U.S. stores. The company said that mix will keep changing. On Thursday, Walmart said sales and profits from e-commerce, advertising and other newer businesses were growing while store sales continued to slide.

Walmart said its strongest growth came from e-commerce, membership and advertising. Global e-commerce sales grew 17%, U.S. e-commerce sales grew 23%, and the U.S. advertising business grew 37%. E-commerce now accounts for around a quarter of Walmart’s total sales.

Walmart has expanded e-commerce in recent years by working to speed delivery and offer more products from third-party sellers to increase sales. More Americans now shop online, and Walmart has been among legacy brick-and-mortar retailers expanding online operations. Amazon surpassed Walmart as America’s biggest company by revenue earlier this year.

Walmart’s latest U.S. sales figure was helped by market-share gains in its food business and hurt by pricing regulation in its pharmacy business. Without the pharmacy regulation, comparable sales would have been 3.6%, the company said. Rainey said Walmart’s food market-share gains were the largest in three years as the company worked to decrease prices. Lower prices helped Walmart sell a million more turkeys than last year at this point in the Thanksgiving season, he said.

The retailer’s results came amid recent sales gains from other companies. Earlier in the week, Target said its comparable sales rose 5.6% in the most recent quarter. TJX, which owns TJ Maxx and other chains, said sales rose 4%. Home-improvement retailers Home Depot and Lowe’s reported sales growth of 2.2% and 1.8%, respectively. The Wall Street Journal said Home Depot and Lowe’s were navigating what it called a “tepid housing market.” The same source described Walmart’s results as a sign that American consumers were spending even as they expressed concerns about their ability to do so.

“Our customers are telling us that they’re continuing to feel pressure, most particularly probably at the fuel pump. But the fact is spending remains strong,” Rainey said.

Chief Executive John Furner said on a call with analysts and investors, “As shopping with us becomes more convenient, customers are turning to us more often,” adding that, “That grows our retail business and creates more opportunity for membership, marketplace and advertising.”
