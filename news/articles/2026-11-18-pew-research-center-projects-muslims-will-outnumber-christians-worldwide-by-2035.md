## U.S. Christians are projected to fall below 50% of adults by 2065

Pew Research Center released detailed projections Wednesday showing that Islam is expected to surpass Christianity as the world's largest religion by 2035. The broader project examines how major religious groups could change between 2020 and 2070, taking into account age profiles, fertility rates, religious switching and migration. Pew, which has issued reports on religious demography for 25 years, said both Islam and Christianity are growing, but Islam is growing much faster.

Conrad Hackett, a Pew senior demographer, said, “It's the babies,” explaining that conversion and other ways of changing religious identity are much smaller factors than fertility rates. During 2020-2025, the fertility rate was 3.1 children per woman among Muslims, 2.5 among Christians and 1.6 for all other women, according to Pew. Pew described 2.1 as the level considered necessary for each generation to replace itself.

Pew's latest estimates put the global Christian population at 2.27 billion in 2020 and the Muslim population at 2.02 billion. The new projections forecast 2.57 billion Muslims and 2.52 billion Christians by 2035. If recent trends continue, Pew projects more than 3.6 billion Muslims and just under 3 billion Christians by 2070.

The Pew analysis comes as the world population is projected to increase 29% between 2020 and 2070. Of the religious groups Pew analyzed, Muslims and Christians are the only ones expected to keep pace, with Christian numbers growing by 30% and Muslim numbers by 80%. Hindus and Jews are projected to grow in absolute numbers but decline as a share of the global population. Buddhists are projected to shrink in number and as a share, partly because Pew expects the overall populations of China, South Korea and Japan to decline.

In the United States, Christians account for 62% of adults, according to Pew's current estimates. Pew projects that the share will fall below 50% by 2065, meaning the United States will no longer have a Christian majority within 40 years. In neighboring Canada, Christians are expected to drop below 50% by 2030. Pew attributed a significant part of the projected decline in both countries to a concurrent increase in people with no religious affiliation. By 2070, the unaffiliated are projected to make up 41% of the U.S. population and to outnumber Christians in Canada.

The increase in people with no religious affiliation is also expected across many countries. Excluding China, where the share of unaffiliated people is high but the overall population faces a sharp decline, Pew expects the unaffiliated population elsewhere to grow by more than 180 million by 2070. The unaffiliated already make up a majority in 10 countries, including the Czech Republic, Japan, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Uruguay and Vietnam. South Korea, France and the United Kingdom are forecast to join that group by 2070.

Most of the world's population growth is expected to occur in Africa, particularly south of the Sahara Desert, where most people are Christians or Muslims. Pew said high fertility and rising life expectancy in the region will increase the global numbers of both faiths. By 2070, more than half of the world's Christians are projected to live in sub-Saharan Africa. Congo is projected to surpass the United States as the country with the most Christians, with the number of Christians there increasing more than threefold to 299 million.

Europe's population is projected to shrink by 17% by 2070, while its Christian population is expected to fall by a third, from 505 million to 324 million. Over the same period, the Muslim share of Europe's population is projected to rise from 6% to 13%. In the United States, the Christian population is projected to decline from about 217 million in 2020 to about 174 million in 2070. Pew expects other major regions to grow more modestly or decline.

Several mainline Protestant denominations in the United States have faced declining membership over many years. Jeffrey Brodd, a religious studies professor at California State-Sacramento, suggested that a rebound was possible following congressional election victories this month by several Democrats active in those moderate-to-liberal denominations.

The projections also address births. Muslim women gave birth to more babies than women in any other religion during 2020-2025, Pew estimated. About 252 million babies were born to Muslim mothers, compared with 208 million born to Christian mothers. Muslims are the only major religious group expected to have growing numbers of births in the decades ahead; other groups are projected to have steady or gradually declining numbers.

If recent trends continue, Pakistan is projected to have the largest Muslim population by 2070, followed by India, Nigeria and Indonesia. Indonesia currently has the most Muslims.

Gina Zurlo, a senior researcher at Harvard Divinity School specializing in religious demographics, said Christian-Muslim relations are “extremely important” in light of the projected changes. She said the “clash of civilization” theory still looms large in some religious communities and that there is a lot of misunderstanding of both religions from both sides. Zurlo also said, “Many Christians are not happy to see that Islam will be the world's largest religion and it's causing even more angst,” in an email.

Zurlo said, “I hope people seek opportunities for greater interreligious education.” She added, “Personal contact is important — when people have personal relationships across religious differences, it 'humanizes' the 'other' in fruitful ways.”

Alan Cooperman, Pew's director of religion research, noted that most of the projected proliferation of Muslims and Christians was occurring in countries where they already were the majority, with conversion playing only a minor role. He said, “I don't think this is a situation in which the growth of Muslims is coming at the expense of Christians,” and warned: “I do worry about mischief being made with headlines or narratives that take this finding and portray it as sort of a clash where Christians are losing to Muslims.”
