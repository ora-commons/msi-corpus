## First-time progressive nominees face test in competitive races

The Wall Street Journal published a video conversation with Democratic voters from across the country, exploring how they view their party's direction ahead of the November midterm elections.

The discussion captures a party divided over its direction. The Journal reports that Democratic voters are divided over what the party should stand for and which candidate is best equipped to defeat Republicans. The publication says it convened the voters to "talk about the party at this moment."

The video arrives as Democrats are positioned to make midterm gains, but the Journal attributes that trajectory to President Trump's unpopularity rather than to the Democratic Party's own image. "In fact, Democrats have received some of their lowest approval ratings in decades since Trump took office last year," the Journal reports.

The Journal reports that the party's far-left flank has been ascendant, with the Democratic Socialists of America and other progressive groups toppling longtime incumbents in some party primaries. For the first time, according to the Journal, several highly competitive Senate and House seats will have far-left candidates as the Democratic nominees, creating a test of progressive power in general election contests.

The Journal plans to publish a follow-up video discussion in the coming weeks with Gen Z men who voted for Trump in 2024, returning to a group it first spoke to a year ago.

Eliza Collins, a national political reporter based in Phoenix, and Denise Blostein, a senior video journalist, are credited with the piece.
