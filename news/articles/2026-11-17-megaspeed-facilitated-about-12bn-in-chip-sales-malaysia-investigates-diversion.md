## Committee chairs cited a potential breach of national security

The Guardian reported that Singapore-based Megaspeed International facilitated about $12bn in chip sales in violation of U.S. export controls. On Friday, Malaysia said it was investigating whether Megaspeed diverted advanced AI hardware to other countries.

The Guardian described Megaspeed as a Singapore-based cloud computing company and reported that it has bought nearly $5bn in AI chips and advanced hardware. The scale of those purchases placed Megaspeed at the center of the reported export-control violations and diversion inquiry.

A recent joint Department of Commerce and Department of Justice letter said Commerce, under Secretary Howard Lutnick, first started an investigation of Megaspeed in 2024 for potential export-control violations. The Justice Department, led by Attorney General Todd Blanche, took over in 2025.

Committee chairs Brian Mast, John Moolenaar and Rick Crawford said in a press release, “We look forward to hearing from Jensen Huang and Tim Teter on their roles in a potential breach of our national security.” Mast chairs the foreign affairs committee, Moolenaar chairs the select committee on the Chinese Communist Party, and Crawford chairs the intelligence committee of the House of Representatives.

The committee leaders also said, “The American people need answers on Mr Huang and Nvidia’s role in allowing dangerous [People’s Republic of China] actors to access our most advanced chips, as well as the role of a potential Chinese intelligence asset in this scandal.” On 12 November, the three chairmen had signed a letter warning Jensen Huang that he could face a subpoena if he did not agree to the closed-door meeting.

Against that backdrop, Nvidia's former chief executive Jensen Huang is scheduled to testify before several House committees on 1 December. Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter has agreed to a closed-door, transcribed interview on 2 December, according to The Guardian.

Alice Huang, also known as Huang Le, had been Megaspeed International's executive director. Megaspeed said Alice Huang stepped down in 2025. The Guardian reported that Jensen Huang stepped down as Nvidia's CEO after allegations of extramarital relationships with suspected Chinese spy Alice Huang and two Nvidia employees surfaced.

Nvidia chips are key to powering the artificial intelligence boom, The Guardian reported, and the Trump administration has prohibited the selling of advanced artificial intelligence chips to China.

In his first remarks since his resignation, Jensen Huang said on Thursday that he would be willing to return to Nvidia as CEO. The Guardian reported that Elon Musk and other Silicon Valley technology companies, along with 165,000 signatories to an open letter, have called for his reinstatement. Trump also signaled openness to Jensen Huang's return.
