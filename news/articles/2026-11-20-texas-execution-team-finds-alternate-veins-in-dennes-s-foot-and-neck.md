## Dennes exclaimed 'The pain!' before a physician pronounced him dead at 7:08 p.m.

Texas prison officials said the execution team had difficulty finding suitable veins for Reinaldo Dennes. Spokeswoman Amanda Hernandez said that after repeated unsuccessful attempts — she could not say how many — technicians found a vein in Dennes's left foot and another on the left side of his neck that were receptive to the intravenous needles.

Graczyk could not see the injections. Dennes's feet were covered by a sheet, and only the right side of his neck was visible to the witnesses.

Michael Graczyk and other witnesses reached the more-than-a-century-old Huntsville Unit of the Texas Department of Criminal Justice at 6 p.m. Thursday as a bell from a nearby church tolled the hour. They climbed the concrete steps and waited in a reception office, normally a very brief stop before the short trek to the death chamber to watch condemned killer Reinaldo Dennes die. A large Texas clock ticked off the moments, and the group had still not moved at about 6:20 p.m.

At about 6:20 p.m., Graczyk said one of two situations was likely: Dennes might have been resisting the execution team's attempts to insert a pair of intravenous needles into his arms, or the team might have been having difficulty finding a suitable vein for the needles.

Thirty minutes later, corrections officers led the witnesses into the chamber, at about 6:50 p.m. Dennes appeared docile, with his eyes closed and his head pointed toward the ceiling. His white prison garb was not disheveled, and no additional restraints were applied to his head, which Graczyk read as no evidence of a struggle.

Asked by the warden if he wanted to make a final statement, Dennes talked about being "extremely blessed" by the love of "the father." He hoped his family and loved ones would "get to know this great gift and great grace of God the father." He then told the warden: "Go."

Almost immediately as the procedure began, Dennes's demeanor changed. "The pain!" he exclaimed. "Go! Go!"

Graczyk wrote: "For nearly three minutes — and standing there furiously scribbling notes, I thought it seemed much longer — Dennes repeatedly moans."

Dennes cried, "Take me father! ... Take me into your arms! ... Take me father!" He occasionally interjected those cries with obscenities, a grunt and then groans. His right leg was "killing me," he said, likening the sensation to a cramp. At one point, he said, "I don't think this is normal."

At 7:08 p.m., after a physician examined him, Dennes was pronounced dead.

Graczyk said he had covered capital punishment for The Associated Press in Texas since 1983 and had witnessed nearly 500 lethal injections. He said the experience had made him accustomed to a certain routine in the procedure, although he remained reluctant to describe any execution as "routine." Over the years, he had seen condemned inmates speak foreign languages, pray, recite poetry, sing, curse, express love, insist on their innocence or acknowledge their crime and, in one case, tell a joke.

Graczyk wrote that Dennes was "like no other." He also said he had heard inmates say the lethal drug was hot or cold, that they could smell or taste it, or that it hurt. "No one I can recall went to such drama as Dennes," he wrote, adding: "On that, he was right."

Hernandez said the injection sites were in line with the prison system's procedures. The alternate sites had been used in previous executions, although the need to do so was relatively rare.

Hernandez also suggested Dennes may have been faking, saying he had complained about pain from areas where no injections were attempted. Graczyk said he wanted to find out the truth about the matter but that there was no way to know for sure.

Dennes himself was not available for further comment.
