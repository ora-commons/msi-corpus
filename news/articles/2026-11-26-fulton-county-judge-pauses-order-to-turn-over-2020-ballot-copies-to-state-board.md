## Georgia officials may investigate under protective terms limiting disclosure

On November 26, 2026, Kelly Lee Ellerbe, a superior court judge in Fulton County, Georgia, paused her own order to turn over electronic copies of 2020 election ballots seized by the FBI to Georgia’s state election board. Ellerbe acted after county election officials filed a notice of appeal, according to The Guardian.

The order at issue concerned electronic copies of the 2020 ballots seized during an FBI raid of the Fulton County elections office. Under Ellerbe’s protective order, the state election board may use the records for its investigations.

The order also bars disclosure of the records to anyone other than people identified in it. Those people include the board’s members and staff, as well as experts the board may consult.

Fulton County election officials filed a notice of appeal before Ellerbe paused her directive. The pause halted the transfer, while her protective terms continued to govern how the records could be used and disclosed.

The seizure forms part of the continuing dispute over the 2020 election results. Some Republicans, led by Donald Trump, claimed the outcome had been rigged, though Joe Biden was certified after several recounts.

Republican state officials refused to overturn the result. Trump had pressed Brad Raffensperger, Georgia’s secretary of state, in a phone call to “find 11,780 votes.”

Earlier in 2026, the FBI raided Fulton County’s elections office and seized 2020 ballots as part of an investigation into alleged “irregularities.” A photograph accompanying The Guardian’s report shows the FBI loading the Georgia general election 2020 ballots onto trucks at the Fulton County election hub in Union City, Georgia, on January 28.

A federal judge later ruled that the government did not have to return the ballots after rejecting Fulton County’s argument that the seizure was improper and unconstitutional.

The raid has also prompted questions about Tulsi Gabbard’s participation. Democrats have asked why the former director of national intelligence accompanied the FBI.

At a hearing in March, Virginia Senator Mark Warner, a Democrat, said he believed Gabbard’s involvement “potentially provide a pretext for the president’s unconstitutional efforts to seize control of the upcoming elections”.

The protective order permits the state election board to use the records for investigations but limits disclosure to identified board members, staff and experts. Ellerbe’s pause leaves the transfer itself unresolved and does not end the county’s appeal or the wider dispute over the 2020 result.
