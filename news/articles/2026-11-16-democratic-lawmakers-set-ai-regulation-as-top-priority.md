- Congressional Democrats plan to make regulating artificial intelligence a top priority when the new Congress begins in January, NPR reported.

## Sanders plans multiple AI bills; Clarke presses data rules

Regulating artificial intelligence is one of the big questions facing the new Democratic-led Congress beginning in January, according to NPR, which reported that lawmakers have ambitious plans but that the Trump administration may not like them.

President Trump has repeatedly said he wants the United States to beat China in the race to develop AI and that regulations could slow things down. At an event with tech CEOs in September, Trump said: "You're going to have a winner, or you're going to have a loser. We're leading now over China and everyone else, and I want to keep it that way."

Trump has rolled back Biden-era AI policies and has tried to stop states from making their own laws about AI. Yet there are concerns about the risks of increasingly powerful AI, NPR reported.

Sanders, a Vermont senator who caucuses with Democrats, told NPR he plans to introduce multiple AI bills next year. "People are worried that AI will take their jobs, invade their privacy, impact the mental well-being of their children, impact our democratic foundations," Sanders said. He also wants a ban on developing super-intelligent forms of AI.

Privacy is another issue on the agenda. Clarke said she wants rules on how people's data can be used by AI companies. "A lot of the challenges that we face is the invasive ways in which our data is being used without permission," Clarke said.

Clarke said she hopes some Republicans will sign on to the bills. "I'm just hoping that when the dust settles, we have enough open-minded and fair-minded colleagues on the other side of the aisle to make our efforts bipartisan," she said.

President Trump can veto any bills that Congress passes. Sanders has doubts about Trump's fitness as a leader, but he told NPR he is optimistic that Trump may still be persuadable when it comes to AI safety. "He is not stupid, and he actually is a good politician. And he changes his mind every day," Sanders said.

Sanders said the public wants AI safeguards, and that it is Congress' job to act.
