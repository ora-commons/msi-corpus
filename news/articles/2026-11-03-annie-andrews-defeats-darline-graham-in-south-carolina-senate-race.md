## Democrats gain a pickup after Trump won the state by nearly 18 points

Annie Andrews defeated Republican Sen. Darline Graham in South Carolina's Senate race, according to an Associated Press race call reported by NPR. The pediatrician's victory makes her the first woman elected to the Senate in South Carolina and the first Democrat elected from the state since 1998.

NPR described the outcome as a stunning upset in a state President Trump won by nearly 18 points in 2024. For Democrats, Andrews's win is a critical pickup in the Senate contest as the party seeks to regain control of the chamber.

Darline Graham had been appointed to the seat after the death of her brother, longtime Republican Sen. Lindsey Graham, in July. She was a key vote for Trump in recent months, fighting Democratic efforts to rein in Trump's war powers and supporting the confirmation of Todd Blanche as attorney general.

Republicans faced a tough political landscape in the final weeks before the midterms. NPR reported that Trump's approval ratings hit record lows, driven largely by opposition to the war in Iran, Trump's tariff policies and high energy prices.

Andrews put affordability at the center of her campaign, arguing that she would fight to end tax breaks for the wealthy, raise wages and push for affordable child care. The pediatrician also drew on her career and said her experience treating patients who faced financial barriers to health care was a major factor that pushed her into politics.

Andrews described what she encountered in her medical practice in an NPR interview last month: "I would diagnose kids with common chronic diseases like asthma and diabetes, and I found that while I would explain my treatment plan to the parents so often, they would say, 'Well, how much is that inhaler going to cost?' Or 'How much is that insulin going to cost?'"

She added: "No parent in America should have to be thinking about their pocketbook when they're hearing news that their child has a chronic illness."

The South Carolina result has direct consequences for Senate control. Democrats need to net four seats to win the majority, and NPR said a favorable political landscape boosted Democrats in battleground states such as North Carolina, where former Gov. Roy Cooper won another key Senate race.

The same environment expanded the competitive map to include redder states such as South Carolina, Kansas and Texas.
