## Bipartisan lawmakers push tighter U.S. restrictions on AI chip exports

Hackers began probing Railway, a cloud-computing company, on a Sunday night in mid-October, signing up for thousands of accounts and taking assets worth more than $1.1 million within days, according to The Wall Street Journal. The Morning Risk Report did not identify who carried out the theft.

The Wall Street Journal characterized the breach as a smash-and-grab theft of the artificial-intelligence era's most valuable commodity: computing power. Most of the haul did not come from cryptocurrency, the report said, and the incident involved neither extortion nor ransomware.

The report's other items covered proposed limits on where AI chips may be sent, lawsuits over speculative ticket resale, the White House's approach to European security talks and Russian diesel cargoes that have not produced new U.S. imports.

Lawmakers are demanding tighter restrictions on chip exports following Jensen Huang's affair with a business partner suspected of being a Chinese intelligence source, a push that The Wall Street Journal said threatens to cost Nvidia and other semiconductor companies billions of dollars in revenue. Top Republicans and Democrats said this week that they hope to include three proposed measures making it more difficult to send artificial-intelligence chips around the world in a stalled defense package. The measures have not been adopted, and the package in which lawmakers want them placed has stalled.

Venue operators say that at shows at smaller events, brokers on resale sites are marking up the prices for tickets even though they do not own them — a practice called speculative selling that is driving away customers who do not know that cheaper tickets are sitting at the box office, damaging the local businesses. Some independent venues have started fighting back: two recently sued StubHub, the largest independent ticket resale marketplace in the United States, for false advertising. StubHub is fighting the lawsuits, saying they are without merit. It has spent millions on lobbying to counter legislation aimed at curbing resale markups, even for small-venue tickets, and several states have passed laws restricting resale markups or the sale of tickets brokers do not own.

The briefing also flagged the volume of orders for penalties and other financial relief secured by the Securities and Exchange Commission in fiscal year 2026. The Trump administration is putting a longstanding Russian demand at the heart of its latest push to end the war in Ukraine: renegotiating pan-European security in ways the Kremlin hopes will give it more influence in the region. Meetings in Abu Dhabi involving American, Russian, Ukrainian and European negotiators were set for Tuesday and Wednesday. U.S. officials said work on a ceasefire in Ukraine must proceed alongside discussions about Europe's future security arrangements. The White House's approach is causing alarm in many European governments, which believe Russia's real goal is to rebuild a sphere of influence in Europe's east, giving it a say over other countries' defense and security policies.

President Trump's efforts to supply the United States with Russian diesel have yet to result in any new imports into the country, as several fuel tankers bound for America shifted course after facing difficulties finding buyers willing to accept the cargoes. Trump saw Russian diesel as a way to lower sky-high prices for the fuel, which contributed to rising consumer costs in the United States in the run-up to the midterm elections. The diverted ships show U.S. oil companies are reluctant to handle the cargoes because of the potential legal and reputational consequences, industry executives and lobbyists said.
