## Two accounts restored, but thousands of critic accounts remain restricted

The Indian Youth Congress, the youth wing of India's main opposition Congress party, posted its warning on X on Saturday, saying it would "storm your offices across the country and stage protests" unless the Instagram accounts of its leaders were restored. The statement followed a day of demonstrations in which its members clashed with police outside a building that houses Meta's offices in Hyderabad.

In Hyderabad on Friday, protesters carrying placards chanted slogans as they stormed into the building, smashing plants to the ground and clashing with police, according to footage released by the Indian Youth Congress's X account.

Two of the accounts were restored after Friday's protests, but thousands of accounts belonging to journalists, creators and influencers critical of the government remain restricted, said Manu Jain, who is in charge of social media for the youth group. Meta must explain how its algorithms restrict posts and must restore accounts taken down on government orders, he told Reuters on Saturday.

India is Meta's biggest market by users. The company declined to comment on Friday's demonstrations.

A video from Friday's demonstration showed protesters spray-painting anti-Meta slogans on walls outside the building that houses Meta's Mumbai office and clashing with security guards who tried to stop them. In Gurugram near New Delhi, Congress workers chanted slogans and held banners reading "restore youth Congress accounts," online posts showed.

The protests drew criticism from political opponents. An opposition politician from Telangana state criticized Congress workers over what he called vandalism at Meta's Hyderabad office, saying it had undermined the city's reputation as a safe destination for businesses. Pradeep Bhandari, a national spokesperson for Modi's ruling party, said on Saturday that Congress had "not only vandalised and destroyed Meta's office in Hyderabad" but was also "flaunting it on social media."

"This is Congress Model: Vandalise offices of Private companies," he wrote.

Debate is growing in India about potential risks to free speech from the increasingly strict efforts of Narendra Modi's government to remove social media content it objects to. India's IT ministry did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

Meta has said it restricts content in India to comply with local laws. Modi's government says it orders content to be taken down only if it breaches local laws, can harm public safety or national security.

Congress has long accused Meta and other social media platforms of curbing dissent online by removing accounts.

Reporters Without Borders has said the removal of journalistic content from social media has been on the rise in India this year, and free speech activists say a new government rule gives social media companies hardly any time to review and decide on takedown requests. In February, Modi's government ordered social media companies to take down unlawful content within three hours of being notified about it, tightening an earlier 36-hour timeline.

Meta disclosures show that it restricted more than 5.4m posts or accounts in India under local laws in the first half of 2026, second only to Indonesia and up from about 70,000 in the whole of 2025.

"The large majority of these items included instances where we were obligated to proactively restrict content … based on local law requirements," Meta said.
