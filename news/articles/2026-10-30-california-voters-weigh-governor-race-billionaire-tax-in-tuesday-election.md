## California voters weigh governor race, billionaire tax in Tuesday election

**Subtype:** fact

- California voters head to the polls Tuesday to elect a new governor, decide U.S. House races that could shift control of Congress, and weigh Proposition 40, the nation's first-ever tax on billionaires.
- Proposition 40 would impose a one-time 5% tax on individuals with net worth exceeding $1 billion, with revenue going toward replacing cuts in federal healthcare funding.
- Supporters of Proposition 40 include Sen. Bernie Sanders, Rep. Ro Khanna, SEIU-United Healthcare Workers West and the California Democratic Party; opponents include Gov. Gavin Newsom, the California Teachers Association, the state branch of Planned Parenthood and the California Republican Party.

## New congressional map could net Democrats five additional seats

California voters go to the polls Tuesday to choose a successor to Gov. Gavin Newsom and to decide a set of U.S. House races that could swing the balance of power in Congress. Also on the ballot is Proposition 40, which would create the nation's first-ever tax on billionaires. Polls close at 8 p.m. PT (11 p.m. ET), and California conducts elections predominantly by mail — about 90% of the vote in the 2022 gubernatorial election was cast early in person or by mail, according to the Associated Press.

The tax measure would authorize a one-time 5% tax on individuals with a net worth exceeding $1 billion. Revenue from the program would go toward replacing cuts in federal healthcare funding.

The issue has divided some traditional alliances and created new ones. Supporters include Sen. Bernie Sanders of Vermont, Rep. Ro Khanna of California, SEIU-United Healthcare Workers West and the California Democratic Party. Opponents include Democratic Gov. Gavin Newsom, the California Teachers Association, the state branch of Planned Parenthood and the California Republican Party. Opponents of the measure have far outspent supporters, according to AdImpact.

In the race for governor, Democrat Xavier Becerra and Republican Steve Hilton were the top vote-getters among 61 candidates in the June 2 all-party primary. Becerra is a former state attorney general who served as Health and Human Services secretary under President Joe Biden. Hilton is a former Fox News commentator who once served as a senior adviser to former Conservative Party British Prime Minister David Cameron. The winner will succeed Newsom, who is term-limited.

The election also carries the balance of the narrowly divided U.S. House, where Republicans hold a slight edge. A new congressional map favorable to Democrats has complicated the reelection bids of several Republican incumbents. Voters approved the boundaries in a November 2025 referendum, a response to President Donald Trump's effort in Texas and other states to boost his party's chances of keeping its House majority. The new districts could net Democrats an additional five seats.

In the 22nd Congressional District, Republican Rep. David Valadao seeks a seventh term against Democrat Randy Villegas. Valadao won his first three terms by comfortable margins until a narrow defeat in 2018, then won the seat back in 2020 and has held it for three more terms, although by much slimmer margins. The Associated Press identifies the race as one of the most competitive in the country.

Also competitive is the open-seat race in the 48th Congressional District to replace Republican Rep. Darrell Issa, who is retiring rather than seek a 13th term in a redrawn district. Republicans are guaranteed to hold the 40th Congressional District, but the matchup between Reps. Ken Calvert and Young Kim will cost the party at least one incumbent.

The Trump administration has sued California election officials to block the processing of 20,000 ballots it alleges were cast by noncitizens until their citizenship can be verified. A district court judge rejected that request on Tuesday, then on Thursday appointed a special master to evaluate the administration's claims. The Justice Department issued subpoenas to election officials on Thursday.

Once reliably Republican in the Eisenhower, Nixon and Reagan years, California has not elected a Republican to statewide federal office since 1988. The party has fared better in gubernatorial elections, winning six of the last 12 — including one recall election — but Democrats have held the seat for the last 16 years.

In statewide elections, Democrats tend to dominate in the heavily populated Los Angeles and San Francisco areas while also winning by comfortable margins in the Sacramento and San Diego areas and along the Pacific Coast. Republicans tend to perform best in Northern California and the Central Valley. The Inland Empire to the south is more competitive: Trump narrowly carried the area in the 2024 presidential election, while Joe Biden and Hillary Clinton won there in 2020 and 2016.

All California counties are expected to release at least some or most of their mail voting results in the first vote report of the night, almost always before any in-person Election Day results are released. Roughly two of every five counties are also expected to release at least some early in-person results in that first report. Because mail voting tends to favor Democrats and in-person Election Day voting tends to favor Republicans, the release of mail results at the start of the night could produce an early lead for Democratic candidates, while Republican candidates may narrow the gap as more Election Day results are counted.

The AP does not make projections and will declare a winner only when it determines that no scenario would allow a trailing candidate to close the gap. In the 2024 general election, the AP first reported results at 11:12 p.m. ET, 12 minutes after polls closed; the count reached the halfway mark at 12:56 a.m. ET; and the last vote update of the night came at 4:24 a.m. ET Wednesday with about 62% of total votes counted. About 11 million votes were cast in the 2022 gubernatorial election. There are no automatic recounts in California; any registered voter may request and pay for a recount.
