## Maryland's carry restrictions stay in place as AR-15 arguments approach

The Supreme Court on Monday declined to take up a challenge to a Maryland law that limits where people may carry concealed guns, according to the Associated Press.

The Maryland measure prohibits carrying concealed weapons into schools, mass transit, bars and other areas the state treats as sensitive places. The court turned the case away without comment.

Justice Clarence Thomas wrote that he is skeptical about the Maryland law and restrictions like it. Thomas said the justices should take the question up again in a "more narrowly focused case."

The refusal arrives while the court is preparing to hear arguments in disputes over state prohibitions on AR-15s and similar semiautomatic firearms — measures often called assault weapons bans.

The court's conservative majority has handed down several decisions backing Second Amendment rights, including a recent ruling striking down wide-ranging gun-carry restrictions in Hawaii.

The justices have also upheld some gun restrictions, among them a law aimed at protecting victims of domestic violence.
