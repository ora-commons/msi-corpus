## Investors expect a wave of other AI IPOs in the coming year

Anthropic finally completed its highly anticipated initial public offering this week, raising $81 billion in the largest offering of all time, The Wall Street Journal's Technology newsletter reported. The Claude developer was the first artificial-intelligence lab to go public, and the newsletter said the IPO lived up to the hype regardless of the volatility that may ensue.

The offering gave Anthropic the firepower to continue funding its audacious plans and delivered major windfalls to employees and early investors, according to the newsletter.

Big questions still loom about the AI trade, the newsletter said, including whether the artificial-intelligence giant's stock can stay above water and how it will fend off competitive threats, from OpenAI to Chinese open-source AI models.

Inside the company, the windfall was broad. More than 5,000 Anthropic employees became millionaires on paper, according to an estimate from Hill.com, a marketplace for private-company shares.

Outside investors did well too. Backers like Lightspeed Venture Partners and Iconiq were sitting on stakes worth more than $25 billion at the IPO price, the newsletter reported.

Some of that capital is sure to recycle back into the AI ecosystem, giving rise to a new generation of stars and, if investors are lucky, helping create the next Anthropic, according to the newsletter.

Depending on how the stock performs, investors also expect a wave of other AI IPOs in the coming year, the newsletter said.

The offering might also push further the sky-high valuations among Silicon Valley startups whose revenue, like Anthropic's, has been growing at an extraordinary pace. "That means a lot of money will get thrown at the wall, too," the newsletter said.

Jai Das, a co-founder of Sapphire Ventures, put the risk plainly: "The Valley is probably going to over-rotate and waste some capital."

The competitive picture has shifted as well. Until this year, OpenAI led Anthropic handily in income, according to the newsletter; Anthropic now has a revenue edge and fewer losses than its older rival.


Anthropic [closed its first trading session at $97 a share](/articles/2026-11-19-anthropic-closes-ipo-debut-at-97-after-nearing-2-trillion/) on Thursday, which leaves the questions the newsletter raises turning on what the stock does from here.
