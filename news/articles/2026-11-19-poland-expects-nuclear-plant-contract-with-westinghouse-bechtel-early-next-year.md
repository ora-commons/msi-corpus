## Three AP1000 reactors would provide around 10% of Poland's electricity

Poland expects to sign a deal with an American consortium early next year to build the country's first nuclear power plant. State-owned Polskie Elektrownie Jadrowe is in the final stages of negotiations with Westinghouse Electric and Bechtel Group Inc. over an engineering, procurement and construction contract for the roughly $50 billion project. Marek Woszczyk, president of PEJ's management board, discussed the negotiations on the sidelines of the Partnership for Transatlantic Energy Cooperation conference in Poland.

Woszczyk said signing by the end of the first quarter of next year looks most likely. The three parties had previously said they hoped to sign the contract by the end of the year. A delayed signing will not hold up the project's timeline, he said.

The project consists of three Westinghouse AP1000 reactors with a total capacity of 3,750 megawatts electric. The Westinghouse-Bechtel consortium will be responsible for constructing the facility, which is located on the banks of the Baltic Sea in Pomerania.

The first reactor is due online in 2036, with reactors two and three scheduled for 2037 and 2038, respectively. PEJ expects electricity from the plant to be supplied to the Polish grid starting in 2036. Woszczyk said the reactors would provide around 10% of Poland's electricity under current forecasts. Renewable energy and natural gas would supplement nuclear generation as coal power plants are phased out.

Woszczyk said bolstering Poland's energy security and minimizing dependence on other suppliers were at the heart of the country's nuclear program. PEJ plans to buy nuclear fuel from several suppliers to avoid reliance on a single source and store fuel on site to minimize potential supply disruptions. He said, “Poland wants to be as independent as possible when it comes to energy.”

Poland historically depended on neighboring Russia for energy, but it moved away from that relationship after the invasion of Ukraine in 2022. The country is forging an energy mix underpinned by LNG and nuclear technology from the U.S. Polish Energy Minister Milosz Motyka said at the conference, “There is no going back to business with Russia.” He added, “Russia used energy sources like a weapon.”

Woszczyk said it was no surprise that American technology and providers had been selected because the AP1000 is proven technology and Poland wants to reinforce its ties to the U.S. The Trump administration has made energy dominance a key part of its foreign policy and has pushed European allies to buy U.S.-extracted hydrocarbons. Poland has been one of the most prolific signers of long-term deals.

Discussions with Poland on nuclear energy cooperation began during President Trump's first term and continued under the Biden administration. The U.S. Energy Department considers Poland's decision to buy U.S. nuclear technology a model that could be replicated in neighboring countries.

Caspar Hobhouse, a research analyst at the European Union Institute for Security Studies, said the project puts Westinghouse and the U.S. firmly at the forefront of Europe's nuclear renaissance. Woszczyk said PEJ has plans for additional nuclear plants and is in talks with French, Canadian and U.S. providers about another power plant elsewhere in Poland.
