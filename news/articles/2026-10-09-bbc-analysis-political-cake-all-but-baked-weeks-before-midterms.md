## Russian diesel deal's electoral impact uncertain as voting begins

A BBC News analysis published Friday concludes that "the political cake, as the saying goes, is all but baked" in the weeks before the November midterm elections, with President Donald Trump's newly announced Russian diesel deal unlikely to shift voter sentiment before Election Day.

The analysis, by North America correspondent Anthony Zurcher and titled "Analysis: Trump's shock Russia deal highlights mounting pressure to curb fuel prices," calculates that Russia's promised 300,000 tons of diesel this month would cover roughly half of one day of US diesel consumption, which runs approximately 3.6 million barrels per day. "Americans may not notice much of an immediate difference," the BBC reports.

The arrangement with Russian President Vladimir Putin represents a "remarkable about-face" from legislation Trump signed just three weeks earlier authorizing fresh American sanctions and tariffs on nations importing Russian oil and gas, the BBC reports. Trump announced the deal Thursday in posts on his Truth Social platform, saying Russia had agreed to supply "hundreds of thousands of tons of diesel fuel" to the US and global markets in the coming months.

"Lower prices for Americans, especially our Great Farmers, Ranchers, and Truckers, is my Greatest Priority," Trump wrote in his post.

The BBC notes that "Trump's Truth Social posts often provide incomplete information," and that the details of how Russia will supply the fuel and what Russia might receive in return beyond "much-needed hard currency" remain unclear.

The deal comes as Trump continues to grapple with the political fallout of the Iran war and its impact on global energy prices. As the cost of fuel has "skyrocketed" in America, Trump's standing with the public — "stung by the higher prices at the pump and knock-on effects that led to across-the-board inflation" — has soured, according to the BBC analysis.

The toll has been felt most acutely in US agricultural states, which rely heavily on diesel to operate farming machinery and to transport their products to market. Iowa, Kansas, Texas, and Ohio have become "unexpected political battlegrounds" in the midterm elections that will determine whether Republicans maintain control of Congress, the BBC reports.

For months, Trump has searched for ways to ease his party's political pain. He has pressured European allies to further tap their oil reserves, deferred federal taxes on the fuel, and authorized supplies designated for farm use to be used in interstate trucking. He has also promised he would not renew attacks on Iran until after the midterms — what the BBC characterized as an "unusually frank acknowledgement" of the political impact of the ongoing conflict.

"His decision to strike a deal with Russia's Putin is only the latest, most dramatic effort," the BBC observes — but the practical effect of the deal on American fuel prices remains far from clear. Russia's diesel refinery capacity has been damaged by repeated Ukrainian strikes — attacks Trump has encouraged Zelensky to curtail — and there is no public information on how quickly Russian fuel could enter the global market. A promised European diesel release has not moved energy prices significantly, the analysis notes.

The deal opens Trump to criticism from European allies and American politicians that his administration is now helping to fund the Russian war machine. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky called the deal a "gift" to Putin that Russia would "repay" with "terror and perfidy." "Allowing Russia to sell petroleum products is an investment in a war that must be ended, not prolonged," Zelensky wrote on X.

"It is also uncertain exactly how much Trump's move will help Republicans at the ballot box," the BBC analysis adds.

Over the past seven months, prices on energy, fuel, and consumer goods have only gone up, the analysis observes, even as Trump and his officials have repeatedly assured an uneasy public that a successful resolution to the war — and an easing of its economic impact — is "nearly at hand." Trump may be left hoping that his latest deal, "setting aside its potentially uncomfortable diplomatic issues," will be enough to convince American voters that his promise of impending economic relief is more than "just empty words," the BBC concludes.
