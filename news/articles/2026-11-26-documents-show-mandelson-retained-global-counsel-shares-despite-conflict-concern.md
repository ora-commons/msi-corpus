## Robbins's email raised the possibility of a 'light touch' process for Mandelson

Newly released documents show that Lord Mandelson retained shares in Global Counsel, the lobbying company he co-founded, while serving as ambassador to the United States. Civil servants raised concerns that keeping the stake risked a perceived conflict of interest, yet Mandelson still held the shares six months after taking the post. The 85-page release is the third since Members of Parliament voted to publish documents about his appointment.

Divestiture was not a condition of the appointment. The documents say the government wanted Mandelson in Washington quickly and show an email from Sir Olly Robbins saying a process to police Mandelson's conflicts of interest could be "up and running in a light touch way." Selling shares in a private company like Global Counsel required finding a new buyer or selling them back to the company.

Global Counsel offered to buy the shares back, but the documents say its offer was not the "fair value". Another attempt to sell to a different buyer fell through. Robbins later confirmed that the shares had not been divested as planned and wrote in an updated memo to Lammy that "the other party has not been able to execute this transaction". Global Counsel then expected to "make an offer in September". Mandelson remained a shareholder until February 2026, the same month Global Counsel went into administration.

The files also record Mandelson's contacts with Global Counsel while he served as ambassador. He tried to arrange a meeting between Global Counsel and a key Donald Trump adviser and sought the firm's advice on a speech about Britain's role in the world. In August 2025, he asked a Global Counsel employee for feedback before a speech on Britain's role "in the age of Trump".

An unnamed Global Counsel director advised Mandelson to use "warmer language" about China and tone down "parts of your apologia of the Trump administration". The director warned: "Scepticism of [the Trump administration] is now so deep – at least in the public policy-interested world outside government – that it would be counterproductive/unpersuasive if you came across as having drunk the Kool Aid." Mandelson responded that he would water down his language.

In another email, Mandelson tried to arrange an "under the radar" lunch meeting about China and trade between a Global Counsel employee and someone from the US President's Council of Economic Advisers. Emails about his dealings with Washington counterparts also show a close relationship. In April 2025, an unnamed White House official told Mandelson that his "Prince of Darkness" label could not be "farther from the truth."

The latest release comes against the background of Mandelson's dismissal from the ambassador post last year. Starmer sacked him after revelations about the extent of his relationship with the late convicted sex offender Jeffrey Epstein. Starmer also sacked Robbins, then head of the Foreign Office, after it emerged that Mandelson had been granted clearance despite concerns raised by vetting officials. The papers had previously been withheld because of the Metropolitan Police investigation into allegations that Mandelson had passed market-sensitive government information to Epstein.

Mandelson has not commented on the latest release. He has repeatedly said he believes he did not act criminally, did not act for personal gain and is cooperating with police. No charges have been made. A Metropolitan Police spokesperson said: "Our investigation into alleged misconduct in public office is ongoing."

The documents also record texts Mandelson sent to Starmer's then chief of staff Morgan McSweeney in September 2025, offering advice on a cabinet reshuffle. Mandelson suggested moving Darren Jones to lead the science and technology department to "solve your Darren issue", but McSweeney did not respond until after the reshuffle decisions had been made. Jones was given a newly created role as chief secretary to the prime minister.

Prime Minister Andy Burnham used the release to question whether the Westminster and Whitehall system had changed enough. Speaking in the Downing Street garden, he said, "I don't think we have yet seen the change that this system down here needs." Burnham added that there is "a lot further to go until people can have confidence that the Westminster and Whitehall system is run on the way that they think it should be run, and that vested interests aren't running large within the system."

The third release now details his continued Global Counsel ownership, the unsuccessful efforts to divest his shares and Robbins's suggestion that a conflicts process could run in a "light touch" way.
