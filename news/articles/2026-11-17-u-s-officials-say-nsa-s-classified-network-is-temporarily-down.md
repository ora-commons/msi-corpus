## Person familiar points to software patching during the outage

A classified internal communications network at the National Security Agency was temporarily down Tuesday, according to U.S. officials. The affected system was NSANet.

A person familiar with the incident said the NSA was updating software to patch the system. The person said NSANet would be offline only temporarily and that there was no evidence of nefarious activity or a cyberattack.

The Wall Street Journal described the incident as the latest in a string of digital systems that have temporarily gone down. CNN had reported the outage earlier.

The outage came against a backdrop of recent disruptions. A service interruption to Microsoft 365 was one of the issues, along with a technical issue that caused a nationwide groundstop for flights.

Shortly before the NSANet outage was disclosed, Mullin spoke with CNBC to calm fears that the earlier incidents were likely part of an attack on the United States.

Mullin said in the CNBC interview, “I can tell you with complete certainty that we are not under attack. There’s no cyber threat that’s taking place. The homeland is very secure.”

Beth Sanner, a former senior U.S. intelligence official, said the firewalled system made an attack unlikely. “This system is firewalled, and by the most competent cyber agency in the world,” Sanner said. “A cyberattack is the least likely scenario, and outside our current context, this probably would not be reported.”

U.S. officials reported that the network was down, the person familiar with the incident supplied the software-patch explanation, and Mullin and Sanner assessed the threat. The NSA had not returned a request for comment.
