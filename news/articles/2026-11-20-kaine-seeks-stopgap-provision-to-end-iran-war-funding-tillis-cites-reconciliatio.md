## Dec. 11 deadline puts Senate vote leverage at issue

Virginia Sen. Tim Kaine aims to include a provision to end war funding in the stopgap bill. Congress must pass a funding measure by Dec. 11 to avoid a shutdown. Kaine's proposal would make the Iran war-funding dispute part of that measure.

N.C. Republican Sen. Thom Tillis told NPR reporter Claudia Grisales that his party may need to explore alternatives if Democrats push for a shutdown. He said one possibility is using reconciliation to pass legislation without Democratic votes.

NPR reporter Claudia Grisales said Democrats hold leverage because their votes are essential for the Republican-controlled Senate to reach the 60-vote threshold. Their leverage therefore extends to whether Congress can clear the government-funding measure by the Dec. 11 deadline.

NPR reported that Democrats who won this month's election are looking to use their influence in Congress to block funding for the war in Iran before officially taking charge of Congress.

Senate Democrats argue they should use their authority to end what they call an illegal war. Kaine's stopgap-bill provision is a specific mechanism for advancing that position through the funding process.

NPR also reported that President Trump launched attacks last winter without congressional approval, breaking from the tradition of seeking bipartisan support for wars. The funding dispute follows that launch as Democrats prepare to take charge of Congress.

Republicans counter that cutting war funding would jeopardize service members in the region. That response sets out the service-member risk they cite in opposing the change Kaine seeks to place in the stopgap bill.

The dispute places Democratic demands to end war funding against Republican concerns about service members and the government's continued operation. The funding dispute also brings congressional approval for the attacks into the budget process Congress must use to avert a shutdown.
