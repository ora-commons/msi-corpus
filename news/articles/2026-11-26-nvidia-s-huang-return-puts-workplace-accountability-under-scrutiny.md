## Government pressure and corporate culture frame Huang's reinstatement

Nvidia's 10-member board unanimously reinstated CEO Jensen Huang on Monday after President Donald Trump and Huang's technology allies pressed for his return, despite allegations of sexual misconduct and Huang's acknowledgment that he violated Nvidia's code of conduct. The return has renewed questions about whether workplace rules can restrain senior executives and whether a board can remain independent when a prominent leader faces personal-conduct concerns.

Huang resigned from Nvidia after he admitted to an extramarital affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy and after reports that he was involved with a second woman who was his subordinate. Huang acknowledged violating the company's code of conduct, which warns against personal relationships that may create conflicts of interest. His return followed what The Associated Press described as an extraordinary turnaround after a pressure campaign by his technology allies and President Donald Trump.

Just over a year earlier, the chief executive officer of an Ohio tech company was forced to resign after video showed him embracing an employee at a Coldplay concert. The CEO of McDonald's was among several executives pushed out over a consensual relationship at the height of the #MeToo movement. Those cases formed the backdrop for current questions about whether corporate power changes the standards applied to senior executives.

The board said it had received assurances from the U.S. government that Huang was not a national security concern. The board's statement did not mention the sexual misconduct allegations at the center of the scandal.

The Associated Press report noted that Trump has a history of marital infidelity and has said allegations of sexual assault against him are false. Trump predicted the scandal would help Huang's reputation and declared, "Sex sells, fellas," in a CNBC interview. Trump also posted a doctored image of Huang raising a red bra in triumph, parodying a famous photograph of Trump moments after he survived a 2024 assassination attempt in Butler, Pennsylvania. The White House did not immediately respond to an Associated Press request for comment Wednesday.

Cheyenne Hunt, an attorney who founded Reckoning Action after her role in bringing to light the sexual-assault allegations that brought down Democratic Sen. Eric Swalwell and Maine Senate candidate Graham Platner, said Trump's response reinforced the impression "that there is a class of men who see violence against women, coercion of women as a currency of power." Hunt added, "This sends a horrible message to men everywhere that it's open season on their direct reports."

Randall Peterson, a professor of organizational behavior at the London Business School, said the #MeToo movement had reinforced the idea that company leaders who engage in relationships with subordinates "are in serious trouble because you cannot rule out coercion. You just cannot." He said the message now is that "you are at risk, as in the days before #MeToo." Peterson said, "I'm sad about that," and added, "I thought we had moved on from that."

At a livestreamed meeting reviewed by The New York Times, Huang apologized to employees a day after returning to Nvidia, saying he had made mistakes and was in daily therapy. He warned that his reinstatement did not mean other employees could violate the code, adding that "sometimes, it's appropriate to forgive" and "sometimes it's not." Huang also addressed The Times report about his management style, which included publicly berating and threatening violence against employees. He acknowledged he had sometimes gone too far and said Nvidia's human resources department would collect feedback about his conduct.

Ann Lipton, a University of Colorado Boulder law professor who specializes in corporate and securities law, said the saga had "made very clear that Huang is untouchable." Lipton said it suggests that "if you've had a certain amount of success, then you should be able to get away with pretty much whatever you want." She said that concern was especially true in the male-dominated tech industry, citing Meta founder Mark Zuckerberg's comment last year that corporate culture needed more "masculine energy."

The controversy has brought scrutiny to the independence of Nvidia's 10-member board and a corporate culture centered around hero-worshiping its celebrity CEO. Thousands of people, including former and current employees, signed a petition calling for Huang's return. Nvidia's collapsing stock fueled fears that the AI bubble would pop, while Huang supporters argued that his vision, connections and deal-making skills were indispensable to the entire industry. Nvidia's shares rocketed up 31.5% upon his reinstatement Monday, although they had since fallen, and employees chanted Huang's name as he returned to headquarters.

Freada Kapor Klein, an investor and activist in equity and access issues around technology, said Nvidia and other successful tech startups "become cult-like. And people get addicted almost to being part of this inside powerful club." Klein said America has a history of industries idolizing big names such as Henry Ford and Andrew Carnegie, but technology adds to that by "glorifying geniuses. And so there is something extra special about technological prowess, more than or on top of just business success."

Women make up 21% of Nvidia's global workforce and an even smaller share of its managers and leaders, according to the company's latest sustainability report. The report touts Nvidia's efforts to achieve gender pay equality, employee resource groups for women and other groups, and family-supporting policies such as generous paid parental leave. Nvidia's code of conduct says employees "must report" suspected violations and offers extensive guidelines and assurances about protection against retaliation.

Fatima Goss Graves, president and CEO of the National Women's Law Center, said Huang's return was the kind of outcome that silences victims of sexual misconduct. Goss Graves said the legacy of the #MeToo movement continues to inspire women to speak out, and that public scandals such as Nvidia's sometimes provoke outrage leading to an uptick in complaints to Time's Up and other organizations. The National Women's Law Center runs the Time's Up Legal Defense Fund, a program supporting workers facing sexual harassment.

Goss Graves noted that on the same day Huang was reinstated, Democratic Rep. Jimmy Gomez of California resigned from Congress after the House Ethics Committee released a report finding "substantial evidence" that Gomez made unwelcome sexual advances toward a House staffer and subjected the staffer to unwanted sexual contact. The Associated Press described Gomez as the latest in a string of lawmakers or candidates brought down by misconduct allegations in recent months. Trump's role in Huang's return drew immediate bipartisan fire, and Huang was expected to testify next week before the Senate Banking Committee. "What comes next really matters," Goss Graves said. "If everybody shrugs their shoulders and says some people are too important to be held accountable, then we will have a problem." Nvidia did not respond to requests for comment.
