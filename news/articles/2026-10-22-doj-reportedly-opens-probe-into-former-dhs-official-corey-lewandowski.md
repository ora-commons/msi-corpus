## DHS inspector general's office does not confirm the inquiry

The Justice Department reportedly opened an investigation into allegations that former Department of Homeland Security official Corey Lewandowski profited from his position. The New York Times, Wall Street Journal and CNN cited unnamed people with knowledge of the situation in reporting the development.

The reported investigation follows a criminal referral by the DHS inspector general for Lewandowski. Lewandowski served as former DHS Secretary Kristi Noem's de facto chief of staff.

Lewandowski and Noem were ousted from DHS in March after disclosures about infighting and spending at the agency.

According to the Wall Street Journal, Lewandowski appeared to use his influence at DHS for personal gain. The newspaper reported last month that this included asking governments in the Middle East to hire him on the side to help with various matters.

The Wall Street Journal also reported that either Lewandowski or close associates approved nearly all DHS spending decisions involving sums of more than $100,000.

That spending authority gave Lewandowski insight into the contractors DHS used and the power to hold up contracts, the Wall Street Journal reported.

Adam Trigg, Lewandowski's attorney, said DOJ had not contacted Lewandowski regarding any investigation. Trigg said, “He denies any wrongdoing when he was proudly serving his country in a voluntary capacity.”

The DHS inspector general's office told the Wall Street Journal that it does not discuss specific investigative matters. The office also said it does not confirm or deny whether an investigation is underway.
