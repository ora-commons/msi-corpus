## Nomura says U.S. chip controls may constrain Tencent’s AI expansion

Tencent Holdings reported third-quarter net profit of 63.61 billion yuan ($9.54 billion), up 0.8%, as capital expenditure surged more than sixfold to 81.43 billion yuan. Revenue rose 8% to 207.34 billion yuan, below expectations, while the Shenzhen-based videogame and social-media company stepped up chip procurement to advance foundational models and AI agent applications.

Tencent said Wednesday that net profit edged 0.8% higher from a year earlier, while analysts had expected a decline. Adjusted net profit fell 6% to 70.55 billion yuan, roughly in line with market consensus. Revenue from value-added services, including Tencent’s gaming business, rose 3%. Domestic games revenue increased 11%, while international games revenue fell 9% because of a high base.

The capital outlay left Tencent with negative free cash flow in the three months ended September. Capital expenditure reached 81.43 billion yuan, more than six times the year-earlier level, as the company increased chip procurement this year to advance foundational models and various AI agent applications. The earnings were the latest in a string of mixed results for the Shenzhen-based company, which ranks among China’s largest by market capitalization.

Macquarie analysts said ahead of the earnings that prepayments for AI chips and components would remain a near-term cost drag. U.S. efforts this week to close loopholes and deny China access to advanced AI computing, including Nvidia chips, could snarl Tencent’s efforts to expand its AI chip inventory. Shares of Chinese internet companies and AI labs, including Tencent, fell sharply on Tuesday after the U.S. tightened chip export controls.

“Over time, this could constrain its ability to sustain the current pace of AI infrastructure investment and potentially drag down its pace of model development and rollout of new AI applications,” Nomura analysts said in a note.

Tencent has yet to demonstrate an AI product as compelling as those from domestic rivals ByteDance and Alibaba, known for the Doubao and Qwen large language models, respectively. After a major organizational overhaul last year, the company accelerated development of its foundational AI models.

Tencent officially launched Hy4 in November, adding visual-understanding capabilities and improving instruction following and long-context reasoning. Analysts said the upgrade was an important step toward closing the gap with frontier open-source AI models and strengthening foundations for agentic productivity across Tencent’s cloud, workplace and consumer products.

Tencent has also started piloting an AI agent embedded in Weixin, the superapp that serves 1.45 billion users. Over time, the tool could help users automate tasks such as ordering food delivery or booking a ride by tapping millions of mini apps in the company’s ecosystem.

Another product in Tencent’s AI portfolio is WorkBuddy, a desktop agent capable of independently executing workflows. The company said WorkBuddy had become China’s most widely used AI product by large language model token consumption.

Tencent’s latest results pair sharply higher capital spending with slower revenue growth and a 6% decline in adjusted net profit. As the company rolls out models and agents, Nomura analysts said U.S. chip controls could slow infrastructure investment, model development and new AI applications.
