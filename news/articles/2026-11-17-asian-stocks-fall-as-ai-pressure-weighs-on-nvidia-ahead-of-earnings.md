## Treasury yields ease while oil prices edge lower

Asian stock indexes fell across much of the region Tuesday after declines in AI-related shares on Wall Street ahead of Nvidia's quarterly results. South Korea's Kospi lost 1%, Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 1.2% and Hong Kong's Hang Seng dropped 1.2%. U.S. futures were mixed, Treasury yields eased and oil prices edged lower.

South Korea's Kospi declined 1% to 5,051.93. Memory chipmaker Samsung Electronics fell 2.4%, while SK Hynix rose 0.7%. The Seoul benchmark had fallen more than 10% over the past month.

Japan's Nikkei 225 lost 1.2% to 58,956.67. Shares of OpenAI investor SoftBank Group tumbled 6.2%, while testing equipment maker Advantest slumped 4.3%. In Hong Kong, the Hang Seng sank 1.2% to 23,458.87, and the Shanghai Composite index was 0.8% lower to 23,458.87. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 fell 1.5% to 8,907.50, and Taiwan's Taiex slid 0.9%.

Regional leaders were gathering for the ASEAN summit in Manila, Philippines, and the APEC summit in Shenzhen, China, this week. Investors were watching for signs of greater cross-border cooperation and progress on trade agreements amid heightened geopolitical tensions.

On Monday, the S&P 500 index was 1.9% lower and the Nasdaq composite index lost 2.5%. The Dow also fell 1.4% as losses broadened into other sectors. Nvidia sank 4.1% and was again the heaviest weight on Wall Street. The company has lost $735 billion in market value since disclosures of CEO Jensen Huang's extramarital affairs and his ties to an alleged Chinese spy.

Nvidia's chips power much of the world's move into AI technology, and its cash has helped companies across the industry grow. Huang's resignation as Nvidia's chief executive is the latest challenge for the AI industry, which has faced criticism that its stock prices were in a bubble. The industry is also worried that Democrats empowered by victories in the U.S. midterm elections may favor restrictions on further construction of AI data centers.

“The market was already worried about this risk before what happened at Nvidia,” Zhiwei Zhang, president and chief economist at Pinpoint Asset Management, said in a commentary. “Now the risk has become even more serious.”

The Trump administration on Monday issued new rules to restrict remote access to advanced U.S. computing chips and servers. Other chip companies also fell as the AI sector remained under pressure. They had climbed during the earlier days of Nvidia's decline on hopes that they could gain a competitive advantage.

Nvidia was scheduled to report its latest quarterly results Tuesday, and interim CEO Colette Kress was set to speak to Wall Street. Investors have become accustomed to Nvidia reporting stronger results than analysts expected each quarter and providing an upcoming revenue forecast well above expectations.

L3Harris Technologies fell 3.9% after officials in President Donald Trump's administration suggested the contractor for the Federal Aviation Administration might be at fault for Friday's nationwide air traffic ground stop. L3Harris said Saturday that it was supporting the FAA's investigation.

The swings in AI stocks have overshadowed another strong earnings-reporting season for U.S. companies. Large businesses in the S&P 500 were on track to deliver summer earnings per share nearly 32% higher than a year earlier, according to FactSet.

Treasury yields eased after last week's jump following a worse-than-expected U.S. inflation report. The yield on the 10-year Treasury fell to 5.13% from 5.15% late Friday, though it remained well above the 4.80% low reached earlier in the month. Higher yields make borrowing more expensive and have hurt the housing industry through expensive mortgage rates.

In early Tuesday trading, Brent crude, the international standard, edged 0.8% lower to $91.66 a barrel. U.S. benchmark crude oil slid 0.8% to $85.01 per barrel. The U.S. dollar fell to 159.35 Japanese yen from 159.52 yen, and the euro rose to 1.1003 from 1.1001.
