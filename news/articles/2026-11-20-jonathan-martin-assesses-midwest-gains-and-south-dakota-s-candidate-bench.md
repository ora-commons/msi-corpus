## South Dakota Democrats gained four legislative seats, all in the Sioux Falls area

South Dakota Democrats gained four seats in the Legislature during the 2026 midterms, increasing their total from eight to 12, with all four gains occurring in the Sioux Falls area. Democrats saw hope in the results, but Politico’s Jonathan Martin told reporters in Sioux Falls that the problem for the party in the state was that “the Democratic bench is thin here.”

Martin, Politico’s politics bureau chief and senior political columnist, spoke to reporters before a lecture at Augustana University’s Center for Western Studies. The talk drew a standing-room-only crowd and opened the Lost Region Recovery Project Conference, a gathering devoted to Midwestern studies that continues through Saturday.

Democrats won major victories across much of the Midwest, including in Ohio, Michigan, Wisconsin, Iowa and Kansas. Martin described the blue wave in South Dakota as a ripple during the midterm elections. He said national results and gains elsewhere in the Midwest showed the red-and-blue map less fixed than it once seemed. “What we’re looking at now is a Midwest that is less red than maybe we assumed,” he said.

The Democratic Party had not held a statewide office in South Dakota since U.S. Sen. Tim Johnson’s career ended when he did not seek reelection in 2014. The share of voters registered as Democrats dropped from 34% in November 2014 to 22% in this year’s midterm elections.

Martin described the party’s situation as a self-reinforcing cycle. “You just lost a lot of institutional memory and then the down ballot losses get worse every two years because it’s a self-reinforcing problem, right? It’s a vicious cycle,” Martin told reporters. “You lose down the ballot, you lose your bench — and so there’s nobody to even run of any stature, statewide.”

That cycle feeds on itself until it’s disrupted, Martin said. He said the party must recruit business people and non-politicians willing to “put their neck out there” and nominate candidates who will challenge the national party’s “orthodoxy” to fit South Dakotans’ needs. “And then guess what?” Martin said. “Luck and timing.”

Martin said some Democrats hurt themselves by deferring to independent U.S. Senate candidates in Idaho, South Dakota, Montana and Nebraska. All four of those independents lost to Republicans. In South Dakota, Brian Bengs ran as an independent against Republican Sen. Mike Rounds this year after running unsuccessfully as a Democrat against Republican Sen. John Thune in 2022. Martin said voters saw through that.

Looking back at the party’s history, Martin said past Democrats from the Dakotas, such as South Dakota’s former U.S. Sen. Tom Daschle, won for so many years by emphasizing local issues, especially agriculture. Martin said politics has since become more nationalized.

Martin said the election was not a “vote of confidence in Democrats” but a “vote of no confidence” in Republican President Donald Trump and a cry for relief from higher prices caused by Trump’s tariffs and war with Iran. “Democrats were the vehicle,” Martin said.

To continue the wave into 2028 and beyond, Democrats “have got to be at least caught trying” to lower costs, Martin said.
