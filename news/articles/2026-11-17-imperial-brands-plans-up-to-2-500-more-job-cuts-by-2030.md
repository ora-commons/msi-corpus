## Operating profit falls 10.2% as revenues decline 1.5%

Imperial Brands told shareholders on the morning of November 17 that it plans to cut up to 2,500 more jobs by the end of the decade, deepening a restructuring aimed at £320m of annual savings.

The tobacco company said it has already rolled out plans to cut 2,100 jobs around the world over the last year. It now expects "a further reduction of between 2,100 and 2,500 roles by the end of 2030," according to its statement accompanying its latest financial results.

The announcement came in Imperial's latest financial results, which showed revenues fell by 1.5% in the 12 months to the end of September, with operating profits down 10.2%.

Chief executive officer Lukas Paravicini framed the year as one of continued delivery. "We continue to perform and transform," Paravicini said. "This is our sixth consecutive year of growth in tobacco & NGP net revenue and adjusted operating profit."

NGP refers to next generation products, such as vapes. Paravicini said that in that segment, "once again we grew net revenues at double digit and market share in all categories."

The chief executive also pointed to change inside the company, saying "we have made tangible progress on our transformation to become more consumer-centric, agile, data led and efficient."

Paravicini's growth reference was to the company's adjusted operating profit. Imperial's reported operating profit fell 10.2% over the 12 months to the end of September, and its reported revenues declined 1.5% over the same period.

Imperial set out the further job reductions and the £320m savings target for shareholders in its latest financial results, with the additional reductions expected by the end of 2030.
