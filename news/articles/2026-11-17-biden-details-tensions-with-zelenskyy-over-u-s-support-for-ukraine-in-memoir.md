## Zelenskyy continued peace talks while seeking to limit concessions to Russia

Joe Biden said in his new memoir that Volodymyr Zelenskyy was “difficult to work with,” could be “moody and stubborn” in private conversations and was not as grateful for U.S. support as Biden had expected. The former president also said Zelenskyy had “tested our patience on many occasions” by demanding more armaments and money, while maintaining that he refused to abandon Ukraine and praising the Ukrainian leader’s fortitude.

*Promise Me, America* was published Tuesday, shortly before Biden’s 84th birthday, as he battles advanced prostate cancer. The Guardian described the book as largely a celebration of Biden’s accomplishments in office, with scant acknowledgment of failures. The Guardian said Biden’s own party had deemed him unfit for office and forced him to withdraw from the 2024 campaign against Donald Trump, and that the book “has not been well reviewed.”

The war in Ukraine occupies much of the middle of the book. Biden describes the buildup to war, frustrating talks with Vladimir Putin and U.S.-Ukraine cooperation from Russia’s February 2022 invasion until Biden left office in January 2025. His portrayal of Zelenskyy is not entirely negative.

Biden recalled worrying that Zelenskyy’s background as a comedian and actor might not equip him for a crisis. He wrote: “I had worried that President Zelenskyy, who had come from such an unlikely background [as a comedian and actor] might not have the right stuff for a crisis like this one.” He added, “This was no TV comedy.” Biden said he found himself impressed by Zelenskyy’s fortitude and “delighted that my worries proved to be unfounded.” Biden also praises the Ukrainian president’s communication skills.

Biden described his secret visit to Kyiv in February 2023. Zelenskyy’s then-10-year-old son handed Biden a drawing of a U.S.-supplied air defense system and a message that read, “Thank you for helping my father to save us all.”

The criticism appears as Biden recounts the Ukrainian government’s demands for assistance. He wrote: “As brave as he and the Ukrainians were, they tested our patience on many occasions, demanding more armaments and money. The United States has tremendous resources, but they are not limitless.” He added: “The American people should know: we got a good return on our investment; almost all the money was spent in the United States on our weapons systems, an expenditure leading to more jobs at home.”

Biden said he made his frustration clear to Zelenskyy “in no uncertain terms,” but he also wrote that he did not withdraw support. “Still, I refused to cut and run on the Ukrainians, who possessed a tremendous fighting spirit. And so did Zelenskyy,” he wrote.

The memoir’s account came after earlier public criticism of Zelenskyy. In February 2025, Donald Trump and JD Vance publicly criticized Zelenskyy at the White House, faulting him for supposedly being ungrateful and “disrespectful.” Senior Trump aides also publicly criticized him. According to The Guardian, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was reported to have called Zelenskyy a “little fucker,” a “special-needs child” and “Mr Bean on crack.”

In response to Trump’s public criticism, Zelenskyy told Fox News: “I’m thankful to President Trump and to Congress, bipartisan support, and I was always very thankful from all our people.”

The Guardian described Zelenskyy as having led Ukraine through four-and-a-half years of war against Russia. A year-and-a-half later, he was still leading the fight, trying to negotiate peace and seeking to keep Trump from making too many concessions to Russia. Zelenskyy did not immediately comment on Biden’s verdict.
