## Clayton linked some failures to maintenance and technical changes

National security officials from the Trump administration went to Capitol Hill on November 18 to answer lawmakers' questions about a series of outages and cyber problems that have alarmed millions in recent weeks. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin and Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton sought to reassure Americans as they testified before the House Committee on Homeland Security, which Rep. Andrew Garbarino (R-NY) called to order.

The committee had assembled a list of disruptions that lawmakers wanted addressed: the National Security Agency, Microsoft, banks and a nationwide grounding of air travel. Rep. Michael Guest (R-MS), a Mississippi Republican, said: "We have seen Microsoft services go offline, a nationwide ground stop, disruption within our major banking institutions, disruption of U.S. national security communications infrastructure." Guest also noted that United Airlines had a nationwide system outage earlier in the week.

Mullin said none of the incidents were tied to any attack. "I have said this publicly, I'm 98 percent confident that this has nothing to do with our adversaries hacking or going after our systems," he said.

Clayton said: "We have no indication that they have come from external threat actors or state-sponsored actors." He also said system upgrades were being performed during some of the outages, and described the events as "separate service outages associated with maintenance and technical changes." PBS News Hour reported that the officials did not provide a definitive answer on the causes.

Rep. Bennie Thompson (D-MS), the committee's ranking member, sharply questioned Mullin about a private-plane flight he took the morning after the grounding. Thompson said Americans were stranded at airports across the country, many sleeping on the floor at their gates, while the secretary boarded a luxury jet for a college football game. "For the secretary of homeland security to do that during a crisis was not only irresponsible, Mr. Secretary, but also incredibly tone-deaf," Thompson said.

Mullin said over 4,000 planes landed when the ground stoppage went out and that there was not one incident, describing the outcome as "a great success." He said the trip to an LSU game, attended with his family and his great-nieces, had been scheduled for weeks beforehand and that he went to be a good uncle. Mullin also said he took the flight to signal to Americans that it was safe to fly again.

Democrats also used the hearing to press Mullin on immigration enforcement and the role of ICE, following what PBS described as their overwhelming election wins. Pablo Hernandez Rivera, Resident Commissioner of Puerto Rico, told the committee that when government agents enter an apartment and zip-tie children and citizens, and when government gunfire on streets leaves families burying the dead, the government itself is the threat.

Mullin defended the administration's policy. "Our policy right now is to go after the worst of the worst," he said, adding that doing so also produces what he called collateral criminals. He said that when officers arrest someone on a warrant, most of the time the person is not by themselves.

PBS News Hour reported that the policy is likely to face further scrutiny from Congress next year as Democrats take new majorities.

The disruptions reached well beyond the skies into daily life, and lawmakers pressed for more specifics about how systems in different sectors failed within weeks of one another. Officials did not provide a definitive answer on the causes: Mullin said none of the incidents were tied to an attack, and Clayton said the failures were not related or coordinated. Clayton said the timing of the incidents could raise questions and should, and that the American people should ask officials those questions. He described the events as separate service outages associated with maintenance and technical changes.

Rep. Andrew Garbarino adjourned the committee without objection, and Stephanie Sy reported the hearing for PBS News Hour.
