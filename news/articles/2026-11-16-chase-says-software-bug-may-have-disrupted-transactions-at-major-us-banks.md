## Chase says outage temporary, not systemic or cyber-related

Thousands of customers at major US banks, including JP Morgan Chase, Wells Fargo and Bank of America, reported trouble accessing services on Monday. Problems started throughout the afternoon on Monday.

Chase Bank — the largest banking institution in the US — said a "software bug" in its hardware systems may have been behind the glitch, and noted the impacts may have affected other banks. The bank added that the issue was "temporary" and not "systemic" or "cyber-related." The exact nature of the bug was unclear, and the BBC has contacted Chase Bank.

Chase told its customers that "some transactions are taking longer than usual to complete" but that they still had "access to their accounts and funds." There were almost 5,000 reports of Chase Bank's services being down, according to the Downdetector website.

Chase, a subsidiary of JP Morgan Chase, serves more than 86 million customers at 5,000 branches across the US, as well as 7.4 million small businesses, according to the company's webpage. It is also the top credit card issuer in the US.

US Bank said some of its systems were not directly affected, but clients using Zelle, a digital payment platform, received error messages saying their payments could not be sent. Zelle has said its service is "operating as it should" and that issues at other banks could be affecting payments.

Customers also reported issues with Truist, PNC and Capital One, but all three companies told the BBC they have not been impacted.

Problems appeared to be easing for many customers by early Monday evening. As of 1800 ET (2300 GMT), Chase, Bank of America and US Bank had seen a fall in the number of reported outages.

The disruption follows a string of unrelated technical problems last week, including a massive Microsoft outage, as well as a telecommunications issue and network failure that grounded all US flights.
