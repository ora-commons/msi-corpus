## Idaho poll finds 96% oppose selling public land

The U.S. Department of the Interior released a strategic plan that News From The States described as the first to prioritize selling public land under the department’s control. The document says the agency will “work to decrease the size of the federal estate.”

In Idaho, the proposal could place Bureau of Land Management lands into a sale process. Mason said Interior’s approach was not a narrow, targeted transfer of a small parcel to a local government. The plan had no acreage limit, no ceiling on how much public land could be sold and no endpoint for the policy.

Public lands were on ballots across the country during the election season. In Idaho, voters reelected leaders who had said public lands were not for sale, according to the report. Rep. Mike Simpson and Sens. Jim Risch and Mike Crapo have advocated keeping public lands in public hands, Mason said.

Mason cited one recent poll showing that 96% of Idahoans oppose selling public land. He described the result as broad bipartisan support across the state and said the position also amounted to smart politics for Idaho’s elected leaders.

Interior’s plan calls for public lands to be sold to state and local governments. Mason said many of those governments would be unlikely to afford the long-term costs of land management and would need to find a way to make a profit. He said that could result in formerly public lands becoming privatized, commercialized or both.

Mason also said BLM land sold under the plan could eventually reach data-center developers, potentially allowing construction to proceed faster. He asserted that many administration officials had been found to have conflicts of interest with data-center development; the report did not identify the officials or describe the findings.

Steve Pearce, a New Mexico Republican and former congressman, was seeking confirmation as BLM director when he appeared before the Senate Energy and Natural Resources Committee on Feb. 25, 2026. Mason said Pearce told the committee that he would not sell public lands without congressional approval.

Risch previously helped defeat an effort by U.S. Sen. Mike Lee to sell 3 million acres of public lands. Responding to Interior’s new plan, Risch said, “If the federal government tries to sell off Idaho’s public lands, I’ll fight to stop it again.”

Mason said Interior’s policy could reduce Idahoans’ ability to roam on public land. He described BLM lands as places where residents hunt, fish, camp and spend time with family and friends, and said familiar tracts could eventually reach the auction block. He also said future generations of Idahoans were counting on officials to preserve public lands.

Mason asked Simpson, Risch and Crapo to make clear that Interior lacked a mandate to sell Idaho’s public lands and to support definitive congressional action to prevent such sales both now and in the future.
