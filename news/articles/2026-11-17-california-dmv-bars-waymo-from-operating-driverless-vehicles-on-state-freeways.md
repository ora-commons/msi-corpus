## California freeway restrictions remain until Waymo shows effective corrective actions

The California Department of Motor Vehicles issued restrictions last Wednesday that bar Waymo from operating or testing its driverless vehicles on all California freeways. Department spokesperson Jonathan Groveman said, “The Department determined that Waymo’s driverless freeway operations pose an imminent hazard to public safety due to recurring incursions by its vehicles into active construction zones where construction workers, vehicles and equipment are present.”

The action followed three Waymo autonomous vehicles entering an active construction closure on U.S. Route 101 near Burlingame on Nov. 5, according to the department. A separate incident the next day involved a Waymo vehicle and California Highway Patrol vehicles on Highway 280 in San Francisco. Video of the Nov. 6 incident circulated widely on social media last week and appeared to show the Waymo vehicle trying to bypass California Highway Patrol vehicles conducting a traffic break. Groveman added that “similar incidents” previously occurred on May 18.

A Waymo spokesperson said the “recent freeway construction zone encounters on U.S. 101 in California” did not “involve any injuries or collisions” and said the company prioritizes safety.

In a statement, the spokesperson said, “In alignment with our safety-first principles, we have also temporarily restricted freeway operations across our other operating markets to evaluate our performance.” The company said it was working with the California DMV and other state officials “diligently and responsibly” to resume freeway operations.

California’s restrictions will remain in effect until “Waymo demonstrates to the Department that it has taken effective corrective actions to address the deficiencies that precipitated the restrictions,” Groveman said.

Waymo’s autonomous vehicles expanded onto freeways and interstates around San Francisco, Los Angeles and Phoenix last year. The company continues to operate the vehicles as normal on California surface streets, according to Waymo.
