## St. Johns River tops 1953 record as Florida water levels keep rising

Scattered rain continued to exacerbate the impacts of historic flooding across central Florida on Saturday, even as officials in Northern California braced for an unprecedented deluge during high tides heading into the heavy Thanksgiving travel week.

Lurie said he based his warning on forecasts from the National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration and the National Weather Service, and urged people to consider not traveling for the holiday.

"We are expecting tides that the National Weather Service told us they have never seen before in recorded history here in San Francisco," Lurie said via the social platform X. "Take this weekend to get yourself ready, please stock up on supplies, get your shopping done, and know that the city will be out all weekend doing everything possible to protect our residents and our small businesses."

The expected deluge coincides with the highest tides of the year, called king tides. Forecasters said up to 2 feet (0.6 meters) of flooding would be likely in low-lying areas near shorelines and tidal waterways through 6 a.m. Monday, and through 4 p.m. Thursday along other areas of the Pacific coast.

The storm systems impacting Florida and California are an offshoot of El Niño, the natural warming of the Pacific near the equator that warps weather patterns worldwide. Florida typically experiences its dry season from October to May.

In Florida, National Weather Service meteorologist Melissa Watson in Melbourne said the heaviest rains have passed, but water levels were expected to continue to rise along the St. Johns River, the state's longest, due to continued lighter showers. Near the city of Cocoa, the river rose above a 1953 record on Thursday.

"Regardless of any additional rainfall, we are expecting these river floods to continue for an extended period of time," Watson said.

Heavy rain earlier this week submerged large swaths of Florida's Atlantic coast, trapping people in floodwaters in the northeastern part of the state.

Forecasters also warned of dangers Saturday along Florida's Atlantic coast. The weather service issued marine warnings to boaters tied to thunderstorms capable of creating destabilizing waterspouts, and advised beachgoers to beware of rough surf and dangerous rip currents.

The California warnings arrive at the front of the Thanksgiving travel window, with Lurie asking residents to spend the weekend getting supplies and finishing shopping rather than waiting until the water arrives.

For San Francisco, the mayor framed the forecast as a call to prepare early: the city, he said, would be working through the weekend to protect residents and small businesses ahead of the tides.
