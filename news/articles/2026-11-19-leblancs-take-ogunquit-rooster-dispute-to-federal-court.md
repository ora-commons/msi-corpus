## Multiple fines and a disability variance marked the town’s enforcement

David and Sara LeBlanc sued the town of Ogunquit, Maine, in federal court this week, arguing that the town’s enforcement of its sign code violates their First Amendment rights. The dispute centers on roughly 6-foot-tall (1.8 meters) metal roosters the couple wants to display for their daughter, Pyper, who is autistic. Ogunquit classifies the ornaments as advertising for the LeBlancs’ adjacent restaurant, the Omelette Factory.

David LeBlanc said in a statement, “The roosters are a symbol of one thing: How much we love our daughter,” and continued: “But the town made up an entirely different meaning for them.”

Before the lawsuit, Ogunquit issued multiple fines. The town subsequently granted the couple a disability variance that allowed them to keep two roosters but required the ornaments to be attached to a railing. The LeBlancs declined to build the railing and chained the roosters to two posts instead, according to the complaint. Ogunquit issued more fines after that change. According to the lawsuit, the LeBlancs have since removed all of the tin statues.

The lawsuit states: “This is governmental discrimination based on the content of speech and the identity of the speaker,” and adds: “Such discrimination is presumptively unconstitutional, and the Town cannot justify it.”

The complaint also points to how the town treats other oversized displays. According to the lawsuit, other local businesses display lobster buoys, antique farm equipment and large tiki statues without incurring fines. That comparison is part of the LeBlancs’ challenge to the sign-code enforcement.

The LeBlancs are represented by the Institute for Justice. In 2025, the institute won a similar case in New Hampshire involving a bakery owner and a large mural painted by high school students. The mural featured doughnuts and other baked treats, and the town of Conway argued that it was a regulated sign. A judge sided with the bakery owner and found that the town infringed on the owner’s free speech rights.

Many of the couple’s original roosters have been adopted by local residents and businesses, while supporters around the world have been buying their own to display. Sara LeBlanc said in a statement: “Every time Pyper spots another rooster in someone’s yard, her face lights up,” and continued: “This town has embraced her. We wish the town government would, too.”

Ogunquit town clerk Christine Murphy did not respond to a message seeking comment Wednesday.
