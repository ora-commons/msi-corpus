## Section 301 route follows narrow Supreme Court ruling

The office of the U.S. trade representative said on November 25 that it would hold hearings on 25 January covering the proposed tariffs and its investigation into unfair foreign trade practices. The White House said the duties could take effect in January at the earliest but did not provide a firm implementation timeline.

The administration is using Section 301 of the Trade Act of 1974 as the legal basis for the proposed duties. The law gives the executive branch power to respond to unfair foreign trade practices after formal investigations.

In February, the Supreme Court issued a narrow ruling that President Donald Trump’s so-called “Liberation Day” tariffs were illegal. The court said the administration had improperly used a specific emergency powers law to implement them, but the ruling did not broadly curb the president’s power to introduce new tariffs.

The proposal would place duties on 14 trading partners. Beyond China and the European Union, it names Bangladesh, Cambodia, India, Indonesia, Japan, South Korea, Malaysia, Singapore, Switzerland, Taiwan, Thailand and Vietnam. For Chinese imports, the administration is proposing an additional 20%, bringing the total rate to around 40%.

The proposal is the second major Section 301 tariff action. It follows the White House’s July announcement of a broad slate of tariffs on more than 80 countries, including the UK, Mexico and Canada, which the administration said had failed to enforce bans on forced labor. A lawsuit challenging those July tariffs is awaiting a decision in the Court of International Trade.

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said in a Wednesday statement: “For decades, many of our trading partners have adopted industrial strategies built on targeting manufacturing, wage and labor suppression, financial and other support, non-market economy inputs, and other market distorting practices that tilt the trade balance in their favor while eroding America’s manufacturing base.”

Greer also said: “President Trump is proposing to take action to bring about the elimination of these unfair acts, policies, and practices. In doing so, we can level the playing field for American workers and chart a new, more sustainable course for the world economy.”

The Guardian also reported: “Tariffs have proven unpopular with US voters, who handed Democrats a decisive victory in the midterm elections that will give the party control of Congress.”

The Yale Budget Lab estimates that tariffs currently in effect have added an additional $900 in costs for each US household. That estimate applies to tariffs already in effect, not the proposed duties.

The current proposal is the second major batch under the same statute, while a lawsuit challenging the earlier duties awaits a Court of International Trade decision.
