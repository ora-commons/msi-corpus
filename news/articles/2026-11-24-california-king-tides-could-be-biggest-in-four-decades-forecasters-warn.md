## Northern California residents collect sandbags ahead of Thanksgiving flooding

On November 24, much of California was bracing for record-high tides as powerful storms threatened Thanksgiving travel and festivities along both U.S. coasts. Forecasters warned that so-called king tides along the Pacific Coast could be the biggest in four decades.

Preparations were already underway in Northern California. Residents collected sandbags, and workers used heavy equipment to pile mounds of earth to protect against coastal floods.

Area officials urged people to keep the weather in mind as they made Thanksgiving plans. Forecasters also warned that the so-called king tides along the Pacific Coast could be the biggest in four decades.

At Santa Clara Valley Water, Christopher Hakes, chief operating officer for watersheds, described the meeting of El Nino and king tides as an unfortunate coincidence. He said: "The fact that it's an El Nino and a king tide makes it a little bit of an unfortunate coincidence. And it will hamper some travel plans."

Hakes also addressed possible travel disruption: "I know I have seen advisories that have gone out that have said people should maybe rethink some of their travel. Some of the airports might be backed up or whatnot. But it seems like every year we have a kind of new normal."

The warning covered the Pacific Coast, while the preparations described were underway in Northern California. Local officials urged residents to consider the weather in their Thanksgiving planning, and Hakes warned that the combination of El Nino and king tides could complicate travel.
