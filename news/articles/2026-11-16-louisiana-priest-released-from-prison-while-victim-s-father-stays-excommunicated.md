## Lawsuit accuses Guidry of sexually abusing another altar server in the 1970s

Michael Guidry, 83, was released from the Dixon correctional institute in Louisiana on Saturday after completing a seven-year prison sentence for sexually abusing an altar server in south-west Louisiana. A Louisiana department of corrections letter dated 21 October informed the Peytons that Guidry would finish serving his prison sentence on Saturday and then have to report to a probation office. A prison official on Saturday confirmed Guidry was released from custody as scheduled.

Guidry confessed in 2018 to molesting a teenaged Oliver Peyton while he, the victim and Scott E Peyton ministered together at St Peter's church in Morrow, Louisiana, which is part of the Lafayette diocese. After authorities arrested Guidry, he pleaded guilty to molestation of a juvenile and received a seven-year prison sentence in 2019.

Guidry was also required to serve three years of probation upon his release from prison, which he reported to after the Lafayette diocese treated him to a farewell lunch for which the organization later apologized. Guidry remains a member of the Catholic priesthood, and the Lafayette diocese has restricted him from public ministry.

Dawn Eden Goldstein, the church appellate lawyer who represented Scott E Peyton in his excommunication appeal, said the appeal process revealed that Guidry remains a member of the Catholic priesthood despite his crime. Guidry "will walk out of prison still able to present himself as a priest of God", Goldstein said in a statement. The church "has shown compassion to the child-molesting priest but not to the victim", Goldstein added, echoing the sentiments of many who have learned of the Peytons' experience amid the continued fallout of the worldwide Catholic clergy abuse scandal. "As a Catholic, I am sickened to witness such un-Christian behavior from an authority in my own church."

Scott E Peyton had served as a deacon alongside Guidry — a clerical role that married men can hold which is largely similar but still different from that of a priest — and resigned from the clergy after his son's abuse, setting off a chain of events that culminated in an unsuccessful appeal of his excommunication from the Catholic church. He temporarily joined an Anglican church before moving on to a nondenominational congregation.

The Lafayette, Louisiana, bishop, J Douglas Deshotel, excommunicated Scott Peyton from the church after the latter man disclosed his having joined an Anglican congregation, saying the resigned deacon had severed ties with the Catholic church. Excommunication bars those who incur it from engaging in certain Catholic sacraments, such as holy communion, aiming to make those people rethink and atone for their behavior.

Peyton appealed to the Vatican entity in charge of Catholic clerical discipline, the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith, saying there was "no pastoral good" in assigning him such a harsh consequence. He also said his excommunication was both unnecessary and made his wife, Letitia, and their six children feel unwelcome to stay in the Catholic faith, therefore harming "the spiritual lives of eight" church members.

Nonetheless, in a decision announced in October, the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith (DDF) rejected Peyton's appeal. The DDF reasoned that Peyton had published his temporarily joining an Anglican congregation and therefore refused to submit to the Catholic pope, who since May 2025 has been Leo XIV.

Deshotel discussed the DDF's ruling with Peyton and Goldstein on 16 October, five days before the letter about Guidry's release from prison. At that meeting, Deshotel confirmed that Guidry remained in the Catholic priesthood, albeit with restrictions, Goldstein and Peyton said.

Many who have encountered Peyton's story in various platforms have bristled at the notion that he was excommunicated while the church has not moved to oust Guidry from the priesthood through a process known as laicization.

Between Guidry's sentencing and his release, the Peytons secured a $350,000 out-of-court settlement from Lafayette's diocese and became advocates for clergy abuse survivors.

Scott Peyton then began grappling with what was diagnosed as a case of neuroinvasive West Nile virus, a mosquito-borne illness, according to social media posts published by Letitia Peyton. The posts described Scott Peyton having to fight for his life after contracting the illness and spending nearly three weeks hospitalized. The outlook was substantially better on Saturday, when a post by Letitia showed a picture of her and Scott standing by a car outside a hospital entrance along with the caption: "We are breaking out!!!! Home sweet home bound!"

Meanwhile, in the days leading up to Guidry's release from prison, the Lafayette news outlet KLFY reported that a recently filed lawsuit accused the priest of having sexually abused another altar server in the 1970s. That lawsuit is unrelated to Oliver Peyton's case, the news outlet reported.

The Lafayette diocese is where the US's reckoning with the worldwide, decades-old Catholic clergy abuse crisis essentially began, when one of its priests, Gilbert Gauthe, pleaded guilty in 1985 to molesting several boys he met through his ministry. Along with numerous other legal consequences in the wake of the Gauthe revelations, more than 40 US Catholic religious institutions have since filed for federal bankruptcy protection.

Goldstein's statement said the Lafayette diocese's place in the history of the US Catholic church's clergy molestation scandal means it "should be the leader of pastoral care for victims of sexual abuse by clergy". "Instead, the diocese of Lafayette under Bishop Deshotel has treated my client's son and the entire Peyton family as though they themselves were criminals, while it has kept the criminal priest on its payroll", Goldstein said. Goldstein also said Deshotel would need to submit his resignation when he turns 75 in January, fulfilling a church requirement, and that she prayed the pope eventually replaced Deshotel with "a true shepherd". The Guardian has asked the Lafayette diocese for comment.
