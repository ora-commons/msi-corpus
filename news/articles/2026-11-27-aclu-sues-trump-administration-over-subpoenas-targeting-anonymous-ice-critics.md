## ACLU sues Trump administration over subpoenas targeting anonymous ICE critics

**Subtype:** fact

- The American Civil Liberties Union filed a federal class action seeking to block Justice Department and Department of Homeland Security grand jury subpoenas that could identify anonymous ICE critics.
- The ACLU says government lawyers issued dozens of grand jury subpoenas to social media companies for information about anonymous ICE critics.
- ACLU attorneys are representing four unnamed social media users whose posts criticized ICE, DHS or Trump.
- Chief Judge James Boasberg scheduled a Jan. 6 hearing on the ACLU’s request for a preliminary injunction.
- The Trump administration has until Dec. 14 to respond in writing to the ACLU’s allegations.

## Federal judge schedules Jan. 6 hearing on injunction request

The American Civil Liberties Union filed a federal class action in Washington on Friday seeking to block grand jury subpoenas that the civil rights group says the Trump administration is using to identify anonymous social media users who criticized immigration enforcement tactics. The lawsuit asks a federal court to enjoin the Justice Department and Department of Homeland Security from conducting what the ACLU calls a “bad-faith investigation” that violates the users’ First Amendment free-speech rights.

ACLU attorneys filed on behalf of four unnamed social media users whose posts included criticism of ICE, DHS and Trump. One plaintiff, a Bluesky user, wrote about an ICE-related shooting in New York and posted, “Burn ICE to the ground. Burn DHS to the ground. Salt the earth.” Another plaintiff is a college student whose posts on X included an expletive directed at DHS, ICE and Trump.

The subpoenas seek account information from social media platforms including Meta, Bluesky and X, formerly known as Twitter, and the information could be provided to ICE investigators, the ACLU said. Government lawyers have issued dozens of these grand jury subpoenas, according to the ACLU, but the scope of the investigation remains unclear.

ACLU attorney Laura Moraff said in a statement: “The government is weaponizing the grand jury process to retaliate against people for expressing their political views online.” The ACLU also alleges that the investigation is a bad-faith fishing expedition intended to gather intrusive intelligence and facilitate retaliation against dissenters.

The ACLU says Pirro’s office has used the term “Investigation 18” to describe the effort to identify critics and obtain their personal information. The civil rights organization calls the Washington lawsuit the first systemic civil challenge to the Trump administration’s use of grand jury unmasking subpoenas and the first case that will be litigated in public view.

The ACLU says DHS sent hundreds of administrative subpoenas to technology companies over the past year to seek information about online ICE critics. According to the civil rights organization, the administration shifted to grand jury subpoenas, a more secretive process, after users and technology companies pushed back against the administrative demands.

ACLU lawyers wrote that the defendants’ use of subpoenas to unmask critics was occurring against a backdrop of repeated suggestions that the defendants were building a database of dissenters. The lawsuit seeks to halt that process through a preliminary injunction.

The defendants include U.S. Attorney Jeanine Pirro, Attorney General Todd Blanche and DHS Secretary Markwayne Mullin, while the ACLU asks the court to enjoin the Justice Department and Department of Homeland Security. Spokespeople for DHS and Pirro’s Washington office did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

Chief Judge James Boasberg ordered the case unsealed Wednesday. He scheduled a Jan. 6 hearing for arguments on the ACLU’s request for a preliminary injunction, and the Trump administration has until Dec. 14 to respond to the allegations in writing.
