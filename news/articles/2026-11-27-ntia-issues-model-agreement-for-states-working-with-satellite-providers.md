## Nebraska evaluates options as satellite accounts for 23% of its BEAD technology

The National Telecommunications and Information Administration released a model agreement Monday for states contracting with large satellite internet providers under the Broadband Equity, Access and Deployment, or BEAD, program. In a statement, the NTIA said the model agreement is meant to "simplify the contracting process" with satellite providers and expedite services to eligible BEAD locations. Nebraska's Broadband Office said it is evaluating all options in response to the guidance and will share decisions in the coming days.

BEAD is a nationwide broadband expansion program that began under the Biden administration. State lawmakers learned of changes coming to the program in late 2025 through President Donald Trump's "Benefit of the Bargain" initiative.

The NTIA statement said some national satellite companies have been slow to sign state contracts, as different states have imposed "a patchwork of obligations" beyond the basic federal requirements.

Under the model agreement, states can contract with Low Earth Orbit satellite providers that agree to meet BEAD service and testing requirements but decline BEAD subsidies. States that choose not to use the model agreement will be responsible for finding a different provider who can serve locations at a comparable cost.

Nebraska has received roughly $57 million of the $405 million it was initially awarded, with the leftover dollars held as "non-deployment funds" that may or may not come to the state. About 23% of Nebraska's BEAD technology is satellite, according to a previous statement from the Nebraska Broadband Office, which falls within the Nebraska Department of Transportation.

Jay Omar, a spokesman for the Nebraska Department of Transportation, said the office was in the middle of evaluating its response to the latest guidance. "NBO is currently evaluating all options in response to the recent guidance around the Model Zero Outlay Agreement and will share decisions in the coming days," Omar said of the changes announced Monday.

Emily Haxby, a Gage County farmer and vocal critic of the changes made to the BEAD program under the Trump administration, said the model agreement will mainly affect Nebraska's working relationship with Starlink, SpaceX's satellite company. Haxby said she believes the model agreement would remove the accountability measures meant to ensure that Starlink serves Nebraska's BEAD locations.

"SpaceX is giving up the subsidy and minimizing exposure, which makes sense because their entire BEAD award nationwide is less than two weeks of revenue for the company," said Claude Aiken, chief strategy officer for Nextlink, another internet provider participating in Nebraska's BEAD implementation. "The long-term benefit for them is keeping other providers from receiving subsidies for these locations."

Haxby said she suspected supporters of the latest changes would frame the model agreement as a cost-saving opportunity. She noted Starlink was already serving Nebraska locations and was not in need of BEAD subsidies in the first place, and said she doubts Nebraska would be able to find another company that can offer comparable or cheaper costs.

"(Nebraska) could have absolutely done better, but I will give them a little bit of a benefit when I tell you that the NTIA and them changing the rules did not help us at all," Haxby said. "Quite frankly, they should just keep their grubby paws out of it."
