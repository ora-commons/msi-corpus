## Cotton says Congress will pass overwhelmingly bipartisan chip export bills

PBS NewsHour White House correspondent Liz Landers said she was the first to speak with President Donald Trump on the morning of Nvidia's announcement. She called him around 8 a.m. Eastern on November 23 to ask about New York Times reporting that had appeared the previous evening. During the call, Trump twice hinted that Jensen Huang might be reinstated and said he was worried China could poach him to work on cutting-edge AI technology.

Trump told Landers, "He's back where he should be, and we see he did absolutely nothing wrong from that standpoint." He also said Huang had "certainly learned a lesson." About an hour after their call, Nvidia announced that Huang would return as chief executive and president, 11 days after he stepped down. Trump then spoke with CNBC for about 50 minutes in a wide-ranging phone call.

After the announcement, Nvidia shares surged more than 30%, and the markets added $2 trillion in value on November 23, according to PBS NewsHour.

Trump told CNBC that "a lot of things changed" at Nvidia over the weekend and that he "felt very strongly" Huang should return. He said, "We have done a very thorough investigation," while describing Huang's conduct as bad judgment involving "somewhat unrelated incidents." Trump maintained that Huang "did nothing wrong."

Trump emphasized several times that the United States needed to protect what he called geniuses. He described Huang as a genius and said he worried China could poach him to work on cutting-edge AI technology. "I can tell you the bigger threat would be losing the brainpower of somebody like Jensen. That would be a threat. To me, that would be a far bigger threat than anything we're talking about," he said.

Landers said Trump had called Nvidia's board several times, both directly and indirectly, and had spoken with Huang by phone the previous day. She said Trump had communicated his preference to the board, and PBS described his intervention as personal and aimed at pushing for Huang's reinstatement.

Amna Nawaz and Geoff Bennett said the allegations that led to Huang's resignation raised serious national-security concerns, including possible ties to Chinese intelligence. PBS NewsHour described Alice Huang as suspected of ties to Chinese intelligence. Landers said Trump did not deny in her interview that he had been briefed on the possible connection between Alice Huang and Chinese spy services. Trump said he did not want to comment and told Landers, "You'll have to figure that out for yourself."

Nvidia said in a statement: "In the process of reaching its decision, the board received input from senior levels of the United States government, and, based on that input, the board is confident that Mr. Huang's instatement poses no national security risk." PBS reported that Nvidia was still conducting its own investigation after Huang returned.

Trump later confirmed on CNBC that the United States had given the board assurances. He said, "There's nothing wrong with a comeback, and they came back and they did the right thing. That was not an easy thing to do. But they did ask for assurance on the national security threat. And we gave them assurance, at least with everything we know, and we know a lot. So, with everything we know, we gave them assurance."

Landers said Trump posted on TRUTH Social a meme showing Jensen Huang holding a red bra in a recreation of Trump's own assassination attempt in Butler, Pennsylvania.

Landers said Congress had made clear that the matter was not over for Nvidia as a company or Huang as an individual. Incoming Speaker Hakeem Jeffries said, "The decision by the Nvidia board to reinstate Jensen Huang is indefensible, irresponsible, and incomprehensible." Jeffries said Congress would continue its investigations, including scrutiny of Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick's role in potentially selling chips to China.

Landers described congressional concern as bipartisan and said some actionable items might be pushed through Congress soon. Republican Sen. Tom Cotton said, "Congress has much to investigate, and we will pass overwhelmingly bipartisan chip export control bills."

Huang agreed to appear before the Senate Banking Committee next week. PBS confirmed that the appearance remained scheduled for next Thursday and would be public, while Nvidia's investigation and the congressional inquiries were still underway.


On November 16, [MSI previously reported that Nvidia's board was not considering Huang's return](/articles/2026-11-16-nvidia-board-won-t-consider-huang-s-return-as-ceo-source-says/). By November 23, Nvidia had reinstated Huang after Trump said he had communicated his preference to the board and Landers reported that he had called it several times.
