## Boebert cites gas prices, infighting and unfulfilled promises for GOP losses

Rep. Lauren Boebert sat in a U-Haul van in Washington, D.C., on Thursday after packing up her congressional office and her “dungeon of an apartment.” The interview came about six years after her first House election in 2020 and one week after voters decided not to return her to Washington, D.C. The Colorado Republican spoke via Zoom from the van in one of her first interviews since losing to Democrat Eileen Laubacher in a district Boebert had won by double digits two years earlier.

Boebert said, “I think about it every day,” referring to the midterm elections. American voters sent Democrats to Congress en masse, and Boebert said she had considered the result a potential referendum on President Trump, Congress or the Republican Party. “This could be a referendum on President Trump. It could be the referendum on Congress or the Republican Party,” she said. Then, lowering her voice, she added, “I think it’s all of it.”

Boebert did not lay the blame for her defeat or other Republican losses squarely on Trump. “I’m not mad at the president,” she said, but “the president did some things that hurt my voters.” She said, “I think it’s more all of us. It’s all of the Republican Party,” citing high gas prices, GOP infighting and unfulfilled promises. “We had solutions and we didn’t take action,” she said.

Though Boebert has broken from Trump at times, those breaks typically involved topics other Trump supporters also opposed, such as the war in Iran and the administration’s treatment of the Jeffrey Epstein files. She also said, “I respect President Trump, I still work with him, I want to continue to work with him. Not for him, with him.”

Boebert was one of the most vocal and visible members of the “Make America Great Again” movement since her first election to the House in 2020, making headlines on television and the House floor. Her time in Congress roughly traced the movement’s arc through its 2024 high point, more recent midterm losses and a more tenuous relationship with Trump among its supporters. As someone who described herself on Fox News as “ultra MAGA” four years ago, Boebert has become less inclined toward definitions. “When Jesus comes back and I’m raptured up, like all these labels are just going to fly off anyway,” she said.

Boebert said she had not changed much in six years, aside from a few more gray hairs and maybe some “personal growth” on bipartisanship. She said she would not have done anything differently, except, as she put it, “not gone to the theater.”

She was referring to a 2023 incident at a Denver theater, where cameras caught Boebert vaping during a performance of “Beetlejuice” and she was escorted from the venue. The clip was replayed in campaign ads against her, and she partly credited it for her loss. Boebert considered the attention an unfair dredging up of a past she had already “owned up to,” while saying there could be “a great number of” unaffiliated or Republican voters who, when it came to the scandal, said, “I don’t care if any of this is true or not, I’m tired of the drama.”

At the time, Boebert apologized for the incident throughout her former district, which covered western Colorado. She later moved to a more conservative House seat covering the state’s Great Plains, won in 2024 and lost two years later. Laubacher raised far more campaign cash than Boebert did. “I didn’t raise millions of dollars and I kinda refused to,” Boebert said. “Colorado voters know me, and, you know, if they know me enough to get rid of me, then I guess that’s my answer.”

Boebert said she did not take the loss as an “L” and instead saw it as a “launching pad” for the future, which she did not specify but gave a hint toward. “Too many people come to Congress and get rich,” she said. “I am not one of those, and I’m gonna go make some money now.” Her camera vibrated slightly as the van started moving, with the nation’s capital outside the window moving by, and she added, “The U-Haul is getting started.”
