## U.S. home prices rose 2.7% in the July-September quarter

The Federal Housing Finance Agency said Tuesday that Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac will be able to acquire single-family home loans of up to $855,400 in most of the country in 2027. The increase expands the size of home loans the government can guarantee against default as home prices rise. The ceiling is about 2.7% higher than the 2026 limit and reflects the agency's annual adjustment for changing home values.

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac buy home loans from banks and other lenders, guarantee the loans against default and bundle them into securities sold to investors. FHFA oversees Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac and sets the ceiling for the loan size the two companies can buy.

Loans within the limit are known as conforming loans, while mortgages above it are called jumbo loans. FHFA adjusts conforming limits annually to reflect changes in home values, which have been rising nationally, albeit more slowly.

The agency's latest House Price Index showed that average U.S. home prices increased about 2.7% in the July-September quarter compared with a year earlier. That nationwide increase is the measure behind the 2027 adjustment.

Most of the country falls under the $855,400 baseline. FHFA also allows higher limits in certain states, including Alaska and Hawaii, and in counties where the local median home value is more than double the conforming loan limit.

Under the 2027 schedule, FHFA set the single-family limit at $1,283,100 in Los Angeles County and $1,224,750 in New York County.

The higher ceiling came as the existing-home market weakened. The Associated Press reported that rising mortgage rates weighed on sales this year, deepening a national housing slump that began in 2022, when mortgage rates climbed from pandemic-era lows.

Sales of previously occupied U.S. homes were essentially flat last year and remained at a 30-year low. They declined last month to their slowest annual pace in more than 16 years as mortgage rates surged.

The raw 30-Year Fixed Mortgage Rate series on [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MORTGAGE30US) stood at 7.28% on Nov. 24, 2026. The higher loan ceiling thus arrived while borrowing costs remained elevated.

FHFA's annual change responds to home-price growth, while mortgage rates remain a separate factor in the market. The agency's index determines the size of the conforming ceiling; the rate series and sales data show the pressure created by higher borrowing costs.
