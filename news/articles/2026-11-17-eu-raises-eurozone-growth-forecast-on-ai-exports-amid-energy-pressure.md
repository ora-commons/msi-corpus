## EU raises eurozone growth forecast on AI exports amid energy pressure

**Subtype:** fact

- The European Commission raised its eurozone growth forecast to 1.2% for this year from 0.9%.
- The European Commission attributed stronger European exports to global AI and electrification investment, including demand for power, cooling and chip-making equipment.
- The European Commission expects government deficits to widen this year as defense spending, energy relief and interest costs increase.
- The European Commission raised its 2027 inflation forecast to 3.1% from 2.3% because it expects high energy prices to persist longer.
- The European Commission projects French government debt at 122.1% of GDP next year, compared with 90.5% for the eurozone.

## Deficits widen as defense spending and interest costs increase

The European Commission raised its forecast for eurozone growth to 1.2% this year from 0.9%, supported by stronger exports tied to the AI investment boom and reduced reliance on fossil fuels. The commission expects growth to remain at 1.2% in 2027 and accelerate slightly in 2028, despite a longer-than-expected period of high energy prices following the U.S.-Iran conflict.

In its twice-yearly economic report, the commission said the European economy continued to display resilience. “The feared scenario of prolonged disruption in global energy supply has largely materialised,” said Maarten Verwey, the European Commission’s director general for economic and financial affairs. “Yet its economic impact has so far remained manageable.”

Europe, a major importer of fossil fuels, had expected rising energy prices following the start of the U.S.-Iran conflict to weigh more heavily on economic activity. But the bloc had reduced its reliance on fossil fuels since the previous price spike in the wake of Russia’s 2022 invasion of Ukraine, helping cushion the drag on growth.

European exports grew faster than expected, largely because demand increased for goods associated with the AI investment boom. The gain was more modest than those experienced by some Asian economies that specialize in manufacturing advanced chips, but the commission described the increase as significant. “The strong global AI and electrification investment cycle supports the improved outlook for goods exports, with firms across the EU supplying power, cooling, and chip-making equipment,” the Commission said.

The EU raised its 2027 growth forecasts for Germany, Italy and Spain but lowered its projection for France. France’s forecast of 0.6% would mark a pickup from this year, when summer heatwaves affected food production and an uncertain budget outlook weighed on investment.

Investors have focused on a steady buildup in French government debt, while the country’s fragmented legislature has been wary of backing a plan to contain borrowing ahead of elections next year. The commission expects French government spending to exceed revenue by 5.5% of gross domestic product this year, up from its previous projection of 5.1%. It sees a small decline next year but a rebound in 2028 under current plans.

The commission expects French government debt to reach 122.1% of GDP next year, far above the 90.5% projected for the eurozone as a whole. It said the increase in borrowing was more widespread, with the U.S.-Iran conflict affecting energy prices and government bond yields and governments taking steps to respond to a more hostile Russia.

“Government deficits are set to rise markedly this year, as a result of increasing defence spending, rising interest expenditure, and some new measures to shelter households and firms from high energy prices,” the Commission said.

The commission’s May forecast had anticipated, in line with futures markets, an earlier end to the U.S.-Iran conflict. Energy prices remained high for longer, prompting the EU to raise its 2027 inflation forecast to 3.1% from 2.3%. The commission expects inflation to decline to 2.1% in 2028.

“Energy price movements after the U.S. mid-term elections suggest inflation might fall faster than projected,” the Commission said.
