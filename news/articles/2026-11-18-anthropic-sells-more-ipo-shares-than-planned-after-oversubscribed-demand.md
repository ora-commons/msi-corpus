## Demand spanned mutual funds, hedge funds, venture-capital firms and individuals

Anthropic sold more shares than it had planned for its initial public offering after investors requested several times more shares than the artificial-intelligence company was selling, according to The Wall Street Journal. The company behind the Claude chatbot sold the shares at $83 each, above the price range it had guided, according to people familiar with the matter. The shares are expected to list on the Nasdaq under the ticker "ANT" at midday Thursday.

The sale was bigger than the one Anthropic had set out to run. The company had been aiming to sell 825 million shares at $70 to $78 apiece. It sold more shares than it had planned, and at a higher price than expected for its initial public offering.

Demand reached across the investment spectrum. Anthropic received strong demand from investors spanning mutual funds, hedge funds, venture-capital firms and individuals, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Some investors spent Wednesday worrying that they might end up getting nothing or very small allocations even after submitting jumbo requests, the Journal reported. Prospective investors typically request far more shares of IPOs than they expect or even want in the offering.

The public offering runs alongside a private-placement share sale. Anthropic had been planning to raise another $26 billion from a group of big investors including Amazon.com, Nvidia and Google.

The roadshow itself was not without disruption. Anthropic's pitch to prospective investors came during a bout of volatility in AI-linked stocks, and it was interrupted by the dramatic departure of Jensen Huang as Nvidia's CEO, according to The Wall Street Journal.

On a fully diluted basis, the offering is expected to value Anthropic at around $1.5 trillion, according to The Wall Street Journal. That pricing sets the reference point for Anthropic's first trading day, and investors will see the first public-market mark on the company's shares.

The price also landed above the range Anthropic had guided. The company had aimed to sell 825 million shares at $70 to $78 apiece; the sale came in larger and at $83 a share.

Shares are expected to begin trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker "ANT" at midday Thursday, and the debut will be the first public-market test of the pricing. The Wall Street Journal said updates would follow as news develops.
