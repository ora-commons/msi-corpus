## Tariff refunds lift guidance while adjusted earnings top analyst estimates

Dollar Tree raised its full-year adjusted earnings outlook on Tuesday after comparable sales rose 4.6% in the quarter ended Oct. 31, outpacing the 3.7% increase analysts expected. The discount retailer now expects $8.05 to $8.25 a share, up from $7.70 to $8.05.

The new range includes a roughly 65-cent-a-share benefit tied to tariff refunds. Dollar Tree also raised the low end of its full-year net-sales forecast to $20.6 billion from $20.5 billion while retaining the high end at $20.7 billion. The company raised the low end of its comparable-sales outlook to growth of 3.5% from 3% while holding the high end at 4%.

FactSet analysts expected full-year adjusted earnings of $7.94 a share, sales of $20.68 billion and comparable-sales growth of 3.6%. Dollar Tree's raised earnings range exceeded the analyst estimate. Shares jumped 12% to $156.25 in premarket trading.

For the three months ended Oct. 31, Dollar Tree reported net sales of $5.07 billion, up 6.9% and narrowly above the $5.04 billion Wall Street estimate. Comparable sales, which account for store openings and closings, climbed 4.6%, compared with the 3.7% gain analysts expected.

Dollar Tree reported net income of $232 million, or $1.24 a share, for the quarter, compared with $244.6 million, or $1.20 a share, a year earlier. After excluding one-time items, adjusted earnings were $1.16 a share, above the 91 cents analysts expected.

Dollar Tree said inflation-weary shoppers seeking value were driving the sales increase. The company also said its value proposition continued to win over budget-conscious shoppers.

“Our broader assortment, better stores and stronger customer engagement give shoppers more reasons to choose Dollar Tree and come back more often,” Chief Executive Mike Creedon said. “As the holiday season begins, our focus is clear: make Dollar Tree the destination for value, convenience and discovery.”

For the current quarter, Dollar Tree guided to adjusted earnings of $2.46 to $2.66 a share, including a 25-cent-a-share impact tied to tariff-refund reinvestments. The company forecast net sales of $5.7 billion to $5.8 billion and comparable-sales growth of 3% to 4%. Wall Street estimates called for adjusted earnings of $2.62 a share, net sales of $5.78 billion and same-store sales growth of 3.4%.


Dollar Tree's move extends a recent sequence of higher annual forecasts among major retailers.
