## Anthropic shares fall 6% toward the $83 IPO price after opening surge

Global stock markets slumped Friday after Anthropic's record-breaking IPO, with AI-linked shares falling across major indexes. The debut drew a strong initial response but did not halt broader selling as investors questioned valuations and the returns expected from large AI investments.

The sell-off followed losses on Wall Street, where the main indexes all fell by more than 2% on Thursday in a session dominated by Anthropic's IPO.

Asian markets were on track for their worst week since 2020 as shares in large chip companies fell. Taiwan's stocks dropped 6.7% to their lowest level since mid-April, with TSMC down about 6%.

Japan's Nikkei index fell 5%, while South Korea's tech-heavy Kospi lost 5.5%. SoftBank shares fell 14.5% in Tokyo, their worst day in more than two years, amid worries about SoftBank's AI bets.

European markets were heading for their worst week since March. The pan-European Stoxx 600 dropped 1.3% in the first 90 minutes of trading. Britain's FTSE 100 hit a five-month low on Friday morning after falling 4.8% for the week, putting it on course for its biggest weekly decline since hostilities with Iran began.

Anthropic shares jumped 17% Thursday after briefly surging almost 40% when trading in the frontier AI firm began. By Friday, the shares were down 6% at $91.30, falling back toward the IPO price of $83.

The debut did not ease broader concerns about the AI trade. Investors increasingly questioned whether companies can deliver the returns needed to justify the billions of dollars being poured into the AI buildout.

Capital.com senior market analyst Daniela Hathorn said: “The IPO demonstrated that demand for AI exposure remains strong, but it has not been enough to overcome concerns about elevated valuations, profitability and the sustainability of investment spending.”

She added: “The broader market mood has shifted from buying the dip towards questioning whether the correction has further to run. The weakness in equal-weighted indices suggests that selling pressure is no longer confined to a handful of technology giants, while Europe’s recent outperformance has also faded.”
