## Cotton says Lutnick ignored his October 2025 request to investigate Megaspeed

**Subtype:** fact

- Sen. Tom Cotton (R., Ark.) said Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and his deputies ignored warnings for more than a year about U.S. AI chips being smuggled to China, before the recent chip-shipment allegations became public.

## Many lawmakers call for Lutnick to brief Congress on chip exports

In an interview, Sen. Tom Cotton (R., Ark.), who chairs the Senate Intelligence Committee, said Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and his deputies ignored his October 2025 letter about the need to investigate Megaspeed, along with warnings that U.S. artificial-intelligence chips were being smuggled to China. Cotton said the warnings were ignored for more than a year, before the recent chip-shipment allegations became public. Megaspeed was among the companies he said were suspected of diverting Nvidia chips, and his letter also pushed for stronger export-control rules.

The Commerce Department declined to put Megaspeed on a trade blacklist and did not tighten export restrictions until recently, while trade talks with China were underway. Many lawmakers were calling for Lutnick to brief Congress about chip exports.

Cotton said, “If Howard Lutnick wanted my support, perhaps he should have responded to my many letters warning about these Chinese communist front companies and, more importantly, taking the threats of communist China more seriously.”

Cotton said, “I’m glad that some folks in the administration are finally coming around to a view that I have championed and that others in the administration have as well,” pointing to national security adviser Marco Rubio, who is also secretary of state. “It’s just a bit late, regrettably,” Cotton said, “and I think we need to catalog the damage that’s been done.” The Commerce Department did not respond to a request for comment.

## House data show Commerce approved more than 800,000 chips for China

On Thursday, House lawmakers released data showing that the Commerce Department had approved sales of more than 800,000 chips from Nvidia and Advanced Micro Devices directly to China this year through May 11. The data included sales to some buyers with links to the Chinese military and to a company tied to Megaspeed and Alice Huang.

Many have raised concerns that Jensen Huang was influencing Trump’s approach to chip shipments while having an affair with Alice Huang. A Nvidia spokesman defended the limited shipments made under the H200 program rather than the broader set of shipments covered by the House data. The spokesman said, “The limited shipments we made under the H200 program were legal and authorized by BIS,” referring to the specific chip approved to go to China and the Bureau of Industry and Security, the agency within Commerce overseeing export controls. The spokesman said, “The review and licensing process spanned more than half a year, with approvals subject to strict conditions and limited volumes.”

## Lutnick’s position and the agencies’ investigations

Cotton declined to support Lutnick but stopped short of saying the Commerce secretary should resign or be fired. Cotton said Trump should ask for Lutnick’s resignation if he had lost confidence in the commerce secretary and move quickly to nominate a successor, allowing the Republican-led Senate to confirm a replacement before Democrats take control of Congress in January.

Lutnick’s agency oversees export-control restrictions and licenses to sell chips to countries that pose security risks. The Commerce Department has investigated Megaspeed since 2024, while the Justice Department now leads several probes tied to Alice Huang. Cotton described Huang as a suspected Chinese intelligence asset and said she was a top Megaspeed executive while having an affair with former Nvidia Chief Executive Jensen Huang.

Trump said he stands by Lutnick and supports his approach to dealing with China. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent criticized the Commerce Department for failing to restrict chip shipments to Southeast Asia. Many companies in the region are suspected of routing AI chips to mainland China in violation of U.S. export controls.

## Questions concerning Cotton’s White House contacts

Cotton did not answer directly when asked whether he had raised his concerns about Lutnick and the short confirmation timeline with Trump, White House chief of staff Stephen Miller or anyone else at the White House. Cotton said Trump is “well aware of my view on China policy and the China threat,” a position he said dated back to Trump’s first-term transition in 2016.

## Congress’s push for tougher export rules

In the wake of the allegations surrounding Nvidia and Alice Huang, Cotton said he expects Congress to codify stricter rules on chip exports and semiconductor-manufacturing equipment. He said numerous bills have widespread support in both the House and Senate and could command veto-proof majorities.

Several of the measures would restrict exports to countries including China, mandate tracking of AI chips before shipment and require allied countries including the Netherlands and Japan to cut off manufacturing equipment from China. The proposals are gaining momentum as part of the annual defense-spending bill.

“They will become law one way or the other,” Cotton said, whether Congress approves them in the spending package next month or under a new Democratic-led Congress.
