## World Liberty's foreign investments draw calls for a congressional inquiry

President Donald Trump and his family have made nearly $3 billion during his second term, mostly through cryptocurrency ventures, according to researchers at the left-leaning Center for American Progress. NPR reported that much of the president's money comes from foreign sources, to the alarm of transparency advocates. NPR reporter Rafael Nam reported Nov. 10 that researchers spent months gathering examples of Trump's conflicts of interest before calling on a future Democrat-run Congress to act.

Will Ragland of the Center for American Progress said: "I think you could probably combine all of the past presidents' income made during their time in office, and it wouldn't come close to what we're seeing here." NPR's report also said Trump had made more money while in office than any other U.S. president.

Part of the total came from Trump's memecoin, a type of cryptocurrency usually based on memes or internet jokes. He launched the coin days before his inauguration and made more than $600 million, according to his own disclosures.

Trump held a dinner last year for the coin's top holders, many of whom were foreign, according to NPR. The report said the event was intended to drum up demand. Ragland said the memecoin gave foreign investors — and, in some instances, foreign countries — a way to seek as much influence as they could and use it as leverage for deals they considered helpful. The White House denied that the president was peddling influence.

World Liberty Financial was Trump's other main crypto venture. Researchers who spoke to NPR said the company had earned Trump and his family $1.4 billion in a company just over two years old. Virginia Canter of the anti-corruption group Democracy Defenders Fund asked: "How many individuals are there in America who made $1.4 billion in a business that they just got into?" Canter said there were several troubling foreign investments in World Liberty.

NPR, citing The Wall Street Journal, reported that Sheikh Tahnoon, a member of the United Arab Emirates royal family, invested $500 million in World Liberty. The report described the investment as an extraordinary show of confidence in a new company that had been struggling to gain acceptance.

NPR said the United Arab Emirates had been lobbying for a shipment of advanced AI chips from the United States and received one months later. NPR said the shipment gave the kingdom a boost in its ambition to become an AI power. Canter said she wanted to know why the initial investment was made.

Canter said Congress would be on its strongest grounds, based on the Constitution, to investigate what was involved and the motivation behind the initial $500 million investment. The Trump administration denied any quid pro quo, and World Liberty Financial declined to comment.

NPR reported that other deals made by World Liberty and foreign investors followed: additional deals with Sheikh Tahnoon, and a deal by a company affiliated with World Liberty Financial with the government of Pakistan.

In an interview with CBS's "60 Minutes," Trump said he did not know much about World Liberty or his other business ventures. He said he devoted almost no time to his net worth, did not care about it and believed he had far more money than ever expected and that it had gone up.

Democrats in Congress were signaling concern. Shortly after the election, Robert Garcia, the top Democrat on the House Oversight Committee, posted "lawyer up" on social media.

The researchers who spent months on the issue are calling on the future Democrat-run Congress to act. Canter's request focuses on the United Arab Emirates transaction, while the Trump administration has denied any quid pro quo and World Liberty Financial declined to comment.
