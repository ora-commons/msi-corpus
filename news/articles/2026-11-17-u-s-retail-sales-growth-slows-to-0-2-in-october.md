## Gas-station sales rose 1.5% as average gasoline prices fell 12%

U.S. retail sales rose 0.2% in October, down from a revised 0.8% gain in September, according to a Commerce Department report released Tuesday. The Associated Press linked the slower spending growth to economic uncertainty and high prices that had made more Americans cautious with their money.

Excluding business at gas stations, retail sales rose 0.1%, the Commerce Department said. Oil prices also have been volatile as the flow of exports from the Middle East rose.

The Commerce Department reported a 1.5% increase in gas-station sales in October. That followed a 30.3% increase in September compared with the previous month.

AAA reported that the average price of regular gasoline fell to $3.78 per gallon Tuesday, down 12% from a month earlier. The average remained well above the price paid at this time last year, when a gallon was closer to $3.

Spending was mixed across merchandising categories, according to the Commerce Department report. Sales at online retailers rose 1.2%. Business at furniture and home furnishings stores fell 2.3%, while clothing and accessories stores declined 0.7%.

Restaurants, the lone services category in the data, registered a 0.2% increase. The report noted that the retail sales data offer only a snapshot of consumer spending and do not include activities such as travel and hotel stays.

Taken together, the figures show that the slower overall growth did not mean every retail category declined. Gas-station and online sales rose, while furniture, home furnishings and clothing stores posted declines.

The retail sales release followed new evidence that inflation had put more pressure on Americans in the previous month, according to the Associated Press.
