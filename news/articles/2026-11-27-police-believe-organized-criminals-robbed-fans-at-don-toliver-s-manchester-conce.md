## Police contact European agencies after two similar concert incidents

Greater Manchester Police believe a group of organized criminals carried out robberies during US rapper Don Toliver's concert at the Co-op Live Arena in Manchester on the evening of Sunday, Nov. 22. Police said an unknown substance was released into the crowd during the performance. Officers said similar incidents at two other Don Toliver concerts in Europe were relevant to their inquiries, and the force was in contact with law enforcement working on those investigations. No arrests have been made.

Eighty-nine people displayed symptoms that police believed were linked to exposure to the substance during the performance. The venue was evacuated and a major incident was declared. People in the crowd described a “horrible smell,” and police initially believed the substance was tear gas.

Footage shared on social media appeared to show concertgoers coughing and covering their mouths as they left the venue. Medical officials urged anyone who had become unwell after leaving to get in touch, and some of the dozens assessed by doctors reported eye and respiratory symptoms.

Greater Manchester Police said detectives were reviewing mobile-phone and CCTV footage. Officers were working to contact more than 100 people who attended the gig to assist the inquiries, and the force thanked people who had already helped.

Assistant Chief Constable Tom Harding said: “We’re working to identify those suspects and the substance that we believe was released in the crowd as part of these incidents. All of this work is ongoing at pace.”

Greater Manchester Police said it was working with law enforcement agencies across Europe. Harding said: “There are similar incidents at two Don Toliver gigs in Europe that are also relevant to our inquiries. We’re in contact with law enforcement involved in those investigations.”

Harding also said: “No arrests have been made, but our detectives are working through footage captured on mobile phones and CCTV. There are over a 100 people at the gig we are working to try to contact to assist with our inquiries. We thank all those that have assisted us so far.”

Harding said the force was aware that some people had been affected psychologically during the Co-op Live incident because of the 2017 terror attack at Manchester Arena, in which 22 people were killed by suicide bomber Salman Abedi.

He added: “We also know many more people were impacted on the night and afterwards, physically and psychologically. What happened on Sunday night and the imagery on our TVs and online will bring back painful memories of 2017.”

“Thankfully, we are not dealing with an incident of such a devastating nature or motive; however, we understand the impact this is likely to have caused,” Harding said.

Investigators were still working to identify the suspects and the substance police believed was released into the crowd. No arrests have been made.
