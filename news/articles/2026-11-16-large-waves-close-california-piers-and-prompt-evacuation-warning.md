## Waves above 50 feet were recorded in San Mateo county Saturday

Huge waves struck the California coast again on Sunday, flooding beaches and local streets and closing popular piers.

The National Weather Service's San Francisco Bay Area office warned that surf reaching 35ft (11 meters) was possible, along with dangerous rip currents and sneaker waves. "Stay out of the water, stay off rocks and jetties, and never turn your back on the ocean!" the weather service said on social media.

Waves above 50ft were recorded Saturday in San Mateo county, just south of San Francisco. Surfers took advantage of the giant waves at the famous Mavericks beach near Half Moon Bay.

Farther south, the Santa Cruz county sheriff's office issued an evacuation warning near the coastal town of Capitola, which took the brunt of the powerful surf.

In southern California, several piers — including Ventura, Manhattan Beach, Huntington Beach and San Diego's Pacific Beach — closed during the weekend after being struck by waves.

At La Jolla Cove near San Diego, known for its lounging sea lions, water overflowed the beach and crashed up a staircase leading to the sand. Bystanders near the Pacifica Pier were caught in a sneaker wave in California on Saturday.

Conditions were calming by Sunday afternoon, but forecasters warned that large waves were expected all week.

The ongoing super El Niño has warmed the ocean, leading to an increased volume of seawater.
