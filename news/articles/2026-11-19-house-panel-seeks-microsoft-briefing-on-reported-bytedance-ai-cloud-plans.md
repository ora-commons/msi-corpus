## ByteDance moved to assemble high-end Nvidia chips outside China earlier this year

The House Select Committee on China has requested a bipartisan briefing from Microsoft on reported plans to sell artificial intelligence cloud services to ByteDance, committee Chairman John Moolenaar (R., Mich.) said Wednesday. Risk Journal's Richard Vanderford reported that U.S. lawmakers want the company to brief them, saying they have serious concerns about a U.S. company helping China catch up in the AI race. ByteDance, the parent company of TikTok, moved earlier this year to assemble computing power with high-end Nvidia chips outside China as the company pursued ambitions to become a global AI leader.

In Risk Journal's Nov. 19 Morning Risk Report, David Smagalla wrote that the ouster of Jensen Huang from Nvidia, where he was co-founder and chief executive, was giving new momentum to policymakers who want to regulate artificial intelligence and establish more guardrails around U.S. business activity with China.

The report also recalled that a year earlier Huang had called the term China hawk "a badge of shame" and "unpatriotic."

In recent weeks, the Trump administration's moves on technology policy, together with congressional scrutiny of U.S. companies' China business, have signaled a change of tack, Risk Journal said. The report described the shift as a move away from the administration's pro-industry approach to tech policy, which mostly relied on companies to balance their business interests and national security while selling sensitive technology.

Claims that Nvidia customers funneled billions of dollars of sophisticated chips to Chinese AI companies have collided in recent days with a string of technological breakdowns, Risk Journal said.

The report listed a nationwide ground stop for airlines, bank-account glitches and money-transfer delays among those breakdowns.

Joseph Hoefer, chief AI officer at Monument Advocacy, which represents companies including Netflix and Waymo, said the operating assumption no longer holds. "Companies that assumed the light-touch approach would hold through 2028 are going to have to recalibrate," he said. "National security gives all sides cover to deal in a way that just wasn't there before."

For technology companies with business at stake in China, the recalibration Hoefer described is already underway in Washington. The House Select Committee on China has asked Microsoft for a bipartisan briefing on reported plans to sell artificial intelligence cloud services to ByteDance, and ByteDance moved earlier this year to assemble computing power with high-end Nvidia chips outside China. Huang's ouster from Nvidia has given policymakers who want to regulate artificial intelligence and establish more guardrails around U.S. business activity with China additional momentum.
