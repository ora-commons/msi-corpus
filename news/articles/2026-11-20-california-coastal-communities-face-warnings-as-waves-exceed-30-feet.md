## A 34.1-foot Monterey buoy reading could set a 30-year high

Beaches and piers closed last weekend as large waves crashed into California's coastline, and multiple coastal hazard warnings were issued across the state throughout the week as successive areas of low pressure generated powerful swells and large breaking waves for coastal communities. Coastal warnings extended from last weekend through this week, with alerts due to remain in place until Friday night.

The coastal forecast called for waves of 10 to 18 feet, with local sets of 18 to 22 feet for San Luis Obispo County beaches and Santa Barbara County's central coast.

Offshore National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration buoys recorded wave heights greater than 30 feet, or 9 metres, last Friday.

The Monterey, California, buoy registered 34.1 feet, a reading that would make it the highest reading in 30 years.

Photographs by Fred Greaves for Reuters showed people watching a large wave break over a seawall in Pacifica and a large wave pouring over the seawall. The Guardian described the waves as California's biggest in years.

The Pacific storm systems were bringing unsettled conditions across the western US through the coming days, with heavy rain, mountain snow and strong winds.

Record-breaking rainfall affected San Francisco on Wednesday. Heavy rain also caused 32,000 customers in Sacramento to lose power.

Into the weekend, a further area of low pressure was expected to bring considerable amounts of moisture to southern Oregon and northern California. By Sunday night, more than 100mm (4in) of rain was expected in places, with further snowfall for areas such as Donner Pass across the northern Sierra Nevada mountains.
