## Septuagenarian inmates reflect the aging U.S. death row population

Florida and Texas were scheduled to carry out lethal injections on Thursday, Nov. 19, 2026. Thomas Mitchell Overton, 71, was set to receive a three-drug injection at 6 p.m. at Florida State Prison in Raiford, while Reinaldo Dennes, 70, was set to receive a lethal injection an hour later at a Texas prison. Overton's execution would be Florida's 20th execution of 2026, while the Texas procedure would be the state's ninth.

Florida's planned execution would surpass its 2025 total, and the two septuagenarian inmates reflect the aging of the U.S. death row population. Florida had carried out more than half of the nation's 37 executions in 2026 under outgoing Republican Gov. Ron DeSantis. The planned cases came amid renewed scrutiny of capital punishment following Tennessee's failed execution of Christa Pike in October.

DeSantis, who leaves office in January, has said he wants to bring justice to families who have waited decades for death sentences to be carried out. He signed the death warrants that led to a record 19 executions in Florida in 2025, the most in a single year in the state since the death penalty's reinstatement in 1976. The Nov. 19 execution would surpass that total, with two more Florida executions scheduled for December.

Overton was convicted in 1999 of two counts of first-degree murder and received a death sentence for each in the 1991 killings of Michael and Susan MacIvor and their unborn child during an August 1991 break-in at their Florida Keys home.

The MacIvors were found dead in their Tavernier, Florida, home on Aug. 22, 1991, according to court records. Autopsies determined that both had been strangled and that Susan MacIvor, who was eight months pregnant, had been raped. The medical examiner also said her unborn male child likely survived for about half an hour after her death.

Investigators collected DNA evidence from the scene but initially found no match in the state's database. Overton, who had worked at a gas station near the MacIvors' home, was arrested on an unrelated burglary charge in 1996. Authorities said a sample taken from him matched the sample collected at the home, and he was charged with the killings. Defense attorneys had argued that investigators had planted the DNA evidence.

Dennes, a jewelry designer, was convicted of capital murder in the death of Janos Szucs, a Houston jeweler and diamond dealer who was shot multiple times in his office on Jan. 24, 1996, during a $3.6 million diamond robbery. Court records said Dennes had an office in the same building as Szucs.

At trial, a cleaning woman testified that Dennes arranged for her to open the building's back door after hours. According to court records, she opened the door for Dennes and his brother the night of the shooting and also distracted the security guard at Dennes' request. The guard, who was shot twice but recovered, identified Dennes as his disguised attacker.

Later that evening, police were called to the building and found Szucs' body upon entering his office, according to court filings. The filings said the jeweler's safe was empty, with no signs of the diamonds or the $200,000 in cash he was supposed to have on hand. The diamonds have never been recovered.

Dennes' attorneys later argued there was insufficient evidence to prove that he killed Szucs. Prosecutors noted in a recent court filing that Dennes, who came to the U.S. from Cuba with his family as a child, admitted guilt during a Spanish-language interview with Telemundo in 2022. "I did those things, so I am guilty," Dennes told Telemundo, adding that he regretted taking a man's life, the prosecutor's filing said.

A year after Dennes was convicted, his brother, Jose Albert Dennes, pleaded guilty to murder in the case and was sentenced to 45 years in prison. He was released on parole in 2019.

The Florida and Texas executions were scheduled to proceed while appeals in both capital cases remained pending.
