## White House calls Miller a 'key player' as House Republican dissents

Democratic lawmakers decried Stephen Miller as a "hatemonger", a "xenophobe" and "vile" after President Donald Trump named him White House chief of staff on Friday. Dick Durbin, a Democratic senator from Illinois, wrote on X: "For Trump to promote this hatemonger shows he has learned nothing." Chuck Schumer, who is expected to become Senate majority leader, said Trump "learned nothing from his historic defeat" and "couldn't have made a worse choice" in elevating Miller.

Hakeem Jeffries, the top Democrat in the House of Representatives, who is widely expected to become the chamber's first Black speaker, called Miller "a dangerous xenophobe and reckless extremist who has American blood on his hands", and demanded he be fired. Chris Van Hollen, a Democratic senator from Maryland, called Trump's decision to elevate Miller "another kick in the teeth to the American people" and "a sign of Trump's contempt for the voters".

Gavin Newsom, the outgoing California governor, called Miller "one of the most vile thugs to ever slither into the Oval Office" and said the move was "an insult to every American who voted for change" on Tuesday.

Trump announced the appointment abruptly on his Truth Social platform on Friday afternoon, days after voters overwhelmingly elected Democrats in the US midterm elections, a result that ended the Republican Party's control of Congress. Miller would replace Susie Wiles, whom Trump confirmed had been ousted.

Miller, who has been serving as Trump's deputy chief of staff, has become one of the most controversial figures in the administration. An author of the Project 2025 manifesto, he often makes television appearances to attack Trump's opponents or bolster policies such as ending birthright citizenship, and has suggested the US could invade Greenland. As a policy adviser, Miller is credited as the architect of Trump's mass deportation agenda, a record Democrats pointed to in denouncing his promotion.

Trump's Republican allies and aides celebrated the announcement. Steven Cheung, the White House communications director, called Miller a "Trump OG" and said: "There is no bigger advocate for the Trump agenda than Stephen." Mike Lee, a senator from Utah, said Miller had been "a staunch defender of law and order against illegal invasions and leftist lawlessness". Eric Schmitt, a senator from Missouri, said Miller was exactly what the country needed. "Our country is at a pivotal point and Stephen Miller understands exactly what is at stake," Schmitt wrote on X. Cabinet members including JD Vance, defense secretary Pete Hegseth, health secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr and secretary of state Marco Rubio also praised Miller's record.

Not all Republicans were pleased by the appointment, however. Representative Marlin Stutzman, an Indiana Republican who held his seat, argued Miller "should be fired" earlier this week amid anger over the administration's deportation drive. Asked on Friday what he made of Trump's choice to promote Miller instead, Stutzman told Semafor it was a "horrible decision".

Miles Taylor, who served as chief of staff at the Department of Homeland Security during Trump's first term and later joined the ranks of anti-Trump Republicans, said: "I spent two years working with Stephen Miller. His appointment as White House chief of staff is among the most dangerous personnel decisions in the history of our republic. I say that without reservation."

Asked about the criticism on Friday, the White House hailed Miller as a "key player" delivering Trump's agenda. "He has spearheaded the administration's crime crackdown including in DC and Memphis, record setting deregulation, unleashed American energy, dismantled drug cartels, and secured our border," it said in a statement. "In his new role, he will continue to deliver historic successes for the American people."

Accepting his new role, Miller thanked Trump and said he was "honored beyond words" to serve. "The mission now is more clear than ever: Fight with all of our soul to support our President and defend this magnificent Republic," Miller wrote on X.
