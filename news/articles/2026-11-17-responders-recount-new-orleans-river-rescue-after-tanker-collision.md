## Madison Stone crew rescued 27 people as firefighters searched the water

The American Heritage river cruise boat toppled after the Atlantic Asphalt tanker struck it in the Mississippi River in New Orleans. The Guardian reported that more than 100 people were pulled from the water after Friday’s collision, which injured more than 30 people. The Guardian said the rescue mission was being hailed as the “Mississippi miracle.”

The American Heritage capsized and began to sink after the collision. First responders described hearing screams from inside rooms of the sinking vessel before they freed those aboard and brought them to safety.

William Brunner, the captain-on-watch of the nearby Madison Stone tow boat across from the cruise ship, told Fox8 that he initially heard the Atlantic Asphalt tanker’s distress call on his radio. “The only one that could get there fast enough was a tow boat,” Brunner said. The Madison Stone pushed against the American Heritage to keep it from moving while Brunner and a five-person crew began taking passengers off.

“We had the only spot that [could] get close enough to get people off,” Brunner said. “We live out here on the water and we know that we have to save each other,” he added.

Captain Ryan Williams, who helped develop a rescue plan in the heat of the moment, told Fox8: “No 1 goal was to get everybody out.” He and several crew members hoisted passengers from the American Heritage onto the tow boat once it came within reach. “We heard screaming coming down from … a hole out of one of the rooms and the glass was closed. It was four individuals in there and we [were] able to get them out,” Williams said. The Madison Stone crew ultimately rescued 27 people from the cruise ship.

More than 60 New Orleans firefighters were among the first responders at the scene. Varrick Dyer, captain of the fire department’s hazardous materials unit, told WWLTV: “We train constantly.” He said firefighters had been conducting rope-rescue training on a roof about an hour before the incident. Dyer said his unit trains in rope skills as well as water, elevator, high-rise and automobile rescues.

The firefighters concentrated on passengers who couldn’t move, couldn’t swim or couldn’t help themselves, Dyer said. “We [were] looking for the people who were injured the most, who couldn’t move, couldn’t swim or couldn’t help themselves.” Many passengers were seniors using wheelchairs or crutches, and they could not use firefighters’ rescue ladders. They managed to disembark through a gangway connecting the cruise ship to the dock before the vessel capsized and began to sink.

Fire Department Capt. Grant Rodriguez told WWLTV: “We did not have a lot of time. It went quick. The gangway went. The boat then retracted very hard to the rear. It then pushed away from the wharf all at the same moment … As it rolled, there was a lot of panic. It was cold, people were shivering. The river was extremely cold.”

New Orleans Harbor Police Chief Melanie Montroll said she ordered two officers onto the American Heritage as it began to capsize. “Looking two officers in the face and sending them into a no-win situation, knowing what the potential outcome would be, it’s tough,” Montroll told Nola.com. “I know my officers. I know their families. I know their kids,” she added.

After the rescues, she waded into the river to pull the two injured officers out; one could not stand. “My goal was, one, to make sure that we got as many people, but two that I didn’t leave my officers in the water. That was a big concern of mine. I made that decision to put them on there, but we don’t leave our own,” Montroll said.

American Heritage captain Leonard Stewart remained on the bridge as the vessel capsized, repeatedly calling for help and keeping the boat by the dock so everyone else could disembark safely. The Guardian reported that Stewart had since received widespread praise from the local community. New Orleans Mayor Helena Moreno said last week: “We really also appreciate the captain of the American Heritage who was on site with us. Every moment of the critical time yesterday he did not want to leave his ship.

“In fact, he was the last to get off of the ship yesterday, and so we just wanted to express our gratitude to him for his bravery.”

The responder accounts described the rescue in stages: Brunner’s five-person crew reached passengers from the nearby tow boat and removed 27 people; more than 60 firefighters searched for people who couldn’t move, couldn’t swim or couldn’t help themselves; and two harbor police officers were pulled from the river after being injured. The Guardian reported that more than 100 people were pulled from the Mississippi River following the collision.

Mariners and first responders who took part in the mission said they were thankful “everything came out all right”.
