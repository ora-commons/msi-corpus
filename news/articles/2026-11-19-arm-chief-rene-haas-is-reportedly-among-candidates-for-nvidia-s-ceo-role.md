## Colette Kress remains interim CEO while Nvidia's board searches for a successor

Nvidia's board-hired recruiting firm has reportedly approached Arm chief executive Rene Haas about the chief executive job, as the chipmaker searches for a permanent successor to co-founder Jensen Huang. The Guardian, citing Bloomberg, said Heidrick & Struggles has started contacting potential candidates, and that Haas is among those approached.

The Guardian reported that Huang resigned last week after admitting to an extramarital affair with a woman accused of spying for China.

Haas, 64, joined Arm in 2013 and became chief executive in 2022. He is viewed as the leading contender for Nvidia's top job, having previously worked at the $3.6tn (£2.8tn) company for seven years as general manager of its computing products.

Reports suggest that other potential candidates include Nvidia vice-president Ian Buck, AMD chief executive Lisa Su and Apple's chief hardware officer Johny Srouji.

Arm, which is listed in New York and retains its global headquarters in Cambridge, has about 500 users of its chip designs worldwide, including Nvidia, Apple, Samsung and Qualcomm. The company employs more than 7,000 staff, including more than 3,000 in the UK, and is valued at $196bn. The Guardian described Arm as the biggest tech firm headquartered in Britain.

At Nvidia's quarterly earnings results this week, Nvidia chair Harvey Jones confirmed that the board “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.” Jones said that “while this was an unexpected transition … the board is proactively dealing with the consequences.”

Colette Kress, Nvidia's chief financial officer, was named interim CEO last week and presented the financial results on Tuesday. Nvidia's quarterly revenue had doubled from the same period a year earlier to $115.84bn.

Nvidia has sought to curb speculation that Huang could return to his role. After he said on Tuesday that he would be willing to come back, Huang wrote in a statement: “If the board of directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.”

The statement came after U.S. President Donald Trump and Silicon Valley insiders — including Elon Musk, prominent venture capitalists and Uber co-founder Travis Kalanick — called for Huang's reinstatement.

At a tech conference in Austin on Wednesday, Haas called Huang “a legend.” “What he built at Nvidia is historic,” he said. “You don’t have many champions like that.”

Nvidia also faces an investigation by the US Securities and Exchange Commission over its business in China, the Wall Street Journal reported on Tuesday. On Tuesday, Huang agreed to appear before the House of Representatives foreign affairs committee, the select committee on the Chinese Communist party and the intelligence committee for transcribed interviews in early December.

Nvidia and Arm were approached for comment.
