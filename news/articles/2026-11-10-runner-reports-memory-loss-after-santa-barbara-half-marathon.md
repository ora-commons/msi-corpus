## Police advise unwell participants to seek medical attention

One runner treated in hospital after Sunday's half-marathon in Santa Barbara, California, told local news outlet KEYT-TV that the runner lost consciousness and could not remember parts of the race. The runner, who declined to be identified, said: "It is awfully suspicious." The runner added: "We had people saying they were hallucinating."

The runner also said: "I am not too familiar with heat exhaustion but I have never seen anyone not remembering a long portion of their run."

Santa Barbara police said there is no specific action recommended for the public at this time, and advised participants who feel unwell to seek medical attention and follow guidance from healthcare professionals.

Santa Barbara police are investigating after nearly 30 people sought medical care at the half-marathon held in the city on Sunday, according to the BBC. Officers said they received a report from Santa Barbara Cottage Hospital about race participants who sought medical treatment "for unusual symptoms" after the race ended. In a statement provided to CBS News, the BBC's US partner, police said: "The cause of the reported symptoms has not been confirmed."

Several thousand people ran the half marathon on a hotter-than-usual day in Santa Barbara along the Pacific Ocean. Temperatures reportedly reached 86F (27C) by midday, compared with a typical early November temperature of low 70s.

Cottage Health, which operates the hospital, told CBS it had seen "a larger than expected number of emergency visits" from those who ran the race. Cottage Hospital said it treated 27 people from the half marathon at emergency rooms in Santa Barbara and the nearby city of Goleta. The vast majority — 24 people — were discharged after treatment, while three people were still in hospital as of Monday in stable condition.

Event director David Monico told US media that 15 people were taken from the medical tent to local hospitals because of dehydration and overheating. The two counts cover different stages of treatment: 15 people were transferred from the event's medical tent, while 27 people were treated at hospital emergency rooms in Santa Barbara and Goleta.

The cause of the post-race symptoms remains unconfirmed, police said. The event director attributed the medical-tent transfers to heat and dehydration. One runner described losing consciousness and being unable to remember parts of the race, and said the runner had heard people saying they were hallucinating.

The BBC has contacted the Santa Barbara Police Department, Cottage Health and the half marathon organisers for further comment.
