## UK investigators passed the case to US counterparts under international protocols

British police said the US military had taken Lt Col Brandon Carroll Tyner, 41, into custody. UK counter-terrorism police had detained him in Gloucestershire on 10 November on suspicion of spying for Russia. The arrest formed part of an investigation by UK Counter Terrorism Policing that included the US military and FBI.

Tyner was arrested on suspicion of offences under sections 1 and 3 of the National Security Act and was in custody at a London police station, police said.

Laurence Taylor, assistant commissioner and head of Counter Terrorism Policing, said CTP presented the case to the Crown Prosecution Service after an extensive investigation. The Crown Prosecution Service engaged with the attorney general’s office and American partners before a decision was made to pass the investigation to the US side. Taylor said, “This decision was made in line with existing international protocols.”

“These are extremely serious allegations, and our collective response should send a strong message that we will act swiftly and robustly where we suspect this kind of espionage activity taking place,” Taylor said. He praised the UK investigation team and said CTP would continue supporting US colleagues as they took the matter forward.

Tyner was identified on Friday after British counter-terrorism police secured more time to question him, police said. Officers searched two locations in Gloucestershire during the investigation, the Metropolitan Police said last week. CTP said the investigation was not linked to any other ongoing Counter Terrorism Policing investigations.

US authorities said Tyner joined the military in 2008 as a senior human resources officer and was deployed overseas to Iraq, Qatar and Afghanistan. The Guardian reported that Tyner was based at Imjin Barracks in Innsworth, Gloucestershire, and that the barracks serves as the headquarters of NATO’s Allied Rapid Reaction Corps.

The FBI director, Kash Patel, said in a Tuesday statement, “This FBI has zero tolerance for anyone who betrays their oath to the United States or puts our national security at risk.” He said, “That’s been demonstrated by the 167 counterintelligence and espionage arrests we’ve made over the last 21 months, a 53% increase.”

Patel attributed the army officer’s custody to an espionage investigation and close cooperation among the FBI’s Philadelphia field office, Legat London, UK Counter Terrorism Policing, MI5, UK Defence Counter Intelligence and the US Army Counterintelligence Command. Patel said the partners worked together to identify a potential threat to national security and take swift action.

US Brig Gen Sean Stinchon said the case “should serve as a stark warning to any service member suspected of violating the oath we all take to protect and defend the constitution.” He said service members suspected of betraying the trust of fellow soldiers and jeopardizing collective defense would be identified and justice served. Stinchon described the investigation as an example of the partnership among Army Counterintelligence Command, army prosecutors, the FBI, the Department of Justice and the UK.

Tyner remains in US military custody, and UK Counter Terrorism Policing said it would continue supporting US colleagues as they took the matter forward.
