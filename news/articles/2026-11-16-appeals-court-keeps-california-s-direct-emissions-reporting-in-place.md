## Businesses weigh Scope 3 data work as court battles continue

Businesses were one day away from a California deadline requiring them to divulge greenhouse gas emissions from their sites and electricity use to the state when the appeals court issued its ruling, which found the state's broader emissions reporting rules likely unconstitutional. The decision capped a long-running saga that saw legal challenges from businesses and a series of delays to reporting deadlines.

The ruling leaves two tracks in place. Direct emissions reporting in the United States via the California law appears to stay: the court essentially codified the state's mandate on Scope 1 and 2 emissions — those deriving from direct operations and electricity use — which companies were required to report by Nov. 10. The same appeals court ruled that companies likely should not have to report their broader Scope 3 emissions under the California law, those originating from suppliers and customers. The California Air Resources Board, the agency overseeing the state's climate reporting, scheduled Scope 3 reporting for next year.

The immediate question for businesses is whether to charge ahead with untangling their complex Scope 3 emissions data or sit tight as the court battles play out.

The California rule is especially consequential because it acts as a quasi-national standard, applying to any company doing business in the state that meets a certain financial threshold. The battle over emissions reporting in the U.S. goes back years, including a similar bid from the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission under former President Joe Biden's administration that was ultimately scuttled under President Trump.

Politicians may put additional pressure on limiting greenhouse gas emissions as a newly empowered Democratic majority in Congress is poised to scrutinize corporate climate efforts. Public worries about heightened energy demands and air quality are escalating nationwide amid the largely fossil-fuel-powered data center build-out, adding another layer to the reporting debate as companies weigh disclosure obligations against a shifting regulatory landscape.

Finally, there might be — at least some — clarity after years of delays and litigation. But the Scope 3 question, and the broader court battles behind it, remain unresolved.
