## Aid response combined gift cards, a short-lived supply center and legal-aid referrals

Flatbush community leaders said families displaced from the three affected residential buildings after the November 11 collapse at 1321 Flatbush Avenue have no housing plan once their hotel stays end on November 30. Mutual Aid for Flatbush, a nonprofit coalition in the neighborhood where the building fell, said the city's response had produced temporary lodging without a stated route to permanent housing for tenants from three affected residential buildings.

Advocacy groups said some displaced tenants were sent to “subpar hotels” that were also home to roaches and mice, and that stays were initially set for up to four days, leaving community leaders to push for longer arrangements. Several residents were also sent to hotels in Manhattan, more than an hour from their communities in Brooklyn by public transit.

“It is a ticking timebomb until there are actual solutions,” Mutual Aid for Flatbush said. The coalition said it has been working with families from all three residential buildings affected by the wreckage and has raised about $131,000 through donations from community residents, which it is distributing to the affected families.

A coalition spokesperson said in a statement that “There is complete disorganization and lack of transparency with the displaced tenants.” The coalition also said responsibility for the response appears divided: “It seems as though right now it is a toss-up of responsibility across multiple people.”

The day after the collapse, Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced that the American Red Cross had set up a supply center at the Flatbush YMCA, down the street from the wreckage. The YMCA provided temporary housing, social services and mental health support, and Mamdani's office said it was working with the Department of Buildings on wreckage recovery. The support center operated for less than a week.

The Red Cross said in a statement that “Our hearts remain with the friends, families, and neighbors whose lives were forever changed by the tragic building collapse.” City officials said $60,000 was distributed to affected residents and families through gift cards for urgent needs, according to the New York Times. Flatbush residents said about $3,000 was on each card, and the Flatbush Development Corporation also provided $200 gift cards to families, though it was unclear how many were given out.

Hotel prices in New York City have fluctuated over the past week, with the holidays a prime season for tourism. Some estimates put the average hotel price across the five boroughs above $300 a night, meaning a $3,000 gift card would cover housing for a displaced resident for roughly 10 days or less.

The mayor's office said on social media that it was helping cover the costs of funerals, connecting immigrants with legal aid, working with the health department and the Flatbush Development Corporation, and accepting donations for further support for victims. Mutual Aid for Flatbush said it is seeking transparency on how much the city is receiving in donations and on what plans are available after November 30.

The coalition said many affected residents were undocumented immigrants and that their status made relocation and rehabilitation efforts more difficult. “A lot of the resources that these families are being told they will get do not apply to them yet because of their paperwork situation,” Mutual Aid said.

The coalition said that as recently as Tuesday it had been meeting consistently with the Flatbush Development Corporation, neighboring advocacy groups and other government officials, but that the conversations had produced no result. The Department of Buildings did not respond to a request for comment.

Citing the New York Times, The Guardian reported that the collapse at 1321 Flatbush Avenue stemmed from a weakened foundation as a result of an excavation at a construction site directly next to the apartment. Eight people were killed and 18 were injured, and the collapse exposed a wall in the building attached to 1323 Flatbush. Evacuation orders for all residents of 1323 and 1327 Flatbush were issued.

On November 15, people observed a moment of silence during a rally against negligence by landlords and contractors at the collapse site, and a family member cried during a prayer vigil for the victims. On November 16, men carried the casket of José Luis Caguana Cosquillo, a 26-year-old Ecuadorian construction worker killed in the collapse, outside St Barbara Roman Catholic Church in the Bushwick neighborhood.

“These families will be essentially homeless,” Mutual Aid said. “There is no plan for them to have a home.”
