## Canadian petition calls for ouster of US ambassador Pete Hoekstra

**Subtype:** fact

- Elizabeth May formally presented a petition signed by nearly 290,000 Canadians to Canada's House of Commons on Thursday, seeking the declaration that U.S. Ambassador Pete Hoekstra is “persona non grata.”
- Elizabeth May opened the petition campaign in July 2026 after a woman from Calgary sponsored it.
- Elizabeth May called Hoekstra “the most undiplomatic diplomat” Canada had ever seen.
- The Canadian government must provide a formal response to the nonbinding petition.
- The U.S. State Department had not responded to a request for comment.

## The nonbinding measure requires a formal response from the Canadian government

Canadian Green party member Elizabeth May brought the petition before the House of Commons on Thursday, formally asking the government to declare U.S. Ambassador Pete Hoekstra “persona non grata.” Nearly 290,000 Canadians had signed the measure by the time it was presented. May had opened the campaign in July 2026 after a woman from Calgary sponsored it.

The presentation came as relations between the neighboring countries remained strained. The petition targets what its signatories describe as U.S. diplomatic interference in Canadian domestic affairs. It accuses Hoekstra of “undermining” Canada-US diplomatic relations through repeated statements about Canadian politics that the signatories say are “inconsistent with diplomatic protocol.”

May said, according to Politico: “He has been repeatedly abusive and insulting to Canadians.” She called Donald Trump's envoy “the most undiplomatic diplomat” Canada had ever seen. The U.S. State Department did not immediately return a request for comment.

The Guardian described Hoekstra as a deeply polarizing figure in Canada, particularly for his response to Trump's desire to annex the country and make Canada the 51st US state. Speaking to a Michigan radio station last year, he described the president's rhetoric as a “sign of affection,” questioned why Canadians were offended by what he characterized as a “generous offer.”

Hoekstra also previously described Canada's 2025 federal election — marked by a come-from-behind victory for Prime Minister Mark Carney's Liberal party — as “anti-American.” The ambassador had suggested Canada could be blamed if Republicans performed poorly in this month's midterm elections.

Carney replied, “That's an interesting way of framing it,” when asked about the comments. Democrats swept the November elections, including key Senate races in border states such as Michigan and Maine. The results came as the tariff war between Washington and Ottawa had hit residents hard.

Carney said last month that American tariffs were hurting American and Canadian households. He said Canadian auto exports to the US had fallen 10%, while US exports to Canada had fallen by more than 20%. “US tariffs have upended a trade bargain that's existed for decades,” Carney said. “They've raised costs for American families while putting Ontario workers and businesses at a disadvantage and under a cloud of uncertainty.”

Hoekstra had also accused Canada of potential “political interference” in the US election. Carney called that claim “no merit” and told reporters in Turkey that he was “disappointed that their envoy sees that as his role, to make these suggestions.”

Although the Canadian government is required to provide a formal response to the petition presented by May, the petition is nonbinding. May said in an interview with CTV: “I think the government will quite likely say, ‘No, we're not prepared to declare Pete Hoekstra persona non grata and strip him of his diplomatic credentials.’”

May nevertheless expects the petition to signal to Trump that the Canadian government has the support of its constituents. She hopes the government can use it as leverage against Trump, with Democrats holding power in Congress for the second half of Trump's term.


MSI previously reported that [more than 60,000 Canadians had signed the petition to expel US ambassador Pete Hoekstra](/articles/2026-08-12-more-than-60-000-canadians-sign-petition-to-expel-us-ambassador/). The campaign has since grown to nearly 290,000 signatures, and May has now formally presented the measure to the House of Commons.
