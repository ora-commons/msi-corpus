## Voter poll found government corruption edged cost of living as crucial

NPR observed two online focus groups Tuesday night with 14 voters in North Carolina who voted for Joe Biden in 2020 and then President Trump in 2024, and 12 of them said they have pretty low expectations for what Democrats can achieve with Trump still in the White House.

In these groups — part of the Swing Voter Project, held by messaging firm Engagious and market research recruiting firm Nelson Recruiting — 10 people identified as independents, three as Republicans and one as a Democrat. Almost all these voters said they disapprove overall of the job Trump is doing.

Rich Thau, president of Engagious, moderated these groups. He said these voters chiefly expect that Trump will be a major obstacle and won't give Democrats the ability to get anything done. "And the Democrats themselves," Thau added to NPR, "[the voters] think that they're going to be sort of all over the place, that they're going to maybe focus too much on investigations. … But in terms of actually helping them do the one thing that they desperately want, which is to bring their prices down and make their lives more affordable, they are definitely dubious."

Many of the participants said they felt Democrats' big wins across the country were a sign that voters "want change." Yet the participants were largely pessimistic that Congress would be able to effect change, once Democrats have control of both the House and Senate come January.

Steven, a 32-year-old self-described independent voter who took part under the condition that his last name would not be used, said he expects little. "I think realistically, it just means nothing's going to happen for the next two years," Steven said. "President Trump would veto anything that comes to his desk, and the House and Senate majorities are not veto proof either, so literally nothing will happen for the next two years."

Focus groups are not scientifically significant like polls. Instead, they provide a snapshot of what small groups of voters are thinking.

In this year's midterms, the Voter Poll, conducted by SSRS, found that more than half of voters said government corruption was "crucial" to their vote for Congress. That was a bit higher than the share of voters who said cost of living was "crucial."

This small group of key voters — swing voters in a battleground state — does not want to see accountability for the Trump administration eclipse the economy. The participants said they want to see Democrats get rid of tariffs, lower the cost of everyday goods and stop the war in Iran, which they blame for rising gas prices.

"I'm really concerned they're going to pursue those investigations and such," said Rick, a 63-year-old Republican. "And whether they're true or not, it doesn't really matter because there are people in the middle that are being forgotten and can't afford gas and healthcare."

Deborah, a 56-year-old independent voter, said she watched Democrats try to hold Trump accountable for years, and it hasn't yielded anything yet. "They haven't found anything on Trump yet, so just let it go and move on," she said. "They haven't been able to prove anything, they just keep trying over and over again, and he's still in office."

Kristen, a 38-year-old independent voter, put it more briefly: "I just think it's wasting taxpayer money." Steve, a 39-year-old independent, said investigations largely amount to political brinksmanship, with the parties "butting heads with each other" each cycle. "They're going to just keep going back and forth and they're just looking for anything to mess with Trump for," he said.

The same participants, however, are no fans of Trump despite voting for him in 2024. Most said they think Trump will block Democrats' every effort in Congress. "Trump's not going to want to compromise," said Coronda, a 45-year-old Democrat. "Trump is very set in his ways. He's very stubborn, he's going to yell 'his way or the highway.'"

Steven said he expects Democrats will introduce a lot of bills "mainly for show" once they get into office, but he doesn't expect anything to actually get signed into law. Trump "doesn't want to be embarrassed and he won't let anything pass even if it's a good thing because he doesn't want to give credit to the Democrats," Steven said.

Thau said this is why most of these voters are setting their expectations low ahead of what could be some serious gridlock in Washington. "If you have low expectations, you're not setting yourself up for disappointment," he said. "And I think these voters are not setting themselves up for disappointment. They're going in clear-eyed, expecting President Trump and the Democrats to not get along."
