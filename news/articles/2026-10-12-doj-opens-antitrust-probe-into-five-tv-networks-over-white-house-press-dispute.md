## Federal judge orders temporary restoration of revoked press passes

The Justice Department opened the investigation against ABC, CBS, CNN, NBC, and Fox News over their actions in a White House press-access legal battle, according to NPR's Up First newsletter for October 12, 2026. The probe follows President Trump's decision to bar CNN, MSNOW, and Politico from the White House, citing unfair negative coverage, NPR reported. In a separate action, the administration revoked White House press passes for more than 70 journalists, and the affected news organizations have sued to restore their access.

Press rights groups have denounced the investigation, NPR reported. The Justice Department is citing national security grounds for its actions. A federal judge has temporarily ordered the restoration of the revoked press passes, with a final decision expected by Tuesday evening.

President Trump is also urging Ukrainians to hold wartime elections and replace President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, blaming him for not ending the war, NPR reported. Trump accuses Zelenskyy of causing rising global fuel prices through Ukraine's long-range drone strikes on Russian oil refineries. Trump said he lifted sanctions on Russian diesel temporarily on Friday and announced that both sides had agreed to an "energy ceasefire." Trump has urged Ukrainians to elect a new leader before, and each time he has done so, Zelenskyy's popularity has risen, NPR's Joanna Kakissis told Up First. Through conversations with Ukrainians in Kyiv, a common sentiment emerged: while they see Zelenskyy as imperfect, they believe outsiders should not dictate their electoral process or leadership changes. Most Ukrainians acknowledged that holding elections now would be risky, given the ongoing conflict, with Russia bombing their cities daily. Zelenskyy said Sunday that he was unaware of any plans for a ceasefire requiring both sides to stop attacking energy facilities.
