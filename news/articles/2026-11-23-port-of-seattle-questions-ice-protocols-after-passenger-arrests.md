## Port of Seattle questions ICE protocols after passenger arrests

**Subtype:** fact

- The Port of Seattle said it was in conversation with the Department of Homeland Security over whether ICE breached airport protocols.
- ICE officers arrested Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza and Sofia Hernandez Mejia on Saturday morning while passengers boarded an Alaska Airlines flight bound for Los Angeles.
- ICE alleged that Mendoza and Mejia, both Mexican nationals, had overstayed their visas.
- ICE said other travelers obstructed and assaulted officers during Mendoza's arrest and described the conduct as a federal crime and a felony.
- ICE said Mejia hurled hot coffee at officers and would be charged with assaulting a federal officer.
- Passenger John Gaedeke said the passenger appeared to be holding the coffee when tackled and that he saw no one throw it.

## Passenger disputes ICE account of coffee throwing

The Port of Seattle said it was in conversation with the Department of Homeland Security over whether ICE breached airport protocols, while Alaska Airlines said it was checking whether proper protocols were followed. ICE arrested Mendoza and Mejia on Saturday morning local time as passengers boarded the airline's flight to Los Angeles at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport. ICE alleged that the two Mexican nationals had overstayed their visas.

During Mendoza's arrest, ICE said, "other travellers began to obstruct and assault our law enforcement officers." The agency described that conduct as "a federal crime and a felony."

ICE separately said Mejia "hurled hot coffee on the officers" and would be charged with assaulting a federal officer. Mendoza and Mejia are father and daughter. Olia Catala, a lawyer for both, told local NBC affiliate King 5 News that they had "absolutely no criminal history." She added: "They're not fugitives. They're going through a legal process."

The family has reportedly said the ICE officers were in plain clothes and approached Mendoza and Mejia without identification. Catala separately said: "You can imagine if somebody's running at you who is not dressed as an officer, you don't know who they are." She described "a lot of panic, a lot of chaos, officers grabbing her, coffee spilling."

Eyewitness video appears to show two agents wearing masks, plain jackets and khaki trousers. John Gaedeke, who was also boarding the plane, told Reuters he thought the agents were passengers and that they "didn't have any identification." Gaedeke said the agents "pointed and then they said now, and then they just lunged forward, didn't identify themselves" before "tackling" a man.

"Coffee definitely went flying, but to me, it looked like the passenger was holding it in their hand, and when they were tackled, it went everywhere. I didn't see anyone throw coffee," Gaedeke said.

The Port of Seattle, which owns and runs the airport, said it was "outraged" about the incident. The Port and Alaska Airlines said they were looking into whether proper protocols were followed. The Port said: "Immigration enforcement actions at SEA are conducted without the Port's participation or knowledge."

The Port said it had "continually asked" about protocols, that local federal immigration authorities had previously agreed to protocols at SEA, and that it was in conversation with DHS about whether any of those protocols were breached.

According to the Port's website, federal agents have access to airport property, port officials cannot interfere with their operations, and the Port has not historically been given advance information about enforcement on its property.

Alaska Airlines said it was not given "appropriate notice of the enforcement action" and that it was "concerned" about the arrests. Alaska recognized "how distressing this incident has been for employees and guests." The airline said it was "required to comply with applicable laws and regulations when interacting with law enforcement officials." It added: "We do not direct or coordinate immigration enforcement activity, and we remain committed to protecting the privacy and safety of our guests and employees wherever possible."

Katie Garrow, executive secretary of MLK Labor, which represents flight attendants, pilots and airport employees, said the incident had been "so upsetting and disturbing to workers" that four of the six crew members on the flight chose not to fly. Some anti-ICE protesters also gathered at the airport after the incident.

Washington's Democratic governor, Bob Ferguson, told a press conference on Saturday that "ICE is completely out of control." He said, "We need a system of immigration enforcement, border security that is effective, ethical, and humane and ICE is not it. It is beyond reform."

BBC News reported that deportations of people found to be in the US illegally have increased significantly under Donald Trump's presidency, while ICE's tactics have made many Americans uncomfortable, including many who support stricter restrictions broadly. The report said immigration raids have caused controversy in a number of cities, including Minneapolis, where ICE agents killed Renee Good and Alex Pretti earlier this year.

The competing accounts over the coffee remained unresolved in the reporting. ICE said Mejia would be charged with assaulting a federal officer, while Gaedeke said it appeared the passenger was holding the coffee when tackled and that he saw no one throw it.
