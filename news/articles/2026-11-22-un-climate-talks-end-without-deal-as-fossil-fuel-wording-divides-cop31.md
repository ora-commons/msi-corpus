## EU and Panama reject draft; Palau says it will support the text

Negotiators at the COP31 United Nations climate summit in Antalya, Turkey, ended their final session Sunday without agreement on a cover decision, the document each annual meeting seeks to produce to give direction on how countries around the world should fight climate change.

The talks could not overcome impasses on whether that final document should mention fossil fuels. The burning of fuels such as coal, oil and gas is what causes global warming.

After reviewing a draft of the agreement released by co-host Turkey, a number of countries spoke up during the final meeting Sunday to say they could not accept a deal that failed to include wording on fossil fuels. Others spoke to reporters on the sidelines of the plenary sessions. Turkey and Australia are the co-hosts of this year's meeting.

Chris Bowen, COP31's president of negotiations and Australia's minister of climate change and energy, confirmed the outcome to the meeting.

"Unfortunately, we were unable to reach agreement on a cover decision," Bowen said. "And that's disappointing, very disappointing."

Looking back on the session, Bowen said many parties had negotiated in good faith but held firm positions on a few key questions.

"Ultimately, looking back, many parties were people of good faith who had very strong red lines on a couple of key issues ... An obvious issue is the transition away from fossil fuels where some parties insisted that it must be mentioned," he said.

He added that the shift away from fossil fuels is proceeding regardless of the text: "Transition away from fossil fuels in the real world is inevitable. It's happening, it's happening at speed, and it's irreversible."

Darragh O'Brien, Ireland's climate minister, told the meeting that the European Union and its member states reject the document.

"I want to clearly state that the European Union and its member states object to this cover decision in both its iterations, and that we're saying that on behalf of our citizens, our member states, because these decisions go nowhere near the level of action or ambition that are required for us to tackle climate change, it is unconscionable that we would have a climate act that makes no reference to fossil fuel," O'Brien said.

Ana Aguilar, Panama's chief climate negotiator and a senior leader in the Climate Change Directorate at Panama's Ministry of Environment, said Panama could not support the decision because "it repeats previously agreed to commitments without closing the gap between reality and what science demands," because it leans too heavily on technologies such as carbon capture and storage "instead of calling for deep changes," and because "it weakens language agreed in previous decisions."

"This is an issue of justice," Aguilar said. "This is a setback, a rollback."

The Marshall Islands delegation said the countries gathered in Antalya had expected a far stronger response on emissions, on protecting vulnerable populations and on finance.

"We came here expecting the world to deliver a comprehensive and urgent response on reducing emissions, on protecting our people and on providing the finance. To do this, we have been let down on all sides," the delegation said. "We were presented with the worst possible response to overshoot. A response that will enhance oil and gas. We will not be forced to accept false solutions. We will not compromise our future to benefit fossil fuel interests. We will not accept an outcome that undermines our security and threatens our future."

The United Kingdom government said the draft did not meet the moment.

"The United Kingdom does not believe the cover decision proposed by the Presidencies meets the moment," the government said. "We believe it moves us backward on the science when we must accelerate implementation. It has compromised where it should have presented bold collective ambition on climate action."

Not every delegation opposed the text. Steven Victor, Palau's minister of agriculture, fisheries and environment, said his country would back it while seeking changes on one element.

"Palau will support this text, not perfect as it is. We support this because there's clean electrification, there's ocean, there is finance," Victor said. "The only thing that we had issues with, and we were willing to engage in trying to improve it, is carbon capture. But unfortunately, others all forget about all these other good things and focus on one aspect of the negotiation."

Victor also said the meeting produced results beyond the cover decision. "Although there's no cover decision, there are these other work programs that got approved today and the last couple of days," he said. "So it's not a failure."


The deadlock closed out a summit that had already run past its scheduled finish: negotiators stayed into an added day of talks that began Saturday, after a draft of the final agreement drew condemnation for failing to mention fossil fuels, as [MSI reported](/articles/2026-11-21-cop31-talks-run-into-overtime-as-fossil-fuels-stay-out-of-draft-deal/).
