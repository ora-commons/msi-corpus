## May 2025 paint spraying left a U.S. plane temporarily inoperable

U.S. prosecutors allege that the April 11, 2026 incident at Shannon Airport near Limerick, Ireland, caused tens of millions of dollars in damage and jeopardized critical military operations, according to a court filing. U.S. prosecutors said protesters had carried out several breaches at the airport in recent years.

In May 2025, three people drove a van through Shannon Airport's perimeter fence. Later that month, a U.S. airplane was sprayed with paint, making it temporarily inoperable.

BBC News described Shannon Airport as a key hub for U.S. military personnel and a frequent scene of anti-war protests. President Donald Trump used the airport during his September visit to Ireland.

U.S. officials charged the Irishman Daithi O'Corrain, 48, with destruction of government property after he allegedly snuck onto the airport's tarmac and used chemicals and various tools, including an axe, to damage a $60m (£45m) U.S. Air National Guard C-130 Hercules parked at Shannon Airport near Limerick. The charge carries a maximum sentence of 10 years in prison. An accompanying BBC News image caption, with the FBI listed as the image source, described the aircraft as effectively destroyed by the vandalism.

In filings unsealed by prosecutors in New York on Tuesday, FBI agents argued that the act jeopardized U.S. national security. The agents said the plane was parked at the airport while en route to another country on a U.S. military assignment.

Prosecutors said in the court filing, "As alleged, Daithi O'Corrain carried out a deliberate, planned attack against a US military aircraft, causing tens of millions of dollars in damage and jeopardizing critical military operations."

FBI Assistant Director in Charge James Barnacle Jr said, "This was not an act of spontaneous vandalism," explaining O'Corrain "spent months researching the aircraft, acquiring tools and materials, and preparing to maximize the destruction he could inflict."

Officials said O'Corrain planned extensively for the alleged 11 April 2026 raid and brought tools with him for the demolition, which took place over several hours. Officials said he entered the plane by breaking a locking mechanism, then used straps to close the doors before covering the windows.

The U.S. indictment said: "O'Corrain then proceeded to inflict severe damage to the interior of the Aircraft by, among other things, cutting wire bundles and hydraulic lines within the Aircraft, crimping a liquid oxygen line within the Aircraft, destroying every gauge in the Aircraft's cockpit, and puncturing the Aircraft's air ducts."

The indictment also said O'Corrain used gallium, a substance that can erode aluminum and steel; ball bearings that he poured into the fuel tank; and an axe to puncture the aircraft's exterior.

The charges said O'Corrain had planned well because he avoided cutting a line that would have risked causing a massive explosion.

Airport crew caught O'Corrain and filmed him standing on top of the aircraft. A Press Association image, described as a handout from the U.S. Department of Justice, showed O'Corrain standing on the rear of the aircraft. He was arrested at the scene and charged by Ireland's national police with causing criminal damages.

The Limerick Post reported that O'Corrain is from Cork City. Supporters of O'Corrain have described him as an anti-war activist.

The Associated Press reported that O'Corrain was not currently in U.S. custody and that it was unclear whether he had a lawyer who could speak on his behalf.
