## Brinkema issued an order from the bench Friday, plaintiffs' lawyers said

At 250 feet, Donald Trump's proposed memorial arch for Washington would be twice the height of the Lincoln Memorial.

Plaintiffs' lawyers said U.S. District Judge Leonie Brinkema issued a Friday order from the bench in Alexandria, Virginia, in the Arlington County Board's lawsuit challenging the project, keeping construction work on hold indefinitely. The injunction Brinkema issued has no specified expiration date.

Brinkema, whom Democratic President Bill Clinton nominated to the bench, became the second federal judge to block the Trump administration from building the arch. Two days earlier, U.S. District Judge Tanya Chutkan, whom Democratic President Barack Obama nominated to the bench, agreed in Washington to extend a temporary restraining order that had blocked the administration from breaking ground, keeping the project on hold until Dec. 3.

Chutkan initially blocked the arch's construction on Nov. 5, shortly after a National Capital Planning Commission hearing ended with the commission giving the arch its final approval in an 8-4 vote. Dozens of witnesses opposed the construction at the hearing.

The case before Chutkan was filed on behalf of Vietnam veterans and an architectural historian, while the case before Brinkema was brought by the Arlington County Board. The Arlington County Board claims Trump's project would exacerbate traffic congestion and jeopardize public safety in the county, which is across the Potomac River from Washington.

Plaintiffs' attorney Kate Talmor said in a statement after Friday's hearing: “We are pleased that the court has recognized the irreparable harm that will come to people who live and work in Arlington and stopped the massive vanity project while our case is heard and considered by the court.”

Members of the Kennedy family filed a separate lawsuit last month, citing the impact the structure would have on Arlington National Cemetery, where family members are buried.

The arch is one of several projects around Washington that President Donald Trump, a Republican, has pushed.

Brinkema's Friday order added a separate hold with no specified expiration in the Arlington County Board case.
