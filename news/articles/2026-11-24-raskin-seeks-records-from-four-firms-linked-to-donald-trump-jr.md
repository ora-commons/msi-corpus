## The inquiry spans defense contracts, vaping rules, prediction markets and firearms

Rep. Jamie Raskin sent letters on Monday to four companies seeking records of their relationships with 1789 Capital, the venture capital and investment firm Donald Trump Jr. joined as a partner after the 2024 election. The incoming House Judiciary Committee chairman demanded communications and other materials tied to 1789 Capital or investments from the firm by Dec. 7.

Raskin alleges 1789 Capital might have had advanced knowledge of federal contracting decisions, raising questions about whether the firm benefited from access to insider information. Trump Jr. has denied allegations that he profited from White House connections after joining 1789 Capital. He said that, as a private citizen, he has the right to make investments and does not make decisions based on insider information.

Alexa Henning, a spokeswoman for 1789 Capital, called the accusations “personal attacks untethered to actual evidence.” She said the firm's response to an earlier Raskin inquiry in September was “pointing out in detail the many flaws in his understanding of how 1789 has operated and a number of basic factual errors.”

The requests come as Raskin and the incoming committee chairs for the House and Senate say they plan to focus on potential corruption inside and outside government. Democrats are moving to launch investigations as soon as they take control of Congress in January and have said they plan to subpoena private corporations with ties to the Trump administration. The Wall Street Journal reported that many such corporations have retained counsel from Washington-based firms.

Raskin's letter says Hadrian Automation, which builds and operates AI-powered factories serving American defense industries, donated to Trump-aligned political-action committees and received investment from 1789 Capital. The letter says the company later won almost $1 billion in contracts from the U.S. government, including work to mass-produce components for American submarines. Hadrian Automation did not respond to a request for comment.

Raskin's letter says Juul, an electronic cigarette company, received 1789 Capital investment and later benefited from the Trump administration's lighter regulations and eased restrictions on the vaping industry. Juul said in a statement: “we intend to cooperate with any Congressional effort to understand our company, our commitment to combating underage vaping, and our mission to help adult smokers of combustible cigarettes transition completely away from smoking.”

The Wall Street Journal has reported that 1789 Capital invested around $300 million in Polymarket, a prediction market. According to Raskin's letter, Polymarket allegedly benefited after the Justice Department and Commodity Futures Trading Commission closed investigations into the company in July 2025, clearing the path for the company to acquire a regulated derivatives exchange and re-enter the derivatives market. Raskin's letter also says Polymarket had not disclosed, at the time it benefited, that 1789 Capital had invested in it. Polymarket did not respond to a request for comment.

Raskin's letter says GrabAGun, an online firearms and ammunition store, might potentially profit from a proposed Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives rule that would allow gun distributors to ship directly to customers' homes. The letter says 1789 Capital helped take the company public. Donald Trump Jr. currently sits on GrabAGun's board. GrabAGun said Raskin's allegations were baseless. “ATF’s review of its regulations was well-known across the firearms industry, and the agency published numerous proposed rules for public comment. GrabAGun’s only input came through public comments on several of those proposals.”

Raskin's letters state that “The House Judiciary Committee is committed to ensuring that public officials and their families cannot profit from and exploit inside government information and insider access.” Trump Jr. has pushed back on the alleged conflicts of interest and corruption accusations. Asked earlier this month at an Axios event if he understands why there might be a perception of corruption surrounding 1789 Capital because it invests in companies that receive government contracts, Trump Jr. said, “I can see what they’re saying. It doesn’t make it so.”

White House counsel Will Scharf said last week that the administration would not comply with postelection oversight demands from the minority party and that any such requests would not carry over into the next Congress when Democrats assume the majority. Late Friday night, President Trump signed an executive order requiring federal contractors to inform the administration of congressional inquiries into their work with the executive branch. The order instructed all executive branch agencies to obtain White House permission before employees testify before Congress. Contractors could lose business with the federal government if they did not comply. Democrats have questioned the order's legality.

Susanne Sachsman Grooms, who leads Cooley's legal congressional investigations practice, said the order was unprecedented and “places federal contractors in the middle of competing demands from coequal branches of government.”

Separately, Rep. Robert Garcia (D., Calif.), the incoming House Oversight Committee chairman, sent records-preservation letters about Trump family business ventures, the Department of Homeland Security's immigration enforcement, White House construction projects and documents related to Jeffrey Epstein, whom The Wall Street Journal described as a convicted sex offender. Garcia's letters to Donald Trump Jr. and Eric Trump allege that they profited from their father's presidency as co-founders of the family cryptocurrency venture World Liberty Financial.

Raskin and incoming Foreign Affairs Chairman Rep. Gregory Meeks (D., N.Y.) are seeking records on whether the Trump administration's response to Nvidia's alleged chip smuggling into Beijing was influenced by Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick's family investments in the company. Meeks also asked the State Department to preserve records for agencies within its jurisdiction.

In the Senate, Sen. Richard Blumenthal (D., Conn.), incoming chair of the Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations, sent letters targeting cryptocurrency firms and major law firms connected to the Trump administration. Raskin last week also asked agency heads to preserve records and messages sent through platforms such as the Signal app, which can delete text messages after a set time. Raskin said that deleting the messages violates government record-preservation laws.

The immediate stage of the inquiry is documentary: Raskin has asked for records by Dec. 7, and 1789 Capital, Juul and GrabAGun have responded while Hadrian Automation and Polymarket have not. The letters do not determine whether any company received favorable treatment or whether 1789 Capital benefited from insider information; they seek information for incoming congressional committees whose chairs have said they will focus on potential corruption inside and outside government.
