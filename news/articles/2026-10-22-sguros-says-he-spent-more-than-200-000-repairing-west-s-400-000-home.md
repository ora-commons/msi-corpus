## Sguros described a trash-filled, unstable house with a partially collapsed roof

Brian Sguros said in an affidavit that he took possession of Dennis West’s home in August 2022, less than a month after finding West dead inside the Lauderhill property during a welfare check. Sguros said he spent more than $200,000 of his own money on repairs over the next 2 1/2 years. The property was a two-story, $400,000 house. According to court documents in a related civil case, the house was in a tidy neighborhood just outside Fort Lauderdale.

Sguros said the house had been filled with trash, that its roof was partially collapsed and that the structure was unstable.

In another court document, Sguros said he took over the property “believing that by cleaning, securing, and restoring a dangerous and abandoned residence he was acting lawfully and responsibly to protect the surrounding community.” He also said he acted to “prevent squatters from taking control of it” and argued that he made no attempts to hide what he was doing.

Sguros, a Lauderhill police officer, was arrested this week and charged with five felonies, including official misconduct and criminal use of deceased personal identification. He attended a brief hearing Wednesday and left the courthouse as reporters gathered outside. Messages seeking comment were left Thursday with Sguros and one of his attorneys. He has been suspended from his Lauderhill job.

Investigators with the Broward County Sheriff’s Office accused Sguros of carrying out a “complex scheme” to take control of the $400,000 home. They said he used West’s identification to make it appear West was still alive, paid taxes and utilities at the home in West’s name, and forged West’s signature to obtain permits for work.

Investigators said Sguros told the county medical examiner that he was friends with West, that West had died of natural causes and that West had no family. The sheriff’s office said the claim that West had no family was false.

Investigators also said Sguros used his access to law-enforcement databases to find information about West.

In February 2025, Sguros filed paperwork with the county appraiser stating that he possessed the home.

According to court documents in a related civil case, Sguros’s attempt to take possession became known after a real estate group bought the house from West’s relatives in 2025. After the group acquired deeds to the property, Sguros filed a civil case against the company seeking reimbursement for the money he spent on repairs.
