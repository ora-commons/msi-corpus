## Trump gave no details and had not informed his own officials

Donald Trump threatened on Friday to impose “tremendous penalties” on American technology companies that build datacenters outside the United States, but he gave no details and said he had not yet informed his own officials about the threat. The threat came during a White House press conference, where Trump said he wanted datacenters built by US companies to stay on US soil. He did not describe an enforcement plan.

## Chip-access rules provide separate policy context

On Monday, the Trump administration issued new rules restricting remote access to advanced US computing chips and servers. The administration said the rules were designed to limit China’s ability to use US chips to train its artificial intelligence models.

A House committee said on Wednesday that the United States had signed off on sales of nearly 900,000 AI chips to China in six months. The Guardian reported that China’s ability to obtain advanced AI chips made by Nvidia had drawn attention in recent weeks after Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang was fired over his affair with a woman alleged to be spying for China. The report noted that Trump is an investor in Nvidia.

Trump’s Friday threat came after both the new chip-access rules and the House committee’s disclosure. He gave no details about the penalties and had not yet informed his own officials, leaving the threat without stated details.

## Domestic siting rationale and local response

Trump said the United States would not allow companies to build datacenters abroad. “We’re not allowing datacenters to go into other countries, because China will take all of them, and we’re not allowing that to happen. And we’re going to put tremendous penalties on companies that try to do it,” he said.

Trump also said Americans should welcome datacenters and urged communities to extract concessions from developers, repeating the position he had made before the midterms. “You ought to take advantage of the datacenters. Make them pay you, make them pay your community, but don’t shut them off,” he said. “We want them to build it here.”

The Guardian reported that, with many Americans turning against the datacenters being built near their homes, the US tech industry was promising to take fewer government handouts, be more transparent and give more money to towns and cities willing to allow server warehouses to be built.
