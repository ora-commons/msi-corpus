## Code yellow prompted staggered arrivals at Fort Meade headquarters

The National Security Agency said Tuesday that some internal systems were affected by “temporary degradation” during scheduled routine maintenance. In a statement, the agency said “mission-critical capabilities remain fully operational” and that its ability to defend national security continued. The NSA said its infrastructure is built with “multiple layers of redundancy.” The statement did not name the affected network, give a cause or duration, or say whether service had been fully restored.

The New York Times and CNN, citing people familiar with the matter, reported that NSANet was offline Tuesday. The network is used to send and receive classified information, including top-secret material. The reports said staff at the NSA’s headquarters in Fort Meade, Maryland, had been told to stagger their arrivals while the agency assessed the problem.

The New York Times reported that a recorded message on an NSA employee information line described a “code yellow” for the day shift. The alert was the second-highest level in a three-tier system. Non-emergency staff were told not to come in before noon, while emergency personnel were to report as scheduled.

The Wall Street Journal, citing one person familiar with the situation, reported that the outage stemmed from a software update to patch the system and that the network would be down only temporarily. The NSA’s public statement did not identify the update as the cause or say how long the problem lasted.

The New York Times and CNN, citing people familiar with the matter, said there was no sign so far that the outage was caused by a cyber-attack or other malicious activity. The New York Times, citing one person, reported that the network had been unstable for weeks and had frustrated staff. A former senior NSA cybersecurity official, who had no knowledge of the incident beyond public reports, speculated that several software patches may have been applied at once and something had gone wrong.

The outage came amid a recent run of technology failures, including a nationwide ground stop of flights, a Microsoft outage and disruption to banking services. None had been tied to a cyber-attack, though the causes of some had not been fully explained. Officials said the ground stop was not the result of malicious activity by a foreign actor.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said officials were investigating whether contractor error or a faulty router was responsible for the flight ground stop.

Asked in Manila on Tuesday about the constant outages, Trump played them down as inevitable, saying, “Things like that happen.” Trump said he had spoken with banks and that the situation would be “very much under control,” adding that Americans traveling before Thanksgiving were safe. Trump had not commented directly on the NSA outage.

Hakeem Jeffries, the House Democratic leader, called for the Trump administration to send officials to brief lawmakers on Capitol Hill about the recent nationwide technical and infrastructure outages. Jeffries said the public deserved more transparency around the widespread disruptions.

The NSA’s public account left several questions open. The agency confirmed temporary degradation during routine maintenance but did not say whether all service had been restored. Reporting identified NSANet as the affected network, while one source attributed the problem to a software update and another said the system had been unstable for weeks. No sign had emerged of a cyber-attack or other malicious activity, but the precise cause and duration remained undisclosed publicly.
