## Justice Department asks court to dismiss Alaska Senate certification case

**Subtype:** fact

- The Justice Department asked the 9th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals to declare its lawsuit challenging Alaska’s certification of the U.S. Senate election outcome moot and dismiss the case.
- Justice Department attorneys asked the appeals court to overturn the lower court’s dismissal to erase the administration’s loss, then dismiss the case as moot.
- Alaska’s Republican-controlled state government called the administration’s dismissal move legally “meritless.”
- Alaska’s state government filed papers supporting dismissal of the initial case.
- Mary Peltola won an outright majority to become Democrats’ 56th senator and deliver the party’s ninth Senate pickup in the midterm elections.
- Mary Peltola’s margin of victory exceeded the number of votes cast for Daniel J. Sullivan Jr.

## Alaska state government called the administration’s move legally “meritless”

The Justice Department’s move would, if granted, end the federal lawsuit seeking to block Mary Peltola’s certification and keep her from taking office. Two days after the race was decided, the department asked the 9th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals to declare the case moot, vacate the lower court’s order and remand for dismissal. The request followed the administration’s loss before the lower court and came after Peltola won the race outright.

Justice Department attorneys proposed a two-step route that would erase the administration’s loss before closing the case. They asked the appeals court to overturn the lower court’s dismissal and then dismiss the suit anyway, writing that the court “should hold that this case is moot, vacate the underlying order, and remand for dismissal on that ground.”

Alaska’s state government, currently controlled by Republicans, objected to the administration’s dismissal request, called it legally “meritless” and filed papers to help dismiss the initial case.

Mary Peltola won an outright majority in the race to become the Democrats’ 56th senator and the party’s ninth Senate pickup in the midterm elections. Peltola’s margin of victory exceeded the number of votes cast for Daniel J. Sullivan Jr.

The legal challenge centered on the presence on the ballot of a second candidate with the same name as the incumbent, Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan. The Alaska Supreme Court had already ruled that the second Sullivan’s candidacy was legal and that he could appear on the ballot. Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. was listed with no party preference, which distinguished him from the senator, who was identified as a Republican and as the incumbent.

The Justice Department filed the lawsuit at President Donald Trump’s direction, saying a Peltola victory should not be certified until an investigation could determine whether voters had been confused. The administration never explained how such an investigation could take place given the secrecy of the ballot.

The case was filed before The New York Times reported on texts and other documents that appeared to show Peltola’s campaign had been involved in recruiting Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., a retired schoolteacher from the tiny fishing community of Petersburg. Dan J. Sullivan has long insisted that he ran on his own, and Peltola has denied any involvement.

President Donald Trump also demanded Peltola’s prosecution, and federal investigators arrived in Alaska before Election Day.

The Associated Press reported, “Tricks involving decoy candidates such as the second Sullivan have been a staple of U.S. politics for decades, and Trump’s allies have also used them, though occasionally the schemes do lead to criminal charges.”

The filing asks the appeals court to declare the case moot, vacate the lower court’s order and send it back for dismissal. It came after the administration’s loss below and after Peltola’s outright victory.
