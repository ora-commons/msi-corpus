## 45 sign letter requesting release, citing prolonged detention without clear individual justification

The Folkston D Ray James ICE processing center, a former state prison in southeast Georgia run by the private prison company GEO Group under a contract with ICE, is the site of a mass hunger strike involving an estimated 73 detained immigrants, advocates told the Guardian this week.

Monica Whatley, an organizer in Georgia with the Campaign to Shut Down the Folkston ICE processing center, estimated that 73 people had joined the protest. The strike targets both the length of their confinement and conditions inside the facility, according to an open letter signed by 45 men this summer and published this week. Some of the men participating in the hunger strike also released a video and the open letter, requesting that they be released while they continue pursuing their asylum and immigration cases.

"Many of us have been separated from our families," the letter reads. "We have missed births, funerals, years of our lives while sitting behind fences without having committed any crime." The letter further reads: "We come from different countries, races, believes [sic], and background, but we share one thing in common: we came to the United States seeking protection and have been detained since then."

The men say in their letter that they have been locked up for a long time, between 16 months and almost three years, from the moment each arrived in the U.S. "yet have never been accused of any crime." They argue they should be released while their cases proceed through the immigration court system.

"Many of us have tried every available legal path to seek release from detention" but without success, they say, adding that their letter is for those who believe in "fairness and human dignity." "Innocent people can remain in detention for very long periods, without any individualized finding that they are dangerous or likely to flee," the signatories complain.

The hunger strike appears to transcend nationality, language and race, according to the detainees' video, letter and report. The majority of the men who signed the letter are asylum seekers from a wide range of countries, including Russia, China, Sudan, Afghanistan, Nepal and others. The majority of the detainees in Folkston who signed the letter are requesting asylum.

The open letter includes the names, government-issued alien numbers, countries of origin and time in detention for the 45 men who signed it. All have been in ICE detention for at least one year. A Chinese man named Liang Sonji, who signed the letter, came to the U.S. and requested asylum in October 2023 and has been detained since.

One of the lead organizers of the hunger strike, a Russian asylum seeker named Idris Makhmudov, has reportedly been placed in solitary confinement this week, according to Monica Whatley. The letter was drafted by Makhmudov.

Advocates told the Guardian that one participant is a Yemeni asylum seeker with lung cancer and a U.S. citizen child.

A separate document, written by two Russian asylum seekers detained at Folkston — Alexei Kovalko and Alexander Borovkov — outlines what they describe as "violations" inside the facility, including allegations of substandard medical care, threats by GEO Group guards, moldy food and a lack of due process. The two men allege that an unnamed medical staffer told one of them: "I have no obligation to treat you; the only thing that matters to me is that you don't drop dead." They also describe moldy food, foul-smelling water, leaky ceilings, plumbing problems and what they characterize as xenophobic and rude behavior by GEO Group employees, including frequent threats of solitary confinement.

"We understand that temporary detention may sometimes be necessary to verify identity or conduct background checks," the open letter reads. "But detention lasting one year, two years, or longer without a clear individual justification raises serious questions about fairness and reasonableness."

GEO Group referred all questions to ICE. The Department of Homeland Security, ICE's parent agency, did not respond to detailed questions about the allegations or requests for comment.

The Folkston facility opened in 1997 and began holding people in ICE custody in 2018. Last year, the Florida-based, for-profit GEO Group extended its contract with ICE to expand the facility's capacity. ICE data from July shows roughly 2,200 people were detained there at that time.

The hunger strike is the latest in a series of protests inside ICE detention centers since the second Trump administration took office in January 2025. Earlier this year, a group of detainees inside the Delaney Hall facility in New Jersey — also operated by GEO Group — launched a hunger strike, and protesters outside that facility, demonstrating in solidarity with the hunger strikers, were met with force by ICE and local officials, making international headlines.

The Guardian has separately reported on individual hunger strikes across the ICE detention network and on what it described as ICE's secretive response, including findings that ICE has quietly sought court orders to provide "involuntary medical procedures" on 19 hunger strikers since January 2025, including force-feeding, a practice widely seen as torture.

ICE, along with other federal agencies and local officials deputized to carry out immigration arrests, has been quietly increasing arrests nationwide, and rising numbers of immigrants and asylum seekers are being detained in federal facilities like Folkston as a result, according to the Guardian's reporting.
