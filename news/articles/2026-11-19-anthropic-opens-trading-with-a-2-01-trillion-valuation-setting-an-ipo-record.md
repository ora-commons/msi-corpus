## Founders hold 50.1% voting power as the public-benefit pledge faces profit pressure

Anthropic, the Silicon Valley startup behind the popular Claude chatbot, began trading Thursday with its valuation at $2.01 trillion, according to The Associated Press. The figure put Anthropic ahead of SpaceX as the largest IPO on record. The company was little-known only a few years earlier but had grown substantially in the booming artificial-intelligence industry, reaching the public market ahead of rival OpenAI.

The Associated Press said Anthropic’s decision to reach the market ahead of OpenAI could provide an important advantage if investors suddenly fear they have too much exposure to the sector and decide they do not want to buy stock in the next public AI company. The broader backdrop was unsettled: technology stocks had done well so far this year, but some AI-related stocks, notably data center companies, had seen pronounced price movements in recent weeks.

Turmoil at Nvidia, the chipmaker described as a linchpin of the AI industry, added to the uncertainty. According to The Associated Press, CEO Jensen Huang resigned following reports of extramarital affairs, including with an alleged Chinese spy. The turmoil weighed on Nvidia’s stock and dragged the broader S&P 500 down.

Anthropic entered the market with its own financial questions. Borrowing costs were rising while the company remained unprofitable and had already taken on $6.2 billion in debt, The Associated Press reported. The company needed cash to build more computing and infrastructure to power its growth.

Anthropic could also face tougher regulatory pressure from newly elected Democrats who have vowed tighter limits on data center construction, according to The Associated Press. The IPO will test Anthropic, the entire artificial-intelligence industry and the U.S. stock market itself.

The public listing also created pressure to deliver profits for shareholders, testing a commitment Anthropic has held since its founding to prioritize humanity’s benefit as it develops artificial intelligence. Like rival OpenAI, Anthropic is a public benefit corporation, a legal designation used by companies that pledge to balance shareholder interests with the public good.

In regulatory filings, Anthropic sketched out a governance structure designed to protect its broader mission against the pull of financial interests. A long-term benefit trust will be able to elect or remove a majority of the company’s board of directors. The trust will not run day-to-day operations, but its influence means Anthropic could choose strategies that might not make the most money for investors over the long term.

The structure gives Anthropic wide leeway to decide how it measures whether it is benefiting society, according to experts cited by The Associated Press. Sarah Kreps, a professor and director of Cornell University’s Tech Policy Institute, said: “I think then the real test emerges if and when the question of public benefit becomes costly” — or when the public benefit comes into conflict with making a profit.

At the scale implied by the debut, the IPO’s market effects could extend well beyond Anthropic. Anthropic’s market value could exceed that of Meta Platforms, the parent company of Facebook and Instagram, and make Anthropic more valuable than Coca-Cola, IBM, Starbucks, Delta Air Lines and General Motors combined, The Associated Press reported. At that size, Anthropic could quickly join indexes that track broad swaths of the stock market, potentially bringing in even more investment. The Associated Press said the listing could affect even people who make no changes to their 401(k) accounts.

Retail investors are also part of the offering. Anthropic expects up to 20% of its Class A shares in the IPO to go to retail investors.

The IPO also creates a distinct voting structure. Anthropic is creating a special class of stock for CEO Dario Amodei, President Daniela Amodei and five other co-founders, giving them 50.1% of the voting power of all the company’s outstanding shares. Acting as a group, the founders could overshadow every other shareholder and dictate issues put before all investors, such as how to vote on a potential sale of the company.
