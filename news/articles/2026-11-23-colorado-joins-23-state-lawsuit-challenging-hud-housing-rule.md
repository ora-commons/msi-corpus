## HUD estimates 20,000 mixed-status families will lose housing assistance

Colorado Attorney General Phil Weiser joined a lawsuit filed by 23 states in the U.S. District Court of Massachusetts challenging a U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development rule. The rule would withhold federal housing assistance from an entire household if at least one member is not a U.S. citizen or eligible immigrant. The states seek to invalidate it and prevent HUD from enforcing it in their states.

Weiser, a Democrat whom voters this month elected to be Colorado's next governor, said families that live with a relative who is not eligible for benefits would have to “choose between keeping their housing or keeping their family together.”

“Those most affected by this cruel policy are seniors and children,” Weiser said in a statement.

The existing federal housing assistance program reduces subsidies for “mixed status” families so that only eligible household members receive benefits. Congress implemented that protection in 1997 to ensure low-income families living with ineligible members could still receive financial support.

Under the newly approved rule, U.S. citizens will also need to verify their citizenship when applying for federal housing assistance.

HUD estimated that 20,000 mixed-status families will lose housing assistance under the new rule. That estimate includes 55,000 people who are fully eligible for federal housing assistance.

The lawsuit says the rule exceeds HUD’s authority, violates federal law that allows prorated assistance for immigrant families and violates the Administrative Procedures Act.

A group of attorneys general, including Weiser, wrote comments opposing the rule in April. They argue it will increase homelessness as families who become ineligible for benefits lose access to housing. “Once again, the federal government overreached and came up with an illegal rule, and we’re suing to stop it and protect housing for families,” Weiser said.

Since President Donald Trump returned to office in January 2025, Weiser has filed nearly 90 lawsuits against the federal government. Weiser has been successful in more than 50 of them, and many others remain pending.

The rule is set to take effect in mid-December. The 23 plaintiff states ask the court to block the federal government from enforcing it in their states and to declare the rule unlawful.
