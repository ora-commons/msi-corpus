## Macy's postpones its 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade to Saturday after consulting officials

Much of the United States faced foul weather on Thanksgiving Day, with a strengthening storm along the Eastern Seaboard, winter storm warnings and watches posted for parts of Missouri and Illinois, and king tides continuing to surge along the West Coast.

Macy's said in a statement that it was postponing its 100th Thanksgiving Day Parade until Saturday "in an abundance of caution" after consulting with city officials. The strengthening storm along the Eastern Seaboard raised worries about how the parade's massive balloons would fare in the wind-swept canyons between Manhattan's skyscrapers.

The National Weather Service said the storm was expected to hamper air travel at eastern U.S. airports from Thanksgiving Day into Friday morning, with reduced visibility, greater turbulence, strong winds and aircraft icing. Wind gusts were expected to hit 50 mph (80 kph) or higher along the Interstate 95 corridor, and the weather service posted wind advisories from northern Georgia to most of the mid-Atlantic to southern New England.

All of New Jersey was under a state of emergency, declared by Gov. Mikie Sherrill on Wednesday night. High winds, heavy rains and flooding plagued both coasts and complicated holiday travel, though the days leading up to Thanksgiving are typically far busier on the roads and in the airports than the holiday itself.

Winter storm warnings and watches were posted for parts of Missouri and Illinois, with up to 13 inches (33 centimeters) of snow expected around St. Louis by Thursday afternoon.

King tides continued surging on the West Coast, with coastal flood warnings issued from Southern California to the San Francisco area to the Oregon and Washington coasts. King tides occur when the gravitational pulls of the moon and sun align to raise water levels. Their effects this year are being amplified by the ongoing super El Niño, which has warmed the ocean, leading to an increased volume of seawater.

A video showed a massive wave tearing down a portion of the pier in the city of Pacifica, California, on Wednesday and slamming it into the ocean. Officials had closed the L-shaped pier in the summer after finding cracks in the structure.

North of San Francisco, Marin County officials issued an evacuation warning for residents of beachfront properties in Stinson Beach, saying large waves expected through the afternoon could send water and debris into roads and homes. The warning was lifted later Wednesday, but authorities advised people to stay away from the beach as large waves continued to pound the area.
