## FDA nominee withdrawn as Alaska inquiries continue and BBC deposition delayed

Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer said “We are all united together” as Democrats prepared to take the Senate majority in the new year, though several caucus members had pledged support for Chris Van Hollen. Schumer again insisted that he had the votes to secure the leadership position. He had described Mary Peltola’s Alaska victory as “a gift to Alaska, the Senate, and all of America.”

The same Guardian live report documented an NSA Net outage, criminal and civil inquiries announced by Alaska’s top federal prosecutor over an alleged ballot scheme, the White House’s withdrawal of Heidi Overton’s Food and Drug Administration nomination and Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy’s request for $50bn in additional air-safety funding.

In a social media post, Schumer wrote, “It’s official: 56 Democratic Senate seats!” He added, “Her knowledge and love for Alaska are unparalleled, and she ran a pitch-perfect campaign devoted to the issues Alaskans care most about – fish, family, and freedom.”

Schumer had enlisted Peltola to challenge Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan. Peltola will be Alaska’s next U.S. senator after defeating Sullivan. The result was called two weeks after the midterm elections and gives Democrats a commanding 56-44 Senate majority. The former U.S. House lawmaker is the first Democrat the state has elected to the Senate since 2008. Her victory came two years after Alaska voters unseated her while supporting Trump by a double-digit margin. The Guardian reported that the midterms saw Donald Trump’s party lose ground across the country.

In a Tuesday statement, Peltola said, “From Juneau to Utqiagvik, the message from Alaskans is clear: the status quo in DC is not working for Alaska.” She added, “As a lifelong Alaskan who has dedicated my life to serving our state, there is no greater honor than having Alaskans’ support in our fight to take on corporate greed, lower costs, and put Alaska first.” Peltola said, “As our next United States Senator, I will stand up to anyone to take on the rigged system in DC and deliver results for Alaska’s working families.”

Last month, The New York Times reported that Peltola’s staffers were involved in a scheme to recruit another Republican challenger, also named Dan Sullivan, to appear on the ballot. Her campaign denied any involvement in encouraging the second Sullivan’s run. After state election officials tried to remove him, Alaska’s supreme court ruled in June that the second Sullivan could appear on the ballot. Donald Trump called for Peltola’s prosecution over what The Guardian described as an apparent ruse. Alaska’s top federal prosecutor announced that criminal and civil inquiries were under way, and the state governor separately announced plans to open a criminal investigation.

The Guardian reported that President Donald Trump was in China for the APEC summit, seeking to reinforce the United States’ status as the world’s leading technology power. The National Security Agency’s internal network, NSA Net, went down on Tuesday. An NSA spokesperson told The Guardian the outage resulted from “scheduled routine maintenance.” Speaking in Manila, Trump said, “It’s like when you’re big and powerful, and you have all the money. Things like that happen. It’s fine.” Secretary of State Marco Rubio said there was “no evidence” that any of the many outages that week were related to each other or to “nefarious actors.” Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said the outages were likely due to system upgrades and not a cyberattack.

The White House dropped its nomination of Heidi Overton to lead the Food and Drug Administration. Overton faced a challenging path to Senate confirmation, particularly after Republican Sen. Bill Cassidy said he remained undecided about her nomination ahead of a Senate health, labor and pensions committee vote scheduled for Wednesday.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said he was making “a nice ask” for an additional $50bn in funding for air-safety upgrades following the nationwide ground-stop of thousands of flights last week. Duffy also said the FBI was now assisting the investigation into the newly installed router responsible for the grounding.

Acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao faced lawmakers on the Senate armed services committee as he sought confirmation as the branch’s permanent top official. During his hearing, Cao said the eight suicide attempts on board the USS Abraham Lincoln during a record-breaking deployment were “suicide mitigations,” and defended conditions on the warship.

Jensen Huang, the former Nvidia CEO, was scheduled to appear before House lawmakers on three key committees on 1 December. The chair of Nvidia’s board said the company “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.”

After the United States lost the measles elimination status it achieved in 2000, Health Secretary Robert F Kennedy Jr., whom The Guardian described as a longtime vaccine skeptic, blamed “worldwide COVID-era disruptions” for interrupting routine childhood vaccination.

A federal district court judge granted Donald Trump a temporary delay before he must give a witness statement in his $10bn defamation lawsuit against the BBC. The judge extended the deadline for Trump to sit for a deposition by the UK broadcaster’s lawyers from 11 December until 22 January.
