## Three judges deliberate as the defense seeks a halt

The US Justice Department has admitted altering the Spanish translation of an extradition request for US activist and philanthropist James "Fergie" Chambers, adding words that do not appear in the original English criminal complaint. The admission came in a notification sent to Spain on 12 November, according to documents reviewed by The Guardian, and reached Chambers's defense attorneys, the prosecutor and the three-judge panel on Tuesday as the judges considered whether to grant the request.

The department cited three of the examples identified in The Guardian's earlier reporting and described them as "substantial [and] inadvertent" alterations. It called a fourth change "an acceptable translation." The three acknowledged changes include adding the word "fire" to a description of late-2023 protests against Elbit, an Israeli weapons manufacturer, and adding the Spanish words for "violent" and "detonate" where the original English text did not contain them. Two of the three charges in the criminal complaint against Chambers relate to the protests, where nothing was set on fire.

Both Spanish law and the extradition treaty between the US and Spain require the requesting country to translate extradition requests into Spanish. The version before the judges is the one defense lawyers say carries altered wording with legal consequences for the charges against Chambers.

Chambers's lawyers raised the issue in a motion filed on 3 November, identifying a dozen alterations to the original criminal complaint. The judges responded in writing that "the translation offered by the requesting country's authorities should be considered trustworthy" and that no action was required, according to Aitor Martínez, a member of Chambers's defense team.

During the hearing, the judges denied the defense request to admit into evidence a 345-page report prepared by a certified Spanish translator. The translator identified what the defense counted as 30 "incriminating changes" the United States had made to a machine-made translation. The judges also refused to allow the translator to appear as a witness.

According to documents reviewed by The Guardian, the Trump administration notified Spain of the alterations on 12 November, the day before a hearing involving Chambers's attorneys, the three judges and the prosecutor. The notification reached all of those parties only on Tuesday, as the judges were deliberating, and the defense filed a motion asking for the proceedings to be halted and for a new translation.

The prosecutor dismissed the issue by referring to "supposed translation errors." Martínez said that characterization and other notions with specific legal implications now form part of the prosecutor's report, which in turn influences the judges deciding on the request.

"I think the fact that the US recognizes what it has done shows that they were trying to fool the judges," Martínez said. "This should touch the pride of the Spanish justice system," he added.

Martínez said the defense might take its case to Spain's constitutional court, as defense attorneys did in 2003, when an extradition request from Turkey was denied after translation errors and the defense alleged due process violations. "We might wind up doing the same," Martínez said.

Chambers is an heir to the Cox family, owners of Atlanta-based Cox Enterprises. He has described himself as a communist and anti-imperialist, sold stock back to his family worth about $250 million in mid-2023, and has since funded an estimated 100 progressive and humanitarian projects in 20 countries, The Guardian reported.

The Guardian described the case as the first known instance of the United States seeking to extradite a citizen over alleged support for Hamas. The allegation appears tied to an international money laundering charge in the criminal complaint underpinning the request and to Chambers's donations to organizations helping Palestinians in Gaza. A week after the 7 October 2023 Hamas attacks on Israel, Chambers posted on X rejecting the description of Israel's response as collective punishment, saying no faction of the Palestinian resistance had done anything wrong and calling the response genocide.

If the panel grants the request, Spain's council of ministers can reverse the outcome. The Guardian reported that the case may be among the last decisions made under Pedro Sánchez, the socialist prime minister, before national elections on 29 November.
