## Disclosure could expose every confidential source over 18 months

A unanimous three-judge panel of the 4th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals ruled Tuesday that a federal judge must narrow an order requiring LinkedIn to disclose journalists' communications as part of the Trump administration's leak investigation. The panel rejected the government's request for a blanket order covering a broad array of user records and also rejected LinkedIn's request to quash the lower court order entirely.

The case stems from the administration's decision to open a leak investigation after an unidentified news organization published an article about a top-secret U.S. military operation in a foreign country. The article revealed classified information, and the government subsequently sought the journalists' LinkedIn communications.

U.S. District Judge Elizabeth Hanes, a Biden nominee based in Newport News, Virginia, issued the order LinkedIn challenged. The order applies to journalists targeted by the leak investigation whose employer is not identified in court records.

The order covers 18 months of reporting during which the targeted journalists published nearly 1,000 stories on many topics. It would require LinkedIn to unmask every confidential source with whom those journalists communicated on the platform during that span.

The appeals court said the government was not entitled to a “blanket” order for the broad array of user records belonging to the journalists. Judge J. Harvie Wilkinson III wrote in the unanimous opinion: “A request by the government to scoop up reporters’ records wholesale and ascertain the identity of their sources would tip the balance against informed discourse in a manner inimical to democratic health.”

The panel also rejected LinkedIn's request to entirely quash the district court's “substantially overbroad” disclosure order. The court recognized that journalists' First Amendment rights must be balanced against the administration's duty to protect national security information. “Serious security and speech claims are in tension in this case. There is space under law for both,” Wilkinson wrote.

The panel consisted of Wilkinson, whom Republican President Ronald Reagan nominated; Judge James Andrew Wynn, whom Democratic President Barack Obama nominated; and Judge Nicole G. Berner, whom Democratic President Joe Biden nominated.

Wilkinson said the original order could reach well beyond sources relevant to the leak investigation. “While that order may catch sources relevant to the government’s investigation, it will also inevitably sweep in every other confidential source with whom the targeted journalists spoke,” Wilkinson wrote.

The panel returned the case to Hanes with an instruction to narrow the disclosure order. “That court was right to accord the government considerable latitude in pursuing and maintaining the integrity of its investigation,” Wilkinson wrote. “Even so, that latitude is not infinite. It is not appropriate for district courts to approve requests purely because the records requested are logistically feasible to compile.”
