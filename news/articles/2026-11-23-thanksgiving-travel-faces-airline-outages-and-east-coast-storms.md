## Delta stabilized after more than 1,000 delayed flights

The biggest unknown for Thanksgiving travelers is whether airlines will be upended again by new system failures as the busiest travel week of the year gets underway. Storms are forecast along both coasts. Roundtrip domestic Thanksgiving tickets are also up more than one-third from the same time last year, according to Hopper estimates made in late October.

Delta said Monday that its operations were stable, a day after more than 1,000 of its flights were delayed following a malfunction in an automated system that calculates aircraft weight. FlightAware said Monday morning that 7% of Delta's flights were delayed. "Delta teams are focused on keeping the operation moving safely and caring for our customers," the airline said.

The recent disruptions began with a Federal Aviation Administration network malfunction that set off the longest nationwide ground stop in 25 years. United Airlines and Delta Air Lines then had separate outages last week that led to hundreds of flight delays.

Joe Wegman, a meteorologist with the National Weather Service's Weather Prediction Center, said holiday travelers can expect a rainy week on the East and West coasts and dry conditions in the nation's mid-section.

On the East Coast, heavy rain and high winds are expected to start Wednesday night along the coast of the Carolinas, hit the New York City area on Thanksgiving Day and reach Maine by Friday, Wegman said. The weather service expects the rain in New York to hold off until after the parade ends, but winds could be an issue.

That forecast has raised concern about whether the large balloons can fly at the 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York. City law prohibits Macy's from flying full-size balloons if sustained winds exceed 23 mph or wind gusts are over 35 mph.

"This is poor timing with the storm coming up on Thanksgiving Day," Wegman said. Wegman said the timing might have been better had the storm arrived on Thanksgiving Day rather than on any of the travel days before or after.

On the West Coast, rainy and windy weather was expected. Moderate to major coastal flooding was forecast from Washington state to Southern California because of possible record-breaking king tides. Rain was forecast for the Pacific Northwest on Monday and expected to spread to northern and central California by Wednesday morning, with snow possible in higher elevations.

Snow was also forecast Monday for parts of the Rockies, northern Michigan, western New York state and northwestern Pennsylvania.

"Across the U.S., certainly check with your local airports and airlines for any possible delays," Wegman said. "I think weather won't be a significant factor except on Thanksgiving Day itself, and if there are any lingering impacts into Friday morning perhaps from delays that happen on Thanksgiving Day."
