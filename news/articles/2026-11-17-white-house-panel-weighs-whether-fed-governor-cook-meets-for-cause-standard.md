## Committee of inquiry comprises Hassett, Lucas and Sonderling

WASHINGTON — The committee considering Lisa Cook’s removal comprises Kevin Hassett, Trump’s top economic adviser; Andrea Lucas, whom Trump appointed to lead the Equal Employment Opportunity Commission; and Keith Sonderling, Trump’s pick for Labor Secretary. The unusual White House “committee of inquiry” will ultimately recommend whether there is cause to fire Cook, the White House has said.

In a Nov. 3 letter, the committee said it “reserves the right to draw adverse inferences against you” for not participating. Trump administration officials have threatened to move ahead with the hearing even without Cook. Cook, through her lawyers, declined to appear next month, calling the process unfair and committed to a predetermined outcome.

The refusal extends the wrangling between the White House and Cook’s lawyers over her appearance; the administration initially demanded that Cook attend for Nov. 6. Trump administration officials said she must appear before the committee to address allegations that she committed mortgage fraud in 2021, before she joined the Fed’s board of governors. Cook denies the charge.

President Donald Trump has sought to fire Cook since August 2025. Cook has argued that his efforts threaten Fed independence and reflect Trump’s desire to force the central bank to reduce borrowing costs.

Abbe David Lowell, Cook’s lawyer, wrote in a letter released Tuesday to White House counsel William Scharf: “Governor Cook has no legal obligation to participate in an unfair hearing — and indeed, has a duty to the Federal Reserve to decline to participate in any proceeding that would undermine the institution’s unique status and independence.” Lowell also wrote that the three-person panel set up by the White House to consider the allegations “appears to be a façade designed to obscure a predetermined decision to fire Governor Cook.”

In June, the Supreme Court blocked Trump from removing Cook while litigation over the attempted firing continued, finding that “the fact of independence but also the appearance of independence is key to the Federal Reserve’s design.” A footnote said Trump could try again if the administration provided adequate notice and an opportunity for Cook to respond. The committee of inquiry is the White House’s effort to meet those conditions.

Under the law governing the Fed, the president can fire a governor only “for cause.” The Supreme Court did not address whether Cook’s actions met that standard. The White House has said the committee will ultimately recommend whether there is cause to fire Cook.

Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh was asked at an Oct. 28 press conference whether he or the Fed would be involved in the hearing against Cook. He said the answer was “not to my knowledge.”
