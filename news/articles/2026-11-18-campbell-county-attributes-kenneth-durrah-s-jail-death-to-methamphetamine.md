## Public-records video shows guards strapping Durrah into a restraint chair

Campbell County's defense attorneys told a federal court that Kenneth Durrah's death in the Campbell County Detention Center was caused by his own concealed methamphetamine ingestion. In the county's first detailed response to the civil rights lawsuit filed Aug. 31 by Durrah's family, Campbell County denied the allegations Monday, arguing that Durrah's death was caused by his own concealed drug ingestion and not deputies' use of force or inadequate medical care. The defense asked the court to rule in favor of Campbell County and the deputies involved and to dismiss the case.

The county's filing argues that the deputies' actions were necessary and lawful. According to the filing, Durrah resisted efforts to remove the drugs, assaulted a deputy and forced his way out of his cell, and the strip search and restraint chair were justified by the suspected contraband and his continued resistance.

A nearly three-hour video obtained by WyoFile and the Gillette News Record through a public-records request shows the hours before Durrah died naked and strapped to a restraint chair. It begins in the early hours of Dec. 1, 2022, after Durrah had been held in the Campbell County jail for about a week on drug and firearm charges and parole violations. Durrah complained of chest pains, and jail deputies took him from his cell to booking, where he was cleared by two EMTs.

Back in a cell, Durrah appeared to ingest methamphetamine from a small bag, the video shows. Deputies entered the cell, pushed him against the back wall and commanded him not to swallow whatever he had put in his mouth. Durrah pushed through the cell door into the booking area, where several guards forced him to the floor, cuffed his hands behind his back and restrained him face down.

Guards carried Durrah to a padded booking cell, where four guards pinned him on his belly for several minutes. One guard knelt on Durrah's head and hip, another knelt on his back, and two others restrained his legs, sometimes kneeling on his lower legs and ankles. Using trauma shears, guards cut Durrah's orange jumpsuit from his body.

While pinned, Durrah periodically strained and resisted, and he complained that he could not breathe. Four guards then lifted him from the ground and placed him in a restraint chair. One guard tightened a strap across his waist, another removed his handcuffs, and officers placed straps across Durrah's arms and chest, retightened them and wheeled the chair into a cell.

Durrah pleaded with deputies that he could not breathe in the restraint chair. On the video, he can be heard saying, "It's too tight, please." Some 40 minutes after strapping him in the restraint chair, deputies found him unresponsive.

An autopsy described Durrah's cause of death as "sudden cardiac death, consistent with 'excited delirium' due to Methamphetamine Overdose." The lawsuit disputes that account and argues the use of restraints produced a fatal chemical imbalance. The complaint states: "Instead of summoning medical aid, the deputies left Mr. Durrah tightly strapped in the restraint chair for half an hour as he died."

According to the complaint, the strenuous prone restraint and Durrah's inability to sufficiently respirate during and after the initial restraint kept him from clearing a dangerous buildup of carbon dioxide and lactic acid in his system. The complaint also says that after witnessing Durrah ingest suspected methamphetamine, none of the deputies "took any other action to mitigate the effects of a potential drug overdose" or called for medical help.

Campbell County disputes the family's claim that deputies were deliberately indifferent to Durrah's medical needs and did not provide adequate care while he was in the jail's custody. The county says deputies placed Durrah on a "special watch," observed steady, non-labored breathing and performed a capillary-refill test, a quick test to check blood flow. When they found Durrah unresponsive, deputies began CPR, applied an AED and called EMS, the response states.

The competing accounts of the medical timeline remain at issue in the federal case: the lawsuit says deputies left Durrah tightly strapped in the restraint chair without summoning medical aid, while Campbell County's Monday response says the restraint followed suspected drug ingestion and continued resistance, that deputies monitored Durrah, and that they called for help as soon as he was unresponsive. Defense attorneys asked the court to rule for Campbell County and the deputies involved in Durrah's death and to dismiss the case against them.
