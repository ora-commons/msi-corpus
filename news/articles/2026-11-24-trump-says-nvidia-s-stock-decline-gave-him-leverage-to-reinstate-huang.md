## Financial disclosures show Trump bought Nvidia stock as security questions remain

In a 50-minute phone interview with CNBC, President Donald Trump said Nvidia's stock had plummeted after Jensen Huang's ouster and that the decline gave him leverage to persuade the company to reinstate Huang as chief executive. Trump demurred when asked whether he had called directly into Nvidia's board meeting, but he said he had pushed Nvidia and convinced it to reinstate Huang. Nvidia had restored Huang as chief executive less than two weeks after his resignation, which followed early reports that he was having an affair with a woman alleged to be a spy for China.

Trump said the reinstatement not only turned Nvidia's stock around but perhaps lifted the whole market after a rough week for stocks. White House correspondent Danielle Kurtzleben observed that Trump often had stock indexes to discuss even when other economic measures were not looking well. In an archived recording, Trump said: "I watched the screen, and I said, boy, that thing's like a rocket ship going in the wrong direction. Now it's a rocket ship going in the right direction, and it's taken the whole market with it."

An unidentified person said, "It's just an incredible turnaround." Trump replied, "Well, I don't know." The unidentified person then told Trump he had been watching corporate America. Trump said, "There could be - it could be that there's never been anything like it, actually."

Shortly after Nvidia announced the reinstatement, Trump posted that it was good for both Nvidia and the U.S. economy. Trump also told CNBC he was concerned that Chinese President Xi Jinping might lure Huang to China to strengthen China's chip business.

Michel Martin said people who do not cover the business world might not know that it is very extraordinary for a White House to weigh in on private business decisions. Kurtzleben called the intervention a huge deal but said it was not really unusual for that White House to intervene in the economy and even in private businesses. She said the administration had taken stakes in dozens of companies.

Kurtzleben said Trump had close personal ties to the technology sector, with CEOs showing up at White House events or donating to his ballroom. She said Trump's meme stood out from that broader backdrop. It repurposed the famous image of Trump raising a fist after he was shot in Butler, Pennsylvania, but replaced him with Huang, who had blood spattered on his face and a red bra in his raised fist referencing the affair. Kurtzleben characterized the post as trolling and said it was celebrating a private business decision.

Host Steve Inkskeep described Huang and Trump as allies. He noted that Huang routinely advised the president on AI policy and that Nvidia donated to the new White House ballroom. Inkskeep said Huang would face questions about that relationship and much more in the planned closed-door congressional interview.

Kurtzleben said Trump's financial disclosures showed he had traded Nvidia stock. She said a purchase made in early September was worth up to a quarter of a million dollars. She described the trade as part of a broader pattern of the president trading in companies affected by actions by his administration.

National-security issues had not been resolved, Kurtzleben said. Trump said on CNBC that the White House had investigated Huang and that he was not concerned about Huang from a security perspective. Secretary of State Marco Rubio had written a memo earlier in the year warning about Huang's affair, Kurtzleben said.

Nebraska Republican Senator Pete Ricketts posted that Huang's behavior did not support reinstatement and said he looked forward to Huang's scheduled Senate testimony in December. Kurtzleben called the objection meaningful because it came from within Trump's own party and said it remained to be seen how many lawmakers would join the charge and how vocal they would be.

Three House committees planned a closed-door interview with Huang for the following week. Representative Raja Krishnamoorthi was scheduled to participate in two of the panels.

Trump's public account said the reinstatement benefited Nvidia and the U.S. economy; his security assurance was that the White House had investigated Huang and he was not concerned about a security risk. Rubio's memo, Ricketts's objection and the planned House interview left the conduct and security questions open. Huang's return settled his leadership status but not those issues.
