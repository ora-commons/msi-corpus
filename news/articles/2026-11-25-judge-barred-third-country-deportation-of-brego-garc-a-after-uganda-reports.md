## Costa Rica agreed to accept Ábrego García as a refugee with legal status

Reports that the Trump administration was considering sending Kilmar Ábrego García to Uganda were followed by a federal judge's order barring the government from deporting him to a third country. The administration continued attempts to send him to other countries, including Uganda and Ghana, and the dispute over his removal lasted for months, BBC News reported.

At one point, the Trump administration asked the judge to lift the order so Ábrego García could be deported to Liberia, according to CBS News.

Ábrego García had repeatedly said he would be willing to be deported to Costa Rica. The Costa Rican government had agreed to accept him as a refugee and provide him with legal status.

Sen. Chris Van Hollen, a Democrat from Maryland, visited Ábrego García at Cecot prison in El Salvador in 2025 and advocated for his release. Van Hollen said in a statement on Tuesday, "A year and a half ago, the Trump Administration wanted to leave Kilmar Ábrego García to rot in a Salvadoran gulag," adding, "Today, Kilmar is travelling to a country of his choice, a free man - unharmed and with dignity."

BBC News reported that Ábrego García was set to take a commercial flight to Spain on Tuesday, according to CBS News, the BBC's US partner. The outlet characterized the planned departure as ending the long-running deportation fight with the Trump administration.

Ábrego García had been deported to El Salvador in March 2025, where he was held in Cecot, a prison BBC News described as known for its harsh conditions. The U.S. Supreme Court then ordered the government to facilitate his return. The administration admitted that Ábrego García had been deported in error and brought him back to the United States, where he was set to face human trafficking charges. The charges were later dropped, and a federal judge dismissed the case, saying Ábrego García had been charged for political reasons.

Ábrego García was married to a U.S. citizen and lived in Maryland, according to BBC News. The outlet reported that he came to the United States illegally from El Salvador as a teenager.

He was arrested in 2019 alongside three other men and detained by federal immigration authorities. A judge granted Ábrego García protection from deportation to El Salvador at the time, citing the risk of persecution by a gang there.

Ábrego García's deportation and return became a flash point in the debate over President Donald Trump's immigration agenda. The BBC has reached out to Ábrego García's lawyers and the U.S. Department of Homeland Security for comment.
