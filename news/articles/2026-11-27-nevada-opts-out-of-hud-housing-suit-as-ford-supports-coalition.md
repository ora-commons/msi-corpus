## Twenty-two states challenge rule scheduled for Dec. 14

Nevada did not sign on to the multistate lawsuit challenging a Trump administration housing rule scheduled to take effect Dec. 14. Nevada Attorney General and governor-elect Aaron Ford is monitoring the lawsuit and “supports the coalition in their efforts to block this new rule that would divide families,” spokesman John Sadler said in a statement to Nevada Current.

On Monday, 22 states announced that they would sue to block the change. Their lawsuit seeks to prevent a U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development rule scheduled for Dec. 14 from taking effect. The rule would reverse a policy allowing undocumented immigrants to live with family members who have legal status and are receiving housing assistance. Under the current policy, housing benefits for mixed-status families are prorated.

HUD announced earlier this year that it would seek to reverse the policy, then said this month that it had finalized the change. In the lawsuit, the states argue that “HUD lacks authority to rewrite federal law to exclude families this Administration disfavors from federal affordable housing programs.”

“The decision to not join this lawsuit was based on the programs’ structure in Nevada,” Sadler said. Nevada's housing programs are the purview of regional organizations such as the Southern Nevada Regional Housing Authority and nonprofits.

Mixed-status households are made up of people with varied immigration statuses, including legal residents and U.S. citizens. The Pew Research Center estimated that 9% of Nevada households include mixed-status families, the highest proportion among states. A 2024 analysis from the Center on Budget and Policy Priorities found that 150 mixed-status families receive housing assistance in Nevada.

News From The States reported that Nevada, like the rest of the country, has experienced an affordable housing shortage. The outlet said the Trump administration has repeatedly blamed that crisis on immigrants without providing evidence.

When HUD announced the proposed change in February, HUD Secretary Scott Turner said households should prepare to “pack your bags” when the rule is finalized. HUD later announced the finalization this month. Local housing and immigrant groups criticized the proposed rule change.

Noé Orosco, coordinator of the Nevada Immigrant Coalition, previously told Nevada Current that the proposal was a “shameful attempt to scapegoat immigrants for the ongoing housing affordability crisis.”

The states are seeking to block the rule before its scheduled Dec. 14 effective date. Nevada remains outside the case, but Ford supports the coalition's efforts, and Nevada's local housing and immigrant groups have opposed the change.
