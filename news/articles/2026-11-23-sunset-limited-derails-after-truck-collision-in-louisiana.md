## Passengers traveled by bus to New Orleans after crews secured the scene

Amtrak's Sunset Limited derailed near Lafayette in south-central Louisiana on Sunday evening after the train collided with a tractor-trailer at a railroad crossing, officials said. The lead locomotive and two baggage cars left the rails. Five passengers were reported to have non-life-threatening injuries.

The Sunset Limited was traveling from California to New Orleans when the collision occurred at about 6:15 p.m. local time near Lafayette, roughly 135 miles (217 km) west of New Orleans. The tractor-trailer was an 18-wheeler carrying a bulldozer.

Sgt. Ashley Wood of the Lafayette police department said the reported injuries were minor and that a few passengers had been transported to a hospital. The Lafayette fire department later released an update saying at least five passengers had been injured.

Amtrak said in a statement reported by ABC News that 95 customers were on board and “All passenger cars remained upright.” No passenger car overturned.

Lafayette fire officials said Hazmat and Rescue personnel ensured the scene was safe for first responders to assist the injured and check the other passengers. “Once it was safe for passengers to exit, firefighters assisted them to a safe location along with their luggage,” the department said.

Lafayette Mayor Monique Blanco Boulet said in a social media statement that the Sunset Limited's passengers were being transported to New Orleans by bus.

A passenger identified by the Acadian Advocate only as Nicole said she “felt two jolts.” She described the second impact in detail: “The first one was light but noticeable. The second one was so forceful it just knocked over everything we had on our table – water, books, everything,” Nicole said.

Ralph and Eli, two New York natives identified only by their first names, said “things were going great” because the Giants football team they support was on its way to winning a game at the time. “Then suddenly we felt a massive jolt, and the train started decelerating very fast,” Ralph said, according to the Advocate.

The Acadian Advocate reported that an investigation indicated the tractor-trailer was trying to cross the tracks and got stuck. According to the Advocate, the crossing arm came down after the semi became stuck because the train was approaching. KATC reported that the train slowed down but was not able to stop.

The Guardian cited various estimates that roughly three train derailments occur daily in the United States, involving different kinds of trains and for different reasons. The outlet said most of those cases did not lead to a major disaster.
