## Kalshi halted Utah trading after a federal judge would not immediately intervene

Utah asked a court in Salt Lake City on Tuesday to dismiss criminal charges it filed last month against prediction market operator Kalshi and two executives, citing the company’s decision to halt trading in the state. The one-sentence motion referenced a “change in circumstance.”

“Kalshi has pulled out of Utah, and if it returns, so will the charges,” Utah Attorney General Derek Brown said in a statement. Brown’s office noted that it could refile the case if Kalshi resumes offering trades in Utah.

The felony charges include 13 counts of violating Utah’s prohibition on gambling and another charge involving profiting off illegal activity.

The state’s charging documents name Kalshi and its co-founders, chief executive officer Tarek Mansour and chief operating officer Luana Lopes Lara. Kalshi, Mansour and Lopes Lara are due in court in Salt Lake City on Dec. 4 unless a judge agrees to dismiss the case.

Utah law prohibits gambling, and Brown’s office said Utah considers prediction markets gambling. Many other states contend that the platforms’ sports trading activities are flouting laws that regulate sports betting and casino gambling.

Utah’s felony charges represented a new tactic in the way states are trying to assert regulatory authority over the fast-expanding activity. Brown’s office also has asked a court to make Kalshi give up the profits it made in Utah.

Kalshi, the nation’s largest prediction market operator, said Monday that it had halted trading in Utah after it became clear that a federal judge hearing a case brought by the U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission would not immediately stop Utah’s attempts to shut it down there.

A Kalshi spokeswoman called it an “unfortunate outcome for the more than 177,000 Utahns who use Kalshi” but said the company will continue to fight in court for the right to operate in Utah.

Kalshi and the U.S. Commodity Futures Trading Commission argue that prediction markets offer event contracts that should be considered swaps under a federal law that grants the agency exclusive regulation. Utah and at least 44 other states oppose that view.

Kalshi sued Utah in February, asking a federal judge to prevent the state from enforcing its gambling restrictions on the platform. A federal judge rejected the request, and an appeal is pending.

Trading activity on Kalshi is halted in Nevada and partially in Michigan and Washington, according to Kalshi.

Utah’s prosecution forms part of a broader battle between states and the federal government over who regulates prediction markets. Litigation has spread across more than 20 states, creating a fragmented legal landscape that analysts said is likely to be settled by the U.S. Supreme Court.
