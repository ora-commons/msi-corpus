## Gonzalez rejects warrant challenge; Belgal can seek bond in immigration court

A US district judge on Friday denied Oscar Belgal's request for release from federal custody while deportation proceedings remain pending, after agents with US Immigration and Customs Enforcement shot and wounded him during an arrest in New York in October. US District Judge Hector Gonzalez said in a written ruling that agents may have made a procedural error, but that the error was technical and did not require Belgal's release.

Belgal, a 28-year-old Dominican national, entered the United States illegally in 2021. About two years later, he was arrested on robbery and other charges and eventually pleaded guilty to grand larceny. He received probation. In 2025, he was arrested again on allegations that he attempted to sell controlled substances; that case remains pending.

Gonzalez said agents may have erred by serving Belgal's arrest warrant before he received notice to appear in immigration court. The judge characterized that mistake as a technical error and said it did not justify releasing Belgal from custody while deportation proceedings continue.

On 8 October 2026, ICE agents found Belgal as he drove his five-year-old son home from school. The arrest occurred amid continued immigration enforcement under the Trump administration.

Security-camera footage released by the New York City Police Department shows a plainclothes officer running toward Belgal's car with a gun pointed at him, then using the barrel to break the driver's-side window. A second officer drew his gun as Belgal pulled away from the two officers. The chase ended moments later with Belgal's bullet-riddled car coming to rest.

Belgal was struck in the shoulder and neck. His son was not injured. His attorneys said Belgal did not learn that the men were ICE officers until he awoke later in the hospital.

In a written statement, the Legal Aid Society said: “It is unconscionable that Mr Belgal should have to endure another day in detention, separated from his family, particularly given everything he has already suffered”

Federal officials maintained that ICE officers fired their weapons because they believed their lives were in danger. They said Belgal smashed his car into two government vehicles and “accelerated toward other officers.”

Belgal's attorneys disputed that account. They said the agents did not have a valid warrant and never identified themselves before running up to the car, smashing a window and firing several shots inside. In court filings, the lawyers argued that Belgal's due-process rights were violated because of the “unreasonable use of extreme force” during the arrest.

Gonzalez disagreed with the lawyers' assertion that the agents lacked a valid warrant. He said the petition for release was not an appropriate venue for arguing that the officers used excessive force.

Gonzalez noted that Belgal can still ask the immigration court for release on bond.
