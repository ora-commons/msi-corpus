## House vote returns give Democrats a 14-point lead in the midterms

**Subtype:** fact

- Democrats held a 14-point House vote lead over Republicans, 56% to 42%, as midterm vote counts neared completion.
- Democrats had flipped 46 Republican-held House seats and led in three uncalled races that could raise the total to 49.
- Winning all three races where Democrats lead would give the party 260 seats, the most since the 102nd Congress.
- NPR described the 14-point spread as the largest midterm win for either party in 50 years.

## Three leading races could lift Democrats to 49 flips and 260 seats

NPR’s analysis by Domenico Montanaro showed Democrats with 56% of the House vote and Republicans with 42% as the closest races were being decided. That 14-point spread was the largest midterm win for either party in 50 years, according to NPR, whose comparison reached back to the aftermath of Watergate.

NPR’s historical comparison said Democrats’ wins in 2018 and 2006 and the 2010 "shellacking" that Democrats took were all House margins below 10 points. Then-President George W. Bush described 2006 as a "thumping," and former President Barack Obama called the 2010 result a "shellacking." Obama’s 2008 election came closest at almost 11 points.

Democrats had flipped 46 House seats currently held by Republicans. Leads in three races that had not been called could raise that total to 49, the second-most number of flips by either party this century. The previous high came in 2010, when Republicans flipped 66 seats and netted 64. NPR linked that result to a backlash against passage of the Affordable Care Act, which the outlet said has since become more popular.

If Democrats won all three races in which they led, they would have 260 seats, according to NPR. That would be the most since the 102nd Congress, which spanned 1991 to 1993. NPR said Democrats would likely have the largest House majority in Congress since the early 1990s once all votes were counted.

The scale of party turnover has also been unusually high. NPR said this century has produced some of the slimmest House majorities in American history and the most changes of party control of the chamber in 130 years. Starting in January, House control will have changed hands five times in 20 years. In a 20-year stretch in the post-Reconstruction era after the Civil War, there were six changeovers beginning in 1875.

NPR also placed the result within the chamber’s modern political history. Democrats controlled the House for 40 years until the 1994 Republican Revolution ushered in GOP control of Congress. The report said House Republicans were back where they had been before the Republican Revolution, three decades after former House Speaker Newt Gingrich’s "Contract with America" and a decade into Trump’s "grip on the Republican Party."

Democrats led House voting by 14 points, had flipped 46 seats and were positioned for 49 flips and 260 seats if they won all three leading races. Taken together, those figures would give the party the chamber’s largest majority since the early 1990s.

Three races remained uncalled, so the final number of flips and seats was unsettled. NPR’s conclusion was that the House result was wide but not yet final: the 56%-to-42% margin and 46 reported flips were established, while the 49-flip and 260-seat totals depended on the three unresolved races.
