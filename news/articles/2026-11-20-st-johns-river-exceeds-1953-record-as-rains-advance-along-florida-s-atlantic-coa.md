## Flood warnings and watches span around 200 miles from just south of Melbourne to Jacksonville

Heavy rains moved farther north along Florida's Atlantic coast on Friday after leaving large swaths of the coast under water and prompting dozens of rescues. Forecasters warned that flooding threats could stretch into next week.

## Rainfall impacts and emergency response

Parts of Florida's east coast have received rainfall totaling more than 17 inches (43 centimeters) in some spots since Tuesday, according to preliminary totals from the weather service. That came in addition to heavy rains earlier in the month, and several places have already seen their wettest November on record. On Thursday, the storms shifted north to the Space Coast, sending rivers over their banks and prompting water rescues of people and horses during Florida's dry season, which runs from October to May.

“Never seen such a thing like this in Florida,” said Jazmin Foster, whose Melbourne home was inundated with water for the second time in two weeks. In Brevard County on Thursday, people with trailers braved knee-deep water to rescue dozens of horses and other animals at the Blue Lake Equestrian Center, an animal hospice and boarding facility in Cocoa. The animals were taken to a dry pasture elsewhere.

First responders conducted nearly 100 high-water rescues across Brevard County, and the count continued to climb, Sheriff Wayne Ivey said. The city of Palm Bay conducted mandatory evacuations for some residents of a mobile home community, and shelters opened for residents forced from their homes.

The Division of Emergency Management reported that nearly 200 Florida National Guard personnel with 50 high-water vehicles had been deployed to help up and down the coast. About 235 officers with the Florida Fish and Wildlife Conservation Commission also responded.

## River forecasts and coastal hazards

The National Weather Service posted flood warnings and watches stretching around 200 miles (320 kilometers) from just south of Melbourne to the Jacksonville area. Additional rainfall of 2 to 6 inches (5 to 15 centimeters) was expected through Saturday, and some areas could see an extra 8 to 10 inches (20 to 25 centimeters).

In a Friday morning forecast, the National Weather Service said: “Severe, widespread flash flooding is expected.” The service added: “In addition, areas that normally do not experience flash flooding will flood. Furthermore, lives and property (are) in great danger.”

The St. Johns River, Florida's longest, rose above a record level set in 1953 near Cocoa on Thursday. Forecasters expected the river to crest well above that prior record; separately, the National Weather Service forecast it would stay at least 2 feet (61 centimeters) above flood stage through early next week.

The National Hurricane Center in Miami said Friday that a nontropical low-pressure system was expected to form and could become a subtropical or tropical storm along the southeastern U.S. coast. The southeast and mid-Atlantic coasts should expect strong winds, dangerous surf and coastal flooding into early next week, ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday.

## El Niño and the storm pattern

This week's storms have been fueled by persistent high pressure over Virginia and a low-pressure system with an associated stationary front in the Gulf, which together have pushed a steady onshore flow across the Florida Peninsula. The pattern was an offshoot of El Niño, the natural warming of the Pacific near the equator that warps weather patterns worldwide. Forecasters said El Niño conditions were expected to persist through the spring, with continued wet conditions in Florida and California likely through the winter.

## California flooding and Highway 1

California's coast, from the Los Angeles to San Francisco areas, was also swamped with flooding in many areas this week. Neighborhoods went under water, and some drivers abandoned stalled vehicles in floodwaters. A long stretch of coastal Highway 1 was closed south of the Monterey Peninsula because of rock and mud slides.
