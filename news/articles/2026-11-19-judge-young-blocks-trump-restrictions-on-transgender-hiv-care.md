## Ruling cites First and Fifth Amendment violations in grant conditions

US District Judge William Young in Boston ruled on Thursday that the Trump administration cannot impose restrictions barring healthcare providers that receive certain federal HIV-treatment funds from acknowledging transgender patients’ gender identities or providing gender-affirming care.

Young sided with a group of national HIV medical associations and healthcare providers that challenged the funding conditions. The American Academy of HIV Medicine was among the plaintiffs.

The conditions were imposed last year after Trump signed an executive order prohibiting federal funding for what he called “gender ideology.” A division of the US Department of Health and Human Services adopted the conditions for money provided through the Ryan White HIV/AIDS Program.

Congress created the Ryan White HIV/AIDS Program in 1990 to provide medical care for low-income and uninsured people living with HIV.

Young, who was appointed by Republican President Ronald Reagan, said the conditions were designed to suppress what the administration considered to be “dangerous ideas.” He ruled that the conditions violated the First Amendment because they penalized doctors for acknowledging that transgender people exist and referring to their patients by preferred pronouns.

Young also said the restrictions reflected unconstitutional discrimination on the basis of sex in violation of the Fifth Amendment. He wrote that the conditions were based on “animus, loathing, and discrimination.”

“They are based in animus, loathing, and discrimination,” Young wrote. “They do not stand for justice – in fact, they defy it. They are contrary to the laws of this nation and cannot continue to stand.”

The 165-page ruling followed a non-jury trial in September. HHS did not immediately respond to a request for comment. Lawyers representing the plaintiffs also did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

Beginning in March, the Health Resources and Services Administration, a division of HHS that administers the Ryan White program, added funding conditions designed to implement Trump’s executive order directing the US government to recognize only two sexes, male and female. The new grant conditions required providers to ensure funding would support activities using “sex-based definitions grounded in biological reality” and not “social or medical experimentation,” the lawsuit said.

The plaintiffs contended that the policy was unconstitutional and conflicted with the Ryan White program’s statutory mandate to provide comprehensive medical services to HIV patients. They said the program’s care had historically included hormone therapy for transgender people.

The Trump administration also moved to restrict gender-affirming care for transgender inmates in federal prisons and backed state laws banning transgender athletes from participating on female sports teams at public schools, including universities.
