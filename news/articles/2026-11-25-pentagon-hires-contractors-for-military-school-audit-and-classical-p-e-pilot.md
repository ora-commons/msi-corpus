## Jordan Adams and Iliad CEO Patrick Whalen both worked at Hillsdale College

The Department of Defense has begun auditing curriculum and policies across all schools it operates for military families and is piloting a classical physical education program at two U.S. campuses. It awarded American Education Solutions a $5.7 million curriculum-audit contract in October without seeking bids from other companies and separately gave Iliad Athletics just over $100,000 for the P.E. pilot.

The Associated Press characterized the department's effort as a continued revamp of the schools to meet conservative education ideals and described the two contractors as well-connected to conservative ideals.

The Department of Defense Education Activity operates about 160 schools across 11 countries and educates roughly 70,000 students who are children of active-duty military or civilian service members. The Associated Press reported that under Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, the Pentagon has sought to use the schools as models for a broader Republican education agenda. The goals of that broader agenda include restrictions on instruction about race and gender, along with an emphasis on patriotic values and classical learning.

In October, the department approved the audit contract, saying it was complying with an executive order from President Donald Trump aimed at limiting how schools teach about race and gender. The department awarded the $5.7 million contract to American Education Solutions without soliciting other bids. Jordan Adams founded the company and previously consulted with conservative-led school boards in Pennsylvania and Texas to overhaul curriculum.

A Department of Defense Education Activity spokesperson said the agency regularly reviews its academic programs and makes improvements. The statement said the audit would review “curriculum, instruction, and professional learning, helping ensure these areas remain rigorous, effective, and aligned with the needs of students and educators.” Two employees of the Pentagon's education division, speaking anonymously for fear of retribution, said a system-wide review by a consultant would be without precedent. The employees said schools typically work with a national accreditor to evaluate and develop standards for subjects.

American Education Solutions advertises curriculum adoption, analysis and development services on its website. The company criticizes curricula used in American schools as “content-light, employ pedagogical practices not supported by the science of learning, and eschew the patriotic civic role of public education.” The company also offers an audit to ensure schools “Do No Harm.”

The website says, “The intellectual and character formation of children is a serious responsibility for school systems.” It adds: “As with medicine, the first requirement is to do no harm — both pedagogically and with respect to parents as first educators. AES reviews school systems to ensure this is the case.” In a statement, the Pentagon praised the approach, saying it “preserves effective practices while identifying opportunities for thoughtful improvement.” American Education Solutions did not respond to questions and said the company does not “share work for past, current or pending clients without authorization.”

Classical education broadly focuses on critical thinking, citizenship and, often, writers from ancient Greece, Rome and other Western civilizations. The classical P.E. course will be offered at LeJeune High School in North Carolina and Quantico Middle High School in Virginia. Iliad Athletics described the course as focused on functional physical fitness rather than introducing students to sports. Inspired by traditional Greek athletics, the course will include running, jumping, climbing, carrying and throwing, the company said. The course also includes a “character development program,” which highlights a weekly virtue and gives examples of historical figures who lived it out.

The Iliad Athletics contract did not involve competitive bids. The Defense Department said the company was the only company that could meet the requirements at the time. Patrick Whalen, Iliad Athletics' chief executive officer, said in an email that he was not aware of any other programs like the company's. A Pentagon official said no company receives preferential treatment: “The War Department always looks for solutions to benefit the warfighter, not any pre-selected entity.” The statement added: “Outside affiliations, investors, or political connections play absolutely no role in the Department's funding decisions.”

Pete Hegseth has had a longtime interest in classical education, especially in a version of the teaching style that incorporates Christian history and virtues, and as defense secretary has pushed those views. U.S. military academies now accept the Classic Learning Test, which positions itself as a classical alternative to the SAT. In September, defense schools announced they would give 100 vouchers to students to take the test. This month, the agency announced that the test would be widely available to students as a college readiness assessment.

This summer, schools operated by the department announced an online course on Western Civilization. The textbook for the course includes suggested lesson plans developed in partnership with Hillsdale College, a conservative Christian school in Michigan that has also developed influential materials for K-12 schools. Some scholars said the text presents a misleading portrait of historical events, often portraying Western civilization and Christianity as one and the same and selectively highlighting details to frame Islam as a violent threat to both.

The new Pentagon contractors also have ties to Hillsdale College. Adams graduated from the college and worked in K-12 curriculum development there, including on the Trump-aligned 1776 Project, a free curriculum for schools. The project was created in response to what the college described as “the weaponization of history.” The 1776 Project's content has been criticized for downplaying slavery and describing the Jamestown colony as a failed communist project. Whalen previously worked in the college president's office.

Leadership of the military school system has also been overhauled. Matthew Kirby, promoted from his former position as the system's Americas regional director, now leads the schools. Kirby is the founder and former CEO of Liberty Classical Schools, an Atlanta-based network of charter schools.

Early in Trump's second administration, defense schools removed books from libraries covering topics such as race, gender and democracy. The ACLU filed a lawsuit and was partially successful in overturning the book bans at a handful of schools.
