## Fernandez spent roughly a year deciphering Francis's handwritten notebooks

The Private Diary of Pope Francis, released worldwide on Nov. 24, 2026, was drawn from notebooks Francis showed to the Vatican's doctrine chief, Cardinal Victor Manuel Fernandez, also known as Tucho Fernandez. The Vatican blessed the publication, which offers selected passages from the 12 years Francis served as pope and his descriptions of feeling that he encountered the devil.

Colleen Dulle, who covers the Vatican for America Magazine and hosts the *Inside The Vatican* podcast, discussed the book with NPR's Scott Detrow. Dulle said Fernandez selected the diary snippets and placed them in context.

While Francis was alive, he showed Fernandez and some others the notebooks. Dulle said they flipped through them quickly and knew they contained writing. After Francis' death, Fernandez asked Pope Leo if he could see the diary and perhaps publish it.

Fernandez then spent roughly a year trying to decipher Francis's teeny, tiny handwriting and passages written in symbols. The resulting book contains snippets rather than a continuous diary, with Fernandez providing context for the selections. It offers a rare look at what Francis was thinking and feeling during his 12 years as pope.

Dulle said Francis referred to a few different times that he felt he had encounters with the devil. One was around 2006 and another around 2013, though the account does not establish whether the latter occurred before or after Francis became pope. Dulle described the episodes as fleeting and visceral.

One episode occurred during Mass, when Francis wrote of seeing the devil's fiery eyes. In another, he described the devil passing in a car and seeing the devil's eyes. The third came in 2018, while Francis was dealing with the Catholic Church's sexual abuse crisis. He wrote that he heard the devil's rasping voice say, “I've gotten everything that I wanted.”

Dulle said the diary does not identify the precise date of the 2018 entry. She said it likely came shortly after Francis's visit to Chile, where reporters challenged his initial handling of the abuse case and told him he did not know all the facts. Francis ultimately admitted he was wrong, called in the survivors and reconciled with them. He also called on all the Chilean bishops to submit their resignations en masse.

Dulle described the period as a crisis point in Francis's papacy. The same summer brought the Pennsylvania grand jury report and the fall of Cardinal Theodore McCarrick. Detrow said it took Francis a while to fully get a handle on the abuse crises.

The diary also documents a separate period of spiritual difficulty from January 2021 through the end of 2022. Francis did not identify this period as a spiritual crisis, Dulle said; Fernandez framed it that way. Francis did not clearly explain what began the episode, but he described a powerful moment when God revealed his sinfulness to him.

Dulle said the difficulty continued until the end of 2022, when Francis chose to move beyond it by admitting when he was wrong and recommitting himself more deeply to the poor and the marginalized. She described those commitments as hallmarks of his pontificate. The published excerpts preserve Francis's private language, while Fernandez's selection and context show how the Vatican prepared the material for publication.
