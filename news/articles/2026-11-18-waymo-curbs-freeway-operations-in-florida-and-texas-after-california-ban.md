## California surface-street service continues as Waymo works to restore freeway access

Waymo temporarily restricted freeway operations across its other U.S. markets, including Florida and Texas, after California's Department of Motor Vehicles barred the company from testing and operating on all freeways in the state. California's action followed incidents including three Waymo robotaxis entering an active construction zone on U.S. Route 101.

California's DMV said in a statement that the ban covered both testing and operations. The department cited several incidents involving Waymo's robotaxis, including the recent case in which three vehicles entered the active construction zone on U.S. Route 101.

Waymo's spokesperson said in a statement, “In alignment with our safety-first principles, we have also temporarily restricted freeway operations across our other operating markets to evaluate our performance.” The company also has operations in states including Florida and Texas.

Waymo continues to operate its robotaxi services on surface streets in California, the company said. The spokesperson added that the robotaxi operator was working with the California DMV and other state officials to resume freeway operations.

The restrictions come as robotaxi safety becomes increasingly important as robotaxi companies scale their fleets and expand overseas. Waymo also faces increased regulatory scrutiny over the safety of its autonomous driving technology.

Earlier this year, a number of Baidu's Apollo Go robotaxis froze in the middle of traffic in Wuhan, China, which led regulators to pause issuing new robotaxi permits. The issuance of permits has since resumed.

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration is investigating the effectiveness of a software fix that Alphabet's Waymo implemented to prevent its autonomous vehicles from passing stopped school buses. The agency said earlier this month that it had identified a large number of reported incidents involving Waymo autonomous vehicles passing stopped school buses with their red lights flashing or stop arm extended.

The software update meant to prevent those traffic-safety violations was completed for all affected vehicles on Nov. 17, 2025. The agency said a majority of the reported incidents occurred after that date.
