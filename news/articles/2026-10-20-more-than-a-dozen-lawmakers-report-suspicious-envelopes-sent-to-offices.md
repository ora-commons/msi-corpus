## Earlier powder mailings targeted Louisiana and Oklahoma election offices last week

More than a dozen U.S. lawmakers said Monday that suspicious envelopes, many containing white powder, were sent to their offices, joining dozens of colleagues already targeted in a wave of mailings weeks before November's midterm elections. Some envelopes arrived at offices; others were intercepted by postal investigators before reaching staff.

The House members who disclosed new mailings Monday were all Democrats: Reps. Suhas Subramanyam of Virginia; Joe Neguse, Jason Crow, Brittany Pettersen and Diana DeGette of Colorado; Chellie Pingree of Maine; Debbie Wasserman Schultz of Florida; Becca Balint of Vermont; Greg Stanton, Yassamin Ansari and Adelita Grijalva of Arizona; and Adam Smith of Washington. Sens. Hickenlooper and Johnson also said Monday that letters had reached their offices.

"I can confirm that my office in Leesburg received an envelope containing a suspicious substance," Subramanyam wrote on social media Monday afternoon, adding that the office had been promptly evacuated. He thanked the Leesburg Police, Loudoun County Fire and Rescue, their Hazmat team and the Capitol Police "for their swift response," and wrote that "these acts of political intimidation have no place in our country."

Johnson said the envelope was delivered to his Milwaukee office at 2:38 p.m. CT Monday and that the return address matched the description of letters sent to other lawmakers' offices, though he did not publicly identify the address. "My staff immediately isolated the envelope, cleared all staff members and called 911 and the Sergeant at Arms," he said on social media. "The first responders confiscated the letter, and thankfully, everyone is safe. Our office operations have now resumed as normal."

Pingree and Wasserman Schultz both published photos of their suspicious letters online, showing that the addresses were handwritten. "Such cowardly acts of coercion will not work and have absolutely no place in our politics," Wasserman Schultz said in a statement. She added that the mailings "will not deter me from fully representing my Broward County constituents."

While the return address on the letter posted by Wasserman Schultz was redacted, the letter posted by Pingree bears a New York City address formerly used by the Democratic Socialists of America, according to UPI. A spokesperson for the DSA, Priscilla Yeverino, said the group is not involved in the letters in any way. "We are glad to learn no one was harmed," Yeverino said.

The Senate Sergeant at Arms, U.S. Capitol Police, FBI and U.S. Postal Inspection Service are coordinating the investigation. Sen. Adam Schiff, D-Calif., said Sunday that at least one envelope containing a suspicious substance was sent to his California offices and was intercepted by Capitol Police and the Senate Sergeant at Arms. "This attempt to intimidate elected officials and our staff will not succeed," Schiff said. "We instead condemn these threats as antithetical to the values of our nation, recommit to our work and thank the law enforcement and postal officials who are helping keep our teams safe."

Other lawmakers whose offices have been targeted in the broader wave include Reps. Seth Moulton, D-Mass., and Eugene Vindman, D-Va., and Sens. Pete Ricketts, R-Neb., Ted Cruz, R-Texas and Mike Rounds, R-S.D.

The wave of mailings apparently began last Tuesday, when Louisiana Secretary of State Nancy Landry and the Oklahoma State Election Board reported receiving envelopes containing a similar white powder.
