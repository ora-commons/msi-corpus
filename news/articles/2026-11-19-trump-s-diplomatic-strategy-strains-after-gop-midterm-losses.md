## Trump, Xi and Putin meet with little sign of progress

President Trump met with Russia’s Vladimir Putin and China’s Xi Jinping on Thursday in a 90-minute session. Trump said history was being made at the first-ever summit with the leaders of China and Russia. The meeting produced little indication of progress on the wars in Ukraine and Iran or the proliferation of nuclear weapons. The Wall Street Journal said the leaders’ willingness to sit together was the point.

The meeting came after the GOP lost the midterm elections, which The Wall Street Journal said would leave Trump much less leeway to appease Russia in its war with Ukraine next year. The Journal said Trump now has perhaps his weakest domestic standing of either presidency and that his international power and leverage might be contracting.

Trump’s approach to Xi has also shifted between presidential terms. The Journal said Trump was going out of his way to befriend Xi, unlike his first term, when he was constantly looking for leverage. The Nvidia scandal has raised questions about the White House’s AI policy and its policy toward China.

The practical outcomes remain unsettled. The Journal asked whether Trump’s outreach would bring new jobs to the United States, new Chinese investment in the United States or the export of more semiconductor chips to China.

Those questions could come to a head in December, when Trump is scheduled to host the G20 summit in southern Florida. The Journal identified Europe, Canada, Mexico and Japan as the Old World Order and said they should be there, as should members of Trump’s New World Order. It portrayed Trump as politically weakened but positioned to create a spectacle.

The Journal said Trump launched the effort after taking office for the second time last year to reshape international institutions to his will and create a New World Order. The Journal listed the G20, G7, NATO, United Nations and USMCA as institutions he was moving away from, and Russia, China, North Korea, Argentina, El Salvador, Hungary, Israel, Saudi Arabia and a post-Maduro Venezuela as countries he was moving toward. The approach worked for a while, according to the account.

The Journal said some new alliances have proved ephemeral or are struggling under their own domestic pressures. It also posed the question of whether Trump’s diplomacy is reaching deals, forging treaties or conducting photo ops. The Thursday summit did not answer that broader question.

Trump also floated the possibility of meeting North Korea’s Kim Jong Un while on the Asia trip and was scheduled to return to Washington, D.C., after wrapping up the trip.

The longer-term question is what survives after Trump. The Journal asked whether the next U.S. president, whether a Democrat or a Republican, would share his fondness for these countries. The durability of the alliances and the practical results of the diplomacy remained unresolved in the account.
