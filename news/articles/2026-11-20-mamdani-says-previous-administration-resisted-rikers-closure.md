## Council approval required; jail population goal is a little over 4,000

At a City Hall news conference, New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani, a Democrat, said the extended timeline for closing the Rikers Island jail complex was needed because of resistance from the previous administration. He called Rikers "a place we have long turned our eyes away from rather than confront head on."

Mamdani said: "The call to close Rikers has echoed across our city for decades. Work has begun to do so, then stopped, then started once again. What will make this chapter different is our commitment to see it through." The New York City Council must approve the proposed deadlines.

The Associated Press reported that the city was all but certain to miss a legal requirement to close Rikers and replace it with four smaller, more modern jails by 2027. Under Mamdani's plan, City Hall expects the borough jails to open in Brooklyn in 2029, the Bronx and Queens in 2031 and Manhattan in 2032.

The plan centers on lowering the city's inmate population from around 6,700 people currently housed at Rikers to a little over 4,000. The Mamdani administration proposed a new slate of diversion and supervised release programs, expansion of housing and social services to prevent recidivism, restoration of an online bail payment system and reduced case delays so people spend less time behind bars.

Mamdani said: "Our nation has a very real mass incarceration problem. We are making a conscious decision to advance a new model of a criminal legal system in our city, one that takes on that problem head on."

Instead of housing prisoners at Rikers, an island in the East River near LaGuardia Airport, the city would shift people to jails in the Bronx, Queens, Brooklyn and Manhattan, as well as a handful of medical facilities for people who need healthcare or psychiatric treatment. The city also plans to reduce the number of beds for women and divert them into community-based programs.

Council Speaker Julie Menin praised the mayor's plan at the news conference, saying that after years of delay, "we are finally getting back on track."

The Associated Press reported that Rikers Island had for years been viewed as a symbol of dysfunction and that the jail network was so plagued by violence and overcrowding that a federal judge, citing "abnormal and unacceptable" conditions, appointed an outside manager to restore order and implement reforms.

Mamdani has supported closing Rikers, while the longstanding plan to build new jails drew opposition, including from his predecessor, former Mayor Eric Adams. Adams opposed federal oversight and borough jails.

The City Council voted in 2019 to close the jail complex and replace it with newer jails located in the city's boroughs by 2027. At the time, city officials called for shrinking the jail population to accommodate the closure, but critics argued that doing so would mean more violent criminals on city streets.
