## Pelosi reflects on historic career and predicts a 'big victory' for Democrats

House Democrats won seats they had not expected to win, according to an Associated Press report republished by PBS News. The same report said Hakeem Jeffries brushed past party skeptics to secure the party's unopposed, closed-door nomination for speaker. The Wednesday vote put Jeffries of New York on track to become the nation's first Black American with the gavel, but the full House must vote separately Jan. 3, 2027. Rep. Katherine Clark of Massachusetts and Rep. Pete Aguilar of California also ran unopposed for majority leader and majority whip.

AP described the party as restive and eager for change after voters ousted Republicans in a rebuke of President Donald Trump and the party in power. Phone calls and social media posts have been flooding the offices of Democrats and newly elected members, demanding a fresh start. Protesters heckled Jeffries last week during a Democratic conference in Denver.

Jeffries said ahead of Wednesday's vote that he was taking nothing for granted. He met with the caucuses that make up House Democrats and talked to almost all members of what will be their large majority, AP reported. "I embrace those conversations," he said.

Clark would be the first woman to serve as majority leader, while Aguilar would be the first Latino to serve as majority whip, AP reported. For dozens of members-elect, Wednesday's vote was the first of their careers, before they were sworn into office in January. With one of the biggest incoming majorities in decades, Jeffries will have room to spare, AP said.

Rep.-elect Brad Lander of New York announced he would support Jeffries when he launched his campaign for Congress. Rep.-elect Randy Villegas of California has said he was supporting whoever would help him best deliver for his Central Valley district.

A PBS NewsHour video featured Nancy Pelosi reflecting on her historic career and predicting a 'big victory' for Democrats. A separate PBS NewsHour item was headlined "House Republicans pick Ohio Rep. Jim Jordan to lead them in the minority."

Returning Rep. Emily Randall of Washington said she heard questions about House leadership during town halls in her district and had been watching direct messages stream into her Instagram feed about the leadership election, so she decided to start dialing them back. "People have messaged me asking if Bernie Sanders could be the speaker of the House," she said, referring to the Vermont independent senator who caucuses with Democrats. "Technically yes, and it would be probably difficult," she said.

AP noted that the House can elect anyone speaker but said it was unlikely to choose Sanders because he already has his day job in the Senate. On one call, Randall said she listened to the constituent's concerns and then replied, "Truthfully, not like blowing smoke, whoever is our speaker — and I think it is going to be Hakeem Jeffries, and I am supporting Hakeem Jeffries for speaker — I think we will accomplish a lot of the things that he was asking us to."

Lawmakers pointed to a livestream in which Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez of New York tried to explain the role of the speaker. "There is one candidate in this race: Hakeem Jeffries," Ocasio-Cortez said at the Capitol. "I think that everyone is far more concerned what that leadership means, what is it promising."

AP described Jeffries, Clark and Aguilar as having risen together as a team and as the first new generation of leaders of the post-Pelosi era. They will soon serve as leaders in the majority for the first time. When Rep. Nancy Pelosi of California decided to step aside from leadership in 2022 after the party's electoral defeat handed Republicans the majority, AP said, it set the stage for this era. Pelosi, No. 2 Democratic Rep. Steny Hoyer of Maryland and Rep. James Clyburn of South Carolina had often moved together, rising and then stepping back from their long-held positions.

A parallel contest is underway in the Senate, where Senate leader Chuck Schumer of New York faces a challenge from Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland, AP reported. Returning Rep. Yassamin Ansari of Arizona said the situations in the Senate and the House "are quite different."

Ansari said Jeffries had been a collaborative leader who was listening to ideas from progressive caucus members like hers and others. "His job is to reflect all of us, not just a faction," Ansari said. "It is more productive to focus on preparing on what we're going to do in the majority and what we need and want to get done in those first 100 days," she said.

Rep. Shomari Figures of Alabama, who won in a redrawn district favoring the Republican candidate, said before the vote that he would support Jeffries. "It's not even like a complex question," he said about Jeffries, who he said "led us through what is likely the most tumultuous two-year tenure of any presidency in the history of this nation." "I'm not going to not stand by him right here on the precipice of history," he said.
