## Each jurisdiction’s rate would reflect its earlier deal with Trump

BBC News reported that the Trump administration is preparing to announce new import tariffs on 16 jurisdictions, including China, the European Union, Mexico and Japan. The duties would hit many of America’s biggest trading partners and follow a Section 301 investigation launched in March after the Supreme Court ruled the administration’s April 2025 “Liberation Day” tariffs illegal.

The planned action would continue the administration’s use of tariffs after the Supreme Court struck down the sweeping global duties Trump unveiled in April 2025 shortly after returning to the White House. Trump has made tariffs a signature policy of his presidency, arguing that they will help boost American manufacturing and create jobs.

U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer argued during that earlier tariff fight that overproduction abroad was displacing American manufacturing and deterring investment.

Under the proposed tariffs, each country would face a different import-tax rate depending on the trade deal it had previously struck with Trump.

BBC News said the announcement is likely to increase tensions between the US and China, the world’s two biggest economies, which remain locked in a trade dispute. A meeting between Trump and President Xi Jinping the previous week did not fully resolve the sides’ differences, and an interim agreement is set to expire on 10 January.

In the run-up to the Shenzhen summit, the US accused China of not fully living up to the terms China agreed in South Korea in October 2025. Washington said Beijing was falling short on its commitments, “particularly as they relate to agricultural purchases, rare earths, and other critical minerals.”

Earlier on Tuesday, China extended a suspension of a planned ban on exports of critical minerals including gallium, germanium and antimony until 27 January. The ban otherwise was due to come into force on Friday.

BBC News also reported that critics had said the Trump administration’s tariff policy raises prices for American consumers and damages the global economy.
