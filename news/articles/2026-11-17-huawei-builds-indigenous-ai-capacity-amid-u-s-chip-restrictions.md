## Nvidia and Huawei emerge as the central AI competitors

Huawei has built indigenous chip and artificial-intelligence capacity after the Biden administration blocked all Chinese companies from the world's most advanced chips and the most advanced manufacturing machines. In a Nov. 17 report from Shenzhen, PBS News said Huawei's revenue fell 30 percent after the restrictions and later returned to 2020 levels, while the Chinese government provided the company with billions. The company's expanding role in AI comes as Nvidia said it would increase oversight of advanced-chip access and as President Donald Trump prepares to meet Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin during his Asia visit.

Nick Schifrin visited the 300-acre research and development campus of state technology champion Huawei outside Shenzhen, a city that has grown from a fishing village into an economic hub and is home to some of China's largest technology companies. The campus has 108 structures and 30,000 employees, mostly researchers. Its tram route passes buildings designed to resemble Fribourg, Switzerland; buildings out of Verona, Italy, fair, if only a decade old; Heidelberg, Germany's castle; and the Sorbonne University in Paris. China describes itself as the Middle Kingdom, while the campus uses European imagery. The report noted that the real Sorbonne's motto, "Creator of the Future," also applies to Huawei. Schifrin said Trump was due to join 20 other APEC leaders in Shenzhen the next day.

The Trump administration blacklisted Huawei in 2019 and labeled it a national security threat in 2020. The Biden administration then blocked all Chinese companies from the world's most advanced chips and from the most advanced manufacturing machines used in high-volume manufacturing. Mike Pompeo said, "We don't want companies to be complicit in Huawei's human rights abuses." Gina Raimondo said, "The Commerce Department is very focused on export controls."

With no access to the best individual chips at the heart of the AI race, Huawei doubled down on designing its own chips and this year began to stitch them together to make them faster and more efficient. At its flagship store, Huawei showed a phone that was the company's first to use a new chip design intended to pack more computing power and work around American restrictions. A man at the demonstration described the Kirin 9050 Pro: "Kirin 9050 Pro, super fast, super intelligent, super cool, the most important Kirin chip ever."

Huawei's revenue dropped 30 percent after the U.S. restrictions but returned to 2020 levels, and the company received billions from Beijing along the way. PBS News described Huawei as a telecommunications giant that had become a leader in China's chips and AI industry, developing indigenous AI capacity out of necessity. The report said Huawei's work on indigenous technology had helped both the company and China become more self-reliant in AI. Huawei also increasingly relies on China's domestic market and newer businesses, including smart-car technology.

Michael Frank, CEO of Radiant Intel, an AI research platform for geopolitical risk with long experience in Asia, called Huawei China's national AI champion. Frank said Huawei's business strategy is part and parcel of China's national AI development and that the company is "the biggest player by far." He also said, "They are the most capable, and, most importantly, they're the most indigenized." Frank said that indigenization left Huawei better insulated from international technology shocks that U.S. policy might have used against it.

Frank also described Huawei's financial and market advantages: "They have a lot of resources at their disposal. They have functionally infinite capital. The Chinese government has plenty of money to throw at the problem. And they have a massive domestic market that they can use as a test bed." Huawei arranged for Schifrin to ride in an autonomous car called Huawei Cockpit. During the demonstration, the demonstrator said, "Sir, I'm not touching it. It's my hand and my feet are released." Schifrin then said, "So it's driving itself." Because the company is blocked from many foreign parts, the vehicle is increasingly made in China.

During the ride, the demonstrator asked the AI assistant to play some music. The assistant currently understands only Chinese because it is intended for the Chinese market. A base model for China's richest costs more than $100,000, according to the report. Bills making their way through Congress would close the U.S. market to all Chinese electric vehicles, which Schifrin said was a reminder that Washington still considers Huawei's success a threat.

Schifrin said President Trump acknowledged that the AI race defines the U.S.-China technology competition. Schifrin also described Nvidia as a primary source of U.S. success in AI. Trump said, "We're leading now China substantially in AI, and we're going to keep it that way." One speaker described Nvidia's Blackwell technology with the words, "Blackwell is an engineering marvel." Schifrin said the U.S.-China technology battle would increasingly become a competition between Nvidia and Huawei.

Frank said, "We have in Huawei the instrument of China's interest in dominating the AI stack globally. And, conversely, Nvidia is our best hope to win over as much of the global market as possible. If we lose the global market to Huawei, even if Nvidia is today the cutting edge, you're going to have Huawei reaping the rewards." Frank also said, "You look at the experience of the last six years, it would be foolish to underestimate their capabilities. This is a zero-sum competition, and it's going to be these two players going head to head for the next few years."

Nvidia said it would increase oversight to make sure China did not smuggle or remotely access its most advanced chips. Trump called the decision "a good idea. It's a great company." The move came as Washington tightened chip-export restrictions again over concerns that Chinese companies could remotely access advanced chips.

Former CEO Jensen Huang departed following an affair with a woman whose Singapore-based company bought advanced Nvidia chips, according to Schifrin's report. The Singapore-based company was accused of carrying out the remote access and smuggling. That development led to further U.S. restrictions on chip exports this week, and Schifrin said the promised increase in oversight was a way for Nvidia's board to clean up the mess Huang had left behind. After stepping down under pressure, Huang acknowledged that he had not lived up to his former company's standards. He then noted an online campaign calling for his reinstatement and wrote on X: "If the board of directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO." There were no indications that Nvidia's board was interested, and the board later confirmed that it had no intention of reinstating him as CEO.

Schifrin began the segment in Manila with Trump's 48-hour visit to Asia. Trump attended a summit of regional leaders in the Philippines before heading to China for meetings with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin on Thursday. It was his first trip abroad since many midterm voters rejected his leadership. Schifrin described the region as eager for more of Trump's attention, while noting that Southeast Asian leaders had not always welcomed it since Trump made them tariff targets.

At the gathering, Trump said, "America is deeply committed to the success, sovereignty, and prosperity of our many friends and partners, many of whom are at this table right now." He announced progress on economic deals with Thailand, saying, "Actually, it's a very big trade deal." Trump also addressed a group of allies described as a counterweight to China: "Ensure that none of our nations is vulnerable to coercion by any single foreign supplier." Schifrin described the supplier language as a barely veiled reference to Beijing and said U.S.-China tensions over technology loomed over the summit and the region.

Trump was due to join 20 other APEC leaders in Shenzhen. He said he wanted to discuss arms control with Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin, although China had given no indication that it was interested in a serious conversation about reducing its nuclear stockpile, which the report described as the world's fastest-growing arsenal. Trump said he would also discuss Ukraine, which Schifrin described as one of Vladimir Putin's top priorities.

Many Russian officials raised the possibility that the meetings could amount to a "Yalta two." Schifrin said Yalta one brought together Roosevelt, Stalin and Churchill, who divided up Europe and embraced spheres of influence, even if today's leaders do not have the same leverage and aren't even allies, at least when it comes to President Trump. Chinese analysts told Schifrin that Xi Jinping's priority was the photo-op to try and prove to the world that the world's two biggest nuclear superpowers, the U.S. and Russia, would come to him, as he tries to portray himself as the source of global stability.
