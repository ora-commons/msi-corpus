## Wallonia report to prosecutors alleges illegal 3-hectare building

Wallonia authorities have ordered Google, a US tech company, to halt work on a large, nearly completed datacentre at Aiseau-Presles in the south of Belgium after the regional government revoked approval for the company’s revised design. Raphaël Stokis, a Wallonia regional official, told RTBF that inspectors found the building almost finished and its structural work completed even though the permit had been refused. Regional officials said they were submitting a report to public prosecutors. Google has appealed the revocation to Belgium’s council of state, where a ruling is expected on 7 January.

Raphaël Stokis, a Wallonia regional official, told the public broadcaster RTBF that “Inspectors visited the site and observed that the building was almost finished, with the structural work completed, even though the permit had been refused.” Stokis said the 300 workers had been given a verbal order to stop work, and that the order had been confirmed in writing to Google. The company said in a statement that it was “committed to maintaining an open and collaborative dialogue” with the regional authorities.

Google’s 2024 permit authorized plans for three smaller, air-cooled datacentres. In 2025, the company applied to merge two of them into one much larger facility and switch to liquid cooling. The revised design would draw 600 cubic metres of water an hour from the nearby Sambre River.

Local authorities initially approved the amendments in April, but residents and protest groups appealed. In August, Wallonia infrastructure minister François Desquesnes revoked the modified permit over drought concerns. Google appealed that decision to Belgium’s council of state, with a ruling expected on 7 January.

The project stands on a 50-hectare business park built at the site of a former coalmine in Aiseau-Presles, about 15km (9 miles) west of Charleroi. The datacentre building occupies about 3 hectares, or 7.4 acres, and was one of three facilities Google originally planned for the site.

Nicolas Yernaux, a Wallonia regional government spokesperson, told Agence France-Presse on Friday that a report was being submitted to public prosecutors alleging “a major offence: the illegal construction of a building covering 3 hectares”.

Greenpeace separately published a report earlier in the week that included drone footage showing construction continuing without the correct permits. The environmental group said, “The tech giant is continuing work, even though in August the latest version of its planning permission to build this datacentre was revoked,” and said it had filed an emergency appeal with local planning authorities. Greenpeace warned, “The climate crisis will only intensify in our region,” adding, “Against this backdrop, the construction of energy-guzzling behemoths cannot be allowed to go unnoticed.”

The Belgium case is the latest legal confrontation involving Google’s datacentre plans in Europe as large technology companies seek to build capacity to support the AI boom. In October, Finland opened a criminal investigation into possible breaches of environmental laws at a planned Google datacentre in the country’s rural north. Work there has been halted until environmental impact assessments are completed.

The immediate result of the Wallonia order is a construction stoppage. The council of state ruling is expected on 7 January, while a report alleging illegal construction is being submitted to public prosecutors.
