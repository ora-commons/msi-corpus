## WTI gains 1.6% to $92 as rebound follows steeper midweek selloff

Oil prices rose Friday, with Brent crude climbing back above $100 a barrel as traders weighed renewed security threats to Middle Eastern oil shipments following a sharp selloff earlier in the week, The Wall Street Journal reported.

Front-month Brent, the global oil benchmark, gained 2.1% to $100.34 a barrel. West Texas Intermediate, the U.S. benchmark, added 1.6% to $92 a barrel.

The advance extended Thursday's rebound, when Brent settled nearly 4% higher after plunging more than 8% in the previous session.

The earlier decline reflected growing expectations of a diplomatic resolution to the Iran conflict following the Democratic Party's victory in the U.S. midterm elections, according to MUFG.

Analysts at ANZ said the selloff appeared excessive. They pointed to continuing tensions in the Middle East, including Saudi Arabian warnings of potential danger near the port city of Yanbu, a major Red Sea oil export hub.

Saudi Arabia's Civil Defense issued an alert Thursday warning of potential danger in Yanbu before saying that the danger had passed. The authority did not specify the nature of the threat.

The warning drew attention to Saudi Arabia's Red Sea export route, which has become increasingly important as producers seek alternatives to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz.

Attacks on shipping had complicated efforts to sustain a recovery in Gulf oil exports.
