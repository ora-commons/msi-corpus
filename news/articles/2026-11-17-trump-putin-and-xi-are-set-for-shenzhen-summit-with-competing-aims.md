## Kevin Rudd says China seeks to be seen as a convening power

President Trump, Vladimir Putin and Xi Jinping are scheduled to meet Thursday in the Chinese city of Shenzhen for a three-way summit. The Wall Street Journal said the meeting is more likely to show the limits of the three powers to impose a new international order as the Pax Americana comes to an end. The report said Russia wants an expanded sphere of influence in Europe as part of any settlement of its war with Ukraine; Washington has promoted the “Donroe Doctrine” of U.S. dominance in the Americas; and China seeks to absorb Taiwan and eventually push the United States out of Asia.

Russian officials are already comparing the gathering with the 1945 Yalta Conference, where the victors of World War II met to decide Europe’s fate. Unlike those statesmen, Trump, Putin and Xi are not convening as winners in a global conflict they fought shoulder to shoulder. The report said Trump and Putin are instead bogged down in wars that have weakened their global standing and show no sign of abating.

The unpopular U.S. conflict with Iran has also had political consequences at home. The Wall Street Journal said the war helped trigger a wave of Democratic victories in the midterms that are likely to curtail Trump’s domestic power. China has gained geopolitical capital by positioning itself as a voice of reason in contrast to what the report calls a bellicose Russia and an at-time unpredictable America. Beijing also benefited from the U.S. war in Iran, which depleted munitions stocks and diverted assets that had been used to protect Washington’s Asian allies.

Tong Zhao, a senior fellow at Carnegie China, said Beijing benefits from the United States’ willingness to engage its geopolitical rivals. “China really enjoys the fact that the United States is now willing to sit down with its geopolitical foes to jointly discuss issues related to global governance,” he said. Zhao said the United States was stuck in the Iran war and Russia was stuck in the Ukraine war, leaving Beijing feeling it could appear relatively more successful, stable and responsible.

China’s position also has limits. The report said its economy is softening, while Russian and U.S. military setbacks, partly attributed to technological changes that give smaller states greater agency, have made Beijing more cautious about military adventures in the immediate future. Carlo Calenda, an Italian senator, called the gathering “a summit of the three weaknesses.” He said Russia was economically broken and militarily experiencing a disaster, Trump was a lame duck with a difficult situation at home, and China faced grave domestic economic difficulties. He added, “All three are in a much more precarious position than it seems.”

Russian officials, by contrast, view the meeting’s symbolism as more important than its substance. Dmitri Trenin, president of the Kremlin-established Russian International Affairs Council, said the imagery would show that three global powers now existed. He described the United States and China as full-spectrum global powers important in all spheres. “But Russia is also a great global power when it comes to military and geopolitical foundations,” he said. Trenin said that, for the past five years and maybe even longer, Western efforts to cancel or thoroughly isolate Russia had failed. He declared, “This didn’t happen, and we consider it our victory.”

China has provided Russia with significant economic and political backing but has stopped short of supplying Moscow with weapons and maintains an embassy in central Kyiv. Western officials believe Beijing is eager to prevent Russia’s defeat but is equally uninterested in a Russian victory that would allow Moscow to reduce its growing dependence on China and deal on a more equal footing with Washington.

Kevin Rudd, a former Australian prime minister who now heads the Asia Society in New York, said “This meeting will not result in the division of any territory in the world.” He described China’s preference for such meetings as minimalist, completely scripted and producing zero outcomes. Rudd said China’s baseline interest was to be seen as a convening power, however symbolic it may be. He added, “China wants to become an acceptable global power in the future, and too close an affiliation with Vladimir Putin doesn’t help that on a global multilateral stage.”

For Trump, the pageantry of the trilateral summit and the decision to elevate Putin reflect a longstanding belief about how the world order should function, according to Phil Gordon, a Brookings Institution scholar who served as national security adviser to Vice President Kamala Harris. Gordon also emphasized the agency of smaller countries: “As we see with Ukraine, human beings who are part of nations don’t just defer to big strong leaders.”

The report said Trump has repeatedly criticized European leaders for undercutting his outreach to Russia. Trans-Atlantic relations plummeted over tariffs he imposed, his demand to seize Greenland and his complaints that European nations did not join the war on Iran. Trump also demanded that Ukraine get a new president after President Volodymyr Zelensky refused requests to stop striking Russian refineries.

Democrats flipped control of both the House and the Senate in the midterms and criticized Trump’s rapprochement with Putin and his attempts to pressure Ukraine. They have already promised to use their powers once the new Congress takes office in January to back Kyiv and oppose Russia. With Trump’s hold over the party weakened, Republicans may join Democrats in that effort as well as promote a harder line against China, especially in the wake of the Nvidia scandal, the report said.

Republican Sen. Tom Cotton of Arkansas said on X recently that talks with Russia led by Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff have become a farce. “Putin will only agree to stop the war when he knows he can’t win. We need to help Ukraine prove that point,” he said.

Putin’s war on Ukraine has already lasted significantly longer than Moscow’s war against Nazi Germany. Rather than raising the Russian flag over the enemy capital, his troops remain engaged in bloody battles over the same villages in southern and eastern Ukraine that they tried to capture a year ago, the report said.

A foreign minister of a European Union nation offered a different historical warning, saying the right comparison for what Putin—and perhaps Trump—seek in Shenzhen isn’t so much Yalta in 1945 as Munich in 1938, a deal between London, Paris and Berlin that sacrificed Czechoslovakia to appease Hitler. The difference, the minister said, is that Ukraine is not Czechoslovakia and, as events of the past five years have shown, will not accept its fate and surrender.

Ukrainian Foreign Minister Andriy Sybiha agreed with that warning. “Putin is floating ideas for redrawing Europe’s security order. In his dreams, he sees himself in Yalta in 1945. The problem is, to propose ideas on that scale, you first need to win a world war,” he said. Sybiha said Russia has lost over 1.6 million troops killed and wounded yet has not achieved a single goal of its invasion.
