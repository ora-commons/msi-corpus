## Convicted Theranos founder's sentence already reduced by 18 months

Elizabeth Holmes, the founder of the failed blood-testing startup Theranos who was convicted of wire fraud and conspiracy to commit fraud by misleading investors, is scheduled to move from a federal prison camp in Texas to a halfway house in August 2027, according to a Department of Justice Victim Notification System update reported by ABC News.

The planned destination is the Austin Transitional Center in Del Valle, Texas — a 460-bed "community corrections center" owned by a private prison company. Holmes is currently held at Federal Prison Camp Bryan, located approximately 90 miles from her hometown of Houston. The Federal Bureau of Prisons declined to confirm details of the transfer, telling CNN Wednesday that the agency does not discuss "the conditions of confinement for any individual, including release plans."

A BOP spokesperson described the factors used to assign individuals to facilities: "BOP assigns individuals to facilities based on several factors. These include the level of security and supervision required, medical or programmatic needs, separation or security considerations necessary for the individual's safety, and other relevant factors, such as proximity to the individual's release residence."

Holmes was sentenced in 2022 to 11 years in federal prison after a jury found her guilty of wire fraud and conspiracy to commit fraud for misleading investors about her company's blood-testing capabilities. Her sentence has already been reduced by a year and a half, and in January she asked the Justice Department for a commutation.

Last year, Holmes' attorneys argued in court filings that she "maintained an excellent record post-sentencing" and had no disciplinary infractions during her time in prison. Holmes "dedicated herself to being an engaged prisoner, serving other women and actively seeking opportunities to make a difference," the attorneys wrote in their motion.

The government countered that Holmes had caused "substantial financial hardship" to victims, had not accepted responsibility for her crimes, had paid minimal restitution, and could reoffend, according to court documents. U.S. District Judge Edward Davila found Holmes eligible for a reduced sentence but said that eligibility "does not diminish the enormity of Holmes's crimes."

Theranos was once valued at $9 billion. Holmes founded the company as a college student, and the business claimed its technology could test for a wide range of diseases and conditions from a single pinprick of blood. The startup secured $945 million in financing, assembled a board of well-known political figures, and gained placements in notable retail outlets before its collapse. A new documentary about Holmes, "You Can See Everything," is scheduled for theatrical release Oct. 16.
