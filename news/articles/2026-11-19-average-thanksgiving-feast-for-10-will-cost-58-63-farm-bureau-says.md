## Farm Bureau: record turkey prices, higher fuel costs could raise dinner bill

Record turkey prices and higher fuel costs could make Thanksgiving dinner more expensive this year, the American Farm Bureau Federation said in a survey released Wednesday. The average Thanksgiving feast for 10 will cost $58.63 this year, 6% more than last year, the Farm Bureau said. The group tracks grocery prices through hundreds of volunteers in all 50 states and Puerto Rico.

That would be the first annual increase in the Farm Bureau's 40-year Thanksgiving cost series since 2022. The feast reached a record $64.05 in 2022, leaving the current estimate below that previous peak.

The survey's 16-pound turkey cost an average of $23.68 this year, up from $21.50 last year, the Farm Bureau said. Turkey prices have risen sharply over the past year. Turkey demand has been rising as more Americans seek out lean protein and U.S. turkey exports rise, and strong demand has limited U.S. inventories and pushed prices higher.

Bird flu has affected U.S. turkey flocks since an outbreak began in 2022. Mark Jordan, executive director of Leap Market Analytics, said cases have been waning and that turkey production increased substantially this year. "Over the last few years there's been a lot of doom and gloom for the turkey industry," Jordan said. "This is one of those years that turkey producers are feeling a little bit better about their lot in life."

Separately, the U.S. Department of Agriculture expects the wholesale price of a whole frozen turkey hen to average a record $1.76 per pound this year, up 29% from last year.

## High diesel prices raise costs for Thanksgiving staples

High diesel prices have made it more expensive for farmers to process and transport Thanksgiving staples such as milk, dinner rolls, potatoes and cranberries, the Farm Bureau said. AAA reported that diesel prices surged since the start of the Iran war and reached a record U.S. average of $6.53 per gallon in September. On Wednesday, diesel averaged $5.96 per gallon, up 58% from a year earlier, AAA said.

Jordan said diesel costs likely did not affect turkey prices this year, since so much of the decision-making and timing related to the turkeys that will be consumed this Thanksgiving happened before diesel prices spiraled. He said diesel costs could have a notable impact on turkey prices next year.

## Some menu items and retailer feasts cost less

Not every item in the meal became more expensive. The Farm Bureau said a 1-pound vegetable tray with carrots and celery cost 5 cents less, while a 16-ounce bag of frozen peas cost 4 cents less. Frozen pie crusts were also slightly cheaper, although a can of pumpkin pie filling was more expensive.

Some stores will price turkey and other Thanksgiving staples lower to draw shoppers, the report said. Aldi said its feast for 10, which mirrors the Farm Bureau's menu, costs $39 this year. Walmart, whose feast adds items such as corn muffin mix and marshmallows but excludes carrots and celery, said its feast for 10 costs $35.

## Food-at-home prices rose 22% from October 2021 to October 2026

The Associated Press report said, “Persistently high grocery prices have been frustrating Americans since the pandemic and played a role in Republicans' recent losses in the midterm elections.”

Prices for food eaten at home jumped 22% between October 2021 and October 2026, according to U.S. government data, almost double the usual pace of food price inflation. The USDA expects prices for food eaten at home to rise 2.3% this year, below the recent annual average of 2.6%.
