## Closed trial in 2024 sentenced Hubbard for Ukrainian army mercenary service

On October 21, Russian President Vladimir Putin said American Stephen Hubbard had been released from a Russian prison and was already at Pulkovo Airport in Saint Petersburg when he made the announcement, NPR reported. President Trump said he had asked Putin to release Hubbard before the talks. The disclosure came as Putin and White House envoy Steve Witkoff held talks focused on U.S. efforts to end the war in Ukraine.

NPR described Saint Petersburg as Russia's former imperial capital. As the talks with Witkoff got underway there, Putin announced that Hubbard had been released and was already at Pulkovo Airport in Saint Petersburg.

Stephen Hubbard was 74 and a resident of Michigan. NPR reported that Russian forces detained Hubbard in eastern Ukraine in 2022.

A closed trial in 2024 imposed a nearly seven-year prison sentence on Hubbard for serving as a mercenary in the Ukrainian army.

Putin said the release followed Trump's request. The U.S. had formally designated Hubbard wrongfully detained under the Biden administration.

NPR reported that Hubbard's release came as Trump had lifted some sanctions on Moscow and openly criticized Ukraine amid U.S. efforts to negotiate an end to the war. The report did not attribute the release to the sanctions change or Trump's criticism.

The immediate result was Hubbard's release from prison. Ending the war remained the larger objective identified for the Witkoff talks.
