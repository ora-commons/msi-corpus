## Macy's says safety of participants and spectators is its priority

NBC quoted Macy's as saying: “The safety of our participants and spectators is our priority.” Macy's added: “Thank you to the thousands of volunteers, performers and partners who create the magic millions enjoy each year. We look forward to celebrating together on Saturday.”

The parade that will be held on Saturday includes a tribute to Dolly Parton led by Megan Hilty, and special appearances by Melissa Joan Hart, Jack McBrayer, Janet McTeer and Holly Robinson Peete, all leading the way for Santa Claus.

Nine Broadway shows will be featured as part of a 10-minute medley, replacing the traditional sprinkling of full show numbers.

Forecasters said an impending storm on Thursday may spoil the annual street party, which is marked by a march of huge balloons, and AccuWeather is forecasting winds of 12 to 25 mph (19 to 40 km/h) and rain later on Thursday. Under guidelines, large balloons must stay grounded if sustained winds exceed 23 mph (37 km/h) or gusts exceed 34 mph (55 km/h).

The parade was postponed because of excessively windy weather and will now be held on Saturday, and its balloons have been grounded only once previously, in 1971.

## The parade began in 1924 to create holiday-window buzz

The Macy's parade began more than a century ago in 1924 as a way of creating buzz for the giant department store's holiday windows at Macy's Herald Square flagship store in Manhattan. Macy's borrowed animals from the Central Park Zoo for the first parades but in 1927 ended that practice after children were getting scared.

The animals were then replaced with air-filled balloons and, a year later, balloons that could be filled with helium — a now familiar sight for people around the world from countless Hollywood movies set in New York City.

The parades have been fully canceled previously only in 1942, 1943 and 1944, because the valuable materials needed to create the huge balloons were diverted to the second world war effort against Germany and Japan.
