## Scheme expands from six prisons to 83 by end of March

Justice Secretary Alex Norris announced that thousands more offenders will be tagged before leaving prison in England and Wales, according to the Press Association. The expanded scheme builds on a pilot that fits eligible offenders with tags before they leave prison or court rather than sending them away with a tagging order already in place.

Eligible offenders under the scheme are fitted with GPS, alcohol and curfew tags before they walk through prison gates or leave court. The move brings the physical fitting of a tag forward to the point of departure.

Six prisons started fitting tags before departure last year under the “tagging at source” scheme. The announcement extends that pilot to more prisons as part of a wider effort to supervise offenders leaving custody.

More prisons are set to join the scheme, bringing the total to 83 by the end of March. The number establishes the planned scale of the pre-departure tagging program.

Further waves of offenders are also set to be released early over the next year. The releases are intended to free up prison space in overcrowded jails in England and Wales, according to the report.

Norris said: “Criminals and defendants should be under no illusion that leaving prison or court means escaping the watchful eye of the authorities.” The announcement presents the pre-departure fitting of tags as a continuation of supervision for people leaving prison or court.

Norris also said tagging was one of the many tools used to monitor offenders and that the evidence showed it worked. The scheme uses GPS, alcohol and curfew tags to provide that supervision.

In the same statement, Norris said: “By fitting tags before they even walk through the prison gates or out of court, we’re tightening the net around those who break the law and making sure there is nowhere to hide from the consequences of their actions.”
