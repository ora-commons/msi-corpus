## Three-day itinerary leads from Manila to nuclear talks with Xi and Putin

President Donald Trump arrived in Manila, the Philippine capital, on Tuesday, met regional partners and sought to formalize a trade deal. Manila is Trump's first stop on a three-day trip that will later take him to China for historic three-way nuclear talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin. Trump said a meeting with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un was also possible. It is Trump's first trip abroad since his party suffered deep losses in the midterm elections.

The trip came amid a series of setbacks at home, ranging from a major disruption to the U.S. aviation industry to a scandal that has shaken the global AI industry. The White House and stock market have been unsettled by Jensen Huang's departure from Nvidia last week after he admitted to an affair with an alleged Chinese government agent. Beijing said it was not involved.

Reporters asked Trump about recent service outages involving Microsoft, the Federal Aviation Administration and online banking with JPMorgan Chase. The disruptions caused mass flight cancellations and disrupted workdays. Trump played down the problems. "It's life," Trump said. "When you're big and powerful and you have all the money, things like that happen. It's fine."

Trump was also asked about a CNN report that Iran was putting together plans to attack Washington with a dirty bomb or chemical weapons. "Well, if there is, they will pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid before," Trump said, adding, "I hope it's not. But we take it very seriously because they happen to be crazy."

Trump's first hours in the Philippines included a cocktail reception and dinner with other world leaders. The Philippines is Washington's oldest treaty ally in Asia, and Trump was to be feted Tuesday night as the "guest of honor" at the annual summit of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations.

ASEAN leaders have been working on a non-aggression pact with China in the contested waters of the South China Sea, a key global trade route. Confrontations have escalated in recent years, particularly between Chinese and Philippine coast guard and naval forces at disputed shoals.

After landing in Manila, Trump met the leaders of the Quad nations — the United States, Australia, India and Japan — for the first time since he returned to the White House last year. The group has aimed to counter China's influence in the Asia Pacific. "We're all friends," Trump said. "It's a lot easier that way."

Trump announced that the Quad would conduct an exercise featuring maritime patrol aircraft and launch an accelerator to speed the development of artificial intelligence.

Trump signed a trade statement with Thailand, the type of agreement he had favored since launching a trade war last year with steep tariff threats. Trump said he was looking forward "to finalizing the details of our formal agreement in the coming weeks." The joint statement included details posted more than a year ago by the U.S. government: Thailand would remove tariffs on most American goods, as well as barriers for importing American autos, food products and pharmaceutical drugs. Trump said Tuesday that Thailand would buy $2.6 billion in U.S. agricultural products.

At the gala dinner, Trump joined a meeting billed as a "special summit" with ASEAN leaders. Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. said the leaders finalized a set of principles to tackle online scams. Leaders were also expected to discuss efforts to deepen trade and security ties, the yearslong violence in Myanmar and escalating South China Sea disputes. Beijing claims the waterway virtually in its entirety; Washington has rejected that claim and has repeatedly said it is obligated to defend the Philippines if Filipino forces come under an armed attack.

About 1,500 left-wing protesters held a noisy rally in a downtown Manila square ahead of Trump's arrival, calling on Philippine leaders not to take foreign policy cues from the United States. They waved red flags and held up a large streamer that read, "Resist U.S. war and plunder!" They planned to burn an effigy of Trump, while riot police with shields and wooden truncheons stood nearby. Protest leader Teddy Casiño told The Associated Press, "The Philippine government should not deal with such a loser so our call to the Marcos administration is to stop being a puppet of Donald Trump and to uphold an independent foreign policy for the Philippines."

Nearly 23,000 police personnel and other law enforcers, including SWAT teams, were deployed to secure the ASEAN summit.

On Wednesday, Trump was set to receive a red-carpet welcome and full military honors from Marcos Jr., followed by a state banquet at the Malacanang presidential palace. Trump and Marcos were expected to affirm their commitment to their countries' longstanding treaty alliance.

Officials were arranging a possible brief reunion at Malacanang with Imelda Marcos if she was well enough and received her doctors' approval. The former Philippine first lady is 97, uses a wheelchair and is the mother of incumbent President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. She is the wife of the Philippine leader ousted in the pro-democracy 1986 "People Power" public uprising.

The friendship with Trump endured, with Trump often asking Marcos Jr. and other Philippine officials, "How's Imelda?" Philippine Ambassador to Washington Jose Manuel Romualdez told The Associated Press. "Personal connections are very important to him," Romualdez said.

When Marcos met Trump in the White House last year, he carried a note from his mother thanking the U.S. president for his strong backing of the Philippines. "President Trump was very happy and told Marcos, 'please say hello to your mother,'" according to Romualdez.

Trump was also expected to announce a series of commercial deals and investments in the aviation, defense, energy and technology sectors before heading to Shenzhen, China, for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit.
