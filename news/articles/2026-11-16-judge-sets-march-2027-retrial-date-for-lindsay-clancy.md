## Judge sets March 2027 retrial date for Lindsay Clancy

**Subtype:** fact

- A judge has set 29 March 2027 as the trial date for Lindsay Clancy, the Massachusetts woman accused of murdering her three children.
- Clancy's first trial this year ended without a verdict when a 12-person jury failed to reach one.
- Defence lawyer Kevin Reddington said prosecutors' stated preference for a jury trial signals an intent to proceed on first-degree murder indictments.
- Judge William Sullivan rejected a defence attempt to throw out the case on double jeopardy grounds, clearing the way for a second trial.

## Defence reads first-degree murder charges into prosecutors' jury demand

Lindsay Clancy, 36, will be tried again in Massachusetts next spring, a judge said on 16 November, setting a date of 29 March 2027 for a second proceeding that follows the collapse of her first trial earlier this year.

Prosecutors told the court they want to proceed with a jury trial. The district attorney did not explicitly say what charges Clancy would face. The defence said it took the jury-trial statement to mean Clancy would again face first-degree murder charges, the same as in the first trial.

"I think you can read between the lines that when they say that the case is going to require a jury, that it's an indirect comment from the DA [district attorney] that they intend to proceed on first-degree murder indictments, so we will pick a trial date," defence lawyer Kevin Reddington said.

The district attorney has not yet officially said he will proceed with first-degree murder charges. Clancy was charged in the first trial with three counts of first-degree murder, which is the intentional and premeditated killing of another person.

The prosecution could choose to bring lesser charges instead. Second-degree murder applies to unlawful killings committed with malice or extreme recklessness, but without planning.

The retrial date was made possible by a ruling from Judge William Sullivan, who a week ago rejected a defence attempt to throw out the case on the grounds of double jeopardy. Double jeopardy is the legal principle protecting someone from being prosecuted more than once for the same crime after an acquittal or conviction. The judge's decision cleared the way for a second trial.

Clancy does not deny killing her children. Her defence team argues she was suffering from severe postpartum psychosis at the time, while prosecutors claim the killings were intentional. That dispute over intent and mental state, left unresolved by the first jury, is what the second trial will be asked to decide.

Clancy is currently held in a state-run psychiatric hospital and medical facility in Massachusetts.
