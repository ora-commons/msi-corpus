## IEA projects 70% rise in data center emissions by 2030

**Subtype:** fact

- The IEA projects a 70% rise in global data center emissions by 2030.
- The IEA expects AI to account for three-quarters of projected growth in global data center electricity use through 2030.
- The IEA estimates AI data centers will consume an extra 350 terawatt-hours annually within four years.
- The IEA expects gas to provide about 60% of US data center electricity by 2030.
- The IEA expects coal to provide about half of China's AI data center electricity by 2030.

## US and China lean on gas and coal for AI growth

The International Energy Agency projects that emissions associated with the global data center sector will rise by 70% by 2030 as the sector consumes twice as much electricity as it does today. The Paris-based agency expects AI to account for three-quarters of that growth and estimates that AI data centers will consume an extra 350 terawatt-hours of electricity every year within four years. The added annual consumption would be more than Great Britain's annual electricity use.

The IEA's annual report said the world's energy future was less certain after the Middle East war, which the agency described as the greatest energy market disruption in history. “I don’t see any credible pathway for the energy world to return to the way it was before,” IEA executive director Fatih Birol said. The agency said policy choices after the crisis would reshape the energy future as governments meet rising energy demand from a growing population, greater air-conditioning needs and a booming AI economy.

The IEA found that data center developers have emerged as the world's biggest corporate buyers of renewable energy to help fuel servers connected to local power grids. The agency nevertheless expected the appetite for fossil fuels to grow even faster, particularly in the United States and China. It tied the projected sector emissions increase to growth in fossil-fuel generation.

By the end of the decade, the IEA expects coal plants to supply around half the electricity needed by China's AI data centers. Renewables would provide the rest, nuclear reactors a smaller share and gas plants very little.

For US data centers, the IEA expects gas to supply about 60% of electricity by 2030. In August, the agency counted plans for about 180GW of new gas-fired power plants linked to US data centers. The planned additions would provide almost as much new gas capacity as the total installed across the European Union.

The scale of planned US gas capacity exceeded other markets. The appetite for gas power among US data center developers was more than 16 times higher than in Canada, where the IEA identified plans for 11GW of gas generation to power servers. In the rest of the world, AI developers had put forward plans for new gas plants with a combined capacity of just 4GW.

The IEA expected renewables to play a greater role in supplying data centers outside the US and China. In the EU, data centers are expected to rely on renewables for about 70% of their electricity by 2030, followed by nuclear power, gas generation and a very small amount of coal.

The agency based its forecasts on data showing that annual global shipments of AI servers had climbed 18-fold since 2020. It also used space-based imagery to track the construction of 93 large “AI factories” around the world.

IEA analysis indicated the world's data center capacity had climbed tenfold in the previous 24 months to 17GW. By the time the AI factories were completed, their combined capacity was likely to be about 50GW.

The world's existing stock of data centers, including those with AI-optimized servers, stood at 120GW, with 80% of that capacity in China, the US and the EU. A further 120GW would be built by the end of the decade under the scenario the IEA often considered its central base case, and three-quarters of the addition would serve AI data centers.

Taken together, the forecast pairs rapid AI infrastructure growth with different regional power mixes: coal in China, gas in the US and renewables in the EU. The IEA's 70% emissions projection comes even as data center developers expand their purchases of renewable power.
