## Huang's return lifts Nvidia's market value by about $1 trillion

Jensen Huang was reinstated as Nvidia's chief executive on November 23 with immediate effect, and Nvidia's shares opened 30% higher. The company's board said it unanimously reinstated its co-founder following "a series of discussions and meetings" with him. The rebound lifted Nvidia's market value by about $1 trillion while the tech-heavy Nasdaq gained 6.5% in early trading.

The reversal came less than two weeks after Huang resigned following his admission of an affair with a woman who was alleged to be a Chinese spy. His return restored him as chief executive of the company he founded after the earlier resignation and a period of significant share-price declines.

Technology stocks broadly rallied after large Friday declines, although much of Monday's advance began in premarket trading before Huang's return was announced. Microsoft and Amazon shares each rose nearly 4% in early-morning trading, while Apple gained less than 2%.

Other chipmakers recorded larger increases. Micron Technology jumped 19%, Intel climbed 14% and AMD rose nearly 7%.

The BBC described Nvidia as a key company at the center of the artificial intelligence boom, with its chips central to the industry. Huang's return placed the co-founder back in charge of a business whose products sit at the center of that market.

Kathleen Brooks, research director at brokerage firm XTB, said Huang's return showed that he was the "godfather" of AI and "has a talismanic influence over the sector." She also said: "Barely two weeks ago this man was ousted from the company that he founded due to serious misconduct and potential national security issues."

Brooks said: "Even though Huang did not hold special voting shares on the board, Nvidia's directors cannot see a future for the company without him."

Nvidia's shares had fallen nearly 14% on Friday before Monday's rebound. The Friday decline followed Reuters reports that Nvidia was being investigated by the US Securities and Exchange Commission over its accounting practices; the BBC said it could not confirm those reports.
