## Leaked data was mostly customer and employee emails, with some phone numbers

Personal information belonging to thousands of JPMorgan Chase customers was leaked online Thursday after cyberattackers targeted third-party vendors that provide services to the bank. Chase said the exposed material was mostly email addresses, with some phone numbers, and did not include credit card or particularly sensitive personal information. Visa and Mastercard confirmed the breach but said company functions and customer payment information were unaffected.

“We have learned that third parties who provide services to the firm were the target of a cyber attack,” Chase spokesman Joe Evangelisti said. Based on its investigation so far, the bank believes attackers accessed certain marketing data held by those vendors, which may include customer names and email addresses.

The leaked data appeared to consist mostly of email addresses for Chase customers and some bank employees, along with phone numbers in some cases. Chase said the information did not include credit card information or particularly sensitive personal information.

The affected accounts numbered in the thousands and represented a fraction of Chase’s customer base. Chase has tens of millions of customers, while Visa and Mastercard have billions.

Visa and Mastercard also confirmed the data breach. The companies said the hack had no impact on company functions or customers’ payment information.

“At this time, the exposure has been contained and our teams are working with the provider to support the impacted cardholders as needed,” Mastercard spokesman Seth Eisen said.

Chase said the cyberattack was unrelated to an earlier disruption in some of its banking services. A software bug caused that outage earlier in the week.

The cyberattack was the latest in a series of technological problems that hit various parts of the global economy. Flights were grounded, while basic email and calendar services from Microsoft were not operational.
