## Herzog will formally give Eisenkot the mandate Sunday at 5 p.m. local time

Gadi Eisenkot will have 28 days to put together a coalition government, with a possible 14-day extension, NPR reported.

That gives him up to six weeks to negotiate with rival parties and secure the 61 seats needed for a parliamentary majority in Israel's 120-seat parliament. President Isaac Herzog will formally give Eisenkot the mandate at 5 p.m. local time Sunday.

Herzog selected Eisenkot, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's main political rival, to try to form the next government. NPR described Herzog's selection of Eisenkot as "a setback for Netanyahu."

Netanyahu's Likud won 27 seats in last month's election, six more than Eisenkot's Yashar party and the most of any party. Neither Likud nor Yashar secured enough support to command a majority in the 120-seat parliament.

Eisenkot leads the centrist Yashar party, which he founded on an anti-corruption and government reform platform. According to NPR, he is a former chief of staff of the Israel Defense Forces, where he served as the country's top military commander from 2015 to 2019. He is a veteran of several military campaigns.

Eisenkot later joined Netanyahu's emergency war cabinet following the Oct. 7, 2023, Hamas attacks on Israel. Eisenkot left the following year, accusing Netanyahu of failing to make progress toward ending the Gaza war and securing the release of hostages.

The war cost Eisenkot's family dearly, according to NPR. Eisenkot lost his son, Gal, a reservist killed in Gaza, as well as two nephews during the war.

If Eisenkot fails to form a government, Herzog can give another lawmaker a chance to do so. If those efforts also fail, Israel could face another election. Netanyahu would remain caretaker prime minister in the meantime.
