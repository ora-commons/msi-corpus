## Hiland denies role in alleged bar exam score tampering

Arkansas Supreme Court Justice Cody Hiland appeared before the Senate Judiciary Committee on Wednesday for a hearing on his nomination to the 8th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals. Democratic Sen. Richard Blumenthal asked Hiland who won the 2020 presidential election, and Hiland said Joe Biden was certified the winner but declined to say who won the popular vote.

President Donald Trump nominated Hiland, a former federal and state prosecutor, the previous week after withdrawing his earlier nomination of U.S. District Court Judge Lee Rudofsky, another Arkansas-based judge. Hiland, who has served on the Arkansas Supreme Court since 2023 and formerly chaired the state Republican Party, opened with a personal objective.

“My goal is for people to say Judge Hiland brought honor to the title of 8th circuit judge,” Hiland said.

Hiland said Joe Biden was certified the winner but maintained that the popular vote was not what mattered. “Senator, the popular vote is not what’s important,” he said. “Respectfully, it’s the Electoral College and Joe Biden received those votes.” He did not identify the popular-vote winner.

Blumenthal pressed Hiland again about the popular vote, and Hiland again declined to identify the popular-vote winner. “I think it’s going down a political track on an issue that happened six years ago and I don’t want to come close to a potential ethical violation,” Hiland said. The question is one Democrats have repeatedly asked of Trump’s judicial nominees.

Sen. Dick Durbin, the committee's top Democrat, asked about the recent arrest of the former executive director of the Arkansas Supreme Court's Office of Professional Programs on charges of tampering with bar exam scores between 2020 and 2025. Hiland said he first learned of the investigation in May and had played no role in the alleged tampering.

He said officials were still trying to determine the full extent of the alterations. “We’re trying to get our hands around exactly what the universe of the problem is,” he said.

Hiland also walked back remarks he made to law students in August, when he described himself as “belligerent” after telling the White House he was not interested in a federal district court seat rather than the appellate bench. Hiland called the remarks an extemporaneous mistake: “This was a 10-minute, extemporaneous speech to first year law students. When we speak extemporaneously, sometimes we use words we shouldn’t,” he said. “The proper word was probably resolute as it relates to being an appellate court judge versus the other.”

Arkansas Republican Sen. Tom Cotton introduced Hiland by saying, “Justice Hiland’s legal experience, his track record from both the bench and the lawyer’s table, and his unwavering respect for the rule of law make him an ideal candidate for this position.”

Republican Sen. John Boozman also endorsed Hiland, citing his legal experience and temperament for the bench. “Cody Hiland has all the qualities we want in a federal judge,” Boozman said. “Extensive legal experience, sound judgement and a commitment to upholding the law rather than legislating from the bench.”

Durbin said he had “deep concerns” about Hiland. He cited Hiland's background leading the state GOP and the state Supreme Court's ruling the previous year that the Legislature can change voter-approved constitutional amendments.

Durbin also accused Republicans of breaking longstanding norms by seeking to rush judicial nominations through the Senate before Republicans lose control of the Senate in January. “Just eight days after the committee’s last nominations hearing, we’re back again because Senate Republicans are determined to confirm as many nominees as possible before control of the Senate shifts in six weeks,” Durbin said in a statement.

The 8th Circuit covers Arkansas, Iowa, Minnesota, Missouri, Nebraska, North Dakota and South Dakota.
