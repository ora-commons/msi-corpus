## Commerce listing covers Megaspeed subsidiaries in Thailand and Malaysia

The Commerce Department published the official notice on Monday, covering Megaspeed and its subsidiaries in Thailand and Malaysia. The Guardian reported that the approval Megaspeed now needs for U.S. exports was likely to be denied, and Megaspeed did not immediately return a request for comment.

The Justice Department is investigating whether Megaspeed violated U.S. export controls by shipping Nvidia chips to China. Megaspeed, an Nvidia partner, has been under investigation by U.S. authorities since 2024 over whether it diverted AI chips to China.

A week earlier, Megaspeed said in a statement that it conducts “legitimate and lawful business, including complying with US export controls.” The company also said Alice Huang officially left Megaspeed in August 2025.

Nvidia said it recently formed an export control committee to oversee trade compliance and national security risks.

The Guardian linked the addition to reports that recently reinstated Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang had a relationship with Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman and Megaspeed’s controlling shareholder who is reportedly alleged to be a spy for Beijing. China has said the spy accusations are “groundless.”

On Monday, The New York Times reported that Alice Huang “kept in contact with an official of China’s top spy agency for more than a year while she was carrying out a secret affair with Jensen Huang, according to multiple people with knowledge of US intelligence reports that describe the encounters”.

Senator Pete Ricketts, a Nebraska Republican, said in a social media post, “The Department of Commerce still needs to explain to Congress why this took so long.”

Semafor reported that Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick had been under pressure to act since the relationship became public. Semafor noted that Alice Huang had previously worked as a reporter for Chinese state television and became a director at Megaspeed in 2023.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said earlier this month that Treasury, the Pentagon, the State Department and the U.S. Trade Representative’s office had all wanted Megaspeed on the blacklist “for months,” but that “Commerce holds the pen.” After the relationship became public, Bessent publicly complained that Commerce had not yet placed the company on its entity list.

Bessent also questioned Nvidia’s client oversight, telling CNBC’s Squawk Box program: “The real story here should be: did Nvidia know their client and was there a robust effort at Nvidia to make sure that there was no leakage and that they were selling to what were obviously pass-through shell companies?”

Last week, the Justice Department and Commerce Department issued a joint statement saying they would “bring the full extent of the law against Megaspeed for any criminal conduct that may have occurred.”
