## Stratton outlines healthcare, immigration and Iran priorities

Incoming Democratic Sen. Juliana Stratton said she would support significantly reforming or abolishing the Senate filibuster after Democrats won an unexpected 56-44 majority, their largest Senate majority in nearly two decades. The filibuster still requires 60 supporters for most successful legislation rather than a simple majority. In an interview with The Guardian published Nov. 24, Stratton said the party should move clear bills quickly and use the new majority to deliver on promises made during the midterm campaign.

Stratton said Democrats should not repeat what she called “waffling” about difficult tasks, arguing that such messages had turned off voters. “Sure, there are hurdles, but we were elected to do hard things,” she said.

Stratton, currently the Illinois lieutenant governor, was a professional mediator before entering politics and ran a dispute-resolution firm for two decades. She said the experience would serve her in divided government. She said she welcomed Republicans who want to stand up to Trump and “have a real spine” to work with her and Senate Democrats.

Stratton said she wanted simple, clear bills to move quickly once Democrats return to power in the Senate. She called for reversing cuts to Medicaid and food assistance, returning subsidies to the Affordable Care Act marketplace, ending tariffs and ending the Iran war. If Trump vetoed those measures, Stratton said, “that’s on Republicans.”

“If he wants to run on how he’s doubling down on taking away people’s healthcare, if Republicans want to say it’s OK that children are going hungry – let them run on that, to their peril,” she said.

Stratton said Chicago was “still feeling the effects” of Operation Midway Blitz, which brought hundreds of federal agents into the metropolitan area and ended with thousands of arrests and detentions. She said Immigration and Customs Enforcement could not be reformed and needed to be abolished. Senators should use the “power of the purse” to withhold ICE funding, she said.

During her campaign, Stratton pledged not to vote for Chuck Schumer to lead Senate Democrats, and she said she was sticking by that promise. She would not say whether she supported Chris Van Hollen of Maryland, who is challenging Schumer for majority leader and has also called for eliminating the filibuster.

“I’m listening to everyone,” she said. “I want to see who steps up. We have one, Senator Van Hollen, who has stepped up. I want to see how this plays out and what vision people have for how we’re going to deliver, and how we’re going to move forward as a party.”

“We need to meet the mandate that we’ve been given by the voters this election cycle,” she added. Stratton said Democrats could preserve their midterm momentum by delivering on their campaign promises. “Show them what’s possible, and not just make it aspirational, but actually do it,” she said.

Stratton, 61, started her political career within the past decade. She ran for the Illinois House in 2016 against the incumbent and received an endorsement from Barack Obama. After one term in the state house, JB Pritzker selected her as his running mate for the gubernatorial ticket. She has served as the state’s lieutenant governor since 2019. Running for the US Senate “was not my dream,” she said.

Stratton said she decided to run for a state representative seat because she was her mother’s caregiver. Her mother had Alzheimer’s, and Stratton said a state-level effort to strip healthcare access prompted her to run. She said helping her mother obtain care was difficult and that the idea of taking healthcare away from vulnerable Illinoisans made her angry. Her mother died the day before Stratton launched her state house campaign. Ten years later, Stratton ran on a Medicare for All platform and said she believes healthcare is a human right.

Stratton often shared the caregiving story on the campaign trail, calling it “the hardest thing that I’ve ever done and the greatest honor of my life.” She said people approached her afterward with similar experiences.

Illinois will be the first state represented by two women of color in the US Senate when Stratton joins Democratic Sen. Tammy Duckworth. Stratton said she and Duckworth recognized the milestone and that representation matters and creates a stronger democracy. “I always say that we don’t want policy just to be made for us,” Stratton said. “We want policy to be made with us.”

Stratton is a mother of four, with three adult children and one child who is 10. She said motherhood equipped her with stamina and problem-solving skills and gave her insight into younger people’s lives. Her youngest daughter’s school was placed on lockdown twice because of ICE activity in the community during Operation Midway Blitz.

One of Stratton’s adult daughters told her about increases in the cost of an ACA marketplace health plan and having to choose a worse plan to afford care. “We really need more people in Washington who are connected to the everyday struggles of the people that they represent,” Stratton said. “I want to build a better world for my children, and I want to build a better world for all children. And that’s what’s always going to keep me grounded as I continue to do this work.”
