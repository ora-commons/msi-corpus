## Russian imprisonment lasted more than four years

Patricia Hubbard Fox, Hubbard’s younger sister, gave a Thursday interview in San Antonio, where he was taken for evaluation. She said her brother did not know why he was being taken from prison to Moscow and may have thought he was going to his death.

Fox said Hubbard understood that he had been released only after he was put aboard a plane and saw the American flag. His Wednesday release came after more than four years in a Russian prison.

Fox described her brother as laughing often in relief. “He’s been kind of laughing a lot,” she said. “Like relief laughing. ‘Oh my God, you know, I’m free. This is really happening.’”

Fox said Hubbard was starved and beaten so severely in prison that some of his fellow inmates from Ukraine had to carry him. She said the only English the Russian guards knew was “Die, American. Die.” Fox added: “And that just made him mad enough to not die, you know? ‘I’ll show them.’ I’m so proud of him.”

## Hubbard’s case and Trump’s request

Hubbard, a U.S. citizen, was accused of fighting alongside Kyiv’s forces after Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. In 2024, he was sentenced in closed court proceedings to nearly seven years in prison. The U.S. government under the Biden administration had designated Hubbard as wrongfully detained, while his legal team disputed the Russian allegation and said he was not involved in any military activity.

President Donald Trump said he had asked Russian President Vladimir Putin during a phone call earlier this month to free Hubbard, a 74-year-old retired teacher from Michigan.

## Hubbard’s life before detention and Fox’s search

Hubbard was a U.S. Air Force veteran who taught English overseas and retired in 2012 to Cyprus, where one of his sons was an Orthodox Christian priest. His lawyers said he later moved to Ukraine to be with a woman he had met and remained in the country after the relationship ended. He was living there at the time of Russia’s invasion.

Fox said she did not know where her brother was until a woman in Ukraine tracked her down after seeing a prison roster with his name on it. “God doesn’t answer prayers immediately sometimes, but he always answers my prayer,” Fox said. “And I said, ‘Jesus, get him home.’”

In a separate interview Wednesday, Fox said Hubbard would probably come live with her in Colorado. The siblings were looking forward to a family reunion.
