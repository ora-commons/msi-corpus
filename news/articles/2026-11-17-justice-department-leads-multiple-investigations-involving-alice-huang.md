## Commerce’s Megaspeed inquiry dates to late 2024

The Justice Department is now leading multiple investigations involving Alice Huang, according to The Wall Street Journal. U.S. intelligence officials believe Alice Huang is working for China’s intelligence services. People familiar with the matter said one of Jensen Huang’s extramarital affairs involved Alice Huang, then an executive at Singapore-based data company Megaspeed, while the other involved an employee.

Megaspeed was one of several companies using Nvidia chips to set up AI data centers in Southeast Asia and was suspected of diverting some of the products to China. The Commerce Department had been investigating Megaspeed since late 2024, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Nvidia earlier this year denied that Jensen Huang had an unusual or unexpected relationship with Alice Huang. The company last year changed how it disclosed international chip sales, eliminating a disclosure that showed its sales to Singapore, where Megaspeed was based.

Since the Huang relationship was disclosed, Nvidia’s business in Asia has been in the spotlight, and Congress has called for investigations.

Nvidia shares have fallen more than 10% since Huang was ousted, according to The Wall Street Journal. Just weeks earlier, the newspaper described Nvidia as a stock-market darling at the center of the booming artificial-intelligence trade and the most valuable company in the world. Since then, investors have questioned the company’s disclosures related to Huang’s affairs and its sales of chips in Asia.

Separately, people familiar with the matter said the Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia following Huang’s resignation over two extramarital affairs. The civil investigation includes whether Nvidia misled investors in its disclosures, some of the people said, while the broader focus could not immediately be learned.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent this week openly questioned whether Nvidia knowingly sold powerful chips to China to lift its earnings. He said, “What was the culture at Nvidia and were they pressing these seemingly illicit sales to goose their revenues and their earnings?” He added, “I think we need answers in the next 10 days from the board and from the outside counsel.”

Earlier Tuesday, Huang said, “I did not live up to the standards expected of everyone at Nvidia,” and added, “I created problems for the Board that I deeply regret. I own my mistakes completely.”
