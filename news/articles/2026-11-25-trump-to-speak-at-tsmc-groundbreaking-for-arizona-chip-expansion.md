## White House says expansion could create 10,000 direct and 11,000 indirect jobs

President Donald Trump will travel to Arizona on Dec. 4 to speak at the groundbreaking for a new computer chip foundry operated by Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co., according to the White House and the Taiwan-based company. The appearance is tied to the start of construction on the fifth phase of TSMC's expansion in the Phoenix area.

TSMC has expanded aggressively in the Phoenix area. Its U.S. facilities manufacture chips for Nvidia, Apple, AMD, Broadcom and Qualcomm.

In July, TSMC announced plans to spend another $100 billion to expand its manufacturing capacity in the United States. That announcement brought the company's total pledged investment in American chipmaking to $265 billion.

Construction in Arizona began during Joe Biden's presidency, and the bipartisan CHIPS and Science Act, signed into law by Biden, also helped provide some financial support for the project. Trump has sought to promote the investments as a function of his tariffs, while the Arizona expansion was already underway before his presidency.

The White House said the expansion would enable the creation of 10,000 direct jobs and another 11,000 indirect jobs in the wider community. TSMC's Arizona site currently accounts for more than 12,000 construction jobs.

The trip comes at the end of a week when artificial intelligence comes under the microscope in Washington, following Trump's intervention to get Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia's chief executive. Trump has staked his economic policy on growth in the artificial-intelligence sector.

Huang is slated to testify at a Senate Banking Committee hearing on Dec. 3. He is expected to face questions about how Nvidia has been selling its chips abroad and about his affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy. Beijing denied the allegation.

TSMC's expansion also comes as Taiwan's dominance in chipmaking has led countries including the United States to address supply-chain vulnerabilities by producing more domestically. The company's investment has grown as artificial intelligence has stimulated demand for more computer chips.
