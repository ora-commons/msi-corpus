## Public vote decides which bird receives the White House ceremony

President Donald Trump is set to pardon the Thanksgiving turkeys Freedom and Feather during a White House ceremony on Tuesday, sparing both birds from ending up on a Thanksgiving dinner table. The public can vote online to choose which of the West Virginia natives receives the in-person ceremonial pardon. Both birds will live out their days at Texas A&M University.

The White House said the event would be the 79th turkey pardoning, though the tradition did not become annual until George H.W. Bush's administration in 1989. The Associated Press described the ceremony as a typically light-hearted moment before Thanksgiving that can offer a brief departure from politics and policy.

An early story of presidential turkey pardoning dates to Abraham Lincoln's administration, according to the White House Historical Association. Lincoln granted a reprieve to a bird destined for Christmas dinner after his son Tad intervened on the gobbler's behalf.

Nearly a century later, a photographer captured Dwight Eisenhower feeding a cranberry to a speckled turkey in 1954. In 1963, John F. Kennedy, just days before he was fatally shot in Texas, pardoned a turkey that wore a sign around its neck saying, “Good Eating, Mr. President.”

In 2024, Joe Biden pardoned Peach and Blossom, two turkeys from a farm in Minnesota, and gave each bird a motto. Biden said Peach “lives by the motto, ‘Keep calm and gobble on.’” Blossom has a different motto: “No fowl play. Just Minnesota nice.”

Politics returned to the ceremony in 2025, the first year of Trump's second term. Trump spared the turkeys but spent part of the event attacking his political opponents, according to the Associated Press.

Trump said the birds should be named Chuck and Nancy, after Democratic leaders Chuck Schumer and Nancy Pelosi. “I would never pardon those people,” Trump said at the time.

For Tuesday's ceremony, the public's online vote settles which of the two birds receives the in-person pardon, while Freedom and Feather will spend the rest of their lives at Texas A&M University.
