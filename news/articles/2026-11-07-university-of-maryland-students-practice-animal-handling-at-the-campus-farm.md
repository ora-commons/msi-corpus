## Maxwell and Sonpar describe farm work changing their veterinary experiences

Host Scott Simon opened NPR's audio postcard with a tour of the University of Maryland's College Park campus. Simon noted a wind tunnel, a statue of alumnus Jim Henson with Kermit the Frog and a farm with red barns. He described the farm as a remnant of the university's agricultural-school roots and a place where students still go to learn. Reporter Ari Daniel then visited the grounds, where interim farm manager Karly Bowe welcomed Daniel and started walking with Daniel toward the horse barn.

Karly Bowe said the campus farm is part of the animal science program. She described the program's enrollment: "Something like 300 undergrads and about 30 grad students."

Daniel and Bowe stepped inside the horse barn and headed up to the hay loft. Daniel noticed a sweet smell and asked what it was. Bowe explained: "It's the hay. The sugars in the hay. This is what you want to smell. This is really good hay. This is what the horses want, too."

Bowe introduced Tex, a chestnut with bird spotting, or small white spots like snowflakes across him, and Dawn, the farm's only mare, whom Bowe called a really good teaching horse.

Dawn and the other horses were part of the day's introductory Principles of Animal Science lab taught by lecturer Kris Mayo. Mayo said: "You are learning how to approach a horse, how to lead them out of their stall safely. You all will each get a hands-on opportunity to do it yourself."

Mayo invited Daniel to go ahead and give it a try. Daniel said it was Hallie Hummer's first time handling a horse. Hummer said: "It's intimidating. I've never dealt with such big creatures before." Daniel then introduced Malia Gibert, who said: "I've ridden horses since I was 5 years old, and I've had the privilege of owning two. I've always been used to being around them."

Daniel announced, "Next stop, cows." Adele Turzillo, who chairs the Department of Animal and Avian Sciences, said, "I'm a dairy cattle person. To me, cattle are beautiful."

Turzillo tied the farm's teaching role to students considering veterinary school: "Many of our students are interested in going to veterinary school. And so this gives them an excellent preview of what it's like to work with animals."

Daniel then introduced junior Shea Maxwell as one of those students. Maxwell said, "I'm a sheep person," then called the sheep over: "Come here, ladies. All you got to do is look at Nutmeg the right way. She just makes it so known that she loves her scratches." Daniel observed Maxwell scratching Nutmeg. Maxwell identified the rest of her flock as Pixie Stick, Calypso, Penelope and Ginger Snap, and Daniel said she knew her flock.

Maxwell said: "Getting the hands-on experience I get with these animals - it's completely switched my trajectory." Ari Daniel described the change: "From becoming a general cat and dog vet to training to be a livestock veterinarian." Maxwell added, "Anything you really eat, that's what I want to focus on."

Daniel said the hands-on work had also changed the perspective of senior Liliana Sonpar. Sonpar recalled, "I actually had the experience of pulling a foal, helping to deliver a horse last spring, and that went in my vet school essay." Daniel asked whether she had pulled the foal out of the mare, and Sonpar confirmed, "Correct."

When Daniel asked about her feelings, Sonpar called the experience "the coolest thing I think I've ever done in my life." She said her professor and co-TA were right next to her, "talking us through the entire thing." Sonpar connected the experience to her education: "And that's the point of coming to school, right? You're coming here to get an education. Yes, you can learn your chemistry, but no one can teach you how to bottle-feed a lamb unless you actually do it."

Before leaving the farm visit, Sonpar said she really had to go because she had a quiz. Daniel asked what the quiz was on, and Sonpar answered, "Fertilization."

Mayo's course provided hands-on instruction in horse handling and safety, while Turzillo said the farm offered students interested in veterinary school a preview of animal work. The reported changes were individual accounts: Maxwell said the hands-on work had switched her trajectory, and Sonpar said a foal-delivery experience had entered her veterinary-school essay; Daniel said the work changed Sonpar's perspective.

Ari Daniel reported from College Park, Maryland, for NPR News.
