## Combined company would expand market by more than $185 billion by 2029

CrowdStrike and Zscaler said Monday that they had agreed to combine in an all-stock transaction valued at about $43.9 billion. Austin, Texas-based CrowdStrike would acquire Santa Clara, California-based Zscaler, a cloud security company specializing in cloud-based network protection.

Under the exchange terms, Zscaler shareholders would receive 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for every Zscaler share they own. The rate represents a 34% premium to Zscaler's closing price on Friday.

Existing CrowdStrike shareholders would own about 84.1% of the combined company. Existing Zscaler shareholders would hold the remaining 15.9%.

CrowdStrike shares tumbled more than 7% before the opening bell Monday, while Zscaler shares rose 20%.

The transaction arrived after a string of high-profile hacks affecting banks, supermarket fridges and crypto platforms. The hacks forced the White House to shake up its cybersecurity leadership and raised alarms among officials around the world.

Cybersecurity experts have warned that artificial intelligence is being used to turbocharge cyberattacks by making it quicker and easier to find software flaws. Reports of advanced AI models deploying agents that work on their own to break into other organizations have added to the concerns.

“Security used to be about keeping the adversary out,” George Kurtz, CEO of Austin, Texas-based CrowdStrike, said in a statement. “Today, safety is required to let AI in.”

“In the AI era, Zero Trust security has become non-negotiable, and AI agents are rapidly becoming the new weakest link,” Zscaler CEO Jay Chaudhry said.

CrowdStrike has focused on endpoint security, with services protecting laptops, mobile devices and desktop computers. Zscaler specializes in cloud-based network protection. The companies were already working together in a partnership that included 2,000 shared customers.

The transaction would expand the combined company's market by more than $185 billion by 2029. CrowdStrike expects the acquisition to close in the second half of its fiscal 2028 year, which begins in February.
