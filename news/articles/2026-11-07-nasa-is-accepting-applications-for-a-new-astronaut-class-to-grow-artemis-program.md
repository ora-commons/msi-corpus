## Only 32 active astronauts remain as NASA plans a 2028 lunar surface return

NASA is accepting applications for a new astronaut class that the agency says will help grow the Artemis program and build a permanent base on the moon. Officials announced the application process Saturday during a Kennedy Space Center air show focused on lunar exploration and advances in aviation and technology. Applicants have until Dec. 3 to submit their applications.

NASA expects to make its final selection by late 2027, with the new candidates beginning training by early 2028. Applicants must be U.S. citizens with college degrees and professional work experience. The two years of mandatory training can be grueling, and it could take another several years on top of that before an astronaut finally rockets into space.

NASA intends to return astronauts to the lunar surface in 2028 after an absence of more than half a century. April's lunar flyaround by the Artemis II crew kicked off the agency's lunar campaign. Artemis III commander Randy Bresnik and his three crewmates are set to blast into orbit next summer to practice docking their capsule with a pair of lunar landers, one built by Elon Musk's SpaceX and the other by Jeff Bezos' Blue Origin. Their mission will stay close to Earth, with no lunar flyby, and serve as a dress rehearsal for the following year's Artemis IV mission, which will attempt to land two astronauts near the lunar south pole.

Under NASA's plans, the moon base will materialize as more rovers, robots, structures and power grids are launched.

NASA has only 32 active astronauts and says it needs more recruits as it looks to expand humanity's presence beyond low-Earth orbit as quickly as possible. The previous call for astronauts came two years ago and drew more than 8,000 applications, with 10 candidates making the final cut in September 2025. The incoming class will be NASA's 25th astronaut class since the original Mercury Seven in 1959.

Artemis III commander Randy Bresnik told The Associated Press on Saturday, "We have far more work than we have astronauts." With the lunar return planned for 2028, he urged potential candidates, "we're going to have people on the moon — so don't delay."

Patience is essential for becoming an astronaut, said Frank Rubio, Bresnik's Artemis III crewmate, whose 371 consecutive days in space stand as a U.S. endurance record. "It's important for them to realize most of that work is not going to the moon, right?" Rubio told The Associated Press. "That will be about 1% of your experience. What you're doing is contributing to the mission of going to the moon."
