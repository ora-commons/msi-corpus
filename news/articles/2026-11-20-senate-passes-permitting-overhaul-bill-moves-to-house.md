## Bipartisan leaders advance measure amid cost and sovereignty claims

The Senate passed the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act 62-33 on Thursday evening, moving the more than 400-page bill to the House. Fourteen Democrats voted for the measure and two Republicans voted against it. Passage came despite concerns among many Democrats that the legislation would weaken environmental-protection laws.

The measure would speed energy, mining and other infrastructure projects by streamlining federal permitting. It would shorten deadlines for many environmental reviews, reduce the window to sue developers over certain environmental concerns, and restrict the circumstances under which approvals on projects can be withdrawn.

Republican Sens. Shelley Moore Capito of West Virginia and Mike Lee of Utah led the bill alongside Democratic Sens. Martin Heinrich of New Mexico and Sheldon Whitehouse of Rhode Island. President Trump has said he supports the measure.

“It’s going to create an environment where America can grow,” Capito said. “We are not going to have this opportunity again.”

Supporters say the permitting changes would lead to lower electricity and other costs for consumers. They also argue that multiyear wait times and burdensome environmental laws make infrastructure projects costly by introducing uncertainty and regulatory risk.

Environmental advocates opposed the measure, saying it would weaken key environmental-protection laws and benefit fossil-fuel companies.

Native American tribes opposed the measure, saying it would reduce tribal sovereignty.

Passage came despite pushback from environmental advocates and Native American tribes. The bill moves to the House with competing claims over consumer costs, environmental protections and tribal sovereignty unresolved.
