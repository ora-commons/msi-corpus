## Federal judge refuses to pause order finding Essayli unlawfully serving

A Justice Department official said officials in the deputy attorney general’s office selected Essayli for the Florida role. Essayli was in Miami last Thursday and will for now serve as a special assistant U.S. attorney in the Southern District of Florida while remaining California’s top federal prosecutor. The Wall Street Journal said the assignment is intended to help build a criminal case over the FBI’s August 2022 search of Mar-a-Lago.

The Journal characterized Essayli as a “legal bulldog” for President Trump in California, where the president’s policies are widely unpopular, and described him as the latest in a line of loyal Trump prosecutors asked to pursue the president’s perceived enemies and antagonists. The Journal reported that Trump “still fumes” about the August 2022 search of his home and private club.

The assignment comes with a separate legal problem. A day before the Florida role became public, a federal judge refused to pause an earlier order finding Essayli unlawfully serving as head of the U.S. attorney’s office. Trump never obtained Senate consent for Essayli’s appointment but has kept him in the job since spring 2025. Several defendants have challenged his status as de facto U.S. attorney in the central district, a sprawling region that covers Southern California’s coast and Inland Empire.

The Justice Department told the court last week that removing Essayli would affect “5,000 cases in the largest district in the country.” Essayli has said the Supreme Court will ultimately decide his job security.

Essayli, 41, is a former state lawmaker. The Journal described him as a conspicuous presence “battling California’s left” and said he had taken on some of the administration’s most divisive priorities, including investigating claims of election interference and prosecuting anti-ICE protesters. Last summer, he charged demonstrators on allegations including interfering with law enforcement officers conducting immigration raids the White House ordered. Many of those cases ended in dismissals or grand jury refusals to indict.

Those outcomes inspired some colleagues to call Essayli “No Bill,” people familiar with the matter said, referring to a grand jury’s decision not to indict for lack of evidence. “He’s pugilistic,” said Laurie Levenson, a professor at Loyola Law School in Los Angeles and a former federal prosecutor. “He was doing Washington’s bidding, and he was extremely aggressive.”

Essayli has also received Trump’s public backing. In October, Trump wrote on social media, “He has done a fantastic job. He is one of the best, EVER!” The post came after an appeals court rejected a lawsuit Essayli filed seeking to force California to set aside up to 20,000 ballots returned by people the administration says are “likely noncitizens.” The Supreme Court later rejected the administration’s emergency appeal. Essayli’s office did not respond to requests for comment.

The Wall Street Journal described the Mar-a-Lago inquiry as a cornerstone of a broader effort to determine whether former officials investigated Trump in a coordinated effort to violate his civil rights. The search led special counsel Jack Smith to charge Trump in June 2023 with mishandling classified documents; a judge ultimately dismissed the case.

Prosecutors trying to tie the search to the broader conspiracy have run into the same judge’s ruling that FBI agents did not lie or omit information when securing the warrant. The Journal reported that building the criminal case has felled several predecessors. The overall conspiracy investigation has also faltered, and the top federal prosecutor in Miami, who had been overseeing it, resigned earlier this month. Officials are now pursuing a narrower but related set of politically charged cases run out of Washington.

Essayli has used social media to take positions on national controversies and has fired messages at critics. After ICE agents killed Alex Pretti, a Minneapolis man who had been filming them, Essayli wrote that if people approach law enforcement officers with a gun, “there is a high likelihood they will be legally justified in shooting you.” The Wall Street Journal reported that video analysis showed Pretti did not brandish his gun as he approached the ICE agents and was shot after the officers disarmed him.

The National Rifle Association disputed Essayli’s interpretation, writing that “responsible public voices should be awaiting a full investigation, not making generalizations and demonizing law-abiding citizens.”

Essayli now enters the Florida inquiry with the assignment to help build a criminal case while his authority to remain California’s top prosecutor remains unresolved. The Justice Department is defending his continued service, a federal judge has found his service unlawful, and Essayli has said the Supreme Court will decide his job-security dispute.
