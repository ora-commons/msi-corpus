## Senate changes put taxes, healthcare and local spending in focus

Eleven Democrats spoke after the 2026 U.S. midterm elections about their governing priorities, and The Guardian described the contributors as Democratic leaders and seat-flipping victors from Tuesday's "blue tsunami" offering post-election comments. Their remarks ranged across household costs, the influence of money in politics and the kind of government the party's wins should produce.

The Guardian characterized the result as a broad repudiation of Donald Trump, his policies and the Republican party, focused on Trump's personal unpopularity, high costs of living and the Iran war, and said the outcome gave Democrats an opportunity to reset the US domestic agenda. The remarks showed how speakers proposed to use that opening: by focusing on costs facing households, directing public resources toward local communities and holding high officeholders accountable.

The contributors were Hakeem Jeffries and Chuck Schumer of New York, former US president Barack Obama, Troy Jackson, James Talarico, Abdul El-Sayed, Amy Acton, Deb Haaland, Adam Hamilton, Roy Cooper and Annie Andrews.
