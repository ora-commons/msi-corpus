## No classified information was compromised in the intrusion

U.S. officials said a recent hack of an unclassified system disrupted networks at the Central Intelligence Agency and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence. According to the officials, the affected open-source intelligence system helps the agencies glean key information and trends from publicly available materials.

People familiar with the situation said the intrusion did not compromise classified information. Officials said no other systems were put at risk, the breach had been patched and the situation was resolved.

Officials could not determine what information was accessed, leaving the scope of the breach unknown. The affected system was unclassified, and the absence of a classified-information compromise did not resolve that question.

The Wall Street Journal said the New York Times had earlier reported the development and described the intrusion as the latest in a string of disruptions that roiled markets and sowed doubt in the reliability of aging digital systems in the age of artificial intelligence.

The report said government agencies and businesses were making upgrades to patch vulnerable systems, and that those upgrades themselves could also cause problems. A classified communications network at the National Security Agency was temporarily down last week after a system enhancement, while the Federal Aviation Administration ordered a groundstop last week after a router installation went wrong.

Data from thousands of customers at Visa, Mastercard and JP Morgan Chase were also leaked last week, the report said.

U.S. officials and experts said such disruptions were likely over the coming years as government agencies and businesses work to improve defenses against individuals aided by AI. They said AI assistance can make hacks faster and less labor-intensive, adding pressure to patch vulnerable systems.

The concern was particularly felt within U.S. intelligence agencies because they keep the country's most sensitive secrets, which the report described as prized targets for cyberattacks.


[MSI previously reported](/articles/2026-11-19-a-week-of-tech-outages-disrupts-flights-banking-and-email/) that the previous week’s technology failures affected flights, banking, email and a classified government network, while officials and companies said they had no evidence that a cyberattack caused those disruptions. The new intrusion adds a confirmed hack to the broader run of separate technology failures without establishing that the earlier outages were cyberattacks.
