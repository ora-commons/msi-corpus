## Surfers take on giant waves at Mavericks near Half Moon Bay

Iconic piers along the California coast were forced to close Sunday as huge waves flooded streets near the shore, washed over beaches and left coastal communities dealing with another round of heavy surf.

The National Weather Service's Bay Area office warned that surf up to 35 feet (10.6 meters) was possible, and it flagged dangerous rip currents and sneaker waves. The weather service said on social media: "Stay out of the water, stay off rocks and jetties, and never turn your back on the ocean!"

In Southern California, several piers were shut during the weekend as waves slammed into them, including piers at Ventura, Manhattan Beach, Huntington Beach and San Diego's Pacific Beach. Several inches of water covered the strand at Seal Beach in Orange County and flooded beachfront homes. At La Jolla Cove near San Diego, a spot famous for its lounging sea lions, water rose over the beach and crashed up a staircase leading to the sand.

Farther north, waves more than 50 feet (15.2 meters) were recorded Saturday in San Mateo County, just south of San Francisco, and surfers were not deterred, taking to the water at Mavericks Beach near Half Moon Bay. The Santa Cruz County Sheriff's Office issued an evacuation warning near the coastal town of Capitola, which took the brunt of the powerful surf.

Forecasters said conditions were calming by Sunday afternoon, but they warned that large waves were expected all week.

The ongoing super El Niño has warmed the ocean, causing seawater to expand.
