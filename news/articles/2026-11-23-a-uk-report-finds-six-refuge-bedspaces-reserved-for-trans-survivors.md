## Galop says it turned away trans survivors weekly after the court ruling

Only six domestic-abuse refuge bedspaces in the United Kingdom are reserved for transgender survivors, according to a new report, with services saying it is “almost impossible” to find refuges for trans people since the Supreme Court ruling on single-sex spaces.

Galop, an LGBTQ+ anti-abuse charity, said it has been unable to secure non-specialist refuge accommodation for any trans women in the UK in 2026; the charity has only been able to find space at specialist LGBTQ+ or trans providers. Galop said many trans survivors were being turned away from mainstream domestic-abuse services.

The charity said that before the Supreme Court ruling on single-sex spaces in 2025, trans people could be placed into some mainstream refuges. Since the ruling determined that same-sex spaces refer strictly to “biological sex,” many refuges that previously accepted trans people are now no longer able to.

Jasmine O'Connor, Galop's co-CEO, said finding housing suitable for trans survivors “has been very challenging for a long time,” and that since the ruling the challenge “has become absolutely acute.” “It's devastating,” she said.

O'Connor described the effect on services: “What was a very limited supply of housing has shrunk to virtually zero. For us as a service, it's desperate to have to turn individuals away, week in, week out, because there is nowhere for them to go.”

Across England and Wales, nine LGBTQ+ specialist accommodation services for domestic-abuse victims provide a total of 106 bedspaces. Only six are specially reserved for trans people, and all six are at one refuge.

Data cited in the report suggests demand far outstrips that supply. LGBTQ+ people are twice as likely to experience domestic abuse as heterosexual and cisgender people, and government data showed that 500 trans or non-binary adults were supported in domestic abuse safe accommodation in 2024-25.

Galop said 40% of its domestic-abuse clients were trans+ in 2025/26, and that many were having to make what the charity called the “unimaginable choice about whether to stay living with their alleged perpetrator or having no home.”

O'Connor said: “We'll never know the amount of people who are making the choice to stay in a really abusive relationship because there is not a safe route out until, sadly, they're in the headlines.”

In a landmark case last year, the Supreme Court ruled that the terms woman and sex in the Equality Act refer to a “biological woman” and “biological sex,” and mean that transgender people can be excluded from same-sex spaces. In October, the NHS announced that trans people would no longer be placed on same-sex wards.

The report says many domestic-abuse services place trans people only in dispersed accommodation — self-contained spaces in the community that are often the most in demand and the most difficult to access. Others place trans people only in mixed-gender homelessness accommodation, where they are more likely to experience further abuse.

One trans woman quoted in the report, who has lived as a woman for over 20 years, said she was turned away from a women's refuge because of the Supreme Court ruling. Galop staff said many people are reluctant to approach services because they fear the humiliation of being rejected.

O'Connor said she hoped the government would discuss ringfenced funding for increasing trans services across the country. “We'd like a conversation with the government and a commitment to create this fund so that there is the right money available to commission the right sorts of services,” she said. She added: “We see this very much in line with the government's commitments on domestic abuse and wanting to ensure that LGBTQ+ survivors are supported within that strategy.”

Support is available through the UK's national domestic abuse helpline on 0808 2000 247 and through Women's Aid. Australia's national family violence counselling service is on 1800 737 732, the US domestic violence hotline is 1-800-799-SAFE (7233), and other international helplines are listed at www.befrienders.org.
