## Cornell president orders full review of fraternities and sororities

**Subtype:** fact

- Cornell University president Michael Kotlikoff announced on Monday a "full review" of fraternities and sororities that will determine their future on campus.
- Kotlikoff's announcement follows a civil lawsuit in which a former Cornell student identified as Jane Doe said she was gang-raped at an on-campus fraternity house after being drugged in November 2024.
- Cornell has banned fraternity parties for the rest of the academic year, expelled two Chi Phi fraternity members, and hired former justice department official Sally Yates to review its sexual assault policies.
- New York attorney general Letitia James is conducting an investigation into the case, and Cornell has scheduled a community meeting plus two listening sessions.

## New York attorney general is investigating Cornell's assault case

Kotlikoff published a letter on Monday outlining steps the university has taken since Jane Doe, a former Cornell student, filed a civil lawsuit against several members of an on-campus fraternity that drew international attention to her rape allegations. Cornell had already banned fraternity parties for the rest of the academic year following the gang-rape allegation.

"I know many in our community have lost confidence in the Cornell administration's ability to prevent and respond to sexual violence," Kotlikoff wrote in the letter. "You have raised necessary questions about what the administration is doing to keep our students safe and where we have fallen short."

"You have called for greater transparency and better communication about what we are doing so that nothing like this can ever happen at Cornell again. I am committed to doing the necessary work to rebuild that confidence: regularly communicating and engaging about the work we have been doing and will do to build a culture of care and responsibility."

The changes Kotlikoff announced include re-evaluating campus policies toward drugs and alcohol, changes to programming for sexual assault and bystander intervention trainings, and the hiring of a new victim advocate to work in the school's Sexual Harassment and Assault Response & Education office, known as Share.

The lawsuit alleges that Jane Doe was raped after she was drugged in November 2024. She filed a complaint with Cornell and the university police, but no criminal charges were filed. Two members of the Chi Phi fraternity were expelled in connection with the case; the others were made to write essays, attend workshops or serve short suspensions. All deny wrongdoing. Jane Doe has expressed feeling unsupported and disbelieved by Kotlikoff.

Attorney general Letitia James is now conducting an investigation into the case.

Cornell was scheduled to hold a community meeting on Monday evening, followed by two follow-up listening sessions for campus community members and staff in the coming weeks. "These will be opportunities for open, frank, face-to-face conversations – and for advancing the work we can only do together," Kotlikoff wrote in a note accompanying the announcement.

The announcement arrives weeks after sustained campus pressure over the administration's handling of the case. Hundreds of Cornell students rallied against the university's actions on October 5 after the assault allegations became public. In early October, Cornell tapped Sally Yates, a former high-ranking US justice department official, to review the school's policies around sexual assault.
