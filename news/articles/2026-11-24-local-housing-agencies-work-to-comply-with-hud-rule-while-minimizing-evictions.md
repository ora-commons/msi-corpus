## Six California jurisdictions sue over housing eligibility rule

Since the Trump administration published the final version of the rule earlier this month, local public housing agencies have been working to determine how to comply while minimizing the eviction of residents and financial collateral damage for the agencies themselves. Some warn that many U.S. citizens and legal residents may struggle and ultimately fail to comply with the more stringent requirements to prove eligibility.

Some local agencies also warn that evicting lower-cost mixed-status households and replacing those households with households entitled to full benefits will squeeze the agencies financially.

Housing agencies and local governments across the country, including in Los Angeles, San Diego, San Francisco, Fresno, Santa Barbara and Santa Clara County, filed their own suit on Monday, the same day as the states' action.

The administration finalized the rule earlier this month. It would bar all households with mixed immigration status — meaning those with some members who are citizens or legal residents and others who live in the country illegally — from receiving housing vouchers or living in public housing. The rule would terminate aid for all of these households on Dec. 14.

The change would affect tens of thousands of low-income households nationwide, including more than 7,000 California households that stand to lose subsidized housing.

California, 21 other states and Washington, D.C., filed a lawsuit Monday in the U.S. District Court for Massachusetts. The lawsuit argues that the Trump administration overstepped its authority by overriding a congressional statute and asks the federal court to freeze the rule before it takes effect.

Undocumented immigrants have been barred from federal housing subsidies for decades. Starting during the Clinton administration, however, Congress has required the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development to provide assistance in proportion to the share of household members who have legal status.

For example, a family with two undocumented parents and two citizen children would receive half of the eligible aid under that framework. The new rule would instead terminate assistance for every household with mixed status.

At a press conference announcing the suit, Attorney General Rob Bonta said, “California won’t stand idly by while the Trump administration forces families to choose between staying together and keeping a roof over their heads.”

In a written response, the HUD press office said the rule “ends the exploitation of taxpayer dollars.”

In a statement attributed to Secretary Scott Turner, HUD said: “This rule guarantees ONLY AMERICAN citizens and certain eligible noncitizens will reside in HUD-funded housing.” The statement continued: “This change represents a reallocation of up to $218 million which will now go to eligible Americans that are currently on a waitlist.”
