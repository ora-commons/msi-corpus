## Accord permits expanded US military presence while reaffirming Greenland's right to self-determination

Greenland's delegation received applause and chanting in Nuuk on Wednesday upon returning from the signing of a security agreement with the United States and Denmark.

Donald Trump, Greenlandic Prime Minister Jens-Frederik Nielsen, and Danish Prime Minister Mette Frederiksen signed the pact in New York on Tuesday, one day before the greeting in Nuuk on Wednesday.

The agreement permits the United States to expand its military presence on the island. The accord simultaneously reaffirms Greenland's right to self-determination.

The agreement concludes a long-running standoff between Washington, Copenhagen, and Nuuk over the strategically located and mineral-rich Arctic island.

Greenland's strategic position and mineral resources had made the island the focus of the standoff among the three governments. The accord allows for an expanded US military footprint on the island while reaffirming Greenland's right to self-determination.

The hero's welcome in Nuuk, captured on video, showed the Greenlandic delegation being greeted with applause and chanting as officials returned from New York.
