## Naveed Akram faces 15 murder counts as coroner investigates survivor's death

Eva Geller, a 90-year-old Holocaust survivor who was wounded in the massacre of 15 people at a Sydney Hanukkah celebration, died Thursday night in a Sydney hospital, officials said Friday.

Executive Council of Australian Jewry director Evan Zlatkis said the Romanian-born Geller died Thursday night, almost a year after the Dec. 14 attack at Bondi Beach.

Geller was one of around 40 people wounded when two gunmen allegedly inspired by the Islamic State group opened fire on a crowd at Bondi Beach. Fifteen victims aged 10 to 87 died. One of the gunmen, Sajid Akram, was shot dead by police at the scene.

Rabbi Aron Moss said Geller had been "strong and pretty healthy right up until the Bondi attack where she was shot." He said she had been admitted to the hospital several times in recent weeks because of surgery complications.

"She's a proud mother, grandmother and great grandmother. A beautiful, positive person who even in these difficult months maintained her positivity," Moss said on the family's behalf.

Moss said doctors told Geller after she was shot that she would never walk again. "But through sheer determination, she got back up and she did walk again," Moss added. She walked with a cane following the leg wound, he said.

The surviving alleged gunman, Naveed Akram, the 25-year-old son of the dead gunman, has been charged with 15 counts of murder, 43 counts of attempted murder and one count of committing a terrorist act. He has yet to enter a plea.

It was not yet clear whether Geller's death would change the charges Akram faces. Police said in a statement they were awaiting advice from a state coroner, who would investigate the circumstances of her death.

Akram is scheduled to appear in court next on Feb. 24, when prosecutors expect to finalize which charges will go to the New South Wales state Supreme Court for trial.

State Premier Chris Minns offered his condolences to Geller's family. "After everything Geller endured, the news of her death is heartbreaking," Minns said in a statement. "Almost a year on, we are still living with the pain and aftershock of that evil act. We will never forget Eva or any victim whose life was changed forever," Minns added.

Prime Minister Anthony Albanese said the devastation of the Bondi attack continued to cause pain for families. "Eva should have been able to celebrate Chanukah in peace and safety and instead, her life was changed forever by an act of terror and antisemitism," Albanese said in a statement, using a different spelling of Hanukkah.
