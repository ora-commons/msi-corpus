## Alaska certifies Peltola’s Senate victory as House recount remains pending

**Subtype:** fact

- The Alaska Division of Elections certified the 2026 general election results on Monday, two days ahead of schedule.
- Mary Peltola won 50.14% of the vote against Dan Sullivan’s 45.78% and will become the first Alaska Native woman elected to the Senate.
- Republican Kevin McCabe requested a recount in House District 30 after losing to Republican Doug Massie by three votes.
- Republican Dan Sullivan conceded the Senate contest and said the ongoing state and federal investigations should provide answers.
- The Alaska Bureau of Investigation continued investigating allegations related to the Alaska U.S. Senate election.
- A U.S. Attorney’s Office spokesperson said a federal criminal investigation into groups affiliated with the Peltola campaign was active last week.

## Sullivan concedes as state and federal criminal probes remain unresolved

The Alaska Division of Elections certified the results of Alaska’s 2026 general election on Monday, two days ahead of schedule. Division officials did not respond to a request for comment on why certification came early. Republican incumbent Rep. Kevin McCabe filed for a recount on Tuesday after losing to fellow Republican Doug Massie by three votes following ranked-choice tabulation in House District 30, leaving at least one race subject to a recount.

In the U.S. Senate contest, Democrat Mary Peltola won 50.14% of the vote, while Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan received 45.78%. Peltola’s total was 12,500 votes higher, and her election will make her the first Alaska Native woman elected to the Senate. Sullivan had held the seat since 2015. Photographs accompanying the report showed Peltola greeting supporters at a downtown Anchorage watch party on election night, Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026. Another showed Sullivan speaking at an Oct. 5, 2026, campaign rally in Fairbanks, where Vice President JD Vance was the headliner and traveled with Sullivan on Air Force 2.

Outgoing Sullivan conceded on Tuesday in an emailed statement. He thanked Alaskans, acknowledged the certified results and did not name Peltola directly. Sullivan said, “As I’ve always said I would, and out of respect for the people of Alaska and our democratic process, I accept the results certified by the Division of Elections. Alaskans deserve confidence in the integrity of their elections, and I trust that the ongoing federal and state investigations will provide answers to the questions that remain.”

Outgoing Republican Gov. Mike Dunleavy and President Donald Trump launched state and federal criminal investigations after a New York Times report said the Peltola campaign recruited spoiler candidates, including Dan J. Sullivan of Petersburg, whose name matched the incumbent’s. The Alaska Beacon said it had not independently verified the New York Times report. Dan Sullivan, Mary Peltola and the Democratic Senatorial Campaign Committee denied the allegations.

On Tuesday, Alaska Department of Public Safety spokesperson Austin McDaniel said by email that the state investigation was ongoing. McDaniel said, “The Alaska Bureau of Investigation continues to investigate allegations related to the Alaska US Senate election. To protect the integrity of the ongoing investigation, we are unable to provide additional details at this time.”

The U.S. Attorney’s Office for the District of Alaska did not respond to a request for information on a federal criminal investigation into groups affiliated with the Peltola campaign. A spokesperson told the Anchorage Daily News that the investigation was still active last week. Separately, a lawsuit filed by the U.S. Department of Justice against the Division of Elections sought to block certification if Sullivan lost re-election. A U.S. District Court judge dismissed the lawsuit, and the federal government dropped its appeal last week.

Republican incumbent U.S. Rep. Nick Begich III won re-election to represent Alaska’s sole seat in the 435-member House. Begich received 47.52% of the vote, ahead of independent challenger Bill Hill’s 41.77%, a margin of 16,503 votes. Nearly 48% of voters participated in the race. The report described Democrat Eric Hafner, a perennial candidate in federal races who was serving a federal criminal sentence in New York, as receiving 7.92% or more than 22,700 first-choice votes.

Alaska elected its youngest governor and its first Democratic governor since 1998, Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins, along with independent running mate Zac Johnson. The pair won with 51.09% of the vote, ahead of three Republican challengers, with 48% of voters casting ballots. Kreiss-Tomkins is scheduled to be sworn in on Dec. 7.

Alaskans rejected a ballot measure that would have overhauled the state’s election system by repealing the open primary system, ranked-choice voting and some campaign finance laws. “No” votes led after the first round of results on election night. The final count was 52.83% no and 46.18% yes, leaving the system enacted in 2020 in place. A similar measure was rejected in 2024.

Voters separately approved Ballot Measure 3 with 68.23% of the vote and 31.77% opposed. The measure clarifies under existing state law that “only” U.S. citizens are permitted to vote and does not change the law.

Fifty seats were up for election, including 10 in the Alaska Senate and 40 in the Alaska House. Former House Speaker Bryce Edgmon, an independent from Dillingham, won Senate District S, representing Western Alaska and the Aleutian Islands, and replaced Sen. Lyman Hoffman, who retired this year. Republican Louise Stutes of Kodiak won District C, replacing retired Sen. Gary Stevens. Republican Frank Tomaszewski of Fairbanks won District Q after former Sen. Robb Myers stepped down following three terms.

In a shakeup in the Matanuska-Susitna Borough, incumbent Sen. Cathy Tilton, a Republican from Wasilla, lost her re-election bid after ranked-choice tabulation to Republican Ryan Berry by 467 votes. Tilton had served in the Alaska House since 2015 and was appointed to the Senate by Dunleavy in 2025 to replace Mike Shower, who resigned to run for lieutenant governor. This year, Mat-Su residents accused Tilton of having a predatory relationship with a teenager starting in the 1990s, according to an Anchorage Daily News report. Tilton denied any criminal wrongdoing. A photograph showed Tilton with Democratic Rep. Zack Fields of Anchorage and Republican Rep. Justin Ruffridge of Soldotna at the Alaska State Capitol in Juneau on Jan. 18, 2023, shortly before her election as House speaker.

The 20-member Senate is expected to govern through a bipartisan majority coalition of 11 members, with all nine members of the minority caucus belonging to the Republican Party. In the House, four Republican seats changed to Democrats and independents, widening the margin for the multipartisan House Majority coalition. That coalition is expected to hold 25 seats, while the Republican-led minority caucus will have 15 members.

Several House contests were decided by narrow margins. On the Kenai Peninsula, Republican incumbent Rep. Sarah Vance of Homer lost her re-election to independent Brent Johnson by 59 votes and conceded last week. In nearby House District 29, Republican incumbent Rep. Garret Nelson won re-election by four votes over Republican challenger Steve Menard. Menard did not respond to a request for comment on whether he planned to request a recount on Tuesday.

House District 30 spans the western part of the Matanuska-Susitna Borough and Denali Borough. McCabe said the cast vote record exports identified 223 contest records coded “invalid contest,” including 109 absentee records and 114 questioned records. He said, “The exported markings and status code leave unresolved whether any properly cast votes were omitted or whether any rankings were incorrectly recorded or treated in tabulation. So there’s some significant anomalies there,” and added, “And I want to know why.” McCabe expects the recount to be completed early next week. A photograph showed McCabe speaking on the House floor during debate on the operating budget on Apr. 13, 2026.

A recount can be requested by a losing candidate, or by 10 eligible voters when the margin is less than 20 votes or .5% of votes cast. Requests must be made by the division’s Saturday deadline, or Friday for the governor’s race.

Alaska voters in the Third Judicial District rejected two judges standing for retention. They were the only two of 21 judges whom the nonpartisan Alaska Judicial Council had recommended against retaining. The council is charged with vetting, nominating and evaluating judges’ performance on the bench.

Voters rejected Kenai Superior Court Judge Kelly Lawson, with 58.88% voting no and 41.12% voting yes. They also rejected District Court Judge Martin Fallon, with 59.92% no and 40.08% yes. Voter turnout in both instances was 48.65%.

Both judges are scheduled to step down in late February, 90 days after certification, and their cases will be handled by retired judges serving as pro tem judges. The council will interview and nominate candidates for the vacancies at its next meeting in May, after which the nominees will advance to Gov.-elect Kreiss-Tomkins for appointment.
