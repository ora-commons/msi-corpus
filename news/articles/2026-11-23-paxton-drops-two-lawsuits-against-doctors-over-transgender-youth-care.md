## Texas's gender-affirming care ban took effect in 2023 before providers were sued

Texas dismissed two lawsuits on Friday against May Lau and M. Brett Cooper, according to attorneys for the doctors. The cases accused the physicians of violating the state's ban on gender-affirming care for transgender youths and threatened steep fines and revocation of their medical licenses. The dismissals came as outgoing Republican Attorney General Ken Paxton prepares to leave office after his failed U.S. Senate run; his office did not immediately respond Monday to a request for comment.

The lawsuits were filed in 2024 and were among a spate of high-profile cases involving Paxton. In the suits, Paxton called Lau, Cooper and Hector Granados “scofflaws” and “radical gender activists.”

Nicole LeBoeuf, an attorney for Lau, said in a statement: “Dr. Lau did nothing wrong, but she was a convenient scapegoat for the Attorney General’s Office, which issued press release after press release about its case — a case that has now collapsed.” LeBoeuf said the timing of the dismissal after Paxton's loss “validates that this case against Dr. Lau was political from beginning to end.” Lau moved her medical practice from Dallas to Oregon during the lawsuit.

Cooper's attorneys, Simona Agnolucci and Anika Holland, said in a statement that the Dallas physician “has always maintained that he acted lawfully and in the best interests of his patients.”

Texas' ban on gender-affirming care for transgender youths took effect in 2023. Other states also have prohibitions on gender-affirming care for transgender youth, but Texas was the first to bring cases against doctors. Its lawsuits against the three providers were among the first in the U.S. to accuse doctors of violating bans on care for transgender youths.

Paxton had already dropped a separate lawsuit last year against Hector Granados, an El Paso pediatric endocrinologist. His office later said Granados did not break the law.

Paxton is a close ally of President Donald Trump. During his second term, Trump launched a broad charge against transgender rights, seeking to reverse years of legal and policy gains for transgender Americans. Even in states where gender-affirming care was allowed under state law, major hospitals and hospital systems have said they were stopping or restricting the care.

Paxton and Republicans made transgender issues a focus during the final phase of the year's midterm elections. Republicans spent an estimated $183 million on television ads focused on transgender-related attacks against Democrats, according to tallies AdImpact provided to The Associated Press.

The Texas Senate contest between Paxton and Democrat James Talarico consumed the largest share of that spending, drawing an estimated $58 million for more than 50,000 airings before the election, according to AdImpact. Voters told pollsters that the state of the economy was among the issues they cared most about during the midterms, in contrast with the Republican emphasis on transgender issues.

Democrats were preparing to retake power in Texas for the first time in decades. In January, Democrat Nathan Johnson will become the state's next attorney general.

Paxton's office also dropped lawsuits after his Senate loss against out-of-state doctors over sending abortion pills to Texas and against the Council on American-Islamic Relations, seeking to stop the organization from operating in the state. The latter lawsuit came after Republican Gov. Greg Abbott declared the group a “foreign terrorist organization.” The council is one of the largest Muslim civil rights and advocacy groups in the U.S.

The office also dropped suits against Jolt Initiative, a nonprofit accused of registering noncitizens to vote, and Annunciation House in El Paso, one of the oldest migrant shelters on the U.S.-Mexico border. Together, those dismissals leave the three 2024 doctor cases closed: Granados's suit was dropped last year, while Lau's and Cooper's were dismissed Friday.
