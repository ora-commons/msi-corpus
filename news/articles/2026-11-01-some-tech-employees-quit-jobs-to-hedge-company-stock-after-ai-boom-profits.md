## Trading firms develop derivatives tied to computing-power prices

Some technology employees are quitting their jobs after making so much money from the AI boom that they are hedging their companies’ stock. The departures are not moves to retire or pursue a passion project, according to The Wall Street Journal’s account of growing demand for protection against AI-trade risks.

The Wall Street Journal characterized the strategy this way: “It’s a striking sign of how nervous some investors have become about Wall Street’s multitrillion-dollar bet on artificial intelligence.” Even some of the people who profited from the boom are looking for ways to limit their exposure.

The hedging extends beyond individual employees. Investors and tech executives are seeking protection against a larger unraveling, according to The Wall Street Journal.

The backdrop is an AI trade that has been enormously profitable. The change described in the report is the arrival of recent disappointments, which have prompted a push for protection against a larger unraveling.

Options and credit-default swaps are the financial instruments cited for that protection. Investors and tech executives are turning to both as recent disappointments have increased demand for ways to limit losses.

One trader put the logic plainly: “I don’t think my house is going to catch on fire, but I still have insurance.” The comparison illustrates why participants can benefit from large AI-related gains while still buying protection against a larger unraveling.

Protection is also taking the form of new products. Trading firms are developing derivatives tied to the price of computing power, expanding the range of price-linked instruments available to market participants.

Gunjan Banerji reported on the growing demand for protection against the AI trade’s risks. His account covers individual departures from work, institutional hedging and the development of new derivatives, showing how the response has spread across the AI trade.
