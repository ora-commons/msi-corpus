## White House says advisers manage Trump’s portfolio without his input

President Donald Trump’s personal Nvidia holdings are estimated at roughly $5 million to $54 million, according to Dan Alexander, a senior editor at Forbes who discussed the financial disclosures with NPR on Nov. 24. The estimate came as Trump said he persuaded Nvidia’s board of directors to reinstate Jensen Huang as chief executive. The White House says independent financial advisers manage Trump’s stock portfolio without his input, but Alexander said third-party management does not by itself resolve the possible conflict.

Alexander said latest reporting suggested the range. He reached the estimate by combining annual reports and transaction reports, with each type carrying its own range. Layering them together broadened the possible total, he said. Nvidia’s share price was also moving a lot, leaving a large but undetermined amount rather than a fixed figure.

Alexander placed the Nvidia estimate within Trump’s broader finances. Trump’s overall liquid holdings were roughly $2 billion, he said, so the Nvidia position was one component of a much larger portfolio and was not his biggest asset. At the higher end of the range, Alexander said, $50 million would matter to Trump.

NPR host Scott Detrow asked whether Trump or people around him might have bought a lot of Nvidia stock over the weekend as the company situation came together and Trump, with a small circle, knew he was pressing Huang to be reinstated. Alexander said the most recent transaction reports he had received a few days earlier showed transactions from September. He said a batch covering the current period could arrive in about two months, leaving the weekend question unanswered in the reports he had reviewed.

Alexander said the issue extended beyond who executed any trades. Even if a third party traded for Trump, Trump would still hold the stock and could know he owned it, which can create a conflict of interest.

Alexander then set out a conditional legal standard. He said that if Trump had to follow the conflict-of-interest laws that other executive-branch officials follow, apart from the president and vice president, his decisions over the past few days making clear specific ties to Nvidia would be a federal crime.

NPR reached out to the White House but had not received a response before airtime. White House spokespeople told other outlets, including Forbes, that independent financial advisers manage Trump’s portfolio without his input. Alexander said the White House had claimed Trump was out of his business, but his extensive reporting showed otherwise. The White House confirmed to Alexander that Trump, while at his golf club, made notes on small details as he did with the White House ballroom.

Alexander said Trump was not involved in every detail, but when he wanted to be, he became closely involved. Detrow used that pattern to ask how Trump’s financial relationship with Nvidia should be weighed alongside his actions concerning Huang.

Detrow noted that Nvidia’s stock price had roughly tripled over the past three years during the artificial-intelligence investment boom and as Huang became a trusted adviser to the Trump White House. He also noted that Trump, or the people who managed his money, had purchased a lot of Nvidia stock since his second term began.

Alexander said potential motivating factors included matters that would motivate any president. He described Nvidia as an enormous and enormously valuable company that led the stock market, and said anything affecting it would affect stocks broadly. National-security considerations were also part of the calculation, he said. His focus was whether an additional motivation could come from Trump’s personal holdings or his ties to Huang.

Alexander cited reports that Jensen Huang and Nvidia helped fund the ballroom. He also described a close personal relationship Trump and Huang developed during the second term, including Huang’s appearances at Mar-a-Lago. Alexander said it was hard to imagine those factors did not somehow play into the calculation, alongside what he called bigger and more traditional considerations.

Alexander oversees money and politics coverage for Forbes and contributes to MS NOW. He is also the author of *White House, Inc.: How Donald Trump Turned The Presidency Into A Business*. The transaction reports for the period around Huang’s reinstatement had not yet arrived; Alexander said the relevant batch could come in about two months.
