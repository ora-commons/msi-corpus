## Duffy urges Congress to send $50 billion for air traffic upgrades

Transportation officials said the ground stop, issued by the Federal Aviation Administration, followed an equipment failure at a facility in El Dorado, Arkansas, where an obsolete 2011 router flooded the FAA network with data during maintenance work. L3Harris was conducting the maintenance work and replacing aging copper wiring with fiber when the router malfunctioned, prompting the decision to stop all flights. The work is part of a larger plan to modernize the country's air traffic control infrastructure.

In Virginia, 532 flights across the four airports were affected before the ground stop was lifted after eight hours. Spanberger, who was in Denver, Colorado, at the time, said in a post on social media Friday that her administration was monitoring the situation, which caused "significant" delays and cancellations at airports. "The commonwealth is working to get answers and will update Virginians as we learn more," Spanberger said.

At a White House press briefing on Friday, Duffy was asked directly whether the FAA disruption would affect students returning home and other Thanksgiving travelers. "I think we're going to be back up and operational as we come into the … to the heat of travel during Thanksgiving," said Duffy. Still, he acknowledged that it would take time to recover from Friday's disruption. "I believe that it's going to take us some time to get back to capacity," he added.

Duffy, whom Congress confirmed as transportation secretary in January 2025, said over the weekend that it is safe to fly. He also emphasized that the FAA's air traffic control operations system remains intact and denied any evidence of a cyberattack. Duffy said the incident is still being investigated as travelers recover and consider future travel, most notably for Thanksgiving next week.

Duffy urged Congress to distribute over $50 billion to the agency immediately to modernize neglected aviation infrastructure. He said the money would replace aging hardware, software and air traffic control towers. "Give us the resources to build a state-of-the-art air traffic control system," Duffy said on Friday. "Let's not do a patchwork fix to a system that's critical for every single family."

Last year, Congress allocated $12.5 billion through H.R.1, the new law President Donald Trump dubbed the "One Big Beautiful Bill," to modernize the country's aviation infrastructure.

Duffy said the administration will not end its partnership with L3Harris, which owns the communication network the FAA uses. In a statement, L3Harris said "safety and reliability" are the company's highest priorities, and that, with its telecommunications partners and the FAA, it has completed more than 70% of the planned airspace communications conversion from legacy copper wires to a fiber optic system.

"We are supporting the FAA in its investigation to understand the conditions that led to the Nov. 13 ground stop of our nation's air travel," L3Harris stated. "L3Harris takes the nationwide ground stop and its impact on travelers, airlines and air traffic operations seriously."

AAA reports that 81.5 million people will be traveling for Thanksgiving on Nov. 26, which is slightly down from a year ago. Flyers remain steady at 5.9 million.
