## First-degree murder suspect is in federal medical facility to restore competency

Charlotte officials have asked a judge to dismiss with prejudice a wrongful death lawsuit over the killing of Iryna Zarutska, a 23-year-old Ukrainian refugee who was fatally stabbed on a light rail train in Charlotte, North Carolina, in August 2025. The motion follows mediation talks between the parties that failed earlier in November.

Zarutska's uncle, Frank Scott Haskell, filed the lawsuit against the city and its transit security contractor, Professional Security Services, while Zarutska and her family were staying with him in Charlotte. Haskell alleges that the city and the security company knew about safety and crime problems on the public transit system and failed to take reasonable and available safety measures to prevent the killing. In the complaint, Haskell alleged that the city and its security contractor "were responsible for protecting lawful passengers from known and foreseeable dangers. Their failures caused the fatal stabbing."

Charlotte's lawyers responded by acknowledging the killing while rejecting the family's claim. In a filing, they wrote: "The City recognizes the tragic and devastating death of Iryna Zarutska as the result of an intentional criminal attack by Decarlos Brown Jr." The same filing said: "The City denies that it ignored passenger safety or that its conduct legally caused that attack."

The city says it "maintained an extensive security program, contracted for armed and unarmed security and company-police services, and undertook initiatives concerning public safety, security planning, training, and transit operations."

Zarutska was fatally stabbed from behind while aboard a Charlotte blue line train, traveling home from her shift at a pizzeria. The attack was caught on surveillance cameras, and the killing drew international attention, becoming a focus of public debate over violent crime, mental illness and public transit safety.

Zarutska had come to the United States after living in a bomb shelter in Ukraine, arriving with hopes of finding safety in the country. Family members described her as determined to build a safer life. A memorial for Zarutska in Charlotte on 11 September 2025 appears in a photograph credited to Peter Zay/Anadolu/Getty Images.

The criminal case against the suspect, Decarlos Brown Jr, remains at the competency stage. Brown was charged with first-degree murder and later deemed incompetent to stand trial. He is now in a federal medical facility to restore his competency.

Brown, a homeless man who once told police he was diagnosed with schizophrenia, according to the Charlotte Observer, wrote in a letter to the criminal court that he has "a body emergency. Someone has full access to my body and they are controlling me wrongfully. And law enforcement refuses to investigate it. And it requires an investigation."

Charlotte's request to dismiss the lawsuit with prejudice now rests with a judge, while the criminal case against Brown continues separately at the competency stage. Both proceedings turn on the same August 2025 killing: one on questions of transit safety and responsibility toward the family, the other on whether Brown can face trial.
