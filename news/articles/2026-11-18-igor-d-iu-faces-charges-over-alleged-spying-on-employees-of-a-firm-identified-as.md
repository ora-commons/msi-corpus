## Prosecutors described remote-access software purchases, falsified identity documents and a notebook detailing employees and families

A Russian man has been arrested in California’s Silicon Valley on allegations that he acted as an agent of a foreign government in a covert operation in Palo Alto, near San Francisco. BBC News identified the target as Palantir Technologies. Prosecutors charged Igor D’iu with two counts related to his alleged work as a foreign agent, one interstate stalking count and one fraud count involving identity documents.

The charging documents do not name the company, but describe it as a publicly traded technology and defense company that supports the Ukrainian military and produces software used by Ukraine in its war with Russia.

US Attorney for the Northern District of California Craig Missakian said: “The allegations in this case describe a state-sponsored operation to target and harass employees of an American technology and defense company because of that company’s assistance to Ukraine in its war with Russia.” He added: “We will not tolerate any efforts - by foreign governments or otherwise - to turn Silicon Valley into a base for foreign intelligence operations.”

## Alleged recruitment and surveillance methods

Prosecutors said D’iu entered the United States unlawfully through the Mexico border after traveling from Istanbul in June. D’iu was allegedly recruited through the Telegram encrypted messaging app. His handlers referred to Russia as “the Motherland” and directed D’iu to gather information about the company because it was supplying weapons used against Russia in Ukraine.

At the handlers’ instruction, D’iu purchased computers, phones, wireless networking equipment and remote-access software as tools for the alleged operation. Prosecutors alleged D’iu went to a coffee shop near the company’s Palo Alto offices, where he photographed laptop screens of employees working there. His handlers also allegedly “instructed him to position his laptop at designated locations near the company so they could remotely access it and attempt to collect information from company employees in the vicinity.”

D’iu also allegedly conducted surveillance of employees and their residences. On September 3, D’iu followed a vehicle driven by one senior executive for nearly 30 miles and fled after the executive drove to a police station. Police later obtained evidence that D’iu had followed other employees’ cars on previous days.

## Seized and discovered records

During the investigation, officials discovered identity cards that had been falsified. The cards included a U.S. passport, a California identification card and a Social Security card bearing the fake name “Hugo Landry Johns.”

An October raid on D’iu’s home in East Palo Alto turned up two Russian passports and a handwritten notebook. Prosecutors said the notebook contained numerous home addresses of employees, family members’ names, educational and employment histories, and assessments of strengths and weaknesses.

## Palantir response and industry assessment

Palantir has not issued a statement in reaction to the charges. An industry source who confirmed that Palantir was the company targeted, and who did not want to be named, said the operation did not penetrate the company’s private systems.

The source said Palantir has been “targeted extensively” by Russia over its involvement in Ukraine and that the attacks are becoming “increasingly aggressive.” The source said this was the first time a Russian operative had been found while on U.S. soil attempting to spy on the company. “I think the surprising thing is that they decided to take the risk of sending someone to the US,” the source said, in an attempt to get into the system “physically.”

Palantir was co-founded by billionaire Peter Thiel and has interests in U.S. defense and security, as well as contracts with the Israeli military.

## Case status and potential sentence

D’iu faces two charges related to his alleged work as a foreign agent, one charge of interstate stalking and one count of fraud related to his identity documents. If found guilty, he faces more than 15 years in prison. He has been held in jail since November 2, and it was unclear whether he had hired a lawyer who could speak on his behalf.

## Wider technology-industry context

The arrest comes amid other espionage allegations involving the U.S. technology industry. BBC News reported that former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang had a romantic affair with a woman suspected of spying for China. It also reported that a former OpenAI employee was charged last month with planting a listening device in a company office; the man was later accused of having “considerable support” from the Saudi government.
