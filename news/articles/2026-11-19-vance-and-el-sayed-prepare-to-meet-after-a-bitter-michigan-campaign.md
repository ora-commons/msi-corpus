## Who sought contact is disputed; Vance will preside over the Jan. 3 swearing-in

Vice President JD Vance and Michigan Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed are preparing to meet after a bitter campaign season that featured sharp and at-times personal attacks between the two men, the incoming Democratic lawmaker and the White House said. The planned contact would bring a sitting vice president and an incoming senator from the opposing party into direct contact, and each side has said the other sought the meeting.

El-Sayed said on Tuesday during an interview on MS NOW that his team had been contacted by Vance's office. Vance's office said on Wednesday that it had contacted El-Sayed's staff after being told the Democrat was seeking time with the vice president. The circumstances of the would-be meeting were not immediately clear, nor was it certain on whose initiative it was being set.

Vance also has an official role in El-Sayed's arrival in the Senate. The vice president will preside over the swearing-in on Jan. 3. El-Sayed is preparing to take office as the nation's first Muslim senator.

The outreach follows a sharp change in Vance's public tone toward the incoming senator. While campaigning for Michigan Republican Senate nominee Mike Rogers outside Detroit in August, Vance called El-Sayed “very, very evil” and appeared to question the Democrat's patriotism. By the Monday Fox News Channel interview, Vance was describing El-Sayed as a potential partner.

“If Abdul El-Sayed wants to work with me and the president to make people’s lives better, he’s going to find us very much a willing partner,” Vance said during the Fox segment.

El-Sayed offered a conditional version of the same outreach on MS NOW. “If he wants to work with us to get money out of politics, put money in pockets and pass Medicare for All, which is what I ran on, I’m a willing partner,” he said.

How the two might cooperate, and on what issues, was not clear. El-Sayed's chief policy objective is a Medicare for All health care system, a proposal the Trump administration, including Vance individually, has dismissed. El-Sayed tied his offer of partnership to getting money out of politics, putting money in pockets and passing Medicare for All. Vance framed his willingness to work with El-Sayed around making people's lives better. Neither statement identified a specific measure the two had agreed to pursue.

The timing of the contact comes after Republicans were routed nationally in this month's midterm elections and lost majorities in both chambers of Congress. Vance is nearing a decision on whether to seek the Republican nomination for the White House in 2028, a decision the Associated Press reported has been complicated by voters' rejection of President Donald Trump.

The Associated Press characterized the outreach as appearing, at least, to represent a rare bipartisan olive branch for President Donald Trump's attack dog.

Both men have said they want to find common ground after the campaign's heated criticism, and each has framed the possibility of cooperation on his own terms. The meeting's purpose and any shared agenda it might produce remained undefined.
