## Retailers offered larger early discounts while reserving the best prices for Friday

Black Friday morning brought a familiar scene at Macy's Herald Square in Manhattan: shoppers formed a line before sunrise and streamed through the doors when they opened at 6 a.m. The crowd returned even as online Black Friday promotions stretched across weeks and steep discounts had already appeared online.

High wind warnings on Thursday had forced Macy's annual Thanksgiving Day parade to be postponed until Saturday, while the broader weather generated flood alerts in Southern California. Among the early arrivals were Nita Lawrence and her two nieces, who were visiting from Miami. The three were disappointed by the postponement but took consolation in Macy's promotions, which included handbags offered at 25% to 40% off and sneakers reduced by 20% to 50%. "We do Black Friday every year, but this Macy's is much bigger. This is special," Lawrence said. "We're going to do some shopping and then we are going to the airport. Today, I am buying for me."

Sensormatic Solutions, a retail technology company, said Black Friday produces more foot traffic at physical stores than any other day despite competition from e-commerce and brands rolling out steep online discounts weeks in advance. Retailers use Black Friday and the weeks before and after it to drive year-end sales. U.S. Census data showed stores had continued to draw a majority of holiday-season spending, although online purchases captured a bigger share every year.

This year's holiday shopping took place in a U.S. economic environment marked by voter frustration over gasoline prices fueled by the U.S. war with Iran and high inflation overall. Consumer confidence jumped during the month as U.S. gasoline prices continued a steady decline. Analysts and economists closely scrutinize retail spending on the five days from Thanksgiving Day through Monday for clues about consumers' financial staying power and commercial prospects heading into the new year.

Adobe Digital Insights reported that consumers spent a record $7.1 billion online on Thanksgiving, a 10.4% increase from the prior year, with gaming and electronics leading the total. Adobe forecast that online shoppers would spend a record $12.9 billion on Black Friday, up 9.2% from the prior year.

Atlanta resident Anna Butler, a retired schoolteacher, bought six pairs of jeans at Macy's flagship in Manhattan after she and five friends ventured there from a hotel across the street early Friday. Butler said she did not know how much she would spend on holiday gifts but felt financially secure because of retirement and Social Security benefits. "The economy is not good, but I am comfortable," she said.

The National Retail Federation estimated that U.S. shoppers would collectively spend $1.01 trillion in November and December. The group forecast 4.1% sales growth in stores and online during that period and said higher prices would account for about half of the increase.

Frank Cardino, a real estate attorney from Lindenhurst, New York, sat on a bench while his wife and two of his daughters tried on shoes and other apparel. "I'm the coat holder and the credit card," he joked. The family planned to return to Long Island later Friday and would miss the rescheduled parade. Cardino said he did not have an amount in mind for what he and his wife would spend on their three children over the course of the holidays. "It's whatever they need or want," he said. "Thankfully, I'm OK."

Retail analysts said Black Friday's evolution into a multiweek event had encouraged shoppers to stock up on shampoo, T-shirts and other everyday items while checking off holiday lists. Salesforce expected the deepest discounts between this past Tuesday and Cyber Monday on clothing, home goods, and health and beauty products. "It's not just a moment where shoppers are buying gifts," Caila Schwartz, a retail and consumer insights leader at Salesforce, said. "They're also buying things that they need, and so that's going to help drive up a lot of the spend."

Adobe Digital Insights, the research program of software company Adobe, found that online sales of household cleaning products were 33% higher and clothing basics were 27% higher from Nov. 1 through Nov. 22 than the October average.

A Reuters photograph by Shannon Stapleton showed shoppers entering a Best Buy electronics store in Levittown, New York, on Nov. 27, 2026, searching for Black Friday bargains past a displayed "Grand Theft Auto VI" advertisement.

Retailers broadly held their prices rather than raising them across the board, industry analysts said, and offered solid discounts that persuaded customers to shop earlier. Salesforce, which tracks digital spending at retailers including grocery stores, said November discounts before Black Friday were 8% bigger than the prior year's. Those promotions pushed the average selling price of physical merchandise 1% below last November's prices, according to Salesforce.

Vivek Pandya, Adobe's director of digital insights, said retailers were nevertheless likely to reserve their best prices for Thanksgiving Day and Black Friday. Adobe's analysis found that Thanksgiving's strong online spending was bolstered by bigger-than-expected deals, with electronics discounts peaking at 29%. On Friday, discounts on TVs were expected to average 23%, compared with 11% during the first 22 days of the month.

Adobe also said AI-powered shopping had ramped up, with AI traffic to U.S. retail sites more than doubling on Thanksgiving from the prior year. The season's retail picture combined the pre-dawn Macy's crowd with an earlier digital promotional calendar and record Thanksgiving online spending.
