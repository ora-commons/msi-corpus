## Saudi airport attacks expose deepening crisis for crown prince

A soaring number of people on sickness or disability benefits is blowing up the United Kingdom's welfare bill and imperiling the country's financial stability, The Wall Street Journal reported. Some seven million people — more than 10 percent of the UK population — now claim at least one of the benefits, driven by a fourfold surge in psychiatric claims, the newspaper reported. With spending on track to hit £130 billion, or $172 billion, Britain faces a looming financial reckoning as leaders struggle to make politically painful cuts, WSJ reporters Max Colchester and David Luhnow wrote.

The same edition of The Wall Street Journal's "The 10-Point" newsletter also examined a weekend attack on Saudi Arabia's main airport that killed at least 12 people and injured hundreds more. The strikes by Iran-backed Houthi militants exposed a deepening crisis for Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman, WSJ reporters Summer Said and Georgi Kantchev wrote. With threats mounting, the crown prince is shuttling between heavily guarded palaces equipped with bunkers, the WSJ reported.

The violence threatens his ambitions to turn Saudi Arabia into a global tourism and investment hub, and his own decisions have contributed to his predicament, Said and Kantchev wrote. Rising defense and infrastructure costs threaten to siphon away funds needed for the economic diversification push, according to the WSJ.

The attacks are also wreaking havoc on global air travel. Airlines canceled more than 1,000 flights as threats to Saudi airspace jeopardize one of the last two major air corridors connecting Europe and Asia, the WSJ reported.
