## Saudi Arabia reported a Houthi attack damaged a Riyadh financial-district tower

Front-month Brent crude futures rose 0.5% to $92.47 a barrel Friday, while front-month West Texas Intermediate crude futures gained 0.6% to $83.69 a barrel. The modest increase came as traders assessed continued threats to Middle Eastern oil shipments against improving global supply conditions.

Both benchmarks were down around 15% so far this month as U.S. President Trump held off on renewed large-scale strikes against Iran. The Wall Street Journal also cited a stronger U.S. dollar and broader weakness across commodity markets as pressure on oil prices. FRED’s raw Trade-Weighted U.S. Dollar Index (Broad) stood at 122.6503 on Nov. 20, 2026, according to [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DTWEXBGS).

The Wall Street Journal reported Thursday that Israel’s spy chief had urged U.S. officials to support renewed strikes against Iran. Gulf countries pressed for negotiations amid concerns about retaliatory attacks on regional infrastructure, while Trump was weighing whether to resume a broader military campaign against Tehran.

Saudi Arabia said Thursday that a Houthi attack damaged several floors of a tower in Riyadh’s financial district, with no injuries reported, according to the Saudi Press Agency. The Wall Street Journal identified the building as the headquarters of the kingdom’s Public Investment Fund.

Pavel Molchanov, an investment strategist at Raymond James, said Thursday that oil markets had become increasingly accustomed to periodic tensions in the Middle East, provided they did not escalate into a broader conflict. He said production among OPEC’s Persian Gulf members had recovered to 87% of prewar levels as the industry adapted to disruptions.

The U.S. Energy Information Administration said Thursday that oil shipments through major Middle Eastern shipping routes increased 19% in the third quarter from the previous three months. The agency said producers used smaller vessels and alternative infrastructure to maintain exports.

Shipments through the Strait of Hormuz and Suez Canal increased, while flows through the Bab el-Mandeb declined, the EIA said. Preliminary shipping data from Vortexa indicated that shipments remained near third-quarter levels in October.

Middle Eastern crude production reached 23.1 million barrels a day in October, the highest level since the conflict began, according to the EIA.

The International Energy Agency said Thursday that oil prices responded less severely to the disruption around the Strait of Hormuz than the scale of the supply shock might have suggested. The agency cited ample global supply and well-stocked inventories.

In its *World Energy Outlook*, the IEA said the global oil market was expected to return to a surplus as Middle Eastern production recovered faster than demand. The agency projected an oversupply of 1.7 million barrels a day in the fourth quarter, widening to about 3.8 million barrels a day next year.

The IEA said prolonged disruptions to shipping through the Strait of Hormuz could accelerate investment in alternative export routes. Around 7 million barrels a day of new pipeline capacity was under construction or consideration. The projects would require an estimated $40 billion in investment and add about $2 to the cost of each barrel of Middle Eastern oil exported.

Taken together, the reported figures showed simultaneous security risk and supply adaptation. Military threats and deliberations threatened infrastructure and trade routes, while output, vessel use, alternative infrastructure and global inventories cushioned the shock to oil prices. The IEA’s projected surplus and potential alternative routes added longer-term supply-side counterweights if disruptions persisted, leaving the Friday advance modest within a market still down around 15% for the month.
