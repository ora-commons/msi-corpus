## Negotiations set on UK entry to the energy single market

France's insistence that the UK cannot have voting rights at any stage during the shaping of legislation on food standards and public-health risks relating to food and agricultural products complicates efforts by the UK and EU to close in on a Brexit reset deal. Andy Burnham was in Brussels for the EU-UK summit preparations, and journalists arrived as those preparations were made at the European Council building.

On the energy single market, there will be negotiations on allowing the UK in, which the Guardian said is in the joint interests of both sides in the wake of the Ukraine war. But there will be no talks on pharmaceuticals or chemicals, according to some capitals.

The Guardian tied the tensions at the ambassadors' meeting to a simple institutional fact: the UK is not a member of the EU, and despite the warm feelings toward the Labour government in European capitals, the benefits of the club are not extended to nonmembers.

That analysis also underlined a feeling expressed by several large member states this week that the way forward after this summit is not more iterative reset summits or mini-deals. That position undercut the idea that the summit could begin a sequence of narrow agreements.

The Guardian described the French position as “a line in the sand” for Burnham. The outlet wrote that “the last night tensions at the ambassadors meeting will be a sharp lesson for anyone in the UK who clings to the idea that negotiating with the EU comes from a position of strength.”

One diplomat said: “It puts down a marker warning the UK that cherry picking bits of the single market mean you are a rule taker.”

The same diplomat said, “It makes the choice for Britain starker and more binary,” and described the alternatives as “basically muddling along with a version of the status quo or rejoining.”
