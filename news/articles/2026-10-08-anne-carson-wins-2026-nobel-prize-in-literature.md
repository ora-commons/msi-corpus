## Swedish Academy cites her 'bold and inventive' playful dialogue with the classical tradition

The Swedish Academy on Thursday awarded the 2026 Nobel Prize in Literature to Anne Carson. The academy cited her "for her bold and inventive oeuvre that, in playful dialogue with the classical tradition, has created new forms for contemporary literature." The prize, awarded for a lifetime body of work, is regarded as the most prestigious award in global literature. The award carries 12 million Swedish kronor, equivalent to about £900,000 or $1.2 million.

Anders Olsson, chairman of the Swedish Academy's Nobel committee for Literature, said Carson has "broken away from lyrical poetry bound to the narrative self and inspired new forms of contemporary literature." Her writing, he said, "leads to playful and ironic works that move not only between classical and modern poetry but also between different genres and artforms."

Ingrid Carlberg, the permanent secretary of the Swedish Academy, said she had broken the news to Carson by phone shortly before the public announcement. Carson, Carlberg said, told her that "she is, for the moment, in Iceland, where she is listening to the wind." Carson "was taken by surprise but very happy," Carlberg said. "I could hear that in her voice."

Born in Toronto in 1950, Carson lives in Michigan and has held Icelandic citizenship since 2022. She has been a professor of classics for more than 30 years, and her writing is often inspired by Greek literature. Her translations have often given a contemporary twist to ancient works.

Her notable books include *The Glass Essay*, *Autobiography of Red: A Novel in Verse*, *Nox*, *Float* and *The Beauty of the Husband: A Fictional Essay in 29 Tangos*.

In 2019, her performance piece *Norma Jeane Baker of Troy* drew a line between Helen of Troy and Marilyn Monroe across different historical periods. In 2021, she released *The Trojan Women: A Comic*, a graphic retelling of Euripides' ancient story. Her translation of Sophocles' *Elektra* was staged in London last year with Brie Larson in the title role.

Olsson said her collaborations with visual artists and choreographers and with opera and theatre directors "demonstrate the freedom with which she relates to literary expectations and rule systems." That freedom, he added, "lies behind the outstanding importance her work since the 1990s has had for the international art and literature scene."

The Nobel Prize in Literature is awarded by the Swedish Academy for a lifetime body of work. Past winners include South Korea's Han Kang, Norway's Jon Fosse and Hungary's László Krasznahorkai.
