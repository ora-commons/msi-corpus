## Judge Reyes delays construction; East Potomac Park stays open through Nov. 28

The Interior Department announced Friday that it will issue a formal solicitation on Nov. 30 for a contractor to begin work on the seawall at Washington’s East Potomac Park. The notice said it was not a solicitation but “pre-notice of an upcoming solicitation ONLY.” The announcement came before a federal judge temporarily delayed work connected to President Donald Trump’s planned redevelopment of the park’s historic public golf course over the weekend.

Trump, a Republican, has targeted the East Potomac Golf Links as one of the projects he says will remake the District of Columbia in his image. He plans to redevelop the historic public course into a world-class venue. Trump has said the course “is in a terrible state of disrepair” and poses a risk to golfers and the public.

The century-old seawall has recently become a central reason in the Trump administration’s argument that East Potomac Park needs renovation. Sections of the structure have settled and eroded over the decades, leading to tidal overflow, poor drainage and crumbling structures along the Potomac River and Washington Channel. Seawall work is expected to cost at least $300 million.

The Trump administration sought to close the 330-acre park for 15 months, citing safety factors that included the seawall, which it argued was in precarious condition. Trump had announced that work would begin immediately despite a legal challenge initiated in February by the DC Preservation League and two local golfers.

During a hearing on Friday, U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes disputed that argument, noting that the park service had documented seawall problems for many years. Reyes issued a temporary restraining order the following day that delays for two weeks any work related to construction of the golf course. Reyes was nominated by President Joe Biden, a Democrat, in 2023.

The government had sought to close the park on Sunday, but the ruling means the park remains open. The 330-acre property includes tennis courts, picnic areas and walking and biking loops. The temporary restraining order is in effect through Nov. 28, and the formal solicitation is scheduled for Nov. 30.

The golf course has not been operational since its lease with a management company expired. Plaintiffs argue that Trump’s plan would change the character of the publicly accessible course and make it unaffordable. Trump has denied that characterization and said the course could host top professional events such as the U.S. Open.

The redevelopment is one of multiple projects in the nation’s capital that Trump has championed to remake the city. They include a large memorial arch, a new White House ballroom and the reshaping of the John F. Kennedy Center for the Performing Arts, among others.

For now, East Potomac Park remains open, golf course construction is delayed and the administration’s seawall solicitation is set to go out Nov. 30. The legal dispute over the park’s future continues.
