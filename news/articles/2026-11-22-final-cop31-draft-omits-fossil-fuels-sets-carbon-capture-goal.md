## Turkey's Kurum gives negotiators three hours to review 10-page text

ANTALYA, Turkey — A proposed final agreement released in the early hours of Sunday at the United Nations climate talks in Antalya does not contain the words "fossil fuels," the chief cause of global warming, or specifically call for transitioning away from the coal, oil and gas that spew carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases into the atmosphere. The words "fossil," "oil," "coal" and "phase out" do not appear anywhere in the 10-page text, according to a review of the draft.

The draft from Turkish Environment Minister Murat Kurum, president of the two-week conference known as COP31, was released after 3 a.m. Sunday. Kurum gave negotiators about three hours to make objections or ask for changes before the wording goes before the conference plenary.

Approval requires near-unanimous consensus. Several countries made vociferous objections earlier in the negotiations to omitting a call to phase out fossil fuels. Because the text went to negotiators and the public after 3 a.m., countries and activists did not immediately respond to the newest version.

The conference was scheduled to end Friday and is now in double overtime. Organizers worry that as negotiators depart there may not be a voting quorum of two-thirds of the delegates, which would prevent passage of the draft.

The proposal's opening line refers to the 2023 agreement reached in Dubai, saying it is "recalling the United Arab Emirates Consensus in its entirety." Fossil fuels went unmentioned in final decisions for more than 25 years, mostly because of objections by Saudi Arabia, said Alden Meyer, a longtime analyst with the European think tank E3G. He added that the world nonetheless agreed in Dubai to "transition away from fossil fuels."

"It would be a real setback if we come out of here with no signal that we were serious about what we agreed to do three years ago," Meyer said.

Earlier in the week, Panama vowed to formally object to a final agreement that did not put a cap on oil production, acknowledge failing goals to limit warming and beef up finance for nations to adapt to warming's harms. Panama Environment Minister Juan Carlos Navarro said the talks were being held hostage by a "petroleum mafia" and need to be reformed.

The newest draft does put a specific goal on carbon capture and storage, a technology supported by the oil industry: 1 billion metric tons. The proposal is aimed at industrial polluters and those that cannot easily switch to renewables, finding a way to capture the carbon dioxide in their emissions and then finding a way to bury it. The plan is more localized and different from large-scale, global carbon removal efforts.

The agreement also calls for countries to increase the share of total final energy consumption that comes from electricity — including switching transportation such as cars, trucks and planes to electricity from gas — to 35% from about 21% now by 2035. That has been Turkey's signature effort at the talks.

Climate activists have sought a provision stating that the electricity should only come from renewable energy, such as wind and solar, arguing that allowing electricity powered by fossil fuel in the goal defeats the purpose of lowering emissions.

"Electrification that runs on coal and gas does not limit warming," said climate scientist Bill Hare, CEO of Climate Analytics. "Continued reliance on coal and gas risks shifting where fossil fuels are burned and locking in further emissions."

Turkey and the head of the International Energy Agency said that because 80% of the new electricity is fueled by renewables, it will work to reduce carbon emissions just the same.

Many developing countries wanted more detail on past conferences' pledge to triple financial aid to poor countries to help them adapt to warming's harms. Past conferences talked of tripling previous adaptation funding but never mentioned a baseline, and some nations wanted an explicit baseline. They did not get it.
