## Fetterman sole Democrat opposed as three Republicans backed the measure

The US Senate rejected a war powers resolution on Thursday that was aimed at forcing President Donald Trump to halt fighting with Iran, voting 48-51. The measure had cleared the House of Representatives in September and was the first war powers vote in either chamber since the 3 November midterm elections, in which Republicans lost control of both chambers beginning next year.

The vote broke largely along party lines. John Fetterman of Pennsylvania was the sole Democrat to oppose the resolution, while Susan Collins of Maine, Rand Paul of Kentucky and Lisa Murkowski of Alaska were the only Republicans to vote in favor.

The Guardian's lead photograph showed an Iranian resident looking out the window of his damaged home after Israeli-American strikes destroyed residential buildings in Tehran in April (photograph: AFP/Getty Images).

The Guardian reported that Republicans largely stood behind the conflict, which it said had played a major role in their sweeping midterm election losses. The outlet also reported that, since the war began in February with a joint US-Israel attack on Iran, Trump's Republican allies had succeeded in voting many of the war powers resolutions introduced by Democrats down.

All House Democrats and seven Republicans had supported the measure before it reached the Senate, and several of those Republicans lost reelection in the midterms.

One resolution passed both chambers in June, but Trump ignored it. That failure prompted Democrats to continue their efforts to make use of the 1973 law to force an end to the conflict.

The Guardian also reported that Thom Tillis of North Carolina, who is retiring at the end of the year after supporting previous resolutions, and outgoing Louisiana senator Bill Cassidy were among the Republicans who stood behind the conflict. Cassidy's support had been crucial for the June measure's passage.

Democratic senator Chris Murphy missed the vote after recently suffering a stroke.

The Guardian reported that polls indicate the conflict is unpopular with the public and that it has increased prices for gasoline and diesel across the US.

The 48-51 outcome is the latest in a run of congressional votes that have failed to halt the fighting. It also records a split inside both parties: three Republicans backed the measure while one Democrat opposed it, leaving the Senate's attempt to make use of the 1973 law unsuccessful.
