## Trump administration calls his departure self-deportation while lawyers describe it as temporary

Kilmar Abrego Garcia left the United States for Spain on Tuesday, according to his lawyers and Trump administration officials, after a legal battle that had spanned nearly two years. The Salvadoran immigrant departed voluntarily, while his attorney Simon Sandoval-Moshenberg described the move as temporary and the administration characterized it as self-deportation.

Sean Hecker, another lawyer for Abrego Garcia, confirmed Spain as his destination. Sandoval-Moshenberg called the departure temporary as Abrego Garcia “waits out the end of this administration.” The lawyer said he expected the next administration to prioritize reunifying Abrego Garcia’s family and bringing him back to Maryland. U.S. Sen. Chris Van Hollen, D-Md., who had championed the case, said Abrego Garcia “is traveling to a country of his choice, a free man, unharmed and with dignity.”

Before Tuesday’s move, Abrego Garcia and the Trump administration were at an impasse over where he should go. Abrego Garcia said he would move to Costa Rica if the government allowed it. His attorneys said Costa Rica had offered him refugee status, but the administration did not support the plan.

The administration instead sought to deport him to Liberia, an attempt that courts ultimately blocked. The dispute over his destination continued until Tuesday’s voluntary departure for Spain.

The Trump administration hailed the move late Tuesday. “We are DELIGHTED to announce that Kilmar Abrego Garcia has self-deported from the United States to Spain,” Department of Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin wrote on X. Mullin also accused Abrego Garcia of being a gang member and a human trafficker, without evidence.

Abrego Garcia’s case became a flashpoint for President Trump’s mass-deportation campaign. It raised questions about due process, third-country deportations and the extent to which the administration was sending people to countries where they might face threats.

Abrego Garcia had lived in the United States for more than a decade. In 2019, an immigration judge ruled that he could not be deported to El Salvador after he argued that he would face torture or persecution there. In 2025, immigration officers arrested him and sent him to a Salvadoran prison, where he said he suffered beatings and sleep deprivation.

Later, immigration officials said Abrego Garcia had been deported by mistake because of an administrative error. He was brought back to the United States in June 2025.

After his return, prosecutors charged him with human smuggling. The allegations were based on body-camera footage from 2022 showing Tennessee police stopping Abrego Garcia with several other men inside his SUV. Prosecutors said he was a human trafficker who helped immigrants move from Houston to Maryland.

Abrego Garcia denied the smuggling allegations. His lawyers argued that the government’s criminal case was retaliation after his mistaken deportation drew national attention, and a judge ultimately dismissed the charges. His departure for Spain occurred while his attorneys described it as a temporary move pending a future administration.
