## Independent directors investigate whether Huang shared corporate or national security information

Nvidia said Monday that its board had “received input from senior levels of the United States government” before reinstating co-founder and longtime CEO Jensen Huang as chief executive, and that it was “confident that Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk.” The return came 11 days after Huang resigned over revelations of at least two extramarital affairs, one with a woman whom U.S. officials believed was working with Chinese intelligence and another with a senior direct report.

The reinstatement does not close the company's open questions. Nvidia has formed a committee of independent directors to investigate whether the chief executive shared corporate or national security information during his romantic relationship with Alice Huang, who was at the time an executive at Nvidia partner Megaspeed. Separately, Nvidia is under investigation by the Securities and Exchange Commission, a probe that is exploring whether the company's disclosures misled investors, The Wall Street Journal reported.

Chair Harvey C. Jones said the board believes Huang's return is “in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers” and that it voted unanimously to reinstate him. Jones also said the board was satisfied with Huang's candor and ability to lead the company.

That was a stark change from last week, when Jones said on an earnings call that the group had “unanimously accepted” Huang's resignation and “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.”

Between those decisions, Nvidia's directors came under intense pressure from some Silicon Valley leaders and employees for ousting Huang without waiting for the results of an independent investigation. In open letters, phone calls and group chats, Huang's allies spent two weeks making the case that his ouster was hasty.

President Trump, who had a close relationship with Huang and had pushed for his return since his ouster, praised the board's decision Monday. He called Huang a “Great Patriot and National Treasure.” “The Country needs Jensen's leadership at NVIDIA in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy. The United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen's return to NVIDIA poses any risk to National Security,” Trump wrote. On Sunday night, Trump had posted that he hoped the board would rehire Huang.

Huang, 63, returns to a company facing a host of challenges. Nvidia is contending with increased competitive pressure from rivals including Advanced Micro Devices and Alphabet's Google, and with investor concerns about the extent to which the company has backstopped the AI economy. The Wall Street Journal said the comeback underscores the leader's importance to a company at the center of an AI boom powering the U.S. economy.

Nvidia shares soared nearly 30% in early morning trading. The company last week suffered its worst stretch since 2008 and weighed on the global AI trade.

Huang said in a statement: “I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment.”

The Wall Street Journal also reported that Huang was known for being demanding and difficult, with a reputation for dressing employees down in meetings and berating those around him for ideas he saw as inadequate. His adult children Madison and Spencer work at the company, as does Madison Huang's husband.
