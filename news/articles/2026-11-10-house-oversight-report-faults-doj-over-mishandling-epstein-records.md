## The release exposed victim identities and obscured alleged conspirators' names

A Republican-led House Oversight Committee released a 137-page report on Tuesday faulting President Donald Trump and the Justice Department for their handling of millions of pages of records concerning Jeffrey Epstein. The committee said the administration reneged on a promise to open the files, leading Congress to pass a law forcing their release, and that DOJ later missed disclosure deadlines. The report also faulted deficient redactions that exposed sensitive information about victims and overinclusive redactions that concealed names of alleged co-conspirators.

The Epstein Files Transparency Act, passed by Congress and signed by Trump nearly a year earlier, requires DOJ to publicly disclose many previously confidential records related to Epstein, a late financier and sex offender. The report said, “The Trump administration has repeatedly committed to releasing Epstein-related files,” and then stated, “However, multiple shortcomings related to releasing documents and failures to adequately communicate about those releases plagued the DOJ’s efforts.”

DOJ released the bulk of the records in December and January: more than 3 million pages of documents, more than 2,000 videos and 180,000 images. The materials included documents concerning Andrew Mountbatten-Windsor, formerly known as Britain’s Prince Andrew, and email correspondence between Epstein and Elon Musk and other prominent contacts from across the political spectrum. Then-Attorney General Pam Bondi’s handling of the files contributed to her firing in April.

The report came after the Nov. 3 midterm elections, in which Republicans suffered heavy House losses and failed to retain their majority, with the release of government records a recurring campaign issue. Democrats used the administration’s handling of the files to accuse Republicans of protecting the wealthy and well-connected, suggesting Trump’s past relationship with Epstein exposed him to potential unethical activity. The report stated, “Trump’s lack of involvement is confirmed by numerous lines of testimony in interviews or depositions with various individuals.”

More than 350 different ads mentioning Epstein aired since Labor Day for Republican and Democratic candidates in the Nov. 3 midterms, according to the ad-tracking firm AdImpact. Ads from Democratic-aligned groups claimed Republican candidates voted against releasing the Epstein files, while candidates from both parties accused each other of taking donations from associates of the convicted sex offender.

The report also examined how federal authorities investigated Epstein nearly two decades ago but failed to bring charges after multiple underage girls told police and the FBI that they had been paid to give Epstein sexualized massages. Alexander Acosta, then the U.S. attorney in Miami, signed off on a deal that let Epstein avoid federal prosecution. Epstein instead pleaded guilty to a state charge of soliciting prostitution from someone under age 18 and received an 18-month jail sentence.

Epstein was permitted to leave jail for up to 12 hours a day, six days a week, on work release. Acosta was Trump’s first labor secretary in Trump’s first term. A woman told the committee last year that Epstein abused her while he was on work release. The report criticized the Palm Beach County sheriff’s office for granting the privileges, calling the decision an “abuse of its discretion” and “absurd leniency that enabled Epstein to commit further crimes.”

Lawyers for some victims said thousands of missing redactions exposed the names of nearly 100 women. The report said, “The DOJ — presumably due to the speed with which files were being reviewed — failed to adequately redact names and personally identifiable information of numerous survivors.”

The report also detailed claims that some redactions were overinclusive, concealing names of alleged co-conspirators and others in Epstein’s orbit. The Justice Department redacted the name of a recipient of an email from Epstein that referenced a “torture video.” According to members of Congress who reviewed unredacted versions of the document, the recipient was Emirati businessperson Sultan Ahmed bin Sulayem.

Epstein died in prison in 2019. Maxwell was convicted in 2021 on counts involving her role in helping Epstein sexually abuse underage girls, including sex trafficking, and is serving a 20-year prison sentence at a minimum-security prison in Texas. Committee Chairman James Comer of Kentucky said, “The federal government has failed the survivors of Jeffrey Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell’s horrific crimes for decades.” He added, “Epstein operated with impunity, shielded and enabled by powerful connections and a justice system that repeatedly failed his victims.”

Justice Department officials said the Epstein case is not closed and that there is no statute of limitations on human trafficking. “The department will continue following all additional investigative leads in matters relating to Jeffrey Epstein,” a department spokesperson said in a statement, encouraging victims to reach out to the FBI.
