## Poundland breakup expected with rival retailers taking store parcels

AlixPartners said Poundland's sale process "did not identify a viable solvent solution for the business as a whole." Poundland's directors appointed the firm as administrators, and the 600-store UK and Ireland chain is now expected to be broken up. Retailers including Poundstretcher, B&M and The Range are expected to take parcels of stores. Poundland's collapse puts 12,000 jobs at risk ahead of Christmas and is expected to bring significant job losses and store closures.

US owner Gordon Brothers had rejected at least two last-ditch bids for the business and had been pressed to accept an offer that would save jobs and protect thousands of the retailer's suppliers. Poundland's board filed a notice of intention to appoint administrators last month, and the protection was later extended, shielding the retailer from creditors' demands until 26 November while a solution was sought. The board was forced to appoint administrators before Thursday's deadline as shelves began to empty because the chain was running short of funds to restock during its peak Christmas trading period.

Gordon Brothers said it could raise more from a breakup of Poundland — selling stock and parcels of stores to rival retailers — than through the first bids, which promised only a small upfront payment for the £30m in shareholder debts that Gordon Brothers took on with fellow shareholder Pepco. Gordon Brothers rejected the deals despite Pepco's willingness to wait for repayment. Pepco, which holds £7.5m of the debt, said it was prepared to wait in the hope of saving the business.

The US-based finance company bought the struggling chain from the European retail group Pepco in June 2025 for a nominal £1. Gordon Brothers rejected an initial management-backed rescue bid financed by Martin Property Group.

Just two bidders interested in the whole company remained in talks right up until this week's deadline. Martin Property Group, a Northern Irish family-owned company, had worked with Poundland's existing management, led by Barry Williams, for about two months and remained interested. Germany's Zachert Private Equity joined with a last-ditch bid; the fund was founded by a former Bloomberg journalist and specialises in acquiring mid-sized companies such as the German photo studio group Studioline and the US food and drink company Brands Within Reach.

Other bidders for the whole group included the finance group Xyra Group Holdings and the Irish investment company Innovest. Both fell out of the bidding race over the past week.

Gordon Brothers' UK director, Mark Newton-Jones, held the latest of several meetings with business secretary Jonathan Reynolds on Monday, more than a week after giving evidence to parliament's business select committee. The meetings followed MPs' calls last week for the government to protect Poundland and other high street stores from "predatory capitalism" and "asset-stripping."

High inflation and heavy competition from budget stores, online specialists and supermarkets left the 36-year-old retailer struggling. Poundland, founded with one store in Burton upon Trent in 1990, was the UK's first and biggest nationwide single-price retailer. It has endured a rocky history under a string of different owners as it faced rising costs and heavy competition from chains such as Savers, Home Bargains and B&M, as well as from the big supermarkets.
