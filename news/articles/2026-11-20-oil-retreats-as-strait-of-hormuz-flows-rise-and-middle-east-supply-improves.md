## EIA expects global inventories to build as Middle Eastern production and exports recover while near-term demand remains relatively flat

Crude futures were heading for a third consecutive weekly loss on Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, with increasing supply out of the Middle East outweighing concerns about conflict in the region. In the 9:54 a.m. ET market update, most-active WTI for January delivery was down 0.8% at $81.75 a barrel and Brent was down 1.1% at $90.90 a barrel.

The Market Talks collection was updated Nov. 20, 2026, at 12:24 p.m. ET. The latest Market Talks covering Energy and Utilities were published exclusively on Dow Jones Newswires at 4:20 ET, 12:20 ET and 16:50 ET.

In the 4:32 a.m. ET update, oil prices were retreating after earlier gains, with Brent crude falling 0.9% to $91.18 a barrel and WTI declining 0.8% to $82.56 a barrel, as traders assessed improving global supply conditions and continued threats to Middle Eastern shipments.

The supply side of that move had a documented driver. The U.S. Energy Information Administration said Thursday that increasing oil flows through the Strait of Hormuz have contributed to the recent decline in crude prices. The agency expects global inventories to build as Middle Eastern production and exports recover, while demand remains relatively flat in the near term.

Walt Chancellor of Macquarie Group, whose shares were down 2.17%, said in a note that "Resilient flows through the Strait of Hormuz remain the story for energy markets broadly, extending to LNG recently." Chancellor said persistence of the status quo with the Iran blockade and convoys through the strait could keep pressure on oil markets, but he added "we cannot altogether dismiss the potential for escalation."

Oil futures had settled near flat in the prior session in a choppy trading session as continuing tensions in the Middle East were countered by improving supply. Raymond James investment strategist Pavel Molchanov said "The oil market has become acclimated to periodic flare-ups, just as long as there is no return to all-out war." He added: "With oil production among OPEC's Persian Gulf members having reached 87% of pre-war levels, the industry is gradually adapting."

WTI slipped 0.1% to $83.21 a barrel ahead of Friday's December contract expiry, and Brent for January delivery edged up 3 cents to $91.98 a barrel.

Between the two Friday readings, prices fell further rather than reversing the earlier retreat, with the 9:54 a.m. ET snapshot showing January WTI down 0.8% and Brent down 1.1%. The conditions behind the move stayed conditional rather than settled: Chancellor said the potential for escalation could not be altogether dismissed, and Molchanov said, "The oil market has become acclimated to periodic flare-ups, just as long as there is no return to all-out war."
