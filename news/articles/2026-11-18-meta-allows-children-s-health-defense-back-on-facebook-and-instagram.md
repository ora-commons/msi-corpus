## Health officials report 10 measles-associated deaths as US loses measles elimination status

The Guardian reported that Meta had allowed Children’s Health Defense, the anti-vaccine group once led by Robert F Kennedy Jr, back onto Facebook and Instagram. Its review found that the organization had reopened or opened six accounts between May and September. Posts on the accounts appeared to violate Meta’s policy against harmful health misinformation, including claims about vaccine safety and measles deaths.

Meta removed Children’s Health Defense from both platforms in 2022 after years of criticism that Kennedy and the group were top spreaders of false information about vaccines, Covid-19 and other subjects. Meta said at the time that it “removed these accounts for repeatedly violating our policies.” The company told Children’s Health Defense that its accounts ran afoul of community standards on “misinformation that could cause physical harm.”

Kennedy, now the US health secretary, continues to have close ties to Children’s Health Defense and headlined the group’s annual conference in September. Meta had restored Kennedy’s Instagram account in 2023, after he had lost it in 2021 for sharing false information about Covid and vaccines. The company said it restored the account because Kennedy was an active candidate for US president, but it also said it would not restore Children’s Health Defense’s accounts.

The Guardian’s findings came after an investigation earlier this month found health and vaccine misinformation rampant on Facebook in the large public group Crunchy Moms. Facebook was recommending the group despite apparent violations of its policies. Crunchy Moms was started a few months after Meta founder Mark Zuckerberg last year announced a rollback of moderation and factchecking. Zuckerberg defended the move at the time by saying he wanted to restore “free expression on our platforms.”

The Guardian reported that Kennedy frequently tussled with Facebook before he entered the government. Children’s Health Defense sued Facebook in 2020 while Kennedy led the group, claiming that the platform censored it. The case was dismissed the following year, with the judge finding that Facebook, as a private entity, could not be held liable for violating the group’s constitutional rights. Earlier this year, the Washington Sun reported that a Meta lawyer argued in a New Mexico lawsuit that a judge should consider Kennedy’s trustworthiness and “unusual” views on vaccines when urging that a government-produced report not be entered into evidence in a lawsuit over the company’s child-safety practices.

Dorit Reiss, a UC Law San Francisco professor who has monitored Children’s Health Defense’s legal cases, said she did not believe there was a legal reason that Meta would be compelled to restore access. Reiss said it was possible Meta had made a political calculation and ceased enforcing its own anti-misinformation policies.

“Facebook has the right to decide what to do here,” Reiss said. “Meta decided it’s more profitable … that it’s more in their interest to let the misinformers back up.”

The Guardian identified a recent Facebook video titled “Vaccines: Your child could die,” which made the misleading claim that vaccines are deadly. Another Children’s Health Defense post earlier this month said, “Somehow there’s this narrative, vaccines are safe and effective, and we know that’s just a lie.”

Other Children’s Health Defense posts addressed measles and vaccination schedules. A video posted last month to at least two accounts said of the six measles-associated deaths then reported in Pennsylvania: “None of them died from the measles.” Another post said the MMR vaccine is “not so safe and effective,” while another said “none of the vaccines ever eliminated any epidemic in history.” A separate post this month suggested it was unsafe to follow doctors’ recommendations to receive multiple vaccinations at once. A June 2026 video described HPV vaccines as “very dangerous,” claimed they had “caused hundreds, thousands of deaths” and said they do not prevent cervical cancer.

Health officials have reported 10 measles-associated deaths in the United States this year, the Guardian said. The country also lost its measles elimination status this week in an official recognition that measles is again endemic after being eliminated for 26 years.

Meta declined to comment on why it allowed Children’s Health Defense back or why it was allowing apparent violations of the company’s misinformation policy. Children’s Health Defense did not respond to requests for comment.
