## Freeman says many survivors outside the U.S. lacked notice of the settlement

A proposed $35m settlement would establish a compensation pool for qualifying survivors in a 2024 Manhattan federal civil complaint against Jeffrey Epstein’s personal lawyer Darren Indyke and accountant Richard D Kahn, according to court papers. A final settlement hearing is scheduled for 2 December, when a judge might sign off on the agreement brokered between survivors’ attorneys at Boies Schiller & Flexner LLP and the two defendants. Jennifer Freeman of Marsh Law said the deal may create roadblocks for separate claims involving child sexual abuse material, or CSAM.

Indyke and Kahn worked for Epstein for decades as his personal attorney and accountant, respectively, and both serve as executors of his estate. They have insisted they knew nothing about Epstein’s crimes and have rejected allegations of wrongdoing. In the complaint, survivors’ attorneys allege that Indyke and Kahn “were personally essential to the Epstein enterprise’s success” and helped structure Epstein’s bank accounts and cash withdrawals to give Epstein and his associates access to large amounts of cash in furtherance of sex trafficking.

Freeman, who leads other litigation against Epstein, said she feared Epstein’s estate could try arguing that the proposed settlement protects the estate from litigation brought by CSAM survivors. She represents two women in a 15 September lawsuit involving CSAM claims and several class members in the trafficking litigation.

Freeman said, “We objected to the settlement on the grounds that CSAM has its own separate claim with its own statutory remedies and this was not, in our view, considered in putting together this settlement.” She also said, “We fully support the existence of a settlement for survivors, and we want that to go forward, and we very much hope it does,” while continuing to object to the estate release. Freeman said the release did not make sense because the case was against two individuals, the lawyer and accountant, rather than the estate.

Freeman also said survivors might not know about the proposed settlement or understand its possible implications for CSAM claims against Epstein’s estate. She wants every survivor who endured abuse involving CSAM, including people participating in the trafficking settlement, to have an opportunity to pursue a direct CSAM claim. Freeman said CSAM claims “should not be included in the settlement at all. That should be explicit,” and said, “The release of the estate should be at least limited and, in my view, eliminated.”

The judge extended the deadline for survivors to submit or opt out of trafficking-related claims on 16 September 2026, but Freeman said she had heard from many survivors, particularly those outside the United States, who had not known anything was happening. She said class actions are not typical in their countries, making the process less familiar and harder to understand. Freeman warned that the proposed settlement might apply to some survivors without their awareness.

Attorneys for Indyke and Kahn dispute that concern. Daniel Ruzumna, who represents Indyke, said the parties told the court and the court appeared to recognize that the putative class included only women who were sexually abused and/or trafficked by Epstein. Ruzumna said a woman with both trafficking-related and CSAM claims could also be included in the class and might receive a larger recovery from the settlement fund. A woman with only CSAM-related claims would not be part of the class and therefore would not release those claims, he said.

Attorneys for Indyke and Kahn deferred to prior statements on the allegations against them. In a separate lawsuit brought by New Mexico Attorney General Raúl Torrez, one of the defendants’ lawyers said they “emphatically rejected” claims that they were complicit in or knowingly facilitated Epstein’s sexual abuse or trafficking. The statement said the conduct occurred behind closed doors, in secluded locations and at times when neither Indyke nor Kahn was present. It also said no woman had ever accused either man of committing or witnessing sexual abuse, or of reporting Epstein’s abuse to them.

Survivors and police have said Epstein possessed, transported, produced and distributed CSAM over decades. An FBI memo among materials released under the Epstein Files Transparency Act said the files included “a large volume of images of Epstein, images and videos of victims who are either minors or appear to be minors, and over ten thousand downloaded videos and images of illegal child sex abuse material and other pornography.”

Maria Farmer, whose sister Annie was abused by Epstein and Ghislaine Maxwell at 16, told authorities in 1996 that Epstein possessed nude images of her siblings. A law-enforcement report quoted Farmer as saying she was a professional artist and had taken pictures of her sisters, 12 and 16, for her own artwork. The report said Epstein stole the photographs and negatives and was believed to have sold the pictures to potential buyers. Farmer also reported pictures of young girls at swimming pools and said Epstein threatened to burn her house down if she told anyone about the photos.

Epstein was a financier whose associates included numerous wealthy and prominent men. He was arrested in July 2019 and charged with sex trafficking minors after years of allegations. The Manhattan U.S. attorney’s office said his victims were as young as 14. He killed himself in jail while awaiting trial.

The proposed agreement remains pending before the court. At the 2 December hearing, a judge might sign the agreement, which would establish the $35m pool. Freeman’s unresolved request is that CSAM claims be expressly excluded and the estate release limited or eliminated; the defendants’ lawyers maintain that women with CSAM-only claims are outside the class and would not release those claims.
