## Surveillance video and witness accounts figure into Myles's arrest

New Orleans police jailed Antonio Myles, 19, on a second-degree murder charge in the beating death of unhoused man David Hyde, according to police statements and court records. Hyde, 68, was found dead outside a Walgreens on November 8 after officers found him with severe facial contusions and prescription medication bottles nearby. Police said store surveillance video and information from a known subject helped identify Myles, who surrendered after a magistrate authorized his arrest.

Officers found Hyde at about 6:55 a.m. on November 8 outside the Walgreens in the 4400 block of Claiborne Avenue, on the edge of the Freret neighborhood. He was near numerous prescription medication bottles and was pronounced dead at the scene. Sworn count documents said investigators believed Hyde had been kicked to the head, citing shoe prints left on his face.

Store surveillance video showed Hyde sitting near the pharmacy's front entrance. The attacker took Hyde's backpack, dumped its contents on the ground and picked up some items before the beating. The deadly violence evidently occurred outside the camera's view. The man who approached Hyde at one point used his shirt to wipe his shoes before leaving the scene.

Police alleged they received a call the next day reporting that Myles had been heard bragging to unknown subjects that he was responsible for beating a homeless man to death. Myles was interviewed by police soon afterward but released pending further investigation.

On November 10, New Orleans police released parking-lot surveillance video showing a figure described as a “suspect wanted in connection with a brutal beating that left a disabled man dead.” Police said in a statement accompanying the video, “The victim … was unable to defend himself due to health issues,” without elaborating. Police asked anyone who recognized the figure to call them.

An unidentified known subject told police that the figure in the video was Myles and alleged that Myles was wearing a beanie-style cap. Investigators later recovered surveillance footage showing Myles wearing the cap when Hyde was killed.

A detective interviewed an unidentified known subject who reported that Myles called hours after Hyde was found dead. According to the sworn police statement, Myles told the person that he loved them, had killed a homeless man and continued kicking Hyde after he was already dead. The known subject also said Myles took prescription medication from Hyde's backpack. The person knew Myles well and identified him in the released video.

Police said they believed the account was true because “no specific details of the investigation were released to the media.” The statement added: “Only a person who was there when the homicide occurred would know that the victim was kicked to his head repeatedly, and that the victim was in possession of prescription medicine inside of a backpack.” The police statement was not available to news media until Monday.

A New Orleans criminal court magistrate commissioner authorized police to arrest Myles on a murder count on Friday. Myles turned himself over to police and was jailed, with no bond set immediately, according to court records. In an unrelated case, Myles pleaded guilty to simple battery on August 11 and was sentenced to about one month in jail.

If convicted of second-degree murder, Myles would receive mandatory life imprisonment. He has not been convicted, and the case remains at the charging stage.

A 2020 US justice department study determined that about half of people experiencing homelessness report being victims of violence, compared with 2% of the general population. In May, the federal Housing and Urban Development said the most recent available point-in-time estimate calculated that nearly 700,000 people were experiencing homelessness nationwide when the count was conducted on a night in January.

The Guardian reported that, as portrayed in criminal court records summarizing the circumstances of Myles's arrest, Hyde's killing is an extreme example of the heightened risk of violence facing people experiencing homelessness in the US. Myles remains jailed, with no bond set immediately after his surrender, and the second-degree murder charge has not been resolved by a conviction.
