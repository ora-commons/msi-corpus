## A lesser charge would allow Clancy to waive a jury

At Monday’s hearing in Plymouth, Massachusetts, Plymouth County Assistant District Attorney Jennifer Sprague told Judge William Sullivan that prosecutors were "ready to pick a jury trial date." The Associated Press reported that prosecutors had signaled that, at least for now, they intended to prosecute Clancy again on first-degree murder charges in the killings of her three young children. The Plymouth County district attorney’s office had not indicated publicly whether it would pursue the same charges in a second trial.

Clancy’s lawyer Kevin Reddington said he took Sprague’s statement to mean that prosecutors intended to proceed with a first-degree murder case because a lesser charge would let Clancy waive her right to a jury. "The die has been cast," Reddington told Sullivan. "I think you can read between the lines that when they say that the case is going to require a jury, that it's an indirect comment from the DA that they intend to proceed on first-degree murder indictments."

Prosecutors had sought a May trial date, citing scheduling conflicts, but Sullivan set the retrial for March 29. Attorneys are scheduled to return to court Dec. 30 to discuss trial logistics, security and media arrangements.

The March 29 date came one week after Sullivan rejected a defense request to dismiss the charges. He ruled that a retrial would not violate Clancy’s constitutional protection against double jeopardy.

Clancy’s first trial ended in a mistrial after jurors could not reach a unanimous verdict on whether she was criminally responsible for the killings. Eleven jurors favored acquittal on the grounds of insanity, while one disagreed.

Clancy, a former labor and delivery nurse, has never disputed that she killed her three children at the family’s Duxbury home in January 2023. Her attorneys said she was suffering from postpartum psychosis, a rare mental illness that can afflict mothers after childbirth.

Prosecutors argued that Clancy planned the killings and understood what she was doing.
