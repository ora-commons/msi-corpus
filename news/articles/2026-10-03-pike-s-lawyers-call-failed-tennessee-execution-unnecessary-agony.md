## Earlier 2026 execution also failed when medical professional mis-placed IV

Christa Pike's lawyers said the failed Tennessee lethal injection caused their client "unnecessary agony" and violated her right to be executed free of cruel and unusual punishment. Pike was placed on a ventilator after receiving two doses of the sedative pentobarbital during Wednesday's execution and remained alive. According to the Death Penalty Information Center, she is the first person to survive after being injected with lethal doses of execution drugs.

Pike's lawyers had previously said her small veins made it likely she would experience unnecessary suffering from lethal drugs. As of Thursday evening, Pike was unconscious and on a ventilator, her lawyers said in a court filing Friday. Pentobarbital is a sedative that more than a dozen states use for capital punishment.

Tennessee Department of Correction Commissioner Frank Strada is resigning this month amid the fallout, Gov. Bill Lee's office said Saturday. Lee also halted all further Tennessee executions for the remainder of the year and ordered an independent review of the incident. The governor's office did not name an interim commissioner on Saturday and said one would be announced in the future.

A spokesperson for the Tennessee Department of Correction declined to comment beyond the governor's statement. The department had said earlier in the week that it had followed established protocol.

Lee said he appreciated Strada's "willingness to put the interests of Tennesseans first during this difficult moment." Strada, in a statement shared by the governor's office, said he believed the department had followed established protocols but that stepping aside was in the state's best interest. "I believe this review is entirely appropriate and necessary, and I look forward to doing everything I can to help determine exactly what occurred," Strada said.

Lee appointed Strada to the commissioner role in January 2023. According to his biography, Strada previously served as deputy director of the Arizona Department of Corrections and spent 30 years with the Federal Bureau of Prisons.

Pike was sentenced to death in 1996 for the murder of Colleen Slemmer.

A different Tennessee inmate underwent a failed execution attempt earlier in 2026 when a medical professional did not place an IV correctly. In a deposition, the provider said he had not performed a similar procedure in more than a decade.

Following pressure from anti-capital-punishment activists, pharmaceutical companies have placed restrictions on their drugs for use in executions. Some states have responded by reinstating other methods of execution, including firing squad and nitrogen gas, or by turning to loosely or even unregulated sources for the drugs. Experts say the shift to less regulated sources makes lethal injections less reliable.
