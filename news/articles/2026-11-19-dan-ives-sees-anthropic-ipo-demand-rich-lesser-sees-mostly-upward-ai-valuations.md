## Dan Ives sees Anthropic IPO demand; Rich Lesser sees mostly upward AI valuations

**Subtype:** fact

## Record $81 billion raise contrasts with SpaceX's about $1.9 trillion value

Dan Ives, partner and senior managing director at Yorkville Ives & Co., called Anthropic's offering a sign of strong demand and a big win for the technology sector after what he described as a white-knuckle time with Nvidia. “This is strong appetite for this major AI IPO in Anthropic, and it’s a big win for the tech sector after a white-knuckle time with Nvidia. The pricing shows strong demand among investors,” Ives said.

Rich Lesser, global chair and former CEO of Boston Consulting Group, said the market valuation for AI companies would be “up and down for many years to come, but more up than down.” Speaking in an Associated Press interview on Tuesday at the Semafor Silicon Valley & The World forum, he said, “Exactly what the flow of that will be, it’s very hard to predict,” and added, “There’s no question what Anthropic and OpenAI and Google and a number of the other players in this industry have done is pretty remarkable.”

Anthropic said the offering consists of 980,000,000 shares of Class A common stock, 956,351,354 of which are being sold by Anthropic and 23,648,646 of which are being sold by certain selling stockholders. The San Francisco-based maker of the Claude AI tool is set to make its Wall Street debut sometime after the stock market opens at 9:30 a.m. EST Thursday under ticker symbol “ANT.”

The $83-per-share price, above Anthropic's previously set $70 to $78 range, put the company's valuation at $1.5 trillion. The $81 billion in proceeds made the deal the largest initial public offering on record, ahead of SpaceX's roughly $75 billion June raise. SpaceX nevertheless held a valuation of about $1.9 trillion as of Wednesday afternoon. The Associated Press said the higher-than-anticipated pricing signaled broad investor enthusiasm for AI stocks even amid some market uncertainty stemming from the ouster of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and concerns about an AI bubble. The bigger test, the report said, would be how Anthropic fares on the stock market.

Anthropic has taken on $6.2 billion in debt, which its financial filings acknowledge is “substantial.” The company is also unprofitable, at least under standard accounting rules, suggesting that its resources for paying interest and principal are limited. Anthropic has ample cash for now, but will likely run through it quickly if it cannot raise more soon, given its outsize spending plans.

The company said it has already signed leases and other deals requiring it to spend $900 billion on computing power and infrastructure in the coming years, including $70 billion in the next 12 months. The IPO provides Anthropic with an equity source of capital as it pursues that spending.

An IPO is essentially a way to raise money instead of going into further debt, and borrowing has gotten more expensive. The Associated Press attributed higher borrowing costs partly to inflation fears and partly to the AI sector's massive cash needs, which have drawn comparisons with the Gilded Age spending splurge on railroads. Lenders fear that AI companies have taken on too much debt to expand while having no clear path to profitability.

The offering also means Anthropic's employees can begin selling vested stock, a payout the Associated Press said would make many of them millionaires. The IPO could give Anthropic substantial resources to accelerate its expansion and potentially add billions of dollars to the fortunes of CEO Dario Amodei and his sister and co-founder, Daniela Amodei.

Formed in 2021 by former OpenAI leaders, Anthropic is locked in a fight for dominance with OpenAI. Anthropic also competes with Google, startups in the United States and China, and SpaceX's AI business. OpenAI is not planning to go public until next year.

Anthropic, OpenAI and SpaceX's AI division have all lost more money than they have made because of the high costs of building AI. Those losses have fueled growing concerns that a bubble is forming in the sector.

In September, Dario Amodei cautioned that the AI industry should slow its fast-moving development to give safety measures time to catch up. Without such a slowdown, Amodei warned that AI could be capable of “letting loose a swarm of agents that could take over the internet within months.”

Anthropic pressed ahead with the IPO despite jitters among some investors over market volatility, possible tighter regulation by a Democratic-led Congress next year and growing anti-AI sentiment among the public. The Associated Press reported that the regulatory environment could change once the new Congress is in session, with newly elected Democrats seeking tighter regulation of the AI industry amid a backlash against data centers. Texas and Georgia, two states that had offered incentives for data-center construction, recently elected Democratic governors who vowed to stop new data-center construction.

Demand for AI shares could drop or collapse as investors confront the sector's massive financing needs. Investors have become anxious about those requirements, and several technology companies have recently pulled their IPO plans. The Associated Press report also cited the ouster of former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang over extramarital affairs, including one involving an alleged Chinese spy, as part of the market turbulence surrounding Anthropic's offering.

The Associated Press said how Anthropic's shares fare after the opening will be the next test for the company. Business writers Bernard Condon, Kaitlyn Huamani and Michelle Chapman contributed to the report.
