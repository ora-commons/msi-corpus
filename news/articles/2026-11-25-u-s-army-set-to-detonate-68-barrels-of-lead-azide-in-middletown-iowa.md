## Army warns dust plume could travel 100 miles northeast into Illinois

The U.S. Army is set to detonate 68 barrels containing nearly 9,500 pounds of lead azide at its plant in Middletown, Iowa, at 10 a.m. Eastern time on Wednesday, according to The Guardian, which is following the operation live. The Army has warned that the blast could release a plume of toxic lead dust extending up to 100 miles northeast, possibly reaching cities such as Wapello and New Boston, Illinois.

The planned disposal has drawn an outpouring of public concern about potential health and environmental effects, and The Guardian reported that the Army changed the date three times before settling on Wednesday. The Guardian described the stockpile as bomb-making chemicals, and the operation remains set for a single window on Wednesday morning.

The town around the plant is small. Middletown, a small town in south-eastern Iowa where the plant is situated, has nearly 400 residents, The Guardian reported. That places the plant's own community at the center of the Army's warning about the dust the detonation could produce.

The warning reaches past the town and past Iowa. The Army said the plume could travel up to 100 miles northeast and named cities such as Wapello and New Boston, Illinois, among the places it could reach, so the communities the service has warned about are not confined to the plant's own state.

The Army's warning is written in conditional terms, and the condition is a projection rather than a measurement. The service said the blast could release a plume of toxic lead dust, and the 100-mile distance is the top of the range the service described. Whether the dust travels that far, or at all beyond the plant, is what the operation itself will show.

State officials have organized around the operation. Iowa Gov. Kim Reynolds, a Republican, activated the state emergency management office last week, and the state created a website for information about the detonation.
