## October inflation rose to 4% ahead of a Commerce Department inflation measure

The Conference Board’s consumer confidence index rose to 103.3 in November from 91.9 in October as gasoline prices continued to decline and election-related uncertainty ended. It was the second straight increase after the index fell in September to its lowest level since April 2014, a reading lower than during the pandemic.

The increase also reflected steep political divisions, according to the survey. Confidence rose sharply among Democrats and independents but declined modestly among Republicans.

Gas prices continued their quick decline, and consumer confidence is closely linked to gas prices. AAA said the average nationwide price at the pump fell to $3.61 a gallon on Tuesday, down 17 cents from a week earlier and sharply below the $4.30 average recorded a month earlier. Prices nevertheless remained higher than they usually are at this time of year, when prices typically fall. The $3.61 average also remained well above the $3.07 motorists paid at this time last year.

“Write-in responses in November were slightly less negative than previous months,” Fergus McCormick, senior economist at the Conference Board, said. “While consumers continued to express frustration with high prices and rising costs, some comments cited hope for the general economy.”

Survey respondents also were more optimistic about the number of jobs available. The improvement came after an unexpectedly positive jobs report for October, when the unemployment rate ticked down to a low 4.1% from 4.2% as hiring accelerated. The [raw FRED UNRATE series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/UNRATE) also showed the official unemployment rate at 4.1% as of Nov. 24, 2026.

Inflation remained elevated and a persistent source of irritation for most Americans. The United States government reported earlier this month that inflation increased to 4% in October, up from 3.7% in September. The Associated Press reported that elevated inflation was a big factor in Democrats’ sweeping victories in the midterm elections earlier this month.

The Commerce Department was scheduled to release on Wednesday a separate inflation measure closely watched by the Federal Reserve. The measure was expected to show inflation remaining noticeably above the Fed’s 2% annual target.

The confidence increase could signal a pickup in consumer spending in the months ahead. Americans had been spending at a solid pace this year, even as consumer surveys remained lackluster. Measures of Americans’ economic outlook have been unusually gloomy particularly since the pandemic, and economists say the surveys are less reliable indicators of consumer behavior than they once were.

The reading could also be a good sign for retailers as winter holiday shopping gets underway. The National Retail Federation forecast sales would rise at a healthy clip over the holidays despite stubbornly high inflation, with prices rising faster than average weekly paychecks.
