## One Libyan official said at least 10 people were wounded

Two Libyan officials said Ukrainian sea drones severely damaged the Russian corvette at Benghazi Naval Base in eastern Libya on Sunday. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said Monday that Ukrainian forces deployed seven domestically made sea drones in the surprise assault on the vessel moored in Libya and that the drones sank the navy vessel in the Mediterranean. One of the officials said at least 10 people, including Libyan and Russian nationals, were wounded, while Russian officials made no immediate comment.

Zelenskyy described the corvette in a post on X as one of Russia's newest vessels, saying it was commissioned a year after Moscow launched the 2022 invasion of Ukraine. The operation was conducted some 2,400 kilometers (1,500 miles) from the front line in Ukraine and was the latest long-range assault by Kyiv's forces. Ukraine's Security Service, or SBU, identified the vessel as the multipurpose corvette Merkury and said it regularly escorted Russian naval convoys, including guarding the dry cargo ship MV Angara.

Libyan officials briefed on the attack said at least four drones were intercepted while two hit the corvette. The corvette is designed to patrol coastal waters and escort other ships. The officials were affiliated with the self-styled Libyan National Army, which controls eastern and southern Libya, and spoke on condition of anonymity because they were not allowed to talk with reporters.

Residents of Benghazi heard loud explosions and saw flames at the base early Sunday. Civil-defense units rushed to the site and extinguished the fire, authorities in eastern Libya said. A statement said the vessel had docked to refuel and take on supplies under a cooperation agreement between Russia and the eastern-based government in Libya, but the statement did not identify the vessel or its cargo.

Ukraine's Security Service described the operation as "unique." The SBU said MV Angara was sanctioned and used to transport Russian military equipment, that sea drones were delivered to a designated location and launched from a so-called "mothership" near the target, and that a Ka-27 helicopter aboard the corvette also went under. Zelenskyy separately said Ukrainian forces struck the Russian dry cargo ship MV Angara, which he said Moscow used to transport weapons, and destroyed the Ka-27 military helicopter. The SBU said Russian defenses tried from the air, sea and land to hit the attacking drones. The SBU also said: "Ukraine's defense forces have once again shown that the Russian navy and its logistics have no safe routes, even far beyond the Black Sea."

The government in eastern Libya sent a letter to U.N. Secretary-General António Guterres and the U.N. Security Council, calling for an international investigation into the potential use of western Libya to prepare and launch Sunday's attack. According to Libyan officials, Ukrainian forces were operating in western Libya under a covert deal endorsed by the West, using the territory to strike a Russian tanker in the Mediterranean in March. The officials said most Ukrainian personnel were drone experts and were based mainly at an air base in the coastal city of Misrata at the time, with other military facilities in the capital Tripoli and the coastal town Zawiya.

Hours after the warship attack, another sea drone strike hit a Maltese-flagged commercial vessel sailing off Benghazi, the Libyan coast guard said. Ambrey, a U.K.-based maritime security and risk intelligence firm, said some explosions at Benghazi Naval Base were likely caused by the detonation of ammunition aboard the Russian warship.

At a live video address Monday to a meeting of the NATO Parliamentary Assembly in Berlin, Zelenskyy said Ukraine has hit Russian oil refineries as far away as western Siberia and Russian warships in the Mediterranean. He described Sunday's attack as "exemplary" and said "the targets we set were achieved." Zelenskyy also said: "We continue our operations in designated waters. We are responding to Russia's aggression. We are developing Ukrainian weapons." Separately, Zelenskyy said: "We keep finding ways to get back on our feet after every Russian attack."

The Associated Press reported that in June last year, Ukraine's Operation Spiderweb used drones launched from trucks to hit over 40 aircraft parked near air bases across Russia. Along the roughly 1,250-kilometer (almost 800-mile) front line through eastern and southern Ukraine, drones and ground robots are helping hold back Russia's bigger army, the report said.

The report also said Russia's strategic bombing had devastated Ukraine's power grid ahead of winter and damaged civilian buildings. A Russian strike on a five-story apartment block in a village near Kyiv killed 11 people, regional police said Monday. Rescue services said a 7-year-old child and a 79-year-old woman were pulled from the rubble after the Sunday morning attack.
