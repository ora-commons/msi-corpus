**Vance reframes Trump's messaging.** In the lead item, Vice President JD Vance's public responses to two of President Trump's recent statements were detailed by reporters Natalie Andrews and Philip Wegmann. When Trump said he was "proudly banning" certain news outlets from the White House, Vance cast it as a reasonable step to withhold access. When Trump promised Americans $5,000 if they keep Republicans in power, Vance went on Fox News to explain what he meant. The reframing is part of a broader role Vance has been carving out, smoothing the harder edges on Trump's words or expanding on them, as he travels the country campaigning for Republican candidates and appealing to critics on the right ahead of the midterms. With the coalition that elected Trump in 2024 now fraying, Vance has been working to mend fences for the midterms and beyond.

**Krasner faces legal scrutiny.** Larry Krasner, one of the nation's most prominent progressive prosecutors, is facing potential legal trouble stemming from a flawed filing from his office backing a new trial for Dennis Johnson, a man convicted of murder who has maintained his innocence, reporter Scott Calvert wrote. Johnson's public defenders have worked with the Pennsylvania Innocence Project to clear him. The filing was assigned to a prosecutor just four months into the job by a supervisor who allegedly told her, "If it's good enough for the Innocence Project, it's good enough for me." After major errors emerged, U.S. District Judge Paul Diamond found that Krasner embraced a plan to blame the junior prosecutor and warned deputies of "consequences" if they alerted the court to internal discord. Krasner said he disagreed with Diamond's findings and that he plans to appeal.

**Social Security dependent benefit.** As more Americans have children later in life, a growing number are discovering a Social Security dependent benefit that pays children up to half of a parent or grandparent's full retirement-age benefit until they turn 18, or 19 if still in high school, reporter Dalvin Brown wrote. The little-marketed benefit is altering the traditional math around when to claim retirement benefits, weighing earlier payouts against bigger lifetime checks.

**Ford's hawk patrols Hermosillo plant.** At Ford Motor's massive assembly plant in Hermosillo, Mexico, a persistent pigeon invasion was posing a health risk to workers, disrupting daily operations, and threatening damage to freshly painted pickup trucks and SUVs. After fake "predator" eyes and artificial bird sounds failed, plant managers turned to falconry, bringing in a two-foot-tall Harris's hawk. Patrolling the skies above the 12.1-million-square-foot facility, the hawk has logged over 300 flights and reduced bird activity by 80%.

**Solar capacity in Trump-won states.** The share of all new solar capacity installed in the first half of the year went to states Trump won in 2024, according to the Solar Energy Industries Association. Those areas also represented eight of the top 10 states for new installations, showing that the solar industry is growing rapidly in those regions despite the administration's disdain for clean energy and a recent bill gutting incentives.

**Workplace reviews evolve.** The traditional once-a-year review is quickly becoming a thing of the past for managers who work with 20-somethings. Used to the instant feedback of digital life, many younger employees feel disoriented and anxious when left guessing where they stand. That has managers leaning on consultants and AI bots to adjust their delivery, with bosses role-playing with machines just to learn how to tell a junior employee, "Let's connect."
