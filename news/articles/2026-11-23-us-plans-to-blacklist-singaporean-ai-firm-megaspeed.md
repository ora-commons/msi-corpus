## A former executive's China allegations intensified scrutiny of chip diversion

The US Department of Commerce announced Monday that it planned to add Megaspeed Ltd to a list of entities blacklisted from buying or selling goods in the United States. The Singaporean company is mainly a developer of data centers.

If Commerce completes the addition, Megaspeed would be barred from buying or selling goods in the United States. The scope would cover goods generally rather than only Nvidia's advanced AI computer chips. Commerce's planned action targets Megaspeed's US goods trade while the scrutiny focuses on a former executive and suspected chip transfers to China.

BBC News technology reporter Kali Hays reported that a former Megaspeed executive was accused of working as a spy for China. The accusation was one of the developments surrounding Commerce's planned listing of the company.

Hays also reported that the former executive had a romantic relationship with Nvidia head Jensen Huang. The BBC report said the relationship led to additional scrutiny by US officials.

That scrutiny heightened concern that Megaspeed was funneling Nvidia's advanced AI computer chips to China. The chip-diversion allegation concerned the advanced artificial-intelligence hardware at the center of the Nvidia scrutiny.

The proposed restriction would place a broad trade limit on a company facing allegations involving espionage and suspected Nvidia chip diversion to China. If the listing is finalized, the immediate consequence for Megaspeed would be exclusion from US goods transactions.
