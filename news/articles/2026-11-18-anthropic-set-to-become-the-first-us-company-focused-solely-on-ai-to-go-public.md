## Some investor concern about revenue and profits accompanies Anthropic's expected $94 billion raise

Anthropic said it will price shares at $83 each when trading begins Thursday on Nasdaq, the New York-based technology stock exchange. The offering would put the company at a valuation of at least $1.4 trillion (£1.1 trillion). The BBC said the price would make Anthropic one of the most highly valued companies in the world. Its Nasdaq debut would make it the first US business focused exclusively on AI to go public.

Earlier in November, Anthropic said it was likely to offer shares for between $70 and $78 each, valuing the company at up to $1.3 trillion. One early investor in Anthropic who spoke to the BBC on condition of anonymity said shares were more than 10 times oversubscribed, meaning demand exceeded the number available. If accurate, that demand could push the valuation beyond the company's target when trading begins.

The listing will bring in more than $68 billion from initial investors, while Anthropic expects another $26 billion from private placements, for a total expected raise of $94 billion. The amount of cash Anthropic has raised from its listing is already more than SpaceX raised in June.

Depending on the strength of demand, Anthropic could exceed Elon Musk's SpaceX early market valuation of $2.2 trillion from its June stock debut, which the BBC described as the world's most highly valued stock debut. SpaceX shares fell significantly in the weeks after that listing.

OpenAI, the maker of ChatGPT and Anthropic's biggest rival, is expected to go public next year. Until then, Anthropic would be the only US public company founded and built around AI tools and products for consumers and businesses. Nearly every other major tech company is pouring huge amounts of money into the technology and infrastructure required for their own AI tools. These companies are built around social media, online retail or online search, with AI tools and applications secondary.

The BBC described excitement around Anthropic's listing but also some concern from investors about the company's ability to generate revenue and profits going forward.

Yang Wang, a financial analyst with Counterpoint Research, said the central issue for Anthropic is whether its business "can keep pace" as hundreds of billions of dollars in financial obligations come due in the next couple of years.

"Nevertheless, it is not an everyday occurrence for public investors to gain direct exposure to a frontier-model developer with the potential to shape the next stage of productivity," Wang said.

Marta Norton, who heads investment strategy at investment firm Empower, said Anthropic's public listing would provide a new view of demand for the technology. "Anthropic going public gives us a whole new window into what AI demand looks like," Norton told the BBC.

Norton said the timing, amid market volatility, comes down to the company's need for more money. "AI is financially demanding," Norton said. "The public markets open up the financial spigot a little bit more, and so the time has come."

Anthropic was started by a group of co-founders led by siblings Dario Amodei and Daniela Amodei, all of whom left their work at OpenAI to start the AI firm. The BBC described the seven Anthropic founders as about to become multi-billionaires. Dario Amodei is Anthropic's chief executive.

Anthropic has distinguished itself within the AI industry by emphasizing safety. The company refused a US Department of Defense demand that its tools be used in an essentially unrestricted way. Dario Amodei has also said AI tools may soon become powerful enough to present a threat to human existence.

Anthropic is seeking to grow quickly and obtain more access to financing through public markets. Broader anxieties have also developed around the AI industry's business model, which relies on expensive, in-demand computer chips, controversial data centers and agreements with other technology companies.

Minutes from an October Federal Reserve Bank meeting about a potential rate rise showed concern about the rapid growth of AI-related debt financed through "complex and opaque" structures. A few participants also noted that "AI-related exposures were becoming increasingly concentrated among a small number of firms."
