## California attorney general serves investigative subpoena on Nvidia

## Some US lawmakers urge Commerce probe into whether Megaspeed diverted Nvidia chips to China

California's attorney general Rob Bonta, the state's top law enforcement officer, said on Thursday: "Nvidia is in a messy, transnational situation but the underlying principle of our corporate securities law is clear: Companies cannot mislead their investors."

Bonta said his office is probing whether actions by Nvidia or Huang resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information. Such actions could violate California law, he said, if Nvidia were found to have misled investors and the public.

BBC News' North America technology correspondent Lily Jamali reported the subpoena, and the BBC has contacted Nvidia for comment. Nvidia's global headquarters are in Santa Clara, California.

The subpoena came after Huang's departure from Nvidia last week. Huang, the company's longtime chief executive and co-founder, left after reports that he had relationships with subordinates, and has acknowledged engaging in extramarital affairs.

Earlier this month, it emerged that Huang had an extramarital relationship with Alice Huang, a suspected Chinese spy who was an executive at the Singapore-based data centre company Megaspeed. The New York Times reported the affair in early November.

Separately, some members of the US Congress have urged the Department of Commerce to investigate whether Megaspeed, a major Nvidia customer, diverted Nvidia chips to China. Megaspeed has previously said it is aware of reports "containing assertions about our business, supply chain and global operations that are not supported by verifiable evidence".

Earlier this week, Nvidia board chair Harvey Jones said the company had no intention of reinstating Huang after the longtime boss and co-founder issued a statement saying he would be "honoured" to return, as some tech leaders including Elon Musk campaigned on his behalf.

Nvidia's shares have fallen 22% since the New York Times reported the affair in early November.

Bonta's inquiry covers whether actions by Nvidia or Huang resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information; he said such actions could violate California law if Nvidia were found to have misled investors and the public. The congressional request asks whether Megaspeed, a major Nvidia customer, diverted Nvidia chips to China.
