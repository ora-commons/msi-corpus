## Trump leads supporters in self-pledge as Democrats decry event

DALLAS — With his approval rating at a historic low, President Donald Trump closed the Republican Party's first-ever midterm convention on Thursday night with an appeal for supporters to treat the November ballot as a personal referendum. Speaking at a basketball arena in Dallas before about 20,000 supporters, Trump asked voters to "pretend I'm on the ticket" and directed the crowd in an improvised pledge of allegiance to himself.

"Raise your right hand," Trump said, and the audience repeated after him: "I pledge to the greatest president in the history of the United States, that loves us so much he can't even breathe, that I will go out with my family, my friends, I'll do it any way — I don't care if I'm registered or not, I'm going to try to cheat like hell like they do."

The two-day event, the Republican National Committee's first national gathering outside a four-year presidential cycle, featured more than 50 speakers or presentations each night and resembled a Trump mega-rally rather than a traditional party convention, with no nominations or formal business. Supporters wore "Make America Great Again" regalia and waved signs displaying the Stars and Stripes. Republicans believe it has been successful in galvanising the party's base in the face of stiff electoral headwinds, according to the Guardian.

The convention had been aimed at turning around Republicans' fortunes. Opinion polls suggest they are on course to lose the US House of Representatives, and possibly the Senate. With Trump facing a growing backlash over rising prices and the war in Iran, sweeping Republican losses could effectively make Trump a lame duck for his final two years, according to the Guardian.

Trump's closing address was an abridged version of his Wednesday keynote. He reiterated a promise of a "Trump dividend" of $5,000 for every US adult if Republicans win both chambers of Congress, a pledge Democrats have dismissed as a bribe and economists have warned would cost more than $1tn. He also claimed credit for reducing prescription drug prices and asserted that his administration had transformed what he called "that dark age of America" into "the golden age of America."

"Less than two years ago, the American people gave us a mandate to save America – and in 19 months, I've delivered on every single promise I made," he claimed. "They gave us wide open borders with 25 million people pouring in from all over the globe. They turned our cities into scenes of death and misery, murder, poverty and destruction, and made America a laughingstock all over world."

The reduction of prescription drug prices alone "should get us a big victory in our midterms", he suggested, before he turned the microphone over to opera performer Christopher Macchio, who belted out a soaring rendition of America the Beautiful as red, white, blue and golden balloons cascaded down from the ceiling of the basketball arena.

"You gotta do me the biggest favour you've ever done me," Trump told the crowd. "You've gotta do this for me. You've got to pretend I'm on the ticket. I'm on the ticket! I'm on the ticket! He's on the ticket! Pretend I'm on the ticket because this is going to turn out to be one of the most important elections."

The convention drew criticism from Democrats, who dismissed it as a stunt aimed at wealthy donors. At a press conference in Dallas, Rep. Robert Garcia of California told reporters: "What's happening right now is Donald Trump is throwing a two-day party to celebrate himself, so that JD Vance can give a speech, and so his donors, some of whom have paid $800,000 a pop for these ticket packages, can spend two days celebrating themselves."

"This is happening as the American public is struggling," Garcia added. "So as Donald Trump and JD Vance party away for two days, the average American family can't afford groceries, the cost of the rent's going up, they're looking at their gas prices skyrocketing and they're making difficult choices at the dinner table. But Donald Trump does not care about the average American family or about affordability."

Earlier on Thursday, speaker after speaker sought to champion Trump's accomplishments while constantly branding the Democratic Party as extremist and communist.

The president's eldest son, Donald Trump Jr, delivered what the Guardian described as a fiery address setting Republican "common sense" against a "dystopian hellscape" of leftwing ideology.

"As we speak, Democrat candidates across the country are openly embracing the radical ideology of democratic socialism," suggested Trump Jr. "They claim that their socialism is for the people, but that's the exact same story we've heard from every communist in human history."

Describing childhood memories of his grandparents in communist Czechoslovakia, he warned of bread lines and rationing. "Communism is on the march and it's up to us to stop it in its tracks," he said.

Trump Jr framed the Republican platform, including pledges to end taxes on tips, overtime and social security, as a direct response to a "war on the working people" by Democrats. "The choice is clear," he said. "Don't let these radicals gaslight you into thinking common sense is extreme, and extreme is somehow common sense. Don't let them convince you that caring about this country makes you a villain."

Sen. Ted Cruz of Texas warned of a "red-green alliance" between "communists and Islamists" who "hate Christians, hate Jews and hate capitalism." Invoking his father's escape from Cuban communism, Cruz declared that "sharia law has no place in America" and is "fundamentally inconsistent with the constitution."

House Speaker Mike Johnson, of Louisiana, made remarks the Guardian described as carrying a conspiratorial tone, suggesting the nation was being "attacked from within". The Democratic Party is now beholden to radicals, he claimed, warning: "They're the ones who have let the barbarians inside the gate, and next year the communists will be in Congress. That's what's so frightening."

Johnson added that, while big names like Zohran Mamdani and Abdul El-Sayed are known entities, the bigger threat comes from "Democrats in the swing seats who say one thing, and then join forces and vote with the socialists that they're afraid of".

The US health secretary, Robert F Kennedy Jr, took the stage to champion the "Make America Healthy Again" (Maha) platform. The son of a former US attorney general and nephew of John F Kennedy said the "irony of my presence here is inescapable".

"The Democrats of that era stood with labour and working people and the American middle class," Kennedy continued. "Nearly every cop and firefighter was a Democrat … The Democratic party, in short, lost its way."

Speaking less than a mile from the spot where his uncle was assassinated in 1963, Kennedy claimed it was a "fact" that "neither my father, nor my uncle, would recognise the Democratic party of today".

JD Vance, the vice-president, gave the keynote address of the night. He said: "The Democrats are the party of hatred. They're the party that despises America and the people who built it … America belongs to those who love it – not those who hate it."

The political attacks were briefly paused for two sombre interludes.

A tribute marked one year since the assassination of rightwing provocateur and organiser Charlie Kirk. Lucas Miles, a Turning Point USA vice-president, said that while "they could take the man, they could not take the mission". Chants of "Charlie, Charlie" filled the air.

The arena was plunged into darkness as Macchio performed Ave Maria on the eve of the 25th anniversary of the September 11 terrorist attacks. Two beams of light stretched upward from the arena floor, reminiscent of the twin towers in New York, as the crowd held up mobile phone lights. A video narration accompanying the tribute urged the audience to: "Say their names. Honour their sacrifice. Defend Freedom. Never forget September 11."
