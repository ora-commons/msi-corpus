## Proposed reduction would cost the Guernsey Insurance Fund £8m to £9m

Guernsey deputies Gavin St Pier and Tom Rylatt will ask the States of Deliberation next week to temporarily reduce 2027 employee and self-employed social security contributions by 0.5%. BBC News reported that the States would decide whether to set primary Class 1 employee contributions at 7.1% and Class 2 self-employed contributions at 12.1%.

St Pier and Rylatt, both deputies from Forward Guernsey, said they would bring an amendment to the Social Insurance (Rates of Contributions and Benefits etc.) Ordinance, 2026. The amendment seeks a temporary 2027 reduction for primary Class 1 employee contributions and Class 2 self-employed contributions.

St Pier said global events, including the Russia-Ukraine and US-Iran wars, had knock-on effects on Guernsey's economy and "created significant pressures for the whole community."

St Pier also described the existing contribution path. "Social security contributions have been increased each year as part of a 10-year programme of increases," he said. "Without this amendment, since 2021, employee contributions will have increased by 1% and self-employed contributions will have increased by 1.6%."

Rylatt said the amendment would give "breathing room for working islanders." He said the Employment and Social Security 10-year plan emphasized a deliberately flexible approach that could account for economic circumstances and the outcome of the long-debated tax reform.

Rylatt said: "With little sign of cost-of-living pressures easing in 2027, and with tax reform now decided, we believe this coming year is the right time to use that flexibility for the benefit of islanders."

Rylatt put the cost of the amendment to the Guernsey Insurance Fund at between £8m and £9m for 2027. He called the change "a one-off departure from the existing trajectory."

The States of Deliberation will debate the amendment next week. Its decision will determine whether Guernsey temporarily reduces both contribution categories by 0.5% for 2027, giving effect to the relief sought by St Pier and Rylatt at the stated cost to the Guernsey Insurance Fund.
