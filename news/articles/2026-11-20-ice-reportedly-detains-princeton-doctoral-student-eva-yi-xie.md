## Princeton secures legal representation as Xie’s immigration status remains undisclosed

Eva Yi Xie, a third-year PhD candidate at the Princeton Neuroscience Institute, was reportedly detained by U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement on Thursday at a Washington-area airport, according to university officials and her colleagues. The Brody Lab, a Princeton neuroscience research group, identified the site as Ronald Reagan National Airport in a post on X. The lab said Xie was returning from the Society for Neuroscience conference in Washington, D.C., where she presented a research poster, when immigration authorities detained her.

The Daily Princetonian, the university’s student newspaper, reported that Xie was believed to be held at an immigration detention facility in Chantilly, Virginia. Princeton University secured legal representation for Xie, university spokesperson Michael Hotchkiss told The Daily Princetonian. The Guardian said it had reached out to ICE and Princeton for comment. Xie’s current immigration status has not been publicly disclosed.

The detention prompted Rodney Priestley, dean of Princeton’s graduate school, to email graduate students at the Princeton Neuroscience Institute late Thursday night. In the message, obtained by The Daily Princetonian, Priestley acknowledged the situation and outlined the university’s response. “We hope she is able to soon resume her studies at Princeton,” the email read. “Please be assured that we will stay in close contact with the student’s family, her legal team, and her PNI colleagues as this situation is resolved.”

A Princeton University photograph credited to its Office of Communications and Denise Applewhite shows Xie in October 2024. Xie moved to the United States from China in 2016. She earned degrees in computer science and mathematics from the Massachusetts Institute of Technology in 2024, alongside a concentration in economics, according to her portfolio. Her professional experience includes internships at Meta, Microsoft AI and IBM Research, among other organizations. At Princeton, her doctoral work focuses on the “neural basis of decision-making,” according to The Daily Princetonian.

The Guardian wrote: “Xie’s detention comes amid an aggressive nationwide immigration enforcement campaign under Donald Trump, whose administration has made mass deportations a defining priority.” Department of Homeland Security figures showed that ICE deported 483,200 people and conducted more than 491,000 immigration arrests during fiscal year 2026, setting new annual records for the agency.

The Trump administration maintained that its enforcement efforts were intended to uphold immigration laws and remove dangerous offenders from the country. Federal figures and independent reviews cited by The Guardian indicated that substantial numbers of people kept in ICE detention had no criminal convictions. The Guardian said the enforcement campaign had affected international students and immigrants who originally entered the United States legally.

The Guardian wrote: “Xie’s detention now joins a string of prominent immigration cases that have drawn worldwide scrutiny and raised serious questions about the administration’s tactics.”

Among those cases was Rümeysa Öztürk, a Tufts University student detained by immigration agents in Massachusetts last year. Videos of Öztürk’s arrest spread rapidly online, drawing widespread attention to the administration’s treatment of international students. The Guardian reported that Öztürk was among numerous foreign students “targeted over pro-Palestinian expression and protest” during demonstrations against Israel’s military campaign in Gaza. The outlet said those protests had been “particularly prominent on American university campuses.”

Another case involved Mahmoud Khalil, a 30-year-old Palestinian permanent resident and Columbia University graduate student who “emerged as a leading figure in campus protests last year.” The Guardian reported: “Khalil was arrested without due process in front of his pregnant wife and subsequently detained at an immigration facility.” Authorities denied him permission to leave detention to attend the birth of his child.

The Guardian reported that the administration’s enforcement policies also came under scrutiny after Kilmar Ábrego García, a Salvadoran immigrant who had been living in Maryland, was deported from the United States to El Salvador in March 2025. The Trump administration acknowledged that the removal was an error.
