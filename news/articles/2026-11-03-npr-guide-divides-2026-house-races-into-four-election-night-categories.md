## Kentucky and Indiana were expected to yield the first House results with national implications

An election-night guide by NPR's Stephen Fowler divides House contests into four groups: districts with early poll closings, races that drew heavy spending in the final week, seats reshaped by redistricting and contests that test Republican performance in conservative communities. NPR used the guide to distinguish a good night for Democrats from a broad wave involving dozens of House seats changing hands. Democrats appeared poised to retake control of the House on Tuesday, while Republicans entered Election Day with a narrow 218-214 seat advantage.

NPR characterized Republicans as facing “severe headwinds” as the party in power. The cycle also included a record number of departures from Congress, while polls showed voters were not happy about the economy and the ongoing war in Iran. A previous NPR analysis found both parties spending heavily across a competitive landscape spanning 70 districts, ranging from longtime toss-ups to newly competitive seats in deeply conservative areas.

NPR said the first results with national implications were likely to come from Kentucky's 5th Congressional District (KY-06) and Indiana's 5th (IN-5), where polls close first in the 6 p.m. ET hour. Trump's 2024 margins were 15 points in Kentucky and 17 in Indiana, NPR said, and the margins could preview “how much voters have soured on Republicans.”

In Kentucky, Democrats and Republicans alike had spent heavily in the race where Democrat Zach Dembo faced Republican Ralph Alvarado. NPR said the increased investment was worth noting as a factor in the outcome. If Democrats flip one or both races, NPR said, “then it will likely be a long night for the GOP.”

Florida also counts and reports votes quickly and is home to more than half a dozen House races viewed as toss-ups or as slightly favoring one party or the other, NPR said. NPR said projected winners in those districts would provide more insight into other races across the country. NPR's other early-count watch list included FL-27, NC-11, SC-01 and VA-01.

## Redrawn maps changed the competitive picture

NPR said this year's Supreme Court decision rolling back part of the Voting Rights Act led many Republican-led states to redraw House maps to eliminate districts that typically favored Democrats. The guide said this election year was not typical: the political environment had deteriorated enough to put three Democratic-held seats into contention.

AL-02, held by Democrat Shomari Figures; OH-09, held by Democrat Marcy Kaptur; and NC-01, held by Democrat Don Davis, were described as toss-ups at best and, at worst for Republicans, as favoring Democrats. In Texas, Latino voters appeared poised to swing back to Democratic candidates, NPR said. NPR said the movement could prevent Republican Eric Flores from flipping the seat held by Rep. Vicente Gonzalez in TX-34 or prevent Tano Tijerina from unseating Democratic incumbent Henry Cuellar in TX-28. Other redistricting watch races included CA-22, FL-09, FL-14 and TX-35.

## Final-week advertising concentrated in a small set of races

AdImpact data showed that a handful of House races received more than $10 million in advertising during the final seven days before the midterms. NPR said the concentration showed where both parties considered the idea that turnout would decide the race more than a cliché. In MI-07, progressive Democrat William Lawrence challenged incumbent Republican Tom Barrett in a seat Trump carried by just over 1% in 2024. In the open MI-10, Democrat Christina Hines faced Republican Mike Bouchard.

New York Republican Rep. Mike Lawler could be one of the rare Republicans to hold NY-17, NPR said, thanks in part to the party's advertising advantage in the closing stretch against Democrat Cait Conley. Three of Iowa's four House races — IA-01, IA-02 and IA-03 — were among the most expensive, partly because competitive governor and U.S. Senate races on the ballot were driving turnout. NPR also listed CO-08, PA-01, WA-03 and WI-03 among the races to watch.

Like the Kentucky contest, both parties had spent significant sums in the open FL-22 race between Democrat Pia Dandiya and Republican Casey Askar and in the newly redrawn FL-25 contest between Democratic incumbent Rep. Jared Moskowitz and Republican Scott Singer.

## Conservative contests tested Democratic support in GOP districts

NPR said one question was how far increased Democratic support could extend into conservative communities. In Arkansas's 2nd district, AR-02, incumbent Republican Rep. French Hill faced Democrat Chris Jones, whom NPR described as a well-known challenger, after the contest received a surprising amount of advertising attention. By the time polls closed, spending in the Little Rock-based seat was expected to come closer to $10 million over the final months. Trump carried the district by 15 points in 2024.

NPR also said many districts in states that redrew their maps were places where "the Republican gerrymander could backfire"; Ohio was an example. In OH-10, held by Republican Mike Turner, and OH-15, held by Republican Mike Carey, Trump won by closer to 10 points in 2024. Both districts are in a state with competitive governor and Senate races and stretch between the suburbs and exurbs of Dayton and Columbus, with an electorate that could swing away from the GOP, NPR said.

North Carolina's Senate race provided another possible guide. Former Gov. Roy Cooper was expected to win against former Republican National Committee Chair Michael Whatley, and NPR said the margin could determine whether NC-03 was also in play. Rep. Greg Murphy's district absorbed more Democratic-leaning voters after the latest map redraw. The remaining outer-edge races on NPR's list were NC-09, NY-21, TN-09 and WA-05.

Taken together, these contests offered separate ways to read whether Democratic gains would be broad or limited. The guide used early timing, late spending, map changes and Republican performance in conservative districts to interpret the results; the size of any House majority was not settled as Election Day began.
