## Independent candidates renew threat to NSW Coalition's north shore seats

## Minns backs Massa as his government fast-tracks Scruby's electorate upgrade

The slate spans Sydney's north shore and the northern beaches. Amelia Loye plans to announce on the weekend that she is running in Willoughby, Elouise Massa will contest Manly, Michael Randall announced last weekend that he will run in North Shore, and Monica Wulff has announced a run in Davidson. Anne Davies's Guardian analysis described the challenge as renewed but so far under the radar, arriving as the NSW Coalition tries to shore up its right flank against a surging One Nation before the March state election.

NSW Labor Premier Chris Minns has appeared with Massa and praised her advocacy, while the Minns government fast-tracks independent Jacqui Scruby's request for an electorate upgrade. The NSW Liberal party has said it will take its case directly to voters.

## The candidate slate

This weekend, Amelia Loye, a management consultant who specialises in stakeholder and community engagement, will announce that she is running in Willoughby on Sydney's north shore. She is seeking to unseat Liberal MP Tim James, who holds the seat on a 2.6% margin.

Loye is backed by community group Willoughby Votes, which came close to securing an independent in the 2023 election with 47% of the two-party preferred vote. “Willoughby has already shown it is ready for an independent,” Loye said. “Nearly half of voters backed one last time. I want to earn their support, and the support of those still deciding.”

Elouise Massa, a small business owner, became a household name after campaigning for the Northern Beaches hospital to return to public hands after her toddler's death in emergency. She will run in the Sydney seat of Manly against Liberal Adelaide Cuneo, who is seeking to replace the retiring MP James Griffin.

Michael Randall, the independent deputy mayor of Mosman, announced last weekend that he will run in North Shore as a community independent. Several of the people behind former independent federal MP Kylea Tink's campaign are supporting him. Monica Wulff, another independent, has announced a run in Davidson.

Voters on the northern beaches have already sent two independents to Macquarie Street, and The Guardian assessed that both have a good chance of being returned. At the federal level, teal independents Sophie Scamps, Zali Steggall and Nicolette Boele hold McKellar, Warringah and Bradfield respectively. Tink held North Sydney for one term before the seat was abolished.

## A renewed challenge to the Coalition

Anne Davies wrote in The Guardian that the New South Wales Coalition faces a renewed threat to its heartland seats on Sydney's north shore. This time, the challengers are independent candidates, and they are backed by the same community activists who put a swathe of teal independents into federal parliament. Davies said the challenge had so far remained under the radar as the NSW Coalition tried to shore up its right flank against a surging One Nation before the March state election.

Her analysis said the effort had produced a lurch to the right on issues ranging from gun control to law and order and, in the case of the NSW Nationals, abandonment of climate targets altogether. The move to the right created an opportunity for progressive independents running in urban seats usually held by Liberal moderates, with several candidates about to launch their campaigns.

Former opposition leader Mark Speakman resigned from the Liberals on Thursday, joining an exodus of MPs who have announced they will not contest their seats in March. He warned his party against becoming “One Nation lite.”

## Government arithmetic

The independents have gained a boost in the form of NSW Labor premier Chris Minns. During the final weeks of parliament before the NSW election, Minns joked that independent Jacqui Scruby, who won Pittwater at a byelection, should be known as “the member for Mona Vale road” for her tenacity in seeking an upgrade. The Minns government is now fast-tracking that request.

Anne Davies, who wrote the Guardian analysis, described Minns as a shrewd political operator and said he had not fallen in love with independents but was “hedging his bets” rather than embracing them. She noted that a year earlier, with the Liberals languishing in the polls, Labor could have expected to increase its majority in 2027. One Nation's primary vote has since reached as high as 30% in some polls, dimming that prospect.

Labor's minority government currently relies on three independents — Greg Piper, Joe McGirr and Alex Greenwich — for confidence and supply. Davies said an expanded crossbench would give the government more options and provide a buffer against Labor losses to One Nation in western Sydney, the Hunter and possibly the Central Coast.

If all the independent candidates were successful — “and that is a big if” — almost the entire north shore region would go to state independents, except for Wahroonga, Davies wrote. She said a teal wave also remained a threat to the Liberal party, particularly as the Coalition positioned themselves to fight One Nation in the regions with policies that do not sit well with urban voters.

Davies noted that most commentary about the 2027 election had focused on the likely Armageddon facing the Nationals, who could lose as many as eight or nine of their 11 seats in the regions. Disclosure: Davies worked as a media and policy adviser to the independent federal MP Sophie Scamps from October 2023 to October 2024.

## Preference mechanics

One Nation could also prove helpful to independents if it makes good on its promise to run candidates in every seat, Davies said. The party is more likely to shave votes from the Liberal primary, making it easier for independents to overtake the Liberal candidate on preferences from Labor and the Greens.

NSW's optional preferential voting makes it hard to predict how strong preference flows will be, and those flows depend on what parties advocate about how to vote. In 2023, only 41% of One Nation voters nominated preferences, compared with 70% of Green voters and 49% of Labor voters.

## Party responses

The NSW Liberal party has not given up on the northern beaches and the north shore despite the trend toward independents. A spokesperson said: “We'll keep making our case directly to voters, including why we oppose Labor's gun buyback that targets law-abiding owners rather than criminals and terrorists.”

NSW opposition leader Kellie Sloane has not ruled out either a preference deal or a post-election arrangement with One Nation, describing the options as “hypothetical.” Davies warned that if the Coalition ends up preferencing One Nation, it may alienate moderate Liberals and ultimately work against them.
