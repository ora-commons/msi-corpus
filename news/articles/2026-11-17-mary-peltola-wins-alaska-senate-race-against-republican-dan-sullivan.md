## Mary Peltola wins Alaska Senate race against Republican Dan Sullivan

## Justice Department plans appeal after judge rejects certification challenge

NPR reported on November 17, 2026, that The Associated Press called Alaska's U.S. Senate contest for Mary Peltola, who defeated Republican incumbent Dan S. Sullivan. The former Democratic representative's victory means Democrats will have 56 senators in the upcoming Congress, and NPR described the race as one of the country's most closely watched Senate campaigns.

Peltola's victory did not depend on Alaska's ranked-choice process. She won a majority of Alaskans' first-place votes, meaning no ranked-choice calculation was required. Preliminary results also showed that Peltola had more votes than the other three candidates combined as of Sunday.

The win flips a Senate seat in a state President Trump carried in each of the last three presidential elections. Trump won Alaska by 13 percentage points in 2024.

Peltola is from Bethel, a western Alaska hub city of about 7,000 that is not connected to the state's limited road system. Her victory makes her the first Democrat to win an Alaska Senate race since 2008 and the first Alaska Native to serve in the U.S. Senate.

Peltola campaigned mostly on the themes that Democrats around the United States focused on: affordability, health care costs and accusations of flagrant Republican corruption. NPR noted that the cost-of-living argument had particular salience in rural Alaska, where groceries and gas can cost double or triple what they cost elsewhere.

Peltola campaigned under the motto "fish, family, freedom." She spoke of the salmon crisis on the Yukon and Kuskokwim rivers, an issue that propelled her to statewide prominence in her first U.S. House race in 2022. She also pitched herself as the candidate who would crack down on industrial-scale pollock fishing in the Bering Sea. NPR reported that Alaskans increasingly blamed that industry for catching and wasting salmon, halibut and other species.

Republican incumbent Dan S. Sullivan is a Marine Corps veteran who was elected to the Senate in 2014 and previously served as Alaska's attorney general. He has advocated augmenting the military and Coast Guard presence in Alaska and has been a steadfast ally of Trump. President Trump flew to Wasilla in the final month of the campaign to hold a rally for Sullivan.

NPR reported that Trump's approval ratings in Alaska and nationally had plunged since 2024, driven largely by opposition to the Iran war and concern about high prices. The report said the election results suggested that unpopularity had been a drag on Republican incumbents.

The final days of the race were roiled by allegations reported by The New York Times that Peltola's team was involved in efforts to recruit Dan J. Sullivan to try to siphon votes from the incumbent. Peltola's campaign denied involvement in the second Sullivan's candidacy. NPR said it had not independently confirmed the reporting.

A federal judge rejected a lawsuit from the Trump administration that sought to block certification of Alaska's election results if incumbent Sullivan lost. The Justice Department plans to appeal the dismissal.

In the lawsuit, the department asked the judge to stop state officials from certifying the results, allowing time for an investigation into whether voters were confused because two candidates named Dan Sullivan appeared on Alaska's ranked-choice ballot. The judge concluded that such an order would "clearly violate" Alaskan voters' right to a secret ballot. Alaska's governor also ordered an investigation into the race.

Ballot stacking and the promotion of spoiler candidates are generally legal in Alaska and have been relatively common. Super PACs aligned with Republicans and Democrats spent more than $2 million on "ballot composition" efforts in Alaska this year, sometimes elevating people who did not live in Alaska, including a federal inmate in New York. The Trump administration, incumbent Sullivan and other Republicans claimed the goal of Dan J. Sullivan's candidacy was to trick voters.

The Justice Department said in a statement: "Alaskan voters deserve a fair election, not to be misled in an attempt to rig outcomes." NPR reported that preliminary results nevertheless suggested the other candidates had not acted as spoilers, with Peltola receiving more votes than the other three candidates combined as of Sunday. The Justice Department had announced its plan to appeal, and Alaska's governor had ordered an investigation into the race.
