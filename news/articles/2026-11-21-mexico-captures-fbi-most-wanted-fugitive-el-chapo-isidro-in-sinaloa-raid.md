## Two other alleged gang leaders seized after gun battle in Guasave

Mexican special forces arrested Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as "El Chapo Isidro," during a raid in the state of Sinaloa on Friday night, according to Mexico's defence ministry. The ministry described him as the alleged leader of the Meza Flores Transnational Criminal Organization, a drug-trafficking group, and said he had evaded capture for more than a decade.

Two other alleged leaders of the gang were taken in the same operation: Meza Flores's alleged second-in-command, Germán N, whose alias is "Serafín," and José N, described by the ministry as an alleged financial operator for the Beltrán Leyva organization.

The ministry said the operation was carried out by "elite military forces" with help from US intelligence. After the arresting force arrived in the "target area," the ministry said, it came under attack "with gunfire and explosives" but managed to enter a house and make the arrests. The men were then evacuated to Mexico City on a Mexican air force plane to be handed over to prosecutors.

The defence ministry also alleged that Meza Flores leads the Beltrán Leyva criminal organization, which is allied to the Mayos, one of two rival factions in the Sinaloa cartel. Video posted by Mexican authorities shows police leading a man away.

The gun battle spread panic in Guasave. Videos and photographs show civilians fleeing gunfire at a baseball game at Kuroda Park stadium in the city, as well as burnt-out cars. According to CNN, a barrage of rapid fire from what appeared to be Mexican security force helicopters was directed at the ground, and players from the Venados de Mazatlán and Algodoneros de Guasave teams abandoned the field mid-game.

The FBI's "most wanted" poster states that Meza Flores's organization "is allegedly responsible for the possession, distribution, and importation of large quantities of heroin, methamphetamine, cocaine, fentanyl, and marijuana into the United States, and has predominately controlled drug production, transportation, and distribution in several locations throughout Mexico." The poster was accompanied by a $5m reward offer, or £3.8m at the Guardian's conversion.

Meza Flores was originally indicted in 2012. In 2013 the US Treasury placed sanctions on him under the Kingpin Act, freezing his assets. In 2017 the US Department of State's Narcotics Rewards Program began offering up to $5m for information leading directly to his arrest or conviction.

He has been charged in the United States with multiple counts of drug-trafficking violations and with use and possession of a firearm, and he was wanted in Mexico for organized crime and offences against public health. All of the trafficking and leadership descriptions attached to him in the ministry's and FBI's accounts are allegations; the raid produced arrests, not verdicts.
