## A 1971 Central Park gust of 63 mph grounded the parade's balloons

The history of Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade includes a 1997 accident in which heavy winds pushed a "Cat in the Hat" balloon into a lamppost. The lamppost fell and injured four spectators, and one person was in a coma for 24 days.

Early in the parade's history, crews released balloons into the sky at the end of the event, and Macy's offered a $100 reward for their safe return. The retailer said at least one balloon, a giant blue hippopotamus, was never found. The practice ended in the 1930s after a 22-year-old student aviator tried to capture a cat-shaped balloon with her biplane and plunged into a dive over Queens. Today, volunteers deflate the inflatables after the parade.

The parade's giant balloons have been grounded only once, in 1971, when a 63 mph gust was recorded in Central Park. The celebration itself has been canceled three times, in 1942, 1943 and 1944, because of a rubber and helium shortage during World War II.

Macy's estimates more than 3.5 million people watch the parade in person each year, and 35 million more tune into its 3.5-hour broadcast. Revelers have at times withstood extreme weather to attend in person, including a frigid 2018 parade day when temperatures dropped as low as 17 degrees.

The 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade brings thousands of visitors to the city each year. Its 2.5-mile route runs from Manhattan's Upper West Side to Macy's flagship store in Midtown, and marching bands and other performers come from all around the country to participate. This year's lineup is set to feature 100 inflatables, including a 33-foot-long Hello Kitty and a "Despicable Me" minion riding a giant banana, in addition to the mainstays: intricate floats, high-school marching bands and themed clowns.

Macy's moved this year's celebration to Saturday over safety concerns as a powerful Nor'easter threatened the city with high winds. The National Weather Service expects winds of roughly 25 to 35 mph in the area on Thanksgiving, with gusts reaching up to 60 mph. The giant balloons are permitted to fly only when winds are under 23 mph and gusts do not exceed 34 mph.

Macy's said Wednesday, "The safety of our participants and spectators is our top priority and after close consultation with our city and agency partners, we have made the decision to move this year's celebration." The National Weather Service warned that the winds could topple trees and power lines, with widespread power outages expected.
