## Authorities said Collier County landfill compaction made Cozzi's recovery nearly impossible

Pinellas Circuit Judge Joseph Bulone sentenced plastic surgeon Tomasz Kosowski to death on Thursday for the March 2023 killing of attorney Steven Cozzi in the bathroom of Cozzi's law office. Cozzi's body was never found after investigators tracked Kosowski to the Tamiami Trail west of Miami.

Bulone was tasked with deciding whether to sentence Kosowski to death or to life in prison without the possibility of release.

Kosowski declined to present any mitigating evidence on his own behalf, but a special counsel appointed by the court highlighted his lack of criminal history and his medical work. A graduate of Dartmouth College medical school, Kosowski specialized in reconstructive breast surgery.

Bulone acknowledged in court that there were several mitigating factors but said none outweighed the aggravating factors, most importantly that the crime was committed in a “cold, calculating and premeditated manner, without any pretense of moral or legal justification.”

“The court concludes that the defendant, under the laws of the State of Florida, has forfeited his right to live,” Bulone said.

Bulone appointed the public defender's office to represent Kosowski in his appeals.

Investigators used cellphone records and surveillance video to track Kosowski to a remote area west of Miami on the Tamiami Trail, also known as U.S. Highway 41. They believe Kosowski tossed Cozzi's body into a Dumpster in the Everglades that was eventually emptied by a garbage truck. The driver noticed an unusually “vile” smell at the stop, authorities said.

Trash from the route is typically hauled to a Collier County landfill. Authorities who searched the property for Cozzi's body said the facility routinely compacts its trash, making recovery efforts nearly impossible.

Investigators said Cozzi's blood and DNA were found in the law-office bathroom and in the garage at Kosowski's home.

Kosowski was convicted in September after representing himself and presenting no defense beyond a closing argument that prosecutors had not proved their case.

Prosecutors said Kosowski killed Cozzi moments after the two men participated in a conference call about a lengthy, acrimonious legal battle over medical billing. Kosowski took the call from a Toyota pickup truck outside the office, according to prosecutors, and had supplies to commit the killing: trash bags, a syringe containing a paralyzing drug and a wagon to haul the body out.

Cozzi represented a Dunedin, Florida-based medical practice. Kosowski alleged that the practice had shorted him thousands of dollars in billings and damaged his reputation as a doctor.

Cozzi seemingly disappeared without a trace. His keys, wallet and cellphone remained on his law-office desk, a work file was open on his computer, and his husband never heard from him.

Investigators said Kosowski had $280,000 in cash, masks, duct tape, firearms, a ballistic vest with “EMS” written on it, law enforcement patches and a vial of succinylcholine, a paralyzing drug, when authorities arrested him. A search of his home turned up about 200 guns, according to court documents.
