## 70th-anniversary conference 'What Must Remain Human?' began Thursday

Dartmouth College announced Wednesday that Provost Santiago Schnell resigned after a faculty committee found he used artificial intelligence to shape his writing, including in a guest column in The Washington Post on AI cheating. President Sian Leah Beilock said in a written statement that Schnell's departure was "his decision" and that the school wishes him well.

The resignation comes as colleges across the country grapple with how to transform teaching when more assignments can be completed with AI tools. It also came at a university that touts itself as the birthplace of AI: Dartmouth says the field got its start at the school in Hanover, New Hampshire, in 1956, when a small group of scientists gathered for a summer research project on the topic.

The issues arose after the news outlet Semafor ran guest columns in major newspapers through an AI filter. Schnell's August column in the Post, titled "Universities are fighting AI cheating. But there's a deeper problem," was found to be entirely written by AI. Schnell defended himself, saying at the time he used ChatGPT to "refine a few arguments" but that the column was based on his "own thinking."

The college paper, The Dartmouth, then ran other publications from Schnell through an AI detector. Writing that predated ChatGPT's 2022 public release was found to be human-written, but high rates of AI use were detected in his recent writing.

Schnell told the paper in an email last month that he uses AI for "language refinement, copyediting, improving clarity and organization and making writing processes more efficient." The paper ran the statement through an AI detector and found it was AI-written. In a follow-up statement, Schnell said he "developed the substance and wrote a draft" of the email, then "used an AI tool to assist with language and clarity."

An investigation ensued, and the faculty committee found that Schnell used AI to "meaningfully alter his writing" and "was not forthcoming about his AI use." Schnell referred questions to his attorneys, who did not immediately respond to an email from The Associated Press seeking comment.

Beilock said in her statement that keeping Dartmouth at the forefront of AI means "leading through an environment that is fluid and fast-changing." "That is why, as leaders, it is so critical that we model the transparency and human judgment that we expect from our students and scholars," the president wrote.

Schnell, a Venezuelan-born mathematical biologist, previously taught and held leadership roles at the University of Notre Dame, the University of Michigan and Indiana University, according to his LinkedIn profile.

A conference commemorating the 70th anniversary — titled "What Must Remain Human?" — began Thursday.
