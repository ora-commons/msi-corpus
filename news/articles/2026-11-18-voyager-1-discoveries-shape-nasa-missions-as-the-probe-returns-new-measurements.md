## Voyager 1 continues toward a distant star in the Little Dipper constellation

NASA Associate Administrator Nicky Fox, who oversees the agency's science missions, told NPR host Leila Fadel that Voyager 1's discoveries during its journey through the Jupiter and Saturn systems have inspired current NASA missions. Fox said the spacecraft continues producing measurements practically every day and said its findings remain a source of inspiration for future missions.

Voyager 1 also supplied discoveries that now shape current NASA missions, Fox said. During its outward journey through the Jupiter and Saturn systems, the spacecraft found volcanic activity, evidence of “a sort of ocean under an ice crust” on Europa, a moon of Jupiter, and the moon Titan.

Fox said the Europa finding helped inspire Europa Clipper, which is already on its way to Europa. At Saturn, Voyager 1 discovered Titan. Fox said the discovery inspired Dragonfly, and NASA plans to launch the mission in 2028 to explore Titan.

Fadel said Voyager 1 has gone farther into space than any other human-made object. Fox said the spacecraft is traveling in “a region of space that nothing has ever been in before,” and another spacecraft would require about the same amount of time to reach that region.

Voyager 1 will continue its journey even after NASA can no longer communicate with the spacecraft, Fox said. Its destination is a distant star about 17 light-years away, which Fox described as a little star in the Little Dipper constellation. She said the journey will take a long time.

Fadel's opening narration supplied background on the spacecraft's distance. She said that at 5:16 a.m. Eastern time on November 18, 2026, Voyager 1 became the first spacecraft to reach one light-day from Earth. Fox put one light-day at “about 16 billion miles” and said the trip took 49 years, two months and 11 days.

Fox used familiar travel times for scale. She said light takes eight minutes to travel from the Sun to Earth, about 1.3 seconds to travel from Earth to the Moon and a full day to reach Voyager 1. It takes astronauts about three days to reach the Moon, she said.

Fadel said Voyager 1 set off nearly 50 years ago on a mission to collect information and connect with extraterrestrials by way of a golden record containing images, sounds and greetings. During the interview, she played an archived recording in which Nick Sagan said, “Hello from the children of Planet Earth.” The excerpt also included an unidentified speaker speaking French and another unidentified person speaking a non-English language.
