## California cited recurring incursions as an imminent public-safety hazard

Waymo stopped offering commercial driverless freeway rides in the five markets where it provided the service — the San Francisco Bay Area, Los Angeles, Phoenix, Miami and Austin — while city-street rides in Las Vegas, Dallas, Denver and many other areas continued. The Guardian described the suspension as indefinite, and a Waymo spokesperson did not say how long it would last.

The California-based Alphabet subsidiary implemented the suspension last Wednesday, according to a Waymo spokesperson. The spokesperson wrote in an email that the company had “temporarily restricted commercial freeway rides in all markets where we operate that service,” though it was “continuing freeway testing with trained autonomous specialists behind the wheel.”

Waymo’s voluntary action came the same day as the California Department of Motor Vehicles banned its robotaxis from state freeways. The ban followed two traffic incidents, including one in which three Waymo driverless vehicles drove into a construction zone, DMV spokesperson Jonathan Groveman said in a statement.

The construction-zone incident occurred on 5 November. The second incident occurred the next day, on 6 November, on a highway in San Francisco, and involved California highway patrol vehicles, according to Groveman.

Video of the 6 November incident circulated widely on social media last week. In the footage, a Waymo vehicle appeared to try to bypass California highway patrol vehicles conducting a traffic break.

“The department determined that Waymo’s driverless freeway operations pose an imminent hazard to public safety due to recurring incursions by its vehicles into active construction zones where construction workers, vehicles and equipment are present,” Groveman said. He added that “similar incidents” previously occurred on 18 May.

A Waymo spokesperson said the “recent freeway construction zone encounters on US 101 in California” did not “involve any injuries or collisions.”

California’s restrictions bar Waymo from operating and testing its driverless vehicles on state freeways, according to Groveman. He said the restrictions would remain in effect until “Waymo demonstrates to the department that it has taken effective corrective actions to address the deficiencies that precipitated the restrictions.”

Waymo’s five-market pause leaves commercial freeway rides unavailable without a stated end date, while city-street rides continue; the company also says it maintains freeway testing with specialists behind the wheel. California’s restrictions separately remain in force until Waymo demonstrates effective corrective actions to the DMV.
