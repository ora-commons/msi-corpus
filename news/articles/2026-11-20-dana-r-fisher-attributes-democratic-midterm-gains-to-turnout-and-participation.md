## Dana R. Fisher attributes Democratic midterm gains to turnout and participation

**Subtype:** fact

- Dana R. Fisher linked the 2026 Democratic midterm gains to participation over the preceding two years.
- Fisher's team and YouGov surveyed 1,500 Americans and found participation at much higher rates after Donald Trump took office in 2025 than during the Biden administration.
- The Brennan Center reported a 10-point turnout increase since 2022 in majority-minority counties affected by changes in the Voting Rights Act.
- Fisher said the midterms shifted both congressional chambers, flipped six state governorships and elected a historic number of democratic socialists.
- Fisher said the Democratic Party's primary strategy failed in 2026 as progressive Democrats defeated mainstream candidates in primary contests in New York, Michigan, Colorado, Maine, California, Minnesota, Illinois and Texas, and in local races.
- Fisher urged the Democratic Party to reinvent itself around the issues behind the 2026 gains and said she feared a less peaceful and less orderly America if it did not.

## Fisher says progressives defeated mainstream candidates in multiple states and local races

Fisher's account joined an annual YouGov survey of 1,500 Americans with turnout data from the Brennan Center to explain the Democratic gains she described. She argued that participation over the preceding two years shaped the 2026 midterm results, pointing to contacts with elected officials, town hall meetings, boycotts and demonstrations at rates much higher after Donald Trump took office than during the Biden administration. She used the column to press the Democratic Party to act on the issues that brought voters to the polls.

Fisher wrote that her team conducts a national survey every year with YouGov, polling 1,500 Americans during the two weeks before the election. Since Donald Trump took office in 2025, her data showed political participation was up. Over the preceding two years, Americans contacted elected officials, attended town hall meetings, participated in boycotts and joined protests or demonstrations at much higher rates than during the Biden administration. The 2026 survey added questions about “participating in election/voter protection efforts” and “joining a strike.”

Fisher said Black respondents were much more likely than white or Hispanic respondents to have participated in election or voter-protection efforts. Hispanic respondents were more likely to have attended a town hall meeting and to have participated in a strike in the past year, she wrote. According to Fisher, the survey provided evidence that people most affected by Trump's policies played a pivotal role in Republican Party losses in 2026: they turned out to vote, overwhelmingly voted for Democrats and did more than voting to bring about social change.

The Brennan Center reported that turnout in majority-minority counties affected by changes in the Voting Rights Act showed a 10-point jump since the 2022 midterm election. Fisher described Texas as “on the frontline of the immigration battles during this Trump administration” and said votes there moved overall toward Democrats. She said turnout rates were “highest by far” in counties on the border with Mexico, which shifted toward Democrats by 35%.

Fisher said the 2026 midterms shifted the majority in both houses of Congress, flipped six state governorships and elected a historic number of democratic socialists to office. She wrote: “In contrast to suggestions that progressive candidates were unelectable, Americans turned out to vote in high numbers, and the overwhelming majority voted for Democrats.” Although turnout did not break records, Fisher said it was particularly high for those most affected by the policies of the Trump administration. She described “a seismic shift in support for Democrats by Black, Hispanic and Asian voters.”

Fisher situated the result within two years in which the Trump administration enacted Project 2025 and the United States descended into what she called “a state of lawless authoritarianism.” She wrote that Americans “rose up” to push back against attacks that included “limitations of voting rights” and “manipulations of political power through the gerrymandering of congressional districts.” She linked Donald Trump's actions to large-scale public demonstrations and localized mobilizations against ICE and the occupation of American cities, followed by what she called “resistance at the ballot box” on November 3.

Fisher wrote, “When people think of revolution, most imagine the violent overthrow of a government by the disenfranchised.” She described November 3 as “a revolution from within the system of government” driven by citizen participation and an electorate demanding social change. She wrote: “This combination of revolution and evolution – call it a [r]evolution – is a common last gasp of a citizenry before they give up.”

Fisher said the election also brought big changes in who ran for office. Writing that the Democratic Party had historically neutralized its left flank through the primary process, she said the tactic “didn’t work this year.” Progressive Democrats — only some of whom identify as members of the Democratic Socialists of America — won victories against mainstream Democrats in primary contests in New York, Michigan, Colorado, Maine, California, Minnesota, Illinois and Texas, as well as in numerous local races.

Fisher wrote: “Defying the party’s stale playbook, these candidates ran against political corruption and economic inequality, campaigning on promises of fixing the broken healthcare system, getting corporate money out of politics, and ending the electoral college.” She added: “And judging by the election results, Americans liked what they heard.”

Fisher said voters had supported an updated version of the Democratic Party and asked whether the party was capable of the changes mandated by voters. She said winning elections was only the first step and that voters had elected a progressive class of Democratic candidates “up and down the ballot” with a mandate for systemic change in the United States. Her warning was conditional: the effort would fail, she said, if the party suppressed the new class or pressured its members to support what she called “historically weak incremental and unsuccessful policies” and return to business as usual.

Fisher warned: “If the Democratic party does not reinvent itself to reflect a focus on the top issues that inspired the blue wave in 2026, I fear that what comes next in America will be less peaceful and less orderly.”

Fisher teaches in the School of International Service at American University and directs the Center for Environment, Community, & Equity. She is the author of *Saving Ourselves* (2024), *American Resistance* (2019) and *Activism, Inc* (2006).
