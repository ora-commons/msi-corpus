## U.S. official says Tyner handed over no information; Kremlin denies any connection

The Metropolitan Police said Lt. Col. Brandon Tyner’s arrest was not related to other counterterrorism investigations and had no connection to a September suspected terror plot linked to Iran at RAF Fairford, a U.S.-run base in Britain near Gloucester.

A U.S. official previously told The Associated Press that Tyner did not hand over any information to the Russians. The Kremlin denied any connection to the American officer.

Tyner was serving in NATO’s Allied Rapid Reaction Corps at Imjin Barracks near Gloucester. The multinational land unit within the military alliance is designed to respond quickly to crises or short-notice operations.

According to the unit’s website, the corps is made up of forces from 20 nations but is led by the British.

Tyner, 41, was an American Army officer arrested in the United Kingdom on Nov. 10 on suspicion of spying for Russia. He was suspected of violating the National Security Act by assisting a foreign intelligence service and obtaining or disclosing protected information.

Tyner had previously been deployed to Afghanistan, Iraq and Qatar. He was a senior human resources officer and had been assigned to a NATO unit at a base near Gloucester, England, about 100 miles west of London.

On Tuesday, the Metropolitan Police in London said in a news release that “a decision was made to pass the investigation” to American partners. Tyner was taken into U.S. military custody rather than charged in Britain. The police said their counterterror force had worked closely with the FBI and U.S. military.

U.S. Brig. Gen. Sean Stinchon, commanding general of the Army’s Counterintelligence Command, called the case a warning to service members. “This case should serve as a stark warning to any service member suspected of violating the oath we all take to protect and defend the Constitution,” Stinchon said in the release.
