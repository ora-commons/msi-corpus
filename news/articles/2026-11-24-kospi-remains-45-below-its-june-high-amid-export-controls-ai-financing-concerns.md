## Kospi futures briefly halt after a more than 6% rise

A more than 6% rise in Kospi futures triggered a brief trading halt as South Korea's Kospi, still down 45% from its June high, rebounded following Jensen Huang's reinstatement. In Seoul, SK Hynix and Samsung climbed 6.7% each, driving the Kospi 6.3% higher. Shares of TSMC, which makes Nvidia's AI chips, surged nearly 10%.

Taiwan's Taiex ended 9.0% higher on Tuesday, logging its biggest one-day percentage gain since April 2025. Even after the gain, the Taiex remained off 19.5% from its October record.

The U.S. on Monday named several Asia-based entities to an export-control list barring U.S. companies from doing business with them. The action was part of broader moves to close loopholes through which advanced American chips could be unlawfully transported.

Analysts cautioned that it was too early to sound the all-clear. The risk posed by tighter U.S. restrictions on advanced chips has not gone away, nor have concerns around artificial-intelligence financing, the sustainability of spending and valuations that have fed fears of a market bubble. Natixis economists cautioned that investors should not assume U.S. tech restrictions have peaked after recent efforts to deny China access to advanced AI chips through overseas data centers. “Jensen Huang’s return to Nvidia does not mean back to normal for Asia,” they wrote.

Citi analysts said the reinstatement removes a key overhang created by the CEO transition and restores continuity across Nvidia's product roadmap, customer relationships and supplier partnerships. The rally reached beyond Nvidia itself, lifting major Nvidia suppliers and partners in Asia.

The rebound followed Huang's brief ouster and resignation, which sent global markets tumbling, and came after intense pressure from Silicon Valley, Wall Street investors and the White House, according to The Wall Street Journal. The scandal that led to the ouster helped set off the most recent market rout in Asia.

The rebound did not erase the damage from the rout that preceded it. AI and technology-related shares had already suffered several bouts of corrections before Huang's resignation. In early November, Japan's Nikkei Stock Average briefly entered a bear market, dropping more than 20% from its June peak. South Korea and Taiwan, two markets more closely tied to the AI trade, also gyrated wildly, prompting regulators to activate emergency measures to staunch steep losses.

External and internal investigations into Huang's affair with a suspected Chinese intelligence asset, whose firm allegedly helped bring Nvidia chips into China, continued. Several U.S. lawmakers criticized Huang's return, and Senator Elizabeth Warren called his reinstatement “reckless,” citing unanswered questions about the CEO and his company.

With Democrats taking control of both chambers of Congress in January, Nvidia could face renewed political scrutiny, congressional hearings and further investigations, said Saxo Markets chief investment strategist Charu Chanana.

The rebound reflected the relief investors showed over Huang's reinstatement, while the export-control list, continuing investigations and open questions about AI spending left the broader outlook unsettled.
