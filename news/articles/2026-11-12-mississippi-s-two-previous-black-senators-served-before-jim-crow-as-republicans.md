Mississippi, described by the BBC as a deeply conservative southern state, last sent a Democrat to the US Senate in 1982 and had not seated a Black senator since 1875.

### Revels and Bruce: the record before Colom

Colom follows Hiram Revels, who became the first Black person ever to serve in the Senate when he was seated from 1870 to 1871, and Blanche Bruce, whose term ran from 1875 to 1881.

Revels was chosen as Mississippi's first senator after the Civil War, serving just over a year and campaigning strongly for racial equality. A minister, he served as a chaplain during the Civil War.

A few years later, Bruce, who was born into slavery, became the first Black person to serve a full term in the US Senate. He remained in Washington after leaving the chamber and held a role in the Treasury.

Both of those terms came before the Jim Crow laws began to enforce racial segregation across Mississippi and other southern US states.

### The BBC's account of the victory

A significant factor in Colom's victory was his success in mobilizing young people and the state's large Black population, who are expected to have turned out in high numbers, the BBC reported.

In a victory speech, Colom referenced how Revels gained support for his Senate campaign through his words. "That's an example of the power of prayer, and through our history, there are so many people that lifted this state up through the power of prayer in very dark times," Colom said.
