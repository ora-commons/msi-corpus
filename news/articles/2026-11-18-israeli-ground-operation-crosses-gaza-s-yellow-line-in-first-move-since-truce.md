## Board of Peace says ceasefire faces risk of collapse

Israeli tanks entered Hamas-controlled central Gaza on Wednesday, crossing the ‘yellow line’ in what The Guardian described as the military's first ground operation across that boundary since the October 2025 ceasefire. The movement forced dozens of displaced Palestinians in Nuseirat to leave tent shelters with their belongings.

Tanks rolled along the Salah al-Din road, described as Gaza’s main north-south route. The crossing came a day after the U.S.-backed, Trump-led Board of Peace, which oversees Donald Trump’s plan to end the war in Gaza, accused Israel and Hamas of failing to honor commitments to end hostilities. An Israeli military spokesperson said troops were carrying out an operation targeting “Hamas infrastructure” west of the yellow line in central Gaza. The military did not comment extensively on the operation.

A Gaza security source told AFP there was “intense Israeli artillery shelling” north of Nuseirat refugee camp and reported Israeli military vehicles in central Gaza “amid ongoing gunfire and airstrikes since midday.” Residents reported hearing tank fire in the area.

Hamas condemned the raid and the forced displacement of Palestinians as “a flagrant violation of the ceasefire agreement” and a dangerous escalation. The Israeli military did not immediately respond to Hamas’s statement.

Palestinian medics said one person was killed in an Israeli airstrike near the site of the raid. In separate incidents on Wednesday, Palestinian medics said Israeli fire killed two people, one in Deir al-Balah and the other in Gaza City. The Israeli military said it was unaware of any incident in Deir al-Balah and that it had struck a militant in Gaza City.

Israel said it had stepped up attacks on Hamas because the group was regrouping and rearming. Hamas accused Israel of trying to derail the U.S.-brokered ceasefire agreed in October 2025. The Guardian reported that Israeli strikes had continued since the ceasefire and that Hamas remained armed.

Last week, an Israeli airstrike on Nasser hospital in southern Gaza killed six people, including a surgeon and a disabled man, and wounded 28 others, according to the hospital director.

The Board of Peace’s second report to the UN Security Council since the truce began 13 months ago said the gap between what had been agreed and what was happening on the ground was the biggest threat to the deal. The report, seen by Reuters, warned that “a ceasefire that delivers nothing visible cannot be sustained indefinitely.” The board said the situation had left Palestinians in dire conditions and the ceasefire at risk of collapse.

The operation came as Israeli opposition politician Gadi Eisenkot was seeking to form a government without caretaker Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. The Guardian reported that Netanyahu has built his political reputation on promises to keep Israel safe.

Gaza’s health authorities said at least 1,600 Palestinians had been killed since the ceasefire came into effect. The Israeli military said five of its soldiers had been killed over the same period. Palestinian authorities said more than 74,000 people had been killed in Gaza since Israel launched its military campaign in response to the Hamas-led attack on southern Israel on 7 October 2023, in which about 1,200 people were killed.
