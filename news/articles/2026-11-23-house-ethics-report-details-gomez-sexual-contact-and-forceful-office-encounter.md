## Earlier 2026 cases involved Swalwell, Gonzales, Edwards, Mills and Miller

The 19-page report from the House Ethics Committee documented two unwanted advances by Rep. Jimmy Gomez toward a House staffer. One incident involved Gomez making sexual contact with her. In the other, the committee said he was “forceful” with her in his congressional office.

The committee found “substantial evidence” that Gomez had “engaged in sexual misconduct,” including by “making unwelcome sexual advances and sexual contact toward a House staffer.”

The report also stated: “Gomez’s conduct also reflects a broader environment of sexual misconduct and toxic working conditions in some Capitol Hill offices.”

The committee made no specific recommendations. It noted: “The constitution charges the House with policing the behavior of its own members, and the House should take whatever action it deems appropriate in light of the committee’s findings.”

Gomez said he had “strong disagreements with the committee’s report and its conclusions,” but also said he took full responsibility for the “personal mistakes I committed outside of my marriage.” He said he cooperated with the investigation and responded voluntarily to requests for information.

Following the report's publication on Monday, Gomez announced that he would resign on 1 December. The California Democrat, who represents parts of Los Angeles, had lost his re-election bid earlier in November to democratic socialist Angela Gonzales-Torres and was already set to leave Congress in January.

The allegations against Gomez were the latest in a series of House ethics investigations that The Guardian said had seen a slew of representatives resign from Congress or lose their bids for re-election this year.

In April, Eric Swalwell, also a Democratic representative from California, resigned from Congress after multiple allegations of sexual assault and misconduct. The allegations ended his bid for governor and reshuffled the race. Gomez, a co-chair of Swalwell's gubernatorial campaign, stepped down from that role after the allegations against Swalwell were made public.

The Guardian reported that Tony Gonzales, a Texas Republican, stepped down that month after admitting that he had an affair with an aide who later died by suicide.

In August, Republican congressman Chuck Edwards of North Carolina announced that he would not run for re-election after an ethics committee report determined that he “fostered a hostile work environment and engaged in sexual harassment” by giving gifts and lavishing attention on two young female staffers.

Cory Mills, a Florida Republican accused of a variety of ethical lapses, was defeated in the Republican primary. In October 2025, a Florida judge granted a protective order against Mills at the request of his former girlfriend.

In November's midterm elections, Max Miller, a Republican from Ohio, lost his re-election bid amid a House ethics investigation into drug use and domestic abuse allegations surrounding his divorce to Emily Moreno, the daughter of Republican U.S. senator Bernie Moreno.
