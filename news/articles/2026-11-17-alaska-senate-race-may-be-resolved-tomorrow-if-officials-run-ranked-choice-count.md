## Trump defends Lutnick and says he would not mind Huang's return

Alaska election officials could resolve the final Senate race of this year's midterm elections tomorrow if they run the state's ranked-choice voting system, The Guardian reported. With 98% of ballots counted, Associated Press figures cited by The Guardian put Democratic challenger Mary Peltola ahead of Republican Sen. Dan Sullivan, 49.98% to 45.93%. Peltola had 141,637 votes compared with Sullivan's 130,162, a lead of just over 11,000.

If Peltola did not reach 50%, voters' second choices would be counted, The Guardian said. Two weeks after the election, the result would determine the chamber's final balance for the next two years; Democrats already held a 55-44 majority.

The race came amid demands involving Peltola's campaign. President Donald Trump demanded that Peltola be removed from the ballot and prosecuted, while Alaska's Republican governor ordered an investigation into the campaign.

A New York Times investigation reported that operatives working with Peltola's campaign helped recruit Dan J. Sullivan, a retired teacher from Petersburg, to split the incumbent's vote. Peltola and her campaign denied the allegation.

Dan J. Sullivan ran as an independent candidate and received 4,684 votes, or 1.65%. The Guardian said the independent candidate appeared unlikely to cause the Republican senator's defeat if Sullivan failed to retain his seat.

Elsewhere, Trump arrived in Manila, the capital of the Philippines, for the Association of Southeast Asian Nations summit, where he was due to meet Southeast Asian leaders and take part in the first leaders' meeting of the Quad partnership — the United States, Japan, India and Australia — since he returned to the White House in January 2025.

Trump defended Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick after Lutnick came under criticism for allegedly slow-walking export restrictions on Nvidia chips to China. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CNBC last week that he had been pushing “for months” to add Megaspeed to the U.S. trade blacklist.

The Guardian described Megaspeed as a data-center company led by an alleged Chinese spy who was reportedly in a relationship with ousted Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang.

Trump said he would “not mind” seeing Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia's chief executive officer. Trump said the former chief executive had learned a lesson and denied that national security had been put at risk.

Trump criticized the new leadership of CBS News' 60 Minutes, which had been overhauled under the network's conservative editor-in-chief, Bari Weiss. In a social media post, he was particularly critical of a Sunday report saying more than 800 American service members had been wounded and 23 killed in the war in Iran.

Trump continued to shift responsibility to the Biden administration for the nationwide ground stop over the weekend. He said more funding was needed to overhaul the outdated telecommunication system responsible for the disruptions.

The Trump administration warned Elizabeth Warren, the senior Democratic senator expected to chair the powerful Senate banking committee, that she could face “significant legal consequences” if she failed to preserve records related to investigations into Trump. The warning mirrored other letters from Justice Department officials to Democratic lawmakers who had indicated they would investigate the administration when the party becomes the majority in Congress next year.

A federal appeals court heard arguments in Donald Trump's hush money case as the president sought to shift it to federal court, where he could seek dismissal on grounds of immunity. The hearing concerned a May 2024 ruling that found Trump guilty on 34 felony counts of falsifying business records to obscure a $130,000 payment to adult film star Stormy Daniels.

The Alaska count remained unresolved in The Guardian's cited Associated Press figures. Peltola was below 50%, leaving second-choice counting possible.
