## Districts weigh dropping Google products absent an AI-free search

Los Angeles Unified has temporarily disabled Google AI Overview, the search engine's AI-generated summaries, on school-issued devices. The district enrolls half a million students, and the move makes it the largest school district to sideline Google, whose education products are deeply embedded in American classrooms. The change was not formally announced, and it caught parents and students by surprise, said Bridie Lee, a leader of Schools Beyond Screens LA.

Several members of Schools Beyond Screens LA, which has pushed for screen-time limits and technology oversight in the district, spoke at a Los Angeles school board artificial intelligence committee meeting on Friday, Nov. 13. By that Monday, AI Overview was gone. The group asked parents to send videos of what their children's laptop screens showed and received clips showing that AI Overview was no longer an option in Google search on district devices. After activists reached out, the district confirmed that AI Overview had been disabled.

Los Angeles Unified said in a statement that it temporarily suspended AI Overview to ensure student experiences were “age-appropriate and safe.” The district said it continually evaluates its digital safeguards, including third-party tools, and was likely using such a tool to disable the feature. Lee said she had no other details on how it was done or how long it was intended to stay disabled.

Lee said, “It was the talk of the playground.” She also said, “We’re thrilled LAUSD did this,” and argued that “AI is seeping into everything. It’s dangerous. It’s hurting learning.”

The stakes extend beyond one setting. Mountain View, California-based Google is the dominant provider of education technology for American classrooms. Its low-cost Chromebooks are the most widely used student laptops, and its Google Docs and Classroom applications have become integral to classwork, homework and communication among students and teachers.

Concerns about students using AI to think for them have ignited a conflict between schools and Google. In September, New York City and Los Angeles, the country's two largest school districts, announced one-year moratoriums on students' use of generative AI. New York's ban applies to all elementary and middle school students, while Los Angeles bars AI on school devices for all K-12 students. Both districts said the pause would give officials time to develop AI policies for the academic year.

Google said in early November that Gemini and all search tools would be off by default for K-12 schools in the coming months, with administrators required to turn on each tool if they wanted students to have access. The announcement reversed the company's earlier move to enable its Gemini chatbot for many K-12 students using Google Classroom. Google said the change gave schools more choice, but its vice president for education, Chris Phillips, told The New York Times that “Search and AI are deeply integrated.” If schools ban AI, students “will not have access to search, and they’ll see a message from their school that indicates it's not available.”

Google introduced AI Overview in 2025, and the company says anyone using Google search receives AI answers for many search results. Google insists AI Overview is an integral part of the search product and cannot be separated from it.

Earlier this month, hundreds of Google employees signed a petition asking company leadership to allow schools to turn off AI features “without disrupting their ability to use search as a whole.” The petition said, “It is our job as Googlers to respect the user, and listen to them when they have concerns about our products.”

Google said it hears parents' and teachers' concerns that students are using AI to “shortcut learning” and that it shares their goal of helping students learn. Instead of offering an AI-free search product for schools, Google announced a pilot “guided learning” tool that walks students “step-by-step through a range of complex problems, instead of just giving quick answers.” The tool is not yet available. Google said, “We are actively building these experiences in partnership with educators, listening closely to ensure our products support students and teachers.”

Other districts are developing workarounds or weighing a wider exit. Fairfax County Public Schools in Virginia have developed plug-ins to remove AI summaries from students' Google searches, according to published reports. School districts are considering other internet search providers and are also weighing whether to drop Google products entirely unless the company provides a permanent AI workaround.

Los Angeles Unified had already disabled Google's AI Mode chatbot at the beginning of the school year on school-issued student devices, while educators and staff still had access. Chief information officer Jack Kelanic told a school board committee meeting on artificial intelligence that “We understand this as a deeper embedding of AI into the education suite.”

Kelanic said the district is not bound to Google's products if officials determine the changes are not in line with the district's priorities, drawing applause. “We have choices,” he said. “We can change the default search engine literally overnight.”
