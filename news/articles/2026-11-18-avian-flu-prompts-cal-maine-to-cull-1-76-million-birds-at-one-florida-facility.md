## The cull covers about 2.7% of Cal-Maine’s total flock

Cal-Maine Foods said Wednesday that it will depopulate about 1.76 million birds after an avian flu outbreak at one of its Florida facilities. Production at the affected site has temporarily ceased.

The cull includes roughly 1.4 million laying hens and 360,000 pullets, according to the egg producer. Those birds represent about 2.7% of Cal-Maine’s total flock.

Cal-Maine is following protocols prescribed by the U.S. Department of Agriculture as it carries out the response. The company said its geographically diversified production network provides flexibility to mitigate the disruption, maintain continuity of supply and minimize potential impacts on customers.

In premarket trading, Cal-Maine shares fell 2.2% to $63.80.

Cal-Maine said it maintains robust biosecurity programs across its facilities, but no farm is immune to avian flu. The outbreak has stopped output and required a large cull at one site.

The Wall Street Journal reported that last year, one of the worst bird flu outbreaks in U.S. history killed millions of egg-laying hens, prompting an egg shortage that left grocery store shelves barren.

During its most recent quarter, Cal-Maine swung to its second consecutive quarterly loss and posted sharply lower sales as an oversupply of eggs weighed on wholesale prices. Cal-Maine plans to provide updated information about the Florida outbreak in its next quarterly report.
