## Quad talks will focus on critical minerals, maritime security and energy security

Anthony Albanese will meet Donald Trump in Manila on Tuesday as Albanese seeks a faster end to the wars in Iran and Ukraine and greater US engagement in the Indo-Pacific. The meeting is part of the Quad leaders’ summit in the Philippine capital, the first gathering of the group since 2024 and Trump’s first Quad leaders’ meeting in either of his presidencies.

The summit follows the US midterm elections. Trump will take centre stage at gatherings of Asian leaders and is preparing for three-way talks with Xi Jinping of China and Russia’s Vladimir Putin this week.

The Guardian reported that federal government efforts to avoid angering China underpinned the diplomatic swing. The Quad leaders are gathering for the first time since 2024, after Australia joined an agreement with countries including the US and Japan on new protections around key AI technologies.

Asia Society senior fellow Premesha Saha said the Quad talks would focus on critical minerals, maritime security and energy security. Albanese and Trump will be joined by Japanese prime minister Sanae Takaichi and India’s Narendra Modi.

Saha said keeping the United States engaged in the region was a core Australian strategic interest. “For Australia, keeping the United States engaged in the Indo-Pacific is a core strategic interest, and the Quad is one of the few settings where the president is tied into a regional agenda alongside three close partners,” Saha said. She added that “After a year in which Washington has been engaged elsewhere and has opened its own channel with Beijing, getting Trump to a Quad leaders’ meeting in the region matters to Australia.”

Saha said Albanese’s objectives included keeping Washington engaged in the region and building a Quad capable of delivering results even during shifts in US policy and interest. Federal government sources told Guardian Australia that the international effort — including Albanese’s attendance at the Apec leaders’ meeting in Shenzhen, China at the end of the week — was about maintaining engagement with Trump in the final two years of his presidency and stopping the US from withdrawing from global leadership.

At the ASEAN-Australia summit on Monday, Albanese said the region needed friendship, mutual trust and optimism to prosper. “At a time of global instability, the cooperation we demonstrate here will help us to confront the challenges we share at home and in our region,” he said.

Separately from the Quad talks, Australia joined the United States and Japan in the San Francisco Declaration, an agreement intended to add protections around key AI technologies. The agreement was designed to strengthen supply chains for chips and critical minerals components and promote cooperation amid China’s efforts to control areas of AI development.

The declaration is part of the Pax Silica international accord. Officials said it would help ensure Trump worked with partners to set AI standards rather than rely on others for leadership. Other signatories include the UK, India, Israel, South Korea and Singapore.

On Monday, Albanese announced a new ASEAN-Australia digital economy program to support AI safety and intelligence standards and digital trade standards. He said humans must remain in control of AI, shaping the technology “rather than let it shape us.”

Saha said Beijing would criticize the Quad, as it always does, and cast it as bloc politics, but cautioned against overstating how much the gathering would anger China. She noted that “The Quad isn’t a military alliance, and Washington has gone out of its way to say it isn’t an anti-China coalition.”

Lowy Institute non-resident fellow Jennifer Parker disagreed that Australia was working to keep the United States engaged in the region. “The US has enduring interests here, both economically and strategically. But sometimes it pays for Australia to remind them of that,” she said.

Parker said criticism of Albanese’s involvement in the annual international summit season was shortsighted. “Our relationships in the region are essential to our security in an increasingly chaotic world, and our security is the backbone of our national prosperity,” Parker said. “It is important for Australia to be seen on the international stage and advocate for our national interests.”

Albanese held one-on-one talks with Philippines president Ferdinand Marcos Jr. and spoke with Saleumxay Kommasith of Laos. Albanese told Kommasith that Australia remained very concerned about the deaths of backpackers Bianca Jones and Holly Morton-Bowles, who died after drinking methanol-laced alcohol at a hostel in Vientiane in 2024. Albanese said Australia remained concerned about the seriousness of charges over the deaths. Investigators said they did not have enough evidence to do more because autopsies were not carried out. Albanese called the case a “tragedy” and said it remained a serious issue in Australia.

Kommasith said the Laos government had attached importance to the case and that officials were trying to bring the perpetrators to justice. Saha said the Quad talks would focus on critical minerals, maritime security and energy security, and she described keeping the United States engaged in the Indo-Pacific as a core Australian strategic interest. Albanese’s requests for an end to the wars in Iran and Ukraine frame his bilateral meeting with Trump.
