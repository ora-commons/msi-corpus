## Lawsuit says Ecuador cleared vessel before alleged US attack

A group of Ecuadorian fishermen sued the Trump administration on Monday, alleging that U.S. military drones attacked the fishing vessel La Negra Francesca Duarte II in the eastern Pacific Ocean off Ecuador on March 17 and that American personnel later seized the crew after mistaking them for drug smugglers. The federal lawsuit says the vessel was engulfed in flames, two men suffered serious burns, and the men were held against their will for around 24 hours and treated in cruel and inhumane ways.

The lawsuit also alleges that Ecuadorian authorities inspected the vessel for three hours and cleared the ship and crew of illicit activity before the strike. The filing further alleges that the fishermen were kidnapped and subjected to cruel and inhumane treatment.

The complaint was filed in U.S. District Court in Pittsburgh under a U.S. admiralty law that allows claims over severe injuries on the high seas, as well as alleged unlawful attacks, kidnapping and torture. The filing was reported earlier by The New York Times.

Daniel Kovalik, the fishermen’s U.S. attorney, said he chose Pittsburgh because the court’s jurisdiction in western Pennsylvania has maritime-law expertise. That jurisdiction includes the three rivers that flow through the city and a port on Lake Erie.

According to the complaint, the fishermen jumped into the ocean after the vessel was engulfed in flames. They then used small emergency boats that had been aboard the fishing vessel and approached a ship marked with U.S. flags, where men in uniforms spoke English.

The complaint says U.S. personnel tied the fishermen’s hands, placed hoods over their heads, gave them little water and provided no food. One of the fishermen, Jordy Alexander Flores Lucas, was told through a translator that the men on the boat had launched the attack. The filing further alleges that the men “laughed about what they had done and about how they had destroyed the fishing vessel — a vessel which provided the livelihood of all of the plaintiffs,” according to the complaint.

According to the complaint, the fishermen spent seven days aboard a Salvadoran ship that took them to El Salvador. The complaint further says most were held in a comprehensive care center for migrants, two injured men were brought to a hospital, and all of the men were eventually flown back to Ecuador by the end of March.

The lawsuit is the second against the Trump administration over U.S. military attacks on alleged drug-smuggling boats in Latin America. At least 240 people have been killed since the administration began targeting people it calls “narco-terrorists” in September 2025. U.S. officials have released little evidence publicly to support claims that the vessels have been carrying drugs.

Democratic lawmakers and some legal experts also say the attacks amount to extrajudicial killings on the high seas.

The Trump administration has justified the strikes in general by declaring drug cartels to be “unlawful combatants” and saying the United States is now in an “armed conflict” with them.

Asked for comment on the lawsuit, White House spokeswoman Anna Kelly said all boat strikes “have been against designated narcoterrorists bringing deadly poison to our shores.”

Kelly also said: “President Trump promised to take on the cartels — and he has taken unprecedented action to stop the scourge of narcoterrorism that has resulted in the needless deaths of innocent Americans.”

The Pentagon said in its own statement that it does not comment on pending or ongoing litigation. The case is the second lawsuit over the administration’s boat-strike campaign and was filed in federal court in Pittsburgh.
