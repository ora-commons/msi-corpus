## An estimated 1.5 million people flooded a Lima air base for Mass

An estimated 1.5 million people flooded a Lima air base for Pope Leo XIV's final Mass in Peru. During a Monday popemobile ride through the crowd before the service, someone threw a rosary that struck Leo in the eye. Leo grimaced, touched his eye and continued as if nothing had happened.

Aboard the papal plane en route home, Leo held an in-flight press conference. A Peruvian reporter apologized for the incident.

Leo, who lived in Peru for two decades and has Peruvian citizenship, brushed aside the apology and described the thrower as someone merely trying to get close to the pope. He also said he had forgiven the person.

During the press conference, Leo joked about the incident:

“The nice thing about Peruvians, including the person who with a lot of faith simply wanted to offer me a rosary, are things you carry in your heart – or maybe in your eye – but that mark the life of someone,” he said.

He added:

“It tells you that here truly there is enthusiasm. Here there is profound faith. Here there is a desire to receive the blessing of the Lord,” he said.

People often toss rosaries, stuffed animals, sports jerseys and other items toward the pope during popemobile rides. Leo has often caught them with quick reflexes.

His bodyguards said the tossed items are among their biggest security concerns when he is out in public. His own response to the Peru incident emphasized the thrower's faith and his forgiveness.
