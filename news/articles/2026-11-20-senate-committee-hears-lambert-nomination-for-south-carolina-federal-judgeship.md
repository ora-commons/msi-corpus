## Lambert represented McMaster in execution, abortion and National Guard cases

A Senate Judiciary Committee hearing on Wednesday opened the confirmation process for Grayson Lambert, President Donald Trump’s nominee for a lifetime appointment to the U.S. District Court for South Carolina. Trump nominated Lambert this week, and the former chief attorney to Gov. Henry McMaster has represented the governor in state and federal courts since May 2021.

The hearing took place as Republicans moved to confirm judges before Democrats take control of the Senate on Jan. 3. Outgoing South Carolina Sen. Darline Graham introduced Lambert to committee members. “To fill this vacancy, President Trump could not have made a better choice than Grayson Lambert,” Graham told the committee.

If confirmed, Lambert would succeed Timothy Cain, the former chief judge of the U.S. District Court for South Carolina. President Barack Obama nominated Cain in 2011, and Cain stepped down as semi-retired on Oct. 1.

Recent cases in which Lambert represented McMaster involved state law keeping the execution process secret, the constitutionality of South Carolina’s six-week abortion ban, and McMaster’s decision to send National Guard troops to Washington, D.C.

McMaster backed Lambert in a letter supporting the nomination. McMaster said he recommended Lambert “in the strongest possible terms.” McMaster wrote: “To be sure, a Governor’s lawyer must be an advocate. A judge, however, must be a neutral arbiter. I have complete confidence that Grayson recognizes and respects the difference, and his temperament and judgment are well suited to the judicial role.” The committee posted the letter on its website.

Before working with McMaster, Lambert worked for law firms in Columbia. Lambert holds a bachelor’s degree from the University of Virginia and a law degree from Duke University in North Carolina. Lambert also clerked for Judge Dennis Shedd on the 4th Circuit U.S. Court of Appeals from 2012 to 2013.

In 2023, Lambert was among five candidates for an opening on the South Carolina Court of Appeals. Lambert testified before the Judicial Merit Selection Commission in Columbia, S.C., on Nov. 6, 2023, as a candidate for the seat. The Legislature’s judicial screening panel found Lambert qualified but did not forward his name to the General Assembly for election.

During Wednesday’s hearing in Washington, Lambert said it had been a “privilege to represent South Carolina’s efforts to legislate within its constitutional sphere and to ensure that federal courts don’t overreach and preclude the state from doing what the Constitution allows.”

Lambert is among about 20 judicial nominees awaiting a Senate vote in the lame-duck session. New senators will be sworn in Jan. 3, when Democrats will take control of the chamber.

New Jersey Sen. Cory Booker said the Judiciary Committee should wait until the new Senate was sworn in. Booker argued that South Carolina, Texas and North Carolina would each soon be represented by one Democrat and one Republican. The senators could make bipartisan decisions together about who should serve as judges on their state benches.

“They would have affirmed the integrity of the judiciary. They would have affirmed the spirit of our democracy,” Booker said during the hearing.

The forthcoming change in control followed a midterm election in which Democrat Annie Andrews defeated Graham. Andrews’ victory was one of nine Senate flips for Democrats nationwide.

Republican Texas Sen. Ted Cruz pushed back on Booker’s argument, pointing to the period after the previous presidential election when Democrats voted to appoint more than 20 federal judges before Republicans took control of the chamber in January 2025. “He cannot believe that a Republican Senate would be confirming judges nominated by a Republican president in the lame duck after an election. You know, you don’t need a particularly long memory to recognize the hypocrisy in what Sen. Booker said,” Cruz said.

Booker, a potential 2028 presidential contender, was among Democrats already crisscrossing South Carolina. Booker was scheduled to return to the state this weekend, the first state on Democrats’ nominating calendar. Booker was set as the keynote speaker at a Friday-night fundraising gala on James Island for the NAACP’s Charleston branch and was scheduled to speak at the Legislative Black Caucus’ gala Saturday night in Columbia.

Lambert’s hearing marked the opening step in the confirmation process. His nomination remained among the roughly 20 judicial nominees awaiting a Senate vote in the lame-duck session, before Democrats take control on Jan. 3.
