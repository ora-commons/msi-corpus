## Authorities allege Kozak emailed the plant claiming explosive devices were placed there

Andrew M Kozak, 41, of Chillicothe, Illinois, pleaded not guilty to two federal charges during his first appearance in federal court on Tuesday, after authorities found an explosive device at the BioUrja Renewables ethanol plant in Peoria. The justice department charged Kozak on Tuesday over last week’s bomb threat.

The first count alleges that Kozak transmitted through interstate or foreign commerce a threat to unlawfully damage or destroy the plant with fire and an explosive. The second alleges that he made threatening interstate communications.

According to authorities, Kozak allegedly emailed the plant last Friday, claiming explosive devices had been placed there. The threat prompted a significant law-enforcement response and forced thousands of residents in parts of Peoria and East Peoria to evacuate last Friday. Authorities issued a mandatory evacuation order for anyone living or working within the 1-mile radius of the plant, and the order remained in effect until last Saturday afternoon.

Authorities found an explosive device at the facility. It did not detonate, and no injuries were reported. The riverfront plant produces ethanol for fuel and alcohol, both highly flammable products, and spans an area roughly equivalent to 44.5 US football fields.

According to Kozak’s LinkedIn page, he has been working as a process trainee at the plant since February, where he describes his role as: “Learning how to operate an ethanol plant.”

Gregory M Gilmore, the US attorney for the central district of Illinois, said: “Threats to public safety generate fear in our community and cause significant disruption to residents, who deserve to go about their days free of worry about impending violence.”

He added: “My office is committed to prosecuting these serious crimes. We are grateful to our federal and local law enforcement partners for their dedicated work in furtherance of community safety.”

Christopher Amon, the ATF’s Chicago field division special agent in charge, said: “An arrest was made in this case within 72 hours, demonstrating the tireless efforts of the law enforcement and intelligence personnel who worked around the clock to investigate an act that caused mass disruption and threatened the safety of the Peoria community.”

The Guardian said it had reached out to BioUrja Renewables for comment. The Guardian’s report included a photograph credited to Matt Dayhoff/Reuters showing a section of the BioUrja plant in Peoria on 12 April 2023.

If convicted, Kozak faces up to 10 years in prison on the first count and up to five years on the second. Each charge also carries a potential fine of up to $250,000 and up to three years of supervised release.
