## Kennedy expects Huang to invoke Fifth Amendment; Lutnick agrees to testify

Half a dozen Republicans on the Senate Banking Committee panel have made clear they will not pull their punches at Nvidia co-founder and CEO Jensen Huang's Dec. 3 hearing. The public hearing will place Huang before the panel on live television. The named Republicans include Sens. John Kennedy of Louisiana, Kevin Cramer of North Dakota, Pete Ricketts of Nebraska, Jim Banks of Indiana and Thom Tillis of North Carolina. The Wall Street Journal described the group as including some of Nvidia's most vocal critics in Congress.

Kennedy said he hopes Huang will be forthcoming but expects him to invoke his Fifth Amendment right against self-incrimination. “I mean, if I were in his shoes, I’d be pretty dadgum careful right now,” Kennedy said. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has also agreed to testify before the banking committee the following week.

Cramer said on CNBC, “It's not what we know that's problematic, it's what we do not know that's problematic.” He added, “We're going to have to put stronger guardrails. We might even have to put a babysitter on Jensen Huang, for crying out loud.”

Kennedy said, “I mean this is serious. It's serious as an aneurysm,” and, “Serious as a heart attack—four heart attacks and a stroke.” He said he had concerns that Nvidia and the Commerce Department had been allowing China access, directly or indirectly, to advanced U.S. chips. “This whole thing disturbs me—that's an understatement,” Kennedy said. “This is a matter of national security.”

The public scrutiny encompasses Nvidia's chip exports to China and Huang's reported affair with a suspected Chinese intelligence asset. Although many headlines have focused on the Nvidia co-founder's affairs, including one with an Nvidia executive, most lawmakers say they are troubled by allegations that Nvidia customers smuggled billions of dollars of AI chips to China and aided the Chinese military. Those allegations are among the issues driving demands for answers from Huang and Nvidia's general counsel.

On personal conduct, Kennedy said, “If Mr. Huang wants to embrace a feral lifestyle, that's none of my business.” He continued: “If Mr. Huang has an affair or cheats on Mrs. Huang with a Chinese spy, that is my business. I'm not saying he did that. That's why we're going to have a hearing.” Nvidia did not respond to requests for comment.

The scrutiny is bipartisan despite Trump's strong backing of Huang. Many Republican lawmakers denounced Trump's assurance to Nvidia's board that Huang poses no national-security risk, and Republicans said they would join Democrats in demanding answers from Huang and Nvidia's general counsel, who is also scheduled to appear privately before the House committees. Lutnick has drawn criticism for moving slowly on chip-export restrictions. Huang is scheduled for private interviews by three House committees on Dec. 1, before his public Senate Banking Committee hearing on Dec. 3. The Wall Street Journal said the appearances would take on added importance after Trump helped reinstate Huang as Nvidia's chief executive.

The congressional action coincides with Nvidia's conference in Washington for developers and customers using the company's technology. Nvidia has not said whether Huang will appear, as he did last year.

The Wall Street Journal reported that lawmakers are seeking answers and investors are weighing regulatory threats facing Nvidia from potential violations of export-control rules and expected investigations from a Democratic Congress next year. While Trump assured Nvidia's board Huang could be trusted, Nvidia still faces an array of related investigations, including from the Securities and Exchange Commission.

The dispute follows Republicans' crushing losses in the midterm elections. The Wall Street Journal described the episode as marking perhaps the starkest break Republicans have made from Trump since the start of his second term. Republicans have been particularly vocal about national-security risks and the technical competition posed by China. Just a few years earlier, the party had criticized the Biden administration after the United States shot down a Chinese surveillance balloon that entered the nation's airspace.

Huang has previously clashed with lawmakers in private briefings while arguing that lawmakers should allow Nvidia to ship its AI chips worldwide to combat Chinese competitors like Huawei and proliferate U.S. technology. Lawmakers are now attempting to pass several bills restricting the flow of chips and semiconductor-manufacturing equipment as part of the annual defense-spending package. The Wall Street Journal said the effort signaled that Nvidia's regulatory challenges would likely persist beyond Trump's term. Sen. Chuck Schumer of New York, the Senate's top Democrat, said there had been an “unfathomable failure of enforcement by the Trump administration.”

The Commerce Department recently took multiple steps to restrict China's access to chips following criticism of Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, The Wall Street Journal reported. Sen. David McCormick of Pennsylvania, a former top Commerce Department export-control official under the Bush administration, said the measures did not go far enough. He said Congress needs to act to protect sensitive technology while keeping the United States ahead in the AI race. “Everything else should only be considered within the context of that question,” McCormick said in an interview.


On Nov. 17, [MSI reported](/articles/2026-11-17-jensen-huang-offers-to-return-to-nvidia-and-agrees-to-testify-to-congress/) that Jensen Huang had offered to return as Nvidia's CEO and agreed to testify before House committees. His later reinstatement and the scheduled House and Senate appearances now put that agreement under bipartisan scrutiny.
