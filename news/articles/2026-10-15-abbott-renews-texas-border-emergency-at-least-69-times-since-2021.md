## Apprehensions dropped from more than 180,000 to about 8,000 a month

Texas Gov. Greg Abbott renewed his border security disaster proclamation in September, the latest in at least 69 renewals or amendments since he first declared the emergency at the Texas-Mexico border in 2021, ProPublica and The Texas Tribune reported. Twenty-one of those renewals have come during President Donald Trump's second term, which began in January 2025.

The renewal stands in sharp contrast to the numbers reported by the Trump administration, which says the drop in migrant crossings has been unprecedented and has reached historic lows since it took office. Border Patrol apprehensions along the southwest border have dropped to about 8,000 per month this year, from more than 180,000 in a single month at the time of Abbott's first declaration in May 2021, four months into President Joe Biden's term.

In his original order, Abbott argued that the federal government's inability or unwillingness to enforce immigration laws had created a humanitarian crisis in many communities in Texas. "The Biden Administration's open-border policies have enabled escalating violence from the cartels, dramatically increased the risk of human trafficking, and created a severe crisis of illegal drugs crossing into the United States," the order stated.

The Republican governor, who is running for his fourth term next month, has consolidated power like no Texas governor before him, building on predecessors' efforts to strengthen what by design is supposed to be a "weak governor" state as the constitution limits what can be done without legislative approval, ProPublica and The Texas Tribune have reported. The repeated emergency declarations have given Abbott powers to shift funds, suspend regulations and award no-bid contracts without going through the state Legislature. "I think it's unprecedented," said Cal Jillson, a political science professor at Southern Methodist University in Dallas. "I don't think that there is a similar example, certainly not as to the length of the emergency, and certainly not after the emergency has seemed to pass."

Since January 2025, two of the top agencies involved with border enforcement — the Texas Department of Public Safety and the Texas Military Department — have awarded more than $5 million in noncompetitive contracts citing the emergency, state records show. Items purchased include a Ford F-150, rifles and pistols, and apartments. Abbott's office did not respond to specific questions for this story, including about the noncompetitive contracts.

Deputy Press Secretary Madeline Waschak said in a written statement that the governor has been clear that border security is an ongoing priority. "The damage caused by four years of failed border policies under Democrat rule did not disappear overnight, and the progress we have made will not sustain itself without continued vigilance," Waschak wrote. "Securing the border requires constant, deliberate action to combat illegal crossings, cartel smuggling, fentanyl trafficking, and other criminal activity that threatens public safety in Texas. Governor Abbott will ensure the tools remain in place to safeguard our state as we work alongside the Trump Administration to deter, arrest, detain, and deport illegal immigrants," she added.

The border security declaration remains Abbott's longest continuously running emergency proclamation. He renewed his 2020 COVID-19 disaster declaration for three years, even as he downplayed the severity of the pandemic. "This is the quiet power of the governor," said Brandon Rottinghaus, a professor of political science at the University of Houston. "It's not something often that they brag about because the more that it becomes a political issue, the more that there might be some contestation of it."

The governor's second-longest-running emergency declaration, after border security, relates to drought. He declared a state disaster in July 2022 and within days amended it to bring the total of affected counties to 189. He has continued to renew it, though it now applies to about a third of the counties originally identified.

State lawmakers have repeatedly tried — and failed — to curb the governor's powers under the state's disaster act. Last year, legislators from both parties filed at least a handful of measures that would have required Abbott to get legislative approval to extend a disaster declaration, but none passed.

The emergency declarations have been accompanied by record spending on border security. Texas budgeted more than $11 billion during the Biden administration and allotted an additional $3 billion last year, after Trump took office. The spending is part of Operation Lone Star, an initiative Abbott launched in March 2021 to stem smuggling through Texas. Because of the emergency declaration, the governor has been able to shift money from other agencies, deploy the National Guard, build border barriers, charge immigrants with criminal trespassing for crossing through private property and get help from other states.

Much of the funding for Operation Lone Star is funneled through the Texas Military Department and the Texas Department of Public Safety, two of the agencies responsible for major enforcement efforts. State lawmakers allotted his office nearly $230 million last year to distribute through grants, largely to local prosecutors and government agencies helping the state with Operation Lone Star.

Though the situation on the ground has changed, the ability of state agencies to award noncompetitive contracts has remained constant. DPS and the Texas Military Department have awarded at least six noncompetitive contracts so far during the second Trump administration for work related to the operation, including a more than $2 million contract to Texas-based CTC Gunworks LLC for rifles and pistols for active-duty personnel. CTC Gunworks officials could not be reached for comment. The Texas Military Department referred questions about its procurement process to the governor's office.

A DPS spokesperson said in a written statement that even though the disaster declaration allows for expedited purchases, the department still follows best practices and "aims to select vendors offering the best value, determined by cost, quality, and ability to meet the specific disaster response requirements." The spokesperson said the specific contracts were purchases tied to existing state contracts.

The continued expansion of Operation Lone Star has come even as the initiative has been plagued with problems. A 2022 investigation by ProPublica, The Texas Tribune and The Marshall Project found that the state's reported success included arrests unrelated to the border or immigration, and counted drug seizures made by troopers from across the state who were not directly assigned to the operation. The Tribune and Army Times have also exposed poor working conditions, pay delays and suicides among National Guard members deployed as part of Operation Lone Star.

State lawmakers have begun to push back. During a Tribune-hosted event this week with state lawmakers from El Paso, Democratic state Rep. Mary González, vice chair of the Appropriations Committee, said legislators have already started to scale back border funding and there is interest from both sides of the aisle to continue doing so in the upcoming legislative session. "I think that the problem is that some of that border security money still lies in the governor's office which has no checks and balances and because we are still under an emergency — which is not an emergency anymore — an emergency border security statement which he can just use that money, you have seen some questionable appropriations under the veil of border security," she said.

Abbott has repeatedly said Texas' work in securing the border is not over. "We must secure the border not just for the next two years of the Trump administration, but we gotta do it forever," Abbott said at a press conference in early October, where he discussed the federal government's $7.5 billion reimbursement for border security expenses the state had incurred.

Rottinghaus said Texas will always have to confront border crossings and drug trafficking, but the existence of those problems does not always justify an emergency declaration. "Even someone who strongly supports the governor's board of policies might ask whether they want a future governor of the opposite party using the same interpretation of emergency authority for years," Rottinghaus said, adding that "governors, once they get a power, are hesitant to give it back."
