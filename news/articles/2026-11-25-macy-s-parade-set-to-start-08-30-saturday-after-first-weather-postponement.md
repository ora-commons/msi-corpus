## Macy's still working through logistics; Moroney and Swims Saturday status unclear

Macy's 100th annual Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York is expected to kick off at 08:30 local time (1330 BST) on Saturday. The retailer moved the centennial celebration off its Thanksgiving Day date because of severe weather — the parade's first postponement because of weather in its 100-year history — and said it was working through operational logistics, with details to come. It remains unclear whether country artist Megan Moroney and Grammy nominee Teddy Swims, both slated to perform at the flagship store, will take part on Saturday.

Preparations to hold the celebration, known for its massive balloons that float among the city's skyscrapers, had already begun when the forecast for heavy rain and strong winds on Thursday led to the delay, according to CBS News, BBC News's US media partner. A powerful coastal storm is set to affect the New York area in coming days, and a potent nor'easter is expected to make conditions too windy for the parade's iconic balloons to fly.

Macy's said, according to CBS News: "The safety of our participants and spectators is our top priority, and after close consultation with our City and agency partners, we have made the decision to move this year's celebration." Citing "expected severe weather," the retailer said it was working through operational logistics and would share details when they were finalized. BBC News reported it had contacted Macy's for comment.

NBC, which airs the parade on US television, will show the 2025 Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade on Thursday, the network said, even though the centennial celebration itself is now scheduled for Saturday.

The parade has never been postponed because of weather. Its last suspension came during World War Two, when New York went without the event from 1942 to 1944 as the US directed materials like rubber and helium toward the country's war effort. In 1971, Macy's decided to ground the balloons, which often feature beloved characters from pop culture, because of storms.

Originally called Macy's Christmas Parade, the celebration debuted in 1924 as a way to boost holiday sales and was founded by Macy's employees and their families. It also features live performers and massive balloons that float among New York City's skyscrapers.

The 2.5-mile (4-kilometer) procession begins at Macy's Herald Square flagship store and concludes in Manhattan's Upper West Side, with performers and spectators lining the streets to greet Santa Claus and ring in the holiday season.

The US National Weather Service has issued a high wind warning, valid from 10:00 local time (15:00 GMT) on Thursday until 01:00 (06:00 GMT) on Friday, with gusts potentially reaching 60 mph (97 km/h), although the very strongest winds are likely to occur after the parade was due to end. Heavy rain and coastal flooding will pose additional hazards across the eastern US as the storm sweeps through, while some inland parts could also see snow, BBC Weather reports. BBC Weather's Elizabeth Rizzini presented the weather forecast affecting travel over US Thanksgiving.

Country artist Megan Moroney and Grammy nominee Teddy Swims were slated to perform at the flagship store, but it is unclear whether they, along with the dozens of celebrities and float performers on the roster, would be returning on Saturday.

Macy's Thanksgiving parade had been set for Thursday as a powerful coastal storm approached New York, and [an earlier forecast had already raised the risk that the centennial parade's balloons could not fly](/articles/2026-11-23-thanksgiving-travel-faces-airline-outages-and-east-coast-storms/). The move to Saturday removes the question the forecast had raised.
