## AI-linked losses pull most Asian stock markets lower

**Subtype:** fact

- Heavy selling of Nvidia and other AI-linked shares pushed most Asian stock indexes lower on Wednesday.
- Taiwan's Taiex fell more than 6%, with TSMC down 8.1% and Mediatek down 10%.
- South Korea's Kospi lost 5.6%, with Samsung Electronics down 9.1% and SK Hynix down 8.6%.
- Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 3.6%, with SoftBank Group down 16.1% and Kioxia down 15.4%.
- Hong Kong's Hang Seng rose 1% after the index closed at its lowest level in more than a year a day earlier.
- Nvidia fell 5.9% on Tuesday after a 31.5% gain the previous session.

## Nvidia's return leaves governance and AI profitability questions unresolved

Most Asian stocks declined Wednesday after another round of heavy selling in AI-linked shares on Wall Street. U.S. futures slipped and oil prices also fell as the declines spread through regional trading.

Relief over Nvidia's decision to restore Jensen Huang as chief executive was tempered by questions about the company's corporate governance and President Donald Trump's influence. Nvidia announced Huang's return Monday, bringing back the leader who championed the AI industry, but the announcement did not settle broader concerns about the technology's financial returns.

Among the largest market declines, Taiwan's Taiex tumbled more than 6%. AI chipmaker Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. sank 8.1%, while chip designer Mediatek plunged 10%.

South Korea's Kospi lost 5.6% to 4,725.84. Samsung Electronics fell 9.1% and SK Hynix declined 8.6%. Japan's Nikkei 225 dropped 3.6% to 56,502.97, while OpenAI investor SoftBank Group slumped 16.1% and semiconductor maker Kioxia plunged 15.4%.

Hong Kong was an exception. The Hang Seng rose 1% to 22,449.86, regaining ground after the index closed at its lowest level in more than a year a day earlier. The Shanghai Composite fell 0.9% to 3,515.05, and Australia's S&P/ASX 200 edged 0.5% lower to 8,683.90.

Australia's consumer price index rose 4.9% from a year earlier in October, topping expectations. The reading could put pressure on the central bank to raise interest rates.

On Tuesday, AI-linked stocks dragged down the U.S. market after the previous day's surge quickly faded. A report showing improving confidence among U.S. consumers by more than economists expected helped lift most stocks in the S&P 500, but the concentrated losses in AI-related shares pulled the index down 0.5%, its eighth loss in nine days. The Nasdaq composite shed 1.5%, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 0.9%. The broader market had been mostly sinking since reaching an all-time high last month.

Nvidia fell 5.9% on Tuesday, a day after soaring 31.5% in its biggest gain in more than two decades. Other AI-related stocks posted similar declines. CoreWeave, which buys Nvidia chips and rents access to them over the cloud, tumbled 11.8%, while GE Vernova, which is helping to power AI data centers, dropped 4.8%.

Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI chatbot, sank 6.5% and dropped below $83, the price at which its shares first sold in last week's initial public offering. The stock had briefly traded as high as $115 on its first day of trading.

The selloff left unresolved whether AI can produce enough profit and productivity to justify massive investments in data centers. High interest rates were making it more difficult for companies to borrow money to finance the industry's growth. Sweeping victories for Democrats in the U.S. midterm elections earlier in November also raised the possibility of more AI regulation, including possible halts to further construction of data centers.

In the bond market, Treasury yields held relatively steady. The yield on the 10-year Treasury rose to 4.93% from 4.92% late Monday after drifting up and down during the session.

Brent crude, the international oil standard, climbed 2.4% to $88.78 a barrel on Tuesday. The price had ranged between $72 and $114 since the United States and Israel attacked Iran in February. Early Wednesday, Brent fell 1.3% to $87.67 a barrel, while U.S. benchmark crude dropped 1.5% to $80.86.

The U.S. dollar rose to 159.29 Japanese yen from 159.26 yen. The euro slipped to $1.0946 from $1.0966.


As [MSI reported on Nov. 24](/articles/2026-11-24-nvidia-s-rebound-fades-as-investors-question-the-ai-boom/), Nvidia's post-return rebound had already given way to investor doubts about the AI boom. The following session carried that reversal across most Asian markets while Huang's restoration, Nvidia's governance and the economics of data-center investment remained unresolved.
