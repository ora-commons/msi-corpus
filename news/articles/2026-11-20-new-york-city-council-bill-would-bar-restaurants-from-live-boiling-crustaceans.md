## Proposal would not prohibit serving lobsters and crabs prepared by other methods

Legislation introduced in the New York City Council and sponsored by Bronx Democrat Oswald Feliz and lower Manhattan's Harvey Epstein would, if passed, prohibit restaurants from boiling lobsters, crabs and other crustaceans alive. The proposal would still allow restaurants to serve lobster and crab prepared by other methods, so it would regulate the killing method rather than prohibit the seafood itself.

A first violation would carry no monetary penalty. A second or subsequent violation would carry a civil penalty of up to $100 per violation.

Suggested methods listed by The Guardian for preparing lobsters before cooking include chilling a lobster in the freezer for 15 to 20 minutes or packing it in crushed ice. The Cornwall Good Seafood Guide explained that “Once it is in a sleep state it can be humanely killed by physically severing the main nervous system and then draining the animal of blood.” The guide said lobsters can also be “quickly and more humanely killed” by cutting the underside along the centre line from the head to the tail. Crabs require a different technique involving spiking the underside to destroy the main nerve.

A team of Norwegian scientists also proposed death by electrocution as an alternative killing method. An accompanying Guardian photograph showed fresh lobsters after a morning at sea in Stonington, Maine, on 15 October. The image was credited to Charly Triballeau/AFP/Getty Images.

According to a memo accompanying the legislation, Feliz and Epstein cited “recent scientific studies” indicating that crustaceans commonly served in restaurants experience significant pain when boiled alive without first being stunned. The memo said, “New York City has a responsibility to ensure that these animals are being consumed ethically without being made to suffer unnecessarily.”

The memo also said, “A growing number of countries, particularly in Europe, have banned this practice in recent years.” Switzerland, Norway and New Zealand have adopted rules prohibiting the boiling of lobsters alive without first rendering them unconscious. The UK's Labour government has also said that boiling lobsters alive when conscious will be banned as part of a government strategy to improve animal welfare. British government ministers said last December that “live boiling is not an acceptable killing method.”

Feliz told the New York Post that “boiling an animal alive is the textbook definition of animal cruelty.” He challenged the idea that lobsters do not feel pain: “Some argue that lobsters don’t feel pain. If they don’t feel pain, why do they thrash around and desperately try to escape when thrown into boiling water?”

The crustacean-killing legislation is the latest effort by New York City lawmakers to restrict culinary practices on animal-welfare grounds.

Legislation to ban the sale of foie gras is caught up in a separate fight between city lawmakers and the state. Earlier this year, state attorney general Letitia James petitioned the state's top court to consider halting New York City's foie gras ban, saying the legislation “threatens farm operations in agricultural districts across the State.”
