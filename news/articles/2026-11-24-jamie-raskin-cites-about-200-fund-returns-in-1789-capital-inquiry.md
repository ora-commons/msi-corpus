## Jamie Raskin cites about 200% fund returns in 1789 Capital inquiry

## Hadrian contracts and Juul approvals follow earlier investments

Raskin used figures cited in his letters to place 1789 Capital's growth beside a series of transactions and government decisions that he said warranted scrutiny. The House Judiciary Committee's top Democrat said the Florida-based firm had grown from about $150m (£113m) in assets under management to about $3bn, while its main fund recorded returns of about 200% by June. Trump Jr. joined 1789 Capital as a partner four days after his father's victory in the 2024 presidential election.

Raskin wrote that 1789 Capital's “striking success” in predicting the award of government contracts or the release of favorable regulations suggested either that Trump Jr. had acquired “magical new clairvoyant powers” or that there was “a brazen conspiracy … to enrich the first family beyond the wildest dreams of any other presidential family.”

Raskin said Hadrian, a California-based defense manufacturer, received a $900m US Navy commitment to produce submarine components and an $80m Army manufacturing contract in March, two months after 1789 Capital acquired a stake. Raskin said that sequence warranted scrutiny.

Raskin also highlighted donations by Hadrian chief executive Chris Power, who gave nearly $500,000 to the Republican National Committee and its legal fund in July. Four days later, Trump issued an executive order intended to increase the Pentagon's reliance on domestic manufacturers. Hadrian was subsequently valued at almost $8bn in an investment round in August, up from $1.6bn earlier in the year.

Raskin highlighted that 1789 Capital acquired a $5m stake in the e-cigarette maker Juul in March 2025. Four months later, the Food and Drug Administration reversed an earlier decision that had threatened Juul's survival and authorized five Juul products, including menthol pods. The FDA authorized three more products in August this year.

Raskin noted that the Wall Street Journal reported in May that Trump pressed then-FDA commissioner Marty Makary to accelerate approval of flavored vaping products. Juul has faced longstanding criticism over the appeal of its products to teenagers, although the company says it is committed to helping adult smokers switch from combustible cigarettes.

Polymarket, the prediction market platform, was the subject of a $1.4m penalty in 2022 for operating an unregistered trading platform. The Justice Department and Commodity Futures Trading Commission closed investigations into the company in July 2025. Days later, Polymarket announced a $112m purchase of a federally licensed exchange and clearing house, paving the way for its return to the US market. It disclosed an investment from 1789 Capital the following month. Raskin said the firm's investment could have increased dramatically in value, with Polymarket reportedly seeking a funding round that would value it at $21bn, compared with a reported $300m when 1789 Capital first invested.

GrabAGun, the online firearms retailer, went public in July 2025 through a merger with a special purpose acquisition company affiliated with 1789 Capital's principals. Trump Jr. subsequently joined its board. In May, the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives proposed a rule that would make it easier for online retailers to ship firearms directly to customers. GrabAGun told investors it was uniquely positioned to benefit from the change. The National Shooting Sports Foundation, the industry's biggest trade association, opposed the proposal, warning it could facilitate illegal firearms sales.

The company-specific requests extend an inquiry Raskin launched in August. He asked GrabAGun, Hadrian Automation, Juul Labs and Polymarket for two years of communications and financial documents concerning their dealings with 1789 Capital. The requested material includes investment agreements, valuations, due diligence and communications with the firm and its representatives dating back to 1 November 2024.

Raskin said the inquiry would examine whether Trump Jr. and his business partners used political connections or advance knowledge of government decisions to identify investment opportunities or helped secure contracts and regulatory changes for companies in which they had financial interests. He also asked all four companies to preserve relevant records, including communications on encrypted messaging services such as Signal, WhatsApp and Telegram, and to identify any deleted material.

The inquiry comes as Democrats prepare to take control of the House in January following their dramatic gains in the midterm elections. Raskin, who is expected to become Judiciary Committee chairman, has made scrutiny of alleged financial conflicts involving the Trump administration and the president's family a priority.

Trump Jr., 48, and 1789 Capital, which is based in Palm Beach, Florida, deny wrongdoing. The firm's lawyer, Bill Burck, said Raskin had failed to address a September response setting out what the firm regarded as flaws in his understanding of its investments. “Facts are facts and Congressman Raskin has them wrong here, but persists in his tall tales against 1789,” Burck told the Associated Press. He described the allegations as personal attacks unsupported by evidence.

At an Axios event last week, Trump Jr. rejected accusations that he had exploited his father's political position for financial gain. “I can see what they are saying. It doesn’t make it so,” he said, adding that he should not be expected to suspend his business activities for the duration of his father's political career.

GrabAGun described Raskin's letter as a political document and said the allegations were baseless. It said the proposed firearms shipping rule had been widely discussed in the industry and that GrabAGun's input had been made through public comments. Juul said it was reviewing the letter and intended to cooperate with congressional efforts to understand its business.
