## Ericksen finds attack premeditated, rejects 'political statement' framing

A federal judge sentenced a Minnesota man to 14 months in prison Thursday for spraying a mixture of water and apple cider vinegar at Rep. Ilhan Omar during a Minneapolis town hall in January, closing a federal prosecution that emerged during one of the most volatile stretches of the city's recent immigration-enforcement operations.

U.S. District Judge Joan N. Ericksen handed down the sentence to 56-year-old Anthony Kazmierczak nearly eight months after the January 27 assault. Kazmierczak had agreed to a plea deal with prosecutors on a charge of assaulting a U.S. officer. He was also sentenced to three years of supervised release that includes a no-contact order with Omar, and U.S. marshals escorted him from the courtroom.

Ericksen rejected any characterization of the act as political protest. "This wasn't a spur-of-the-moment act," the judge said before sentencing, noting that Kazmierczak had secured tickets to the town hall weeks in advance. "An attack on a public figure is not a political statement. It is misguided, selfish and inconsistent with any concept of a free or democratic society," Ericksen added. She also said spraying anything at an elected official "is a terrifying thing. And you knew that when you did it."

The attack occurred during a town-hall meeting in Minneapolis, where witnesses saw Kazmierczak jump up from the audience and spray liquid from a syringe at Omar while saying she was "splitting Minnesota apart," according to the Guardian. Omar's security officers tackled him and turned him over to police, who arrested him. Omar was not injured and continued with the town hall after the arrest. Authorities later determined he had sprayed her with a mixture of water and apple cider vinegar.

Kazmierczak, who has been diagnosed with Parkinson's disease, shook visibly at times as he sat at the defense table. He spoke quietly and haltingly, occasionally wiping away tears as he stood before Ericksen. "I've spent enough time in jail thinking," Kazmierczak said, his voice barely audible. "I really apologize to her for having put her through that," he added, referring to Omar. He had earlier written to her, "I'm sorry if I scared you."

In an earlier hearing, Kazmierczak was unable to explain his actions, telling Ericksen only "it's fuzzy" when she asked what he remembered. His attorney, John Fossum, argued that Kazmierczak had not been taking his mental-health medications before the attack, lowering his inhibitions and contributing to what happened.

A government sentencing memorandum submitted by prosecutors said Kazmierczak had texted at least three people indicating he planned to do something at the meeting involving Omar "but would not answer their questions about his intent." He also sent messages to a neighbor arranging for dog care and requesting bail-bond-company contact information. The memo said Kazmierczak had long struggled with alcohol abuse and "a history of mental and emotional health concerns" along with health issues tied to his 2022 Parkinson's diagnosis.

In social-media posts, Kazmierczak had criticized Joe Biden — who was president between Trump's two Oval Office terms — and referred to Democrats as "angry and liars." He wrote that Trump wants the United States to be "stronger and more prosperous." Omar, a refugee from Somalia, has long been a target of Donald Trump's anti-immigrant rhetoric across his two presidencies. In 2019, after Omar's election to Congress, Trump said she should "go back" to her country of birth. He has described her as "garbage" and said she should be investigated.

The sentencing came against a backdrop of heightened tension in Minneapolis tied to an immigration crackdown that led to protests, federal arrests and the killing of two U.S. citizens by federal officers. The attack occurred as the city was on edge, with protesters frequently facing off against convoys of immigration officers and conservatives criticizing the protesters.
