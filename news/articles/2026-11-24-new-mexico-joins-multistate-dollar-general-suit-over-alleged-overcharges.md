## Regulators found failures in 53% of 73 pricing inspections

On Monday, the New Mexico Department of Justice announced that New Mexico had joined a lawsuit against Dollar General with the Federal Trade Commission and a 30-state coalition. The complaint, filed in the U.S. District Court for the Middle District of Tennessee, alleges that the Tennessee-headquartered discount chain violated federal and state consumer-protection laws by overcharging consumers at checkout. Dollar General operates more than 20,000 stores nationwide.

The FTC and 30 states allege that Dollar General systematically charges more at checkout than the prices advertised. The complaint says the company has not implemented safeguards to correct the issue.

The lawsuit cites at least 29,265 complaints categorized as “Did Not Honor Marked Price” between Jan. 1, 2023, and March 31, 2026, in all states where Dollar General operates. It says the complaints “demonstrate widespread consumer harm and confirm that customers nationwide have been charged more than the advertised price.”

Undercover FTC agents made purchases earlier this year at Dollar General locations in Ohio, Missouri, Pennsylvania and Maryland, and those transactions resulted in overcharges compared with the advertised prices.

New Mexico state regulators conducted 73 pricing inspections at Dollar General stores across the state from January through April. Fifty-three percent of the inspections resulted in failures, and one store in Clovis failed three consecutive inspections.

New Mexico Attorney General Raúl Torrez said, “Families across our state, particularly those in rural communities who rely on Dollar General for affordable groceries and everyday necessities, should not have to worry about being overcharged when they check out.”

Dollar General disagreed with the lawsuit’s allegation that it “willfully disregarded the affordability needs of our customers.” The company said, “Nothing could be further from the truth,” and stated, “Our goal is 100% accuracy between shelf and register prices in every store.”

The plaintiffs asked the court to order Dollar General to stop and prevent the practices alleged in the complaint, provide restitution to affected consumers and pay civil penalties.

The New Mexico Department of Justice brings the lawsuit under the state’s Unfair Practices Act. New Mexico has used the consumer-protection law against vape retailers and Meta, which the First Judicial District Court ordered to pay $25 billion in October.

Torrez also said, “Pricing transparency is a basic responsibility of every retailer.” He added, “Consumers should not be forced to spend additional time checking receipts, challenging incorrect charges or returning to stores to recover money they should never have been charged in the first place.”


[MSI previously reported](/articles/2026-10-26-ftc-and-20-states-sue-dollar-general-over-alleged-register-overcharges/) that the FTC and 20 states had sued Dollar General over alleged register overcharges, and [later detailed](/articles/2026-10-31-dollar-general-drew-over-29-000-price-complaints-across-48-states-ftc-suit-says/) the complaint’s count of more than 29,000 price complaints across 48 states. New Mexico’s addition expands the plaintiff group to 30 states and adds state inspection results, while Dollar General continues to dispute the allegations.
