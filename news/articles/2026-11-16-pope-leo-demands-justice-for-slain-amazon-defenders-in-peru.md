## Pope cites 57 killings of environmental defenders between 2010 and 2024

Pope Leo XIV travelled from high in the Andes into the Peruvian Amazon on Sunday, the most ambitious day of his three-nation South American trip, and addressed Indigenous representatives and missionaries gathered along the Ucayali River in the north-eastern city of Pucallpa.

"The cry of the Earth and the cry of the poor are inseparable," Leo told the crowd, many of whom wore traditional feathered headwear and face markings of the region.

The pope said he came to Pucallpa to support the community in its search for justice and peace, and referred to what environmental groups estimate have been 57 murders of environmental defenders between 2010 and 2024. "The blood of these brothers cries out to God and demands justice," Leo said to applause.

The speech channelled one of Pope Francis's core teachings linking care for the most vulnerable with care for creation. In addition to deforestation, illegal gold mining has become an increasingly lucrative business in the Amazon, devastating forests and polluting rivers, and illegal mining and coca production have also driven deforestation. Leo addressed the combination of threats drawing on his years of experience as a Peruvian bishop and Francis's preaching about the environment and the plight of Indigenous groups.

During the encounter and a subsequent mass at the Villa Deportiva Regional Ucayali, Leo demanded a different model of economic development that respects the Earth and the Indigenous peoples who care for it, rather than the "irresponsible exploitation" by outside actors.

"Very little has been done – and very slowly at that – while the means to exploit and pollute creation have accelerated," he said at mass. "In the face of these evils, let us not bury the Lord's gifts, for he gives us the strength to repair and heal the suffering caused by an insatiable thirst for domination."

In Peru, most murders of environmental defenders go unpunished; only a few have resulted in convictions. The most recent conviction came in the case of Kichwa leader Quinto Inuma Alvarado, who was murdered in 2023 when he was ambushed by hitmen on a river in the San Martín region, the Amazonian territory he was defending.

Leo also referred to a rise in sexual violence against Indigenous Awajún and Wampis schoolgirls in the northern Amazon, and criticized the "impunity" and "corruption" surrounding the cases. The Awajún and Wampis Women's Council has recorded 370 cases of sexual violence in the Condorcanqui province between January and July.

Leo's last full day in Peru was also his most ambitious, crisscrossing the nation to minister to communities high in the Andes. His first stop was the mountain city of Cusco, where huge crowds greeted him. Peru's air force released footage of the pope inside a helicopter and called it "an unforgettable memory" to take Leo around the country.

"It is all worth it," said Margarida Salomon, 63, who said she came from Sucre in Bolivia to see the pontiff, spending about $500 to take a bus and sleep in a tent. "I am still getting to know this pope, but the fact he is here in the Andes means a lot," she said.

The visit to Cusco, 3,400m (more than 11,000ft) above sea level, tested Leo's stamina as he continues recovering from lung cancer surgery last month. Leo sat in the chair of his popemobile during part of the ride around Cusco, though it was not clear whether that was to preserve his energy or to steady himself on the uneven streets.

The 71-year-old pontiff has seemed unfazed by the toll of the trip, the longest of his pontificate.
