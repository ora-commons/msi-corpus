## NTSB had no Monday update; spokesperson expects preliminary report within 30 days

Four of the 32 people transported for treatment remained hospitalized Monday after the Atlantic Asphalt, a 520-foot (160-meter) tanker, struck a smaller river cruise ship docked on the Mississippi River in New Orleans on Friday, according to New Orleans officials. One of the remaining patients was in serious condition. All 190 passengers and crew members aboard the cruise ship survived, and officials said no one suffered critical injuries. The smaller ship's crew had handed life jackets to passengers and was evacuating them when the vessel overturned, sending dozens into the river. New Orleans Mayor Helena Moreno called it “a miracle on the Mississippi.”

The National Transportation Safety Board released no new information Monday. An agency spokesperson said the NTSB expects to release a preliminary report on the crash within 30 days, and a 16-member team plans to spend a week in New Orleans investigating the collision. U.S. authorities are still working to learn what caused the steering failure.

In an earlier update Sunday, NTSB officials said the Atlantic Asphalt's steering system failed, causing its rudder to become stuck. The tanker veered toward the river's east bank and crashed into the docked cruise ship.

Salvatore Mercogliano, a maritime historian at Campbell University in North Carolina, said the steering most likely failed because of a mechanical breakdown in the ship's hydraulic or electrical systems. He said human error also may have been a factor.

U.S. Coast Guard records show no problems were reported from the tanker's last inspection on Oct. 19. Separately, inspections at U.S. and Canadian ports revealed more than a dozen safety and mechanical problems on the Atlantic Asphalt during the past seven years.

In July and August 2024, the U.S. Coast Guard reported back-to-back malfunctions in its online records. First, the ship's captain reported that its autopilot system was malfunctioning as the Atlantic Asphalt headed to port in Baltimore. The following month, the tanker “experienced loss of communication between the bridge and main engine,” according to a Coast Guard report.

Mercogliano said the two 2024 U.S. malfunctions seemed fairly common and not very concerning, as were six other mechanical deficiencies the Coast Guard reported aboard the tanker since 2019. According to Coast Guard documents, all of the malfunctions and mechanical deficiencies it reported were soon fixed. The most recent issue was reported in June, when an inspection found faulty wiring to the lighting in the ship's pump room.

Inspections at Canadian ports also uncovered problems on the tanker, including four fire safety deficiencies reported in 2024 and an issue last year with the ship's emergency electrical generator, according to online public maritime records.

Built in 2017, the Atlantic Asphalt specializes in carrying large quantities of asphalt and bitumen, a semi-solid substance often used for road construction and roofing, according to the ship-tracking website MarineTraffic. The tanker is owned by Nave Shipping SA, which is registered in Monrovia, Liberia, according to the International Maritime Organization. Nave Shipping is a subsidiary of the China State Shipbuilding Corporation, a Chinese state-owned conglomerate.

The company that manages the tanker, OSM Thome, expressed regret over the collision in a statement over the weekend. “OSM Thome is fully complying with the local authorities as they carry out the necessary investigations,” the company said. The company did not immediately respond to an email seeking further comment Monday.

The NTSB says it recovered the tanker's voyage data recorder, which is similar to an airplane's flight data recorder. The recorder contains information such as the ship's speed and audio from the bridge. Investigators have also urged people with photos or video of the crash to submit them to the NTSB.

Port of New Orleans officials have filed a federal lawsuit saying the tanker caused at least $1 million in damage to the port's terminal. A judge issued a warrant granting U.S. officials control of the tanker, which was towed away from the crash site.

The cruise ship remains on its side in the river, and moving it could take weeks, according to the U.S. Coast Guard. The agency said crews must first remove 16,000 gallons (60,500 liters) of fuel from the ship.
