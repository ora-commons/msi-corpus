## A 52-year-old Chinese national faces fraud charges in Singapore

Alice Huang, the romantic partner of ousted Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, was the public face of Megaspeed, the Singapore company suspected of funneling advanced computing power to China. Behind the scenes, The Wall Street Journal reported, was Li Ming, a 52-year-old Chinese national now facing fraud charges in Singapore.

The Journal's account places Li at the center of the company's operations rather than at its edges. Li was deeply involved in Megaspeed's activities and major decisions, the newspaper reported, while also playing a role in other companies trading Nvidia chips.

Those links had not previously been reported. The Journal attributed the findings to reporting by Stu Woo, who it said uncovered the connections behind a scandal that brought down one of Silicon Valley's biggest names — Jensen Huang, the Nvidia chief executive the newspaper described as ousted.

Alice Huang's position at Megaspeed was the visible one. The Journal described her as the public face of a company that U.S. authorities suspect of channeling advanced computing power toward China, a country that is subject to restrictions on the export of the most capable Nvidia chips.

The scandal has also reached into the administration's economic team. The commerce secretary, Lutnick, has battled with key players in the administration yet proved remarkably difficult to dislodge, officials and allies told the Journal, aided by Trump's fondness for his longtime friend.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent recently blamed Lutnick for slow-walking chip restrictions at the heart of the Nvidia scandal, according to the Journal. Sen. Tom Cotton said Lutnick repeatedly ignored warnings that U.S. AI chips were being smuggled to China. Lutnick also faces scrutiny over whether his decisions benefit his family's firm.


The Journal's account of Li's role adds a named figure to a case MSI has followed since [Nov. 9](/articles/2026-11-09-u-s-justice-department-probes-megaspeed-over-suspected-nvidia-chip-diversion/), when MSI reported that the Justice Department was investigating Megaspeed over suspected diversion of Nvidia's advanced AI chips to China.
