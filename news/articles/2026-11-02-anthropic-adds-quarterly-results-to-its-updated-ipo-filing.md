## Updated document shows $23.3 billion loss and $895.4 billion in AI computing and infrastructure commitments

The updated filing covered Anthropic's latest three months. Anthropic reported $17 billion in revenue for the quarter and a $23.3 billion net loss, according to the document.

For the first nine months of the year, Anthropic said revenue grew by 13 times to $33.2 billion. The company reported a $104 billion net loss for the same period.

The nine-month loss included a $94 billion charge for an accounting adjustment related to financing agreements. The document, like the earlier filing, showed Anthropic's revenue growing while profit, as with its AI rivals, remained elusive.

Anthropic also disclosed that it had committed $895.4 billion to AI computing and infrastructure, including data centers. The company said $70 billion of that total was due to be spent during the next 12 months.

The disclosures came about a week after Anthropic issued its original IPO filing in preparation for an expected Wall Street debut later this month. The Monday document supplied updated figures rather than serving as Anthropic's first IPO filing.

The filing did not indicate how much Anthropic ultimately expected to raise or what the shares will be priced at when it goes public. It therefore left two basic terms of the offering unresolved.

Anthropic is based in San Francisco and is the company behind the Claude chatbot. The company has previously said it will list its stock on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker symbol ANT.

Together, the disclosures present a company with $17 billion in quarterly revenue, a $23.3 billion quarterly net loss and $895.4 billion in committed AI computing and infrastructure spending. The filing placed Anthropic's financial results and those commitments alongside its IPO preparations while leaving the amount sought and initial share price unstated.

For prospective investors, the filing's operating disclosures were more complete than its IPO terms: Anthropic provided quarterly and nine-month results, plus $895.4 billion in AI computing and infrastructure commitments, but not the amount it expected to raise or the initial price of its shares.
