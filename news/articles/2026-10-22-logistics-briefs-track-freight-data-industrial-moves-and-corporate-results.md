## Thieves took nearly $1 million in trading cards from FedEx truck

The Wall Street Journal's Esther Fung reported that thieves stole nearly $1 million worth of trading cards from a FedEx truck in Santa Ana, California, the previous week. The freight roundup said thieves were finding weak links in transportation networks to steal high-value cargo. The truck was carrying cards from Professional Sports Authenticator, which helps owners value their collections, and was going to ship the cards back.

Santa Ana police said two pallets of sports and Pokémon cards were taken from the FedEx truck, while a third pallet showed signs of being disturbed. The truck's lock was also gone. Professional Sports Authenticator said the items were not in its possession at the time of the burglary and that it learned of the theft from the shipping carrier. The company said it deactivated the certificates of the stolen items, notified marketplace partners and was taking steps to compensate owners for the fair market value of their stolen cards.

In the RAV4 background report, The Wall Street Journal's Christopher Otts and Owen Tucker-Smith reported that North American production of the Toyota RAV4 plunged 30% through September and U.S. RAV4 sales were down 29% this year. Toyota's U.S. dealers had about four days' worth of the crossover on their lots, buyers had to order months ahead and nearly every RAV4 that arrived at a dealership was already spoken for. The report described the RAV4 as America's bestselling vehicle that was not a pickup truck and the country's most popular SUV.

Toyota produced few RAV4s in January and February after two plants in Ontario, Canada, underwent retrofits for the overhauled 2026 model. Its Kentucky plant got a late start after Toyota decided not to phase out the RAV4 there. The supply crunch followed Toyota's decision to keep producing the 2025 model well into the fourth quarter of 2025. Carmakers normally build buffer stocks before factories are reworked for a new model, but an analyst said the strategy was harder for Toyota because its vehicles sell at a faster rate.
