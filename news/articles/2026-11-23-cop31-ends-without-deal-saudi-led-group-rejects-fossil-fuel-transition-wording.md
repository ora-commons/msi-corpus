## Nearly 40 countries reject proposed text after extended final session

The COP31 summit in Antalya, Turkey, ended without a deal after more than two weeks of talks, according to The Guardian. The final negotiating session ran more than 40 hours past its deadline. More than 130 countries demanded that the final agreement endorse the “transition away from fossil fuels,” but the proposed Antalya text did not.

The proposed Antalya pact invited nations to electrify their economies, called on wealthy countries to fund climate adaptation for poorer countries and urged signatories to minimize any “overshoot” of the Paris agreement’s 1.5C global heating threshold.

The pact also included provisions on carbon capture and storage technology. Critics argued that those provisions would perpetuate fossil-fuel use.

Ireland’s environment minister, Darragh O’Brien, said the proposed deal “failed to meet the moment.” The Guardian reported that major powers rejected what they called an “unacceptable deal,” which “failed even to mention fossil fuels.”

According to The Guardian’s environment editor, who was at the summit, a small group led by Saudi Arabia refused to accept any mention of a “transition away from fossil fuels.” The refusal came after the marathon negotiating session.

The environment editor said such holdouts normally would force through what the editor called a “watered-down compromise.” This time, nearly 40 nations — led by the European Union, the United Kingdom, small-island countries and least-developed countries — “broke with precedent and openly rejected the weakened text,” the editor wrote.

The editor wrote that the countries were “refusing to salvage the usual face-saving semblance of unity.” The countries’ rejection left the summit without a final agreement to guide countries’ climate action, and the proposed Antalya text still lacked language on transitioning away from fossil fuels.
