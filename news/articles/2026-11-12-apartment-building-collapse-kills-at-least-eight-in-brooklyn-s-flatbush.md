## Mayor pledges accountability as officials investigate 100 sites linked to collapse

A three-story apartment building collapsed Wednesday afternoon near a construction site on Flatbush Avenue in the Flatbush neighborhood of Brooklyn. City officials have confirmed at least eight deaths — six adults and two children — and at least 18 other people sustained non-fatal but serious injuries.

Mayor Zohran Mamdani said at a press conference Thursday that officials are working with the Department of Buildings to investigate 100 other sites in New York City with connections to the building that gave way. "If there is anyone or any entity operating in this city that bears responsibility for what happened, we will hold them fully accountable," Mamdani said.

Ahmed Tigani, the Department of Buildings commissioner, said at the same briefing that no violations or complaints in the area had been reported regarding the building or the nearby construction, but that examinations will continue. He echoed the mayor's pledge that anyone or any entity found responsible would be held fully accountable.

During the investigation, officials have ordered the evacuation of all residents in the three buildings still standing that were previously connected to the collapsed structure.

As of Thursday morning, the New York City Police Department had rerouted several buses and blocked off Flatbush Avenue along Bedford Avenue and Clarendon Road to make space for responders and firefighters continuing their search for victims. Media teams, residents and employees of the small businesses along the street congregated on the sidewalk trying to get a view of the rubble.

Prudence Foster, a retired utility worker with ConEdison who has lived in the neighborhood most of her life, was among those confronting the aftermath. "The last time I saw something like this was when I went to work at 9/11," she said. Foster, who lives on 19th Street about a 20-minute walk from the wreckage, said she heard helicopters on her way home Wednesday and then received a call from a friend about the building's destruction.

"I was glad the building didn't blow up because of the gas lines," Foster said. "Who is going to be held accountable for the loss of lives?" She said her biggest concern is the residents left in the three standing buildings, and she said the city should consider taking them down before they also collapse.

James Williams, a Brooklyn native who works at the Chelsea Deli Grocery directly across the street from the wreckage, said he heard hours of loud banging from the construction site before the building came down. From about 7 a.m. to 4 p.m., he said, the noises "sounded like a big gunshot." Williams said he was familiar with the construction workers, who often came into the deli for lunch.

At about 4 p.m., Williams said, he smelled metal fumes and saw bricks falling from the building; about two minutes later, he watched it sink. "There were people trying to get out," he said. "I don't even really want to think about that. I couldn't even go over there to even help anybody."

The Red Cross set up a supply center at the Flatbush YMCA down the street from the collapse, providing temporary housing, social services and mental health support. "The city has come together to stand along those who are experiencing this hardship," Mamdani said.

Staff at Cyrus Medical Group, which sits across the street from the rubble, spent Thursday morning checking surveillance cameras to see whether any of their patients were caught in the collapse. "We don't know anything yet," said Winsome Rogers, a New York-based family nurse practitioner who has worked at the practice for less than a year. Rogers, who was working at another site Wednesday when she heard the news, described the area as usually calm. She said she is concerned about unhealthy air and dust coming from the wreckage that might endanger firefighters and others near it. "It's a possibility," she said of that risk. "I hope whoever's in the rubble is wearing masks."

Wis Santilous, who works at a nearby homeless shelter, said he hoped no one from the shelter was involved in the collapse. "I saw it on social media and I was like: 'Let me come down here and see what's going on,'" he said. "Everybody just wants everybody to just be alright. Who knows how many people could've just passed away in front of us?"
