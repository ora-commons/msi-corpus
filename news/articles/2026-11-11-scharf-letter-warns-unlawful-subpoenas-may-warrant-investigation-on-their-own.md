## Supreme Court has held that congressional investigations cannot be used for harassment

White House counsel Will Scharf wrote in a letter to incoming House Oversight Committee chairman Robert Garcia, dated earlier this week, that congressional investigations are subject to constitutional limitations. The letter says the Supreme Court has held that congressional investigations must further a legitimate legislative task and cannot be used for harassment.

Scharf wrote that "threats to executive branch officials designed to deter them from public service, or process designed merely to abuse or instill fear in the recipient may cross the line into blatant criminality," and the letter says unlawful subpoenas may warrant investigation on their own.

Scharf wrote in the letter that "any forthcoming investigative processes will be deployed to harass and aggrandize, rather than to advance any legitimate legislative end." He made that statement after citing "abusive threats and public statements about the indiscriminate issuance of subpoenas" and threats directed at prospective targets within the administration.

The letter also says the administration is "monitoring the abusive and capricious threats that are now proliferating from Democrat legislators who seek to abuse the legislative process to intimidate and to interfere with the lawful functioning of government," and it adds that some Democrats have crossed the line with verbal threats that may be criminal. The letter was first reported by Punchbowl News.

The Wall Street Journal reported that the letter is an early sign of the aggressive and combative posture the White House plans to take against investigations and of its intent to go after any Democrat that officials believe has crossed a line. Over the weekend, President Trump said in a Truth Social post that he viewed investigation threats as "extortion at the highest level" and said Democrats should be held accountable.

Garcia, a Democratic representative from California, is set to chair the Oversight Committee when Democrats take control of Congress in January. A spokeswoman for the House Oversight Committee didn't immediately respond to a request for comment. When Democrats officially won the majority, Garcia's statement was to "lawyer up."

House and Senate Democrats have a lengthy list of topics they say they plan to investigate once they take control of Congress in January. At the top of the list are corporations that have cut deals with the administration, as well as companies that donated to the president and his legacy projects, such as the White House ballroom.

The investigations are expected to stretch beyond the Oversight Committee and include Judiciary, Intelligence and other panels.

Democratic lawmakers don't expect White House officials to cooperate willingly with investigations and have talked about using their subpoena power to push officials to engage. They also expect to investigate corporations that have worked with the administration, anticipating that companies may be more willing to cooperate.

Scharf, a former prosecutor and the president's personal attorney before Trump's second term, defended him against federal criminal charges alleging interference in the 2020 election. He was named White House counsel in August after serving as staff secretary.

The correspondence puts the White House on record warning the incoming committee chair before Democrats take control of Congress in January, when House and Senate Democrats say they plan to investigate the administration and connected corporations and have talked about using their subpoena power to push officials to engage.
