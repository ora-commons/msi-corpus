## Judge found Trump plausibly pleaded actual malice over the documentary edit

The BBC has lodged an urgent appeal to the United States court of appeals for the 11th circuit, asking the court to throw out Donald Trump's $10bn defamation lawsuit. The broadcaster is contesting a ruling by federal judge Jeffrey T Kuntz, whom Trump nominated, that denied the BBC's attempt to have the case thrown out and allowed the case to proceed in Florida over a Panorama documentary that combined two parts of Trump's speech before the 6 January attack at the US Capitol. The BBC's lawyers say the programme never aired in the United States and that the BBC did not have the rights to broadcast it there.

In a legal submission seen by The Guardian, the BBC's lawyers stated that the documentary "never aired in the United States". They said the BBC did not produce, film or air the programme in Florida, but broadcast it on UK television channels and made it available on the UK iPlayer streaming service. The filing also said: "The BBC never broadcast it in the US, nor did it have the rights to do so." The filing said third parties acquired international distribution rights but never aired the programme in the United States.

The filing says the Florida court "ignored the BBC's rigorous and systematic efforts to prevent access to the documentary and its other streaming content in the United States". The BBC's lawyers also argue that Kuntz found the Florida court had jurisdiction "despite the complete lack of evidence that anyone accessed it there, as both Florida law and this circuit's precedent require".

The corporation's lawyers called the ruling an error that violates due process and the doctrine of international comity: "This erroneous exercise of jurisdiction over the BBC is anathema to the due process requirements of personal jurisdiction and to the comity interests in allowing other sovereign nations to police their own affairs."

"We are surprised and disappointed by the court's decision that this case should be heard in Florida," said a BBC spokesperson. "As we have made clear from the start, the BBC broadcast this documentary in the UK – and UK only. We did not show it in Florida, nor did any other broadcaster." The spokesperson added: "Given the urgency of this issue, we are now requesting a higher court review the ruling."

The documentary featured in the 2024 Panorama programme Trump: A Second Chance. It spliced together two sections of a Trump speech delivered before the 6 January attack at the US Capitol, taken from moments almost an hour apart. The edit suggested that Trump told a crowd: "We're going to walk down to the Capitol and I'll be there with you, and we fight. We fight like hell."

The BBC has apologised over the edit but has always denied that the film defamed Trump. It has also said the documentary was never broadcast in Florida, where Trump's legal action is being heard.

Kuntz ruled earlier this month that Trump had sufficiently pleaded actual malice on behalf of the BBC's editing. "Accepting the allegations in the complaint as true, the court finds Trump plausibly pled damages where Trump alleges a defamatory documentary released toward the election caused reputational harm," he wrote.

Kuntz was nominated to his current role by Trump and confirmed by a Senate vote earlier this year that split down party lines, with Republicans in favour and Democratic and independent senators voting against the appointment. Three senators did not vote.

Trump described the ruling on his Truth Social site as a "huge victory against fake news BBC".

The case is currently heading to court next year. Trump remains due to give a deposition, in which he will be questioned by lawyers under oath, but the appearance has been postponed until January.

The litigation comes as the BBC makes huge cuts at home and holds talks with the UK government over its future funding, The Guardian reported, which described the case as taking the BBC's time and adding to the corporation's expense and political pressure.

The appeal asks the 11th circuit to decide whether a Florida court may hear a $10bn defamation claim against the BBC over a documentary the BBC says it broadcast in the UK and had no US broadcast rights to air.
