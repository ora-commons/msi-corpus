## Anti-Rohingya hate speech preceded the 2017 operations, Iwasawa said

The International Court of Justice's judges estimated that around 815,000 Rohingya lived in northern Rakhine State before the 2017 military operations. Delivering the judgment, Judge Yuji Iwasawa said the scale and nature of the military's actions went "well beyond what would be required to punish the population."

Iwasawa also said the actions were "characteristic of an intent to destroy a substantial part of the group."

He said the operations were preceded by an atmosphere of anti-Rohingya hate speech, with the community portrayed as "sub-human" and "a violent threat."

The judges concluded that around 10,000 Rohingya were violently killed during the 2017 clearance campaign.

The court's overall judgment found Myanmar responsible for genocide against the Rohingya during the 2017 military operations in northern Rakhine State. It also found that Myanmar's security forces "acted with genocidal intent."
