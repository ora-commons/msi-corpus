## Data-center financing and Chinese competition remain unresolved after Huang's return

On Nov. 24, Nvidia shares slid as the market rally that followed Jensen Huang's return lost momentum at the Nasdaq. BBC News economics editor Faisal Islam said the shares were down almost 30% over six months, with that decline beginning before the scandal involving Huang emerged earlier this month.

Islam described the concerns about the staying power of the tech boom as legitimate and said they had not gone away with Huang's return. He described investors as jittery and said the extraordinary volatility reflected a fragile mood in the market.

Anthropic, the first purely AI company to be publicly listed, was trading notably lower than at last week's stock-market flotation. Islam said the listing was being seen as a major test of continued investor appetite for the technology. Anthropic's decline also raised questions about OpenAI's planned listing on the stock exchange.

Trump said during a phone call to CNBC that he had told Nvidia's board to reinstate Huang. Trump suggested a two-day investigation of allegations had been completed and cleared Huang of national security concerns. He said he had acted because Huang was a "national treasure" who had the next generation of AI chips "in his head." BBC News also reported that U.S. politicians were questioning Trump's role in Huang's comeback.

Trump's intervention showed the extent of the operation involving Silicon Valley and the U.S. administration to restore Huang to the helm, Islam wrote. Skeptics of the AI boom had never seen an operation to prop up a stock market boom like this, he said, leaving bond vigilantes and short sellers opposed by Silicon Valley and the White House.

Investors were trading on levels of political support rather than revenue numbers, Islam wrote, as U.S. voters delivered a backlash against AI data centers. Nvidia and its $400bn (£304bn) in annual sales of advanced chips remained the central cash-generating foundation of the tech boom, according to Islam. Against that backdrop, Islam said Nvidia's recovery looked politically fragile.

Islam said the fading recovery worsened other uncertainties, including Nvidia's central role in a web of financing, borrowing and cross-ownership that supported data-center expansion. Interim chief executive Colette Kress had already signaled a rowing back from elements of that strategy the previous week, and Islam asked where that position stood after Huang's return.

Islam said Anthropic and OpenAI might ultimately be the most critical parts of the AI boom. He described both as valuable companies with phenomenal technology relied on by the world's biggest companies, but questioned whether corporate reliance would ease as corporations eschewed expensive frontier models for cheaper generic models offered by China. He said political backlash could affect those developments.

Islam said it was simply not normal for individual companies to experience trillion-dollar swings in value. He said the extraordinary volatility showed the fragile mood of investors. For Nvidia, the recovery remained politically fragile while questions about financing and strategy stayed open.


[MSI previously reported](/articles/2026-11-23-jensen-huang-returns-as-nvidia-ceo-after-trump-s-intervention/) that Nvidia reinstated Huang after Trump said his message to the board changed the decision. The Nov. 24 pullback showed what that earlier development did not resolve: the rally did not settle investor concern about the AI boom or Nvidia's political and financial exposure.
