Le Pen has long cast France as a nation adrift, buffeted by waves of immigration and lawlessness that she says have torn at the country's social fabric.

Bands of rioters have descended on city centers across France, raiding city halls and looting thousands of stores, according to Meichtry. President Emmanuel Macron's government struggles to maintain control, and Le Pen has sought to present herself as the sole candidate in next spring's presidential election willing to restore order. Meichtry wrote that Le Pen's approach "has given her an air of inevitability in her pursuit of France's highest office."

The same edition of the Journal's 10-Point newsletter turned to the U.S. nursing workforce, where pay and education have climbed even as nurses leave the bedside.

America's nurses are more educated and better paid than ever, Te-Ping Chen reported. Most hold bachelor's degrees, and median annual income has grown since the pandemic to $97,550. Their hands-on work is not easily automated away, and nursing schools have seen their ranks swell, minting thousands of new graduates every year. At the same time, Chen wrote, burnout and complaints about understaffing, verbal abuse and workplace violence are pushing many nurses off the front lines and into other careers where their skills are in high demand.

In a first-person report, the Journal's Annie Linskey returned to Greenwich, Conn., the town where she grew up, to gauge what some of the town's leaders and donors were thinking as Donald Trump's final midterms approached. Greenwich, she wrote, has been shorthand in political circles for one word: fundraising. Linskey described Democratic and Republican donors opening their homes for parties where candidates for office mingle, drink wine, munch on appetizers and take questions from people the hosts hope will fund their campaigns. Among the Democrats and Republicans she encountered, Linskey found a similar refrain: "Politics in the U.S. has got too extreme."

Real-estate giant CBRE is acquiring Onnec, a London-based company that equips data centers, in a $1 billion deal the Journal called the latest sign that commercial real estate is tying its growth to the AI spending boom. CBRE has agreed to pay about $610 million in cash, plus up to $400 million tied to growth targets over several years. Onnec's 800 employees install and maintain cabling and equipment racks inside data centers for major technology companies in Europe and Asia-Pacific.
