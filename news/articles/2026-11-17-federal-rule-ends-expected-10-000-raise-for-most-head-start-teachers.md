## Michigan Head Start Association says educators may seek other jobs

The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services finalized a federal rule over the weekend under President Donald Trump. Titled “Restoring Flexibility to Support Head Start Program Access,” the rule had been issued in May and cancels a mandatory pay increase and expanded benefits established in 2024 by former President Joe Biden’s administration.

The rollback removes an expected $10,000 pay raise for most Head Start teachers as well as basic benefits for full-time staff. Head Start leaders had requested an overall 4.97% cost-of-living adjustment, often called a COLA, which would have allowed programs to pay staff at a more competitive rate. The requested increase also had the potential to retain experienced educators while allowing families to continue receiving consistent Head Start services.

The rule also rescinded 2024 requirements for programs to provide full-time staff with health care coverage, paid leave and behavioral health access. Part-time staff were also eligible for health care access and other benefits under the earlier rule.

The pay-and-benefit change is separate from an August Health and Human Services proposal to rewrite Head Start performance standards.

The Biden-era rule would have affected more than 250,000 teachers and staff. News From The States reported that the Trump administration’s course change may put nearly 716,000 children and their families’ education access at risk.

In a social media post, the Michigan Head Start Association said: “Programs will no longer be required to move toward pay comparable to public preschool teachers by 2031, leaving currently low wages stagnant for Head Start educators and encouraging qualified and valued individuals to seek other employment opportunities outside of our programs.”

The association said educators are the heart of Head Start classrooms and that fewer children and families could be served without professional, supportive educators. It said families would lose access to critical early learning and interventions because of insufficient federal investment in program operations and finances.

U.S. Rep. Hillary Scholten, D-Grand Rapids, said she joined a June letter opposing the decision. In a statement, she said, “The Trump Administration’s decision to cancel cost-of-living adjustments for Head Start teachers sends a clear message about its priorities: penny-pinching educators’ salaries while covering the White House in gold.” She called the decision unacceptable.

Scholten said Head Start has for more than 60 years supported early education, health and dental care, and healthy meals while connecting families to job training and housing support. She said the program was now asking for “the bare minimum: funding to maintain its operations and to help absorb higher costs amid the affordability crisis.”

Scholten said the decision would force Head Start programs to “choose between a livable wage for educators, spaces for more children, and healthy meals on the table.”

“I have heard directly from my constituents who run these programs about how this will negatively impact our community,” Scholten said. She said she would continue fighting to protect Head Start because educators and families who depend on it deserved better.
