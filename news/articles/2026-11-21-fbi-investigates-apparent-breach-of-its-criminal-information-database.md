## Trump says he "just heard" about the hack

The FBI is investigating an apparent breach of its criminal information database, according to US media reports that cited two people briefed on the issue.

The compromised system, the National Crime Information Center, is used by federal and local law enforcement agencies to share information on warrants and background checks. The bureau did not immediately respond to a BBC request for comment.

It would not be the first time hackers have targeted the FBI, the domestic intelligence and security service of the United States.

When asked about the breach in a Fox News interview on Saturday, President Donald Trump said he had "just heard" about the hack. "I will say that it's not good. I don't like that," he said. "But maybe we'll learn things that we would've never learned otherwise," the president added.

The apparent breach of the FBI database comes after a string of technology outages that hit banking, air traffic control and other sectors in the US last week. Officials have said cyberattacks did not cause the blackouts, but the failures have sparked scrutiny from lawmakers.

In the same week, White House National Cyber Director Sean Cairncross was fired, with Trump saying he was not good enough.

The report of an intrusion at the bureau follows a breach the FBI acknowledged in September. The agency said then that it was investigating an apparent breach by the cyber-crime group ShinyHunters, which claimed it had stolen sensitive information on all bureau staff — around 38,000 people.

After that hack, one former FBI agent told the BBC he feared for the safety of current employees, whose personal information could soon become available online to criminals and hostile nation-state hackers.

In a separate development, NBC reported an apparent cyberattack against the US Department of Agriculture. Employees were informed on Thursday that its farm payment system had been targeted, NBC reported. The USDA has not publicly commented on the issue.
