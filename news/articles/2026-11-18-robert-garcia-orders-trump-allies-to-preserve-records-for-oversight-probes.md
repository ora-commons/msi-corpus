## Twelve letters outline probes into family finances, DHS contracts and Epstein files

Robert Garcia, the incoming Democratic chair of the House Oversight Committee, sent 12 letters on Wednesday to relatives and allies of Donald Trump, directing them to preserve records ahead of investigations he plans to open when he takes control of the committee and gains subpoena power in January. Recipients included Donald Trump Jr, Eric Trump, Jared Kushner, Vice President JD Vance, Attorney General Todd Blanche and Susie Wiles, Trump's former White House chief of staff.

The letters define the scope of those inquiries: Trump family finances, alleged corporate corruption, alleged misconduct at the Department of Homeland Security and what Garcia calls a White House cover-up of the Jeffrey Epstein files. "These investigations will lay the groundwork for legislation which helps all Americans," Garcia said. "This is just the beginning."

The letters were sent weeks after Democrats decisively won the House in the US midterm elections. "Oversight Democrats are fighting to put an end to this administration's self-dealing, misconduct and abuse," Garcia said.

Garcia directed his questions about the Epstein files at Attorney General Todd Blanche, who became attorney general after Pam Bondi was fired in April and who has taken responsibility for redaction errors in documents released under the Epstein Files Transparency Act, the 2025 law that ordered the government to release its records. Garcia's letter cites an outstanding committee subpoena and the Department of Justice's failure to comply with the law.

Another letter asks federal prison officials why Ghislaine Maxwell, Epstein's convicted accomplice, was moved from a low-security prison in Florida to a less-restrictive camp in Texas a week after she told Blanche she had never seen Trump behave inappropriately. Garcia's letters say the move came days after favorable testimony and ask about the privileges she has received since.

Garcia also wrote to Vance and Wiles about Situation Room meetings in 2025 on containing the political fallout. "Why are we having meetings in the Situation Room about Epstein strategy?" Garcia asked.

Over the Department of Homeland Security, Garcia wrote to former Secretary Kristi Noem and her former senior adviser Corey Lewandowski, accusing them of using their positions to enrich themselves and their allies. The Justice Department is already investigating allegations of contract misconduct at DHS, including Lewandowski's role, following a criminal referral from the department's inspector general.

Noem required her personal written approval for contracts, grants and funding obligations above $100,000, and questions about how a $220m advertising contract was awarded helped prompt the inquiry. Lewandowski has not been charged and has denied wrongdoing.

Garcia's letters to Donald Trump Jr and Eric Trump focus on World Liberty Financial, the cryptocurrency venture the family co-founded. The conflict-of-interest questions stem from a 2025 Wall Street Journal investigation into an Emirati-backed firm taking a 49% stake in the company for $500m shortly before Trump's inauguration. The White House has said the president's assets are in a trust managed by his children and that there are no conflicts of interest.

Garcia wrote that Eric Trump's role represents "pay-to-play schemes" and told Donald Trump Jr that "foreign interests also funded your lavish wedding celebrations."

The administration has signaled it will fight the investigations. White House counsel Will Scharf sent Garcia a letter dated 9 November warning that the planned investigations may amount to "blatant criminality," a response to Garcia telling Trump officials to "lawyer up" after the midterm victories. Scharf has since vowed to fight the searches "tooth and nail."

The White House has added 15 lawyers to the counsel's office and told executive agencies they need White House approval before agreeing to testify on Capitol Hill. Garcia said Democrats will not back down. "Oversight Democrats will not stand for noncompliance and obstruction," he wrote in one of his letters.
