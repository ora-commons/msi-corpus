## Israeli settlers attack olive harvesters, beat Washington Post photographer

**Subtype:** fact

- Israeli settlers attacked Palestinian olive harvesters across the occupied West Bank on Friday, injuring at least six people near Ramallah and beating Washington Post photographer Heidi Levine for more than 10 minutes, according to the Palestinian Red Crescent Society.
- The Israeli Foreign Press Association called the attacks "the latest in a long line of violent attacks by Israeli settlers against journalists and other civilians" and said its calls for action have been "ignored by Israeli authorities."
- Israeli rights group Yesh Din documented at least 170 settler attacks since September 27, tied to the olive harvest; Palestinian authorities reported four Palestinian deaths and more than 100 injuries over the past month.
- New Israeli rules barred Palestinians from harvesting in about 8,800 hectares of West Bank agricultural land, a 30 percent increase from the prior year, while Israeli citizens were given access to more land, according to the Israeli rights group Peace Now.

## Israeli military fired teargas at Palestinian farmers defending themselves

Masked settlers wielding clubs and other weapons attacked Palestinians attempting to harvest their olives in towns across the West Bank on Friday. At least 80 settlers, some of whom were carrying firearms or clubs, gathered in the town of Abu Falah, near Ramallah, using slingshots to hurl rocks at the farmers in view of Israeli military vehicles. When some of the Palestinians picked up rocks to defend themselves, the Israeli military fired teargas in their direction.

Photos from the scene showed Israeli soldiers near a Palestinian man lying wounded on the road after soldiers shot him during the settler attack.

Washington Post photographer Heidi Levine was beaten by settlers in an attack that lasted more than 10 minutes, according to witnesses. Settlers stole her passport, phone, wallet, and equipment.

"I was attacked, pushed to the ground. Luckily, I had my flak jacket and helmet, otherwise I probably wouldn't even be here, because they just kept kicking and kicking me," Levine said from a hospital bed in Ramallah. She said the attack ended when her Palestinian colleagues intervened to save her.

In a statement, the Washington Post said Levine had been wearing visible markings clearly identifying her as a journalist.

The Israeli Foreign Press Association said: "This is the latest in a long line of violent attacks by Israeli settlers against journalists and other civilians in the occupied West Bank. Yet our repeated calls for action to confront this lawlessness have been ignored by Israeli authorities."

The Friday attacks fit a documented surge in settler violence. Yesh Din, an Israeli rights group, said settlers had carried out at least 170 attacks since September 27, around the start of the annual olive harvest. Palestinian authorities said at least four Palestinians have been killed and more than 100 others injured over the past month, with thousands of trees damaged.

Every year, settlers attack Palestinians trying to harvest their olives, but this year's harvest has seen an uptick in violence as Israel's elections approach at the end of October.

Israel imposed new rules on this year's olive harvest, barring Palestinians from harvesting trees located in Area C, an area under Israeli military control that comprises most of the West Bank's agricultural land. The new orders prohibit Palestinians from entering about 8,800 hectares (about 21,800 acres) of agricultural land — a 30 percent increase from the prior year — without approval of the Israeli military. By contrast, Peace Now said the military gave Israeli citizens access to more land this year, decreasing the amount Israeli citizens were prohibited from entering from about 1,070 hectares in 2025 to 680 hectares in 2026, for the purpose of preventing settler violence.

The olive harvest is an economic lifeline for Palestinians and has grown in importance since Israel revoked work permits for many Palestinians in the West Bank after the Hamas-led attack of October 7, 2023.

Photos and videos showed large swaths of agricultural land with uprooted olive trees in Kufur Malek, outside Ramallah, where Israeli bulldozers had razed trees.

Settlers rarely face accountability for their violence, even when well documented, according to rights groups, who say settlers enjoy the protection of the Israeli military, which intervenes only if Palestinians try to defend themselves. Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu has on occasion condemned the violence, which he says is due to "just about 100 settlers," but he has taken few steps to stop it. Settlements have rapidly expanded across the West Bank under his rightwing government, leading to international condemnation; the United Nations and most countries judge settlements to be in violation of international law.

Friday's attack in Abu Falah followed a similar attack hours earlier near the West Bank village of Turmus Ayya, where settlers burned three cars after attacking Palestinians harvesting olives. Videos shared by the Israeli rights group B'tselem showed hundreds of bags of olives "violently" stolen by Israeli settlers from Palestinians and taken to Israeli olive presses.

Sarit Michaeli, an independent activist, said settlers were stealing Palestinians' olive crop on a scale "never seen before in my 22-year history of working on this issue."

A spokesperson for the U.S. embassy in Jerusalem said it was aware of reports of the attack and declined to comment further, citing privacy laws.
