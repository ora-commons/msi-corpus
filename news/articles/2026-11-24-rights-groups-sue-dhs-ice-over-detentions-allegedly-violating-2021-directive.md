## Complaint says FOIA records show rise from nine to 128 pregnant detainees

The Robert and Ethel Kennedy Human Rights Center and several other groups sued the U.S. Department of Homeland Security and U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement on Tuesday in the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia. The lawsuit alleges that ICE has unlawfully detained pregnant, postpartum and nursing immigrants in violation of agency policies. It cites a 2021 ICE directive saying agents “should not detain, arrest, or take into custody for an administrative violation” anyone known to be pregnant, postpartum or nursing.

The plaintiffs ask the court to declare that the 2021 directive remains effective and order the U.S. Department of Homeland Security and U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement to comply. ICE did not immediately respond to Stateline’s request for comment. A hearing in the case will likely be scheduled in the coming weeks.

According to the complaint, ICE stopped tracking the number of women who were pregnant, postpartum or nursing and held in detention under the Trump administration. The Women’s Refugee Commission, one of the plaintiff organizations, obtained data through a Freedom of Information Act request.

Those documents showed the number of pregnant women in ICE detention increased between January 2025 and March 2026 from nine to 128, a 1,320% increase, the complaint states. The complaint also says the length of detention for pregnant women increased substantially.

ICE detained more than 557 pregnant women since 2025 for an average of 43 days, according to the complaint. Nearly 14% of those women were detained for 90 days or longer, with one woman detained for 219 days, almost seven months of her pregnancy.

The complaint details the experience of a woman detained in Kentucky who sought emergency assistance because she was pregnant and had been bleeding for more than a day. She eventually miscarried while shackled to a hospital bed, according to the lawsuit.

According to the lawsuit, another woman, detained in the third trimester of a high-risk pregnancy, was told by ICE officers that they would “take her baby away forever” after she gave birth. The lawsuit said she waived her right to see an immigration judge out of fear and was quickly deported.

Other women who were nursing have been separated from their infants and denied breast pumps, the complaint says.

The 2021 directive also prohibited the use of GPS ankle monitors for women in this population who were granted supervised release. The complaint says women have been forced to wear the monitors anyway. One woman said the monitor was making her high blood pressure worse. Another woman, who was nursing an infant at the time, said she could not receive treatment for a painful hernia while the monitor was attached because of burn risks in the operating room.
