## Shenzhou 24 docked after a three-and-a-half-hour flight

Footage showed Shenzhou 24 lifting off from the Jiuquan launch centre in north-west China on Wednesday, carrying Muhammad Zeeshan Ali, a 40-year-old Pakistan air force pilot and payload specialist. The spacecraft docked with Tiangong three and a half hours later, making Ali the first Pakistani to reach orbit and the first foreign astronaut to enter the Chinese station.

Ali entered Tiangong through a hatch and grinned as the onboard crew embraced him. China's space agency called the greeting the country's first foreign “space handshake”. The agency said the flight would begin what it called the globalization of China's space operations.

Tiangong, completed in 2022, is part of China's effort to establish a larger human-spaceflight program. The Chinese government has invested billions as it seeks to catch up with the US and Russia, two of the five partners involved with the International Space Station, along with Europe, Japan and Canada. After 25 years of continuous occupation, the ISS is set to cease operations from 2030, according to NASA.

On Wednesday, Chinese President Xi Jinping told his Pakistani counterpart that Beijing was “willing to work with all countries … to uphold the banner of the peaceful use of outer space”.

Ali will conduct one experiment cultivating microalgae as a potential food and another testing the effects of spaceflight on quinoa seeds. To prepare for the mission, he trained for seven months in Beijing, including intensive Chinese-language classes. At a press conference the day before the launch, Ali recited a line from a classical Chinese poem. Commander Tang Hongbo told reporters that differences in language and culture had made learning to work together more challenging, but that the effort had paid off.

Shenzhou 24 is the latest mission in a series that rotates Tiangong astronauts every six months. Ali will not stay for the six-month term usually demanded of Tiangong astronauts. Instead, he will return to Earth with two members of the Shenzhou 23 crew, while Shenzhou 23 commander Zhu Yangzhu will remain to complete the first full-year stay in space by a Chinese astronaut.

Space cooperation between China and Pakistan dates to the early 1990s. China has launched Pakistani satellites, and a Pakistani-built rover is due to fly on a Chinese lunar mission around 2028.

China is the third country to put humans in orbit and has landed robotic rovers on Mars and the moon, where it hopes to send crewed missions within the next decade. Beijing is competing with the US's Artemis programme as it races to the moon before 2030.

The US has excluded China from the ISS since 2011, banning NASA from collaborating with Beijing. In 2025, NASA went further by blocking Chinese citizens from its facilities altogether.

“Sending another country’s astronaut to space is a major undertaking,” Blaine Curcio, the managing director at Orbital Gateway Consulting, said. Curcio said Pakistan was placing a lot of trust in Beijing by having Ali fly into space on a Chinese-built rocket. He also raised security considerations, saying Ali would receive detailed insight into China's crewed space programme. Curcio said the exchange would leave China better prepared to repeat the cooperation and signal to other countries that China was open to such collaboration.

Curcio separately said, “We know that China is quite keen to develop Tiangong as an international hub.” He pointed to China Manned Space Agency documents published in coordination with the UN that outline “resources for international cooperation” onboard the station. Curcio said he would expect countries such as Brazil and Egypt to seek opportunities to send astronauts on Chinese missions. He added that if the ISS retired without a western government or commercial replacement, European or other ISS-involved countries, including Russia, might eventually send astronauts to Tiangong.
