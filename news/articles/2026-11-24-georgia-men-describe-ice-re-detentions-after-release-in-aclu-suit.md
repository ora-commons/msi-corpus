## ACLU says policy permits detentions without changed circumstances

Alvaro Ibañez-Cardenas and Ambrosio Trujillo Zavaleta are the two Georgia residents at the center of a class-action lawsuit filed by the ACLU in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Georgia. The complaint, filed earlier this month, challenges a Department of Homeland Security policy that allows U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement to re-detain immigrants who were previously released without assessing whether their circumstances have changed. The Department of Homeland Security did not respond to a request for comment.

Under the decades-old practice, immigrants with pending immigration cases generally were not re-detained by ICE as long as they followed the conditions of their release, including attending court hearings and immigration check-in meetings. Raneem Ashrawi, an ACLU of Georgia staff attorney, said officials had to show that a person's circumstances had changed, such as through a new criminal charge, before taking the person into custody again.

The lawsuit says DHS reversed course in May 2025. It argues that the new policy violates the Fourth Amendment and leaves immigrants across Georgia, North Carolina and South Carolina vulnerable to re-detention without a clear reason. The Atlanta ICE field office has jurisdiction in the three states. The complaint asks a court to block the policy, as a federal judge in California did last year.

The four plaintiffs include two Georgia residents and two North Carolina residents. Ibañez-Cardenas, a 35-year-old Venezuelan man who lives in Athens, was initially detained in 2022 after arriving in the United States and was later released. According to the complaint, ICE detained him for a second time in September during a routine check-in at the Atlanta field office, transferred him to Irwin County Detention Center and released him in October on a $20,000 bond.

The lawsuit says Ibañez-Cardenas lost his warehouse job roughly three days after the detention. It also says his pending asylum case, submitted in 2023, was cancelled as a result of the re-detention. In a statement provided by the ACLU, he said, “I feel very paranoid and worried all the time, but I have to keep going in order to make money to take care of my family.”

Trujillo Zavaleta, a 46-year-old man from Mexico who lives in Savannah, was detained with his wife and adult son in September, according to the complaint. He is a plumber who has lived in the United States since 2004. He was arrested once in 2014 for driving without a license, then released, paid the associated fines and obtained a driver's license.

After his second arrest, ICE sent Trujillo Zavaleta to a processing center in Folkston in South Georgia. He was held nearly a month before being released on a $20,000 bond. His wife was eventually released, but their adult son remains in ICE custody. Their daughter, a U.S. citizen and a student at the University of Georgia, relies on Trujillo Zavaleta for financial support, the lawsuit said.

Though he is now back at work, Trujillo Zavaleta said the detention has had a lasting impact on him and his family. In a statement provided by the ACLU, Trujillo Zavaleta said, “My wife, who was also detained at the same time as me, now lives in constant fear of leaving the house.” He also said, “I get very nervous going out in public or driving to work because I know ICE might choose to re-detain me at any time.”

Ashrawi said the detentions left both men and their families unsettled. “They relied on what they were told when they were released,” she said. “They followed the conditions of their release, and so to be abruptly snatched from their family and their community has shaken them.”
