## Japanese chip-equipment makers lead the regional declines

Asian equities fell Monday morning as Friday's selloff of U.S. chip-maker shares spilled over into the region, according to The Wall Street Journal. Analysts said investors are reassessing the artificial-intelligence trade following the resignation of Nvidia Chief Executive Jensen Huang. Crude oil futures also fell Monday morning as prospects of a global supply surplus offset Middle East tensions.

The equity declines were steepest in Japan. The Nikkei Stock Average dropped 1.1%. Advantest, a leading manufacturer of automatic test equipment for the semiconductor industry, slid 9.4%, while Lasertec, a designer and manufacturer of inspection and measurement systems for the semiconductor industry, dropped 8.8%. South Korea's Kospi fell 2.0% and Singapore's FTSE Straits Times Index lost 0.2%.

Commerzbank Research analysts said the continuing fallout from leadership turmoil at chip giant Nvidia was the dominant driver of risk sentiment heading into this week. "The episode has triggered a broader reassessment of the [artificial-intelligence] trade, with investors rotating out of AI-adjacent semiconductor names and into rival chip makers before unwinding those positions as well," the analysts said in a research report.

Axel Rudolph, chief technical analyst at IG, said in an email that investors are reassessing the risks around the AI trade in the wake of the resignation of Nvidia Chief Executive Jensen Huang. "Concerns over Chinese access to Nvidia chips [are] adding to scrutiny of the sector," the analyst added.

## Oil futures fall as IEA forecasts return to global surplus

Crude oil futures fell Monday morning as prospects of a supply surplus offset Middle East tensions.

The International Energy Agency said Friday that the global oil market is expected to return to a surplus in the fourth quarter for the first time since the Iran war began, as a recovery in Middle Eastern exports outpaces demand.

Global oil supply increased by 2.3 million barrels a day to 105.4 million barrels a day in October, prompting the IEA to lift its fourth-quarter supply estimate by 3.3 million barrels a day. The agency also reported the first monthly increase in observed global oil inventories since June, with stocks rising by 52 million barrels in October.

However, price declines in oil futures may be limited by potential supply disruptions stemming from continuing Middle East tensions.

Iran launched an attack on Bahrain early Sunday, the latest incident targeting the island kingdom amid skirmishes between Iran and the U.S. as talks to end the conflict in the Middle East remain at a stalemate. It wasn't immediately clear if there was any damage, though people around Manama, the capital, said they heard blasts.

"The threat of renewed hostilities remains ever persistent," ANZ Research analysts said in a research report.

Front-month West Texas Intermediate crude oil futures fell 0.4% to $88.20 a barrel and front-month Brent crude oil futures dropped 0.6% to $95.28 a barrel, ICE data showed.
