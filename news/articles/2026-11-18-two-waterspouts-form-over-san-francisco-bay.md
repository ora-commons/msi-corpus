## NWS forecasts coastal flooding risk and 70 mph East Bay gusts

Two waterspouts formed over San Francisco Bay this past weekend as storms hit the region, according to social-media footage. The National Weather Service confirmed the occurrence in a Monday special marine warning.

The footage showed two vortices of air and water mist spiraling above the bay. Waterspouts are vortexes of air and water mist, essentially tornadoes.

Federal storm data indicate that waterspout sightings are uncommon in the Bay area, while the phenomenon is prevalent around the Great Lakes and along the south-east Floridian coast.

The National Weather Service said some waterspouts, dubbed “fair weather,” are weak, while other, typically tornadic waterspouts can pose a risk to boaters and coastal areas if they travel on shore.

The broader inclement weather was expected to continue for a few more days. An agency forecast called for the Bay area to be drenched in rain Tuesday, with potential thunderstorms Wednesday and Thursday.

Elevated tide levels were expected to raise the risk of coastal flooding through at least Thursday, according to the National Weather Service.

Powerful wind was also in the forecast, with gusts in the East Bay Hills expected to reach up to 70 mph (112 km/h).

After Thursday evening, showers may subside, though some rain could linger over the Pacific and adjacent inland communities.
