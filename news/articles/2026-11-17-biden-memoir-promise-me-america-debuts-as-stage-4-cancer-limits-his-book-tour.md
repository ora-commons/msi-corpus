## Ricchetti: midterms were 'a referendum in many ways' on Trump's first two years

Joe Biden released “Promise Me, America” on Nov. 17, 2026, almost two years after leaving office. The memoir recounts his White House years and his decision to seek reelection in 2024. The former president is not doing the typical run of media interviews; Biden's former counselor Steve Ricchetti said he and other people who worked closely with the former president would share the load of promoting the book.

Ricchetti said Biden was diagnosed with stage 4 cancer last May and that the cancer had moved into his bones, so there is pain and discomfort with it and it affects his energy level. Biden is still taking heavy doses of medication to help manage the cancer, Ricchetti said. Ricchetti said those conditions were why Biden is not doing the traditional book tour he might otherwise have wanted.

Biden wrote in the memoir: “I was elected to the Senate as a 29-year-old kid. I left the presidency as an 82-year-old man. I have seen the long sweep of history.” PBS News' Amna Nawaz, who interviewed Ricchetti, asked: “You say he wants the book to speak for itself. So what is it he hopes to accomplish by putting it out at this time?” She said the memoir was comprehensive and detailed Biden's experience with the pandemic, the Jan. 6 attack on the Capitol, the American Rescue Plan, the Infrastructure Act, the CHIPS Act and efforts to rally NATO after the Russian invasion of Ukraine. Nawaz said that as people work through the book, Biden's decision to run again is getting a lot of attention.

Ricchetti said he thought Biden waited until after the midterms to release the memoir because he did not want the book to become the subject of the political dialogue for the midterm elections. The midterms themselves were, as it was, “a referendum in many ways” on President Trump's first two years in office, Ricchetti said, and the book was timed to follow them. Biden wanted the release close enough to his time in office that he could remember the events and talk through what he experienced while it was still fresh in his mind, and to discuss it “very completely and thoroughly,” Ricchetti said.

Biden also defended his decision to run again. He wrote: “I believe I was the best person to do it, the only person who'd ever beaten Donald Trump. I felt I was the best person to do it again.” Nawaz cited one journalist who called the reflection “pugnacious, unrepentant, and unreflective.” Ricchetti said he did not consider that criticism fair.

Ricchetti said it is now hard to remember and suggested there may be some kind of psychological effect from what they went through during COVID, but that people forget Biden “inherited as severe a set of challenges as any president in our lifetime.” Ricchetti argued that Biden inherited three crises: an international pandemic, an economy “flat on its back,” and the aftermath of the Jan. 6 attack on the Capitol. By Jan. 20, 2021, hundreds of thousands of people had died of COVID, he said. The attack occurred two weeks before Biden was sworn in, and Ricchetti said the heart of democracy, the institution of the presidency and respect for other branches of government were on the line. Restoring confidence in and respect for the presidency itself and reaffirming the United States' reputation and role in the world were also part of Biden's first year, he said.

Nawaz said the party was still sort of reckoning with the decision of how Biden's candidacy for reelection ended and what happened in the wake of President Trump's reelection. She said she did not see the book address whether Biden might not have run again, whether the race should not have taken a disastrous debate or the intraparty feud, and asked Ricchetti whether, in his conversations with Biden, Biden had ever reflected on those points; she noted it is not in the book. Ricchetti responded that Biden's April 2023 decision had been a pretty straightforward equation. He said Biden believed the country was making progress in his own mind, Ricchetti believed it in his and those around Biden believed it as well. They knew the country had endured real difficulty and challenge and that more work remained on the economy and prices. Biden believed he was healthy and “more prepared than anyone” to take the next step and lead the country for four more years, Ricchetti said.

Biden was in Dublin on the day of the book's release to support his son as he received an award, and he was heckled by protesters outside who opposed his support for Israel and the war in Gaza. Ricchetti said Biden's legacy is still being debated, and appropriately so. Biden spent a lot of time and real attention on the decisions involving Israel and at times even struggled with the decision-making that was occurring there and with Prime Minister Netanyahu over the direction that Netanyahu and the Israeli military were taking, and made every effort with the power and the influence of the United States to try to direct that in a somewhat different way, Ricchetti said.

Ricchetti called the midterm elections “seismic” for Democrats, saying the victories were deep and wide, including races in states that many people for years did not think could be competitive, alongside sweeping gains that flipped seats and, in some cases, states. Ricchetti said the moment called for a pivot in American politics because, as people put it, “I will work with anyone, Republican, Democrat, or even President Trump, to attack America's problems.” He said the country has an affordability crisis, people around a kitchen table are struggling to get by and need help, and he would work with anyone to produce results for them. Ricchetti said the country should put a premium on people willing to reach across the aisle or do whatever it takes to help move things along to make progress in the country, to achieve legislative results in a bipartisan way, as President Biden did on several of the measures he discusses in great detail in the book.

Nawaz said she hoped to have the chance to speak with Biden directly about the book as well.
