## Likely questions cover CISA leadership vacancy, air traffic funding and deportation practices

Markwayne Mullin is scheduled to appear before the House Homeland Security Committee on Wednesday as the Department of Homeland Security faces questions over aviation, technology and immigration enforcement. FBI Deputy Director Christopher Raia and national intelligence director Jay Clayton are also expected to appear, according to the Associated Press.

The annual hearing gives members of Congress an opportunity to question senior administration officials about national security. The Associated Press said the session is Democrats’ first opportunity to question Mullin after Republicans lost the midterms. Mullin, a former senator from Oklahoma, oversees a department that includes Customs and Border Protection and U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, and is a key official in President Donald Trump’s mass-deportation agenda.

The ground stop and cyber threats are likely to account for much of the questioning. Mullin took part in a Friday White House news conference with Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy, where he pushed for more funding for the aging air traffic control system and said the government was not seeing signs of a deliberate cyberattack. The Trump administration attributed last week’s grounding of all aircraft to problems with newly installed routers that flooded the Federal Aviation Administration’s telecommunications system with data and forced the agency to ground flights while addressing the issue.

The hearing also follows a series of other technology outages. Microsoft suffered an outage on Nov. 12 that lasted for hours and severely affected Outlook, Teams and the artificial intelligence assistant Copilot; the company said a technical issue arose during an upgrade. The banking giant Chase and the airline carrier United also experienced technology outages. An internal network used by the National Security Agency to communicate classified information went offline Tuesday after the agency described the interruption as routine maintenance. In a Monday Fox News Channel interview, Mullin said he could say with “98%” certainty that the string of cyber problems was not the result of a cyberattack.

Mullin is also likely to face questions about an agency under his department that is supposed to have a central role in the federal response to cyber incidents. The Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency works with industry to protect infrastructure, including water and electric generation, election systems, chemical facilities and cyber networks. According to the Associated Press, CISA has been without a Senate-confirmed leader since Trump took office for a second term in early 2025. Trump’s original pick to lead the agency withdrew his nomination earlier this year after it languished for months in the Senate, and CISA lost staff last year during cuts aimed at reducing the size of the federal government.

The secretary’s decision to travel to the LSU-Texas college football game is another line of scrutiny. Mullin flew to Louisiana on Saturday to watch the game, after the stoppage had been lifted and planes were back in the air. Thousands of flights had been canceled during the ground stop, and travelers across the United States were still scrambling for alternatives when Mullin flew. During a CNBC interview, Mullin said the airways had been open for 21 hours by the time his flight took off.

“We would have never taken airspace if airspace wasn’t open. And we still gotta live our lives at the same time,” Mullin said.

Immigration enforcement is likely to occupy a substantial part of the hearing. Democrats are vowing rigorous oversight of matters ranging from immigration detention contracts to the use of force by ICE officers. Trump has indicated that he could work with Democrats on immigration legislation, but he has elevated Stephen Miller to chief of staff. The Associated Press described Miller as the architect of Trump’s immigration crackdown and said his elevation raised questions about how easily compromise could be reached on the issue. The outlet also reported that Mullin took over from ousted Department of Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem in March after immigration enforcement officers killed two American citizen protesters in Minneapolis, an episode that forced the administration to adjust its mass-deportation strategy.

The Associated Press also reported that immigration arrests initially fell but then rebounded as ICE hired new officers and maximized cooperation from local and state law enforcement agencies across the country. The agency had billions of dollars in new congressional funding.

Wednesday’s hearing will bring these issues into one congressional session: explanations for the flight grounding, cybersecurity preparedness, CISA’s leadership vacancy and staffing losses, and immigration enforcement. Mullin is expected to address the aviation and cyber matters and the department’s immigration record.
