## NatWest orders 13,000 staff back to office two days a week

Santander allows its UK back-office staff to record annual leave and sick leave as days spent working in the office, letting employees satisfy workplace attendance requirements while away from their desks. The Times first reported the policy, and a Santander spokesperson confirmed to the paper that the bank's monthly in-office requirement is adjusted for periods of leave. Back-office staff at the Spanish-owned lender have been expected to spend at least 12 working days each month in what the company calls a "head office location"; the requirement does not apply to staff working in bank branches.

The mechanism works by crediting leave against the attendance target. Under Santander's system, a staff member taking a two-week holiday is logged as spending 10 days that month working in the office, meaning that person would only need to attend another two days over two weeks. In practice, the arrangement makes the office attendance rules less strict than the 12-day requirement alone would imply, because time booked as leave counts toward the same tally.

A Santander spokesperson told the Times, which first reported the story, that "The 12 days a month requirement is adjusted for periods of leave, including public holidays, annual leave and sick leave (meaning if a colleague is not well enough to work, they do not have to make up their in-office days)." The spokesperson added: "Our policy allows for collaboration and enables colleagues to develop, while providing flexibility to suit individual needs."

Santander formalised hybrid working arrangements that had been in place since the pandemic in September 2024, joining a range of other banks that tightened remote working requirements. The bank's previous guidance permitted employees to spend two or three days each week in the office and to work from home for the rest of the time.

Mike Regnier, Santander's former UK chief executive, was one of the proponents of hybrid working. He told the Guardian that he would not have accepted the role had he not been permitted to spend at least one day a week at his desk in his family home in Yorkshire. Regnier also said at the time that he hoped the bank's new campus-style headquarters in Milton Keynes would attract workers back to their desks more frequently.

Santander said Regnier's successor, Mahesh Aditya, who took over in March, would spend five days a week in the office, amid the lender's work to integrate the high street bank TSB, which it bought last year for £2.65bn. The company has its UK headquarters in Milton Keynes, England, and has offices in Glasgow and central London.

The Santander disclosure emerged as employers across the sector moved the other way, in a dispute between staff and employers over the right to continued working from home. Staff unions reacted angrily to NatWest's October announcement that it was ordering 13,000 staff back to the office for two days a week, up from two days a month.

From February 2027, nearly all of the 40,000 UK staff working for the Royal Bank of Scotland, NatWest, Ulster Bank and Coutts brands will be required to spend more days at their desks, ending a remote-first policy for thousands of employees.
