## Eight of the 12 U.S. measles deaths this year have been in Pennsylvania

A child younger than 5 has died in Virginia after testing positive for measles, the Virginia Department of Health said Friday, the first death from the virus reported in the state this year.

The department released few details about the case, saying only that the child was under 5 years old.

The death was reported days after the United States, along with Mexico and Bolivia, lost its designation as having eliminated the highly contagious virus.

Virginia has recorded 220 measles cases overall this year, most of them in the central part of the state. The vast majority of those cases were among people who were not vaccinated against measles.

Across the country, states have reported at least 12 measles-linked deaths in 2026. Eight of them have been in Pennsylvania, including one last week. One death was reported in New York and two in Wisconsin.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention has tracked more than 5,800 measles cases nationally in 2026 — by far the most of any year since 1991.

The Virginia death lands as officials have voiced concern about the virus spreading while families and friends gather for Thanksgiving next week and for Christmas and other holidays in December.

Experts say most adults do not need a booster shot, though some are good candidates for one.

This month the CDC issued new guidance on measles and pregnancy, noting an increased risk of miscarriage and of babies born with congenital infections, as well as health problems such as pneumonia for pregnant women.

The vaccines are not given to pregnant women, but they are recommended before pregnancy.
