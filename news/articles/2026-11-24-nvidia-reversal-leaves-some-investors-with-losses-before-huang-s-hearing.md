## The Wall Street Journal says Huang faces a 'bipartisan maelstrom' next week

Some Nvidia investors sold shares after Nvidia's board said Jensen Huang would not return as CEO. When the board reversed course and reinstated him, those investors were burned by the U-turn and left with losses. Their decisions turned the board's reversal into a direct market consequence for people who had acted on its earlier position.

The Wall Street Journal said Huang is slated to face a 'bipartisan maelstrom' when he appears for a congressional hearing next week. The hearing would place the reinstated chief executive in the bipartisan conflict described by the newspaper.

The board's change of course connects the two developments. It first said Huang would not return as CEO, then reversed course and reinstated him. Investors who sold after the first decision were burned when the second decision arrived, while the planned hearing places Huang before Congress as chief executive.

At an all-hands meeting at Nvidia's headquarters, Huang thanked the Trump administration for providing Nvidia with quick access to intelligence. According to people familiar with the matter, Huang said the material helped the board determine that the national-security risks the company faced in reinstating him were low.

Huang also apologized to Nvidia staff during the meeting. His remarks placed the administration's assistance and the board's risk assessment before employees after the company reversed its earlier leadership decision.

The acknowledgment of the administration's intelligence access connected the staff meeting to the board's decision. In Huang's account, the material informed the board's assessment of the national-security risks of reinstating him.

For investors, the consequence was financial: selling after the board's initial position left them with losses when the company reversed itself. For Huang, next week's congressional hearing is the immediate political event, with The Wall Street Journal forecasting a 'bipartisan maelstrom'.
