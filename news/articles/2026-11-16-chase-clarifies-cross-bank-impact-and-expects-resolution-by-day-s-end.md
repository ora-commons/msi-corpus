## Early Warning Services spokesperson says Zelle network fully operational

Joe Evangelisti, a spokesperson for JPMorgan Chase, said other banks “may have been impacted by slowness in transactions only to the extent that they were a party to a transaction with us.” Chase expected the issue to be resolved by the end of the day Monday and said customers still had access to their accounts and funds.

Bank of America, Citigroup and other banks were not having issues with their own services, but customers trying to transact with Chase may have seen their transactions slow.

Zelle was not directly affected by the Chase glitch. However, customers at other banks may have experienced transaction delays while trying to send or receive Zelle payments involving a Chase customer. A spokesperson for Early Warning Services said the Zelle network was fully operational.

A technical issue at JPMorgan Chase, the largest U.S. bank by total assets, caused problems throughout the financial system Monday. Chase said a software bug affecting its internal hardware systems was causing transaction delays, and the company was working to bring banking services back online.

The technical issue resulted in thousands of customers reporting difficulty accessing online accounts at several major U.S. banks and at the money transfer service Zelle. Some Chase customers received a message that said, “Some services aren’t available. We’re working on them. Please try back later.”

Chase said the technical problem was not the result of a cyberattack, stressing that it was an isolated incident and not related to anything other than a bug.

Zelle is a rapid peer-to-peer payment system used by major U.S. banks. The Arizona-based Early Warning Services owns the platform and is in turn owned by financial institutions including Bank of America, Capital One, JPMorgan Chase, PNC Bank, Truist, U.S. Bank and Wells Fargo.

Microsoft's major outage Thursday lasted for hours and severely disrupted services including Outlook, Teams and the AI assistant Copilot. The outage was due to a technical issue that arose during an upgrade. Microsoft has said there is “no indication” that the disruptions were caused by malicious activity, such as a cyberattack or due to action by artificial intelligence agents.

Another outage affected the nation's air traffic control system Friday, grounding all U.S. flights for several hours and disrupting domestic air travel over the weekend. It was caused by a router that malfunctioned and overwhelmed the aging air traffic control system.

Government officials and company leaders said the Chase incident, the Microsoft outage and the air traffic disruption were unrelated, even though the events took place just days apart.
