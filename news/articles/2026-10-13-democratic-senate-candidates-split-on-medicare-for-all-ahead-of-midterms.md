## WSJ poll: top three healthcare policies drew 80%+ support across parties

Aaron Zitner of The Wall Street Journal framed Medicare for All as "a defining policy fight within the Democratic Party" in his Politics newsletter, writing that it has become "a measuring stick of how far to the left the party wants to move." Two Democratic Senate candidates took opposing positions in separate Sunday appearances. Troy Jackson, the party's candidate in Maine, told a rowdy rally: "I want Medicare for All." That evening, Annie Andrews, the party's candidate in South Carolina, said in a televised debate: "To set the record straight, I don't support Medicare for All."

As a presidential candidate in 2020, Joe Biden said he would veto any Medicare for All bill as too costly and disruptive. Then-candidate Kamala Harris, an early supporter of the policy, soon softened her position.

Sen. Bernie Sanders, the Vermont independent, defines Medicare for All as a government-run, single-payer system that would overtake most private insurance.

This year, Democratic socialists and others on the party's left flank are calling for Medicare for All. In many of the competitive states and districts now represented by Republicans, the Democrats trying to flip those seats are taking a more centrist position, akin to Biden's. They say they would work to boost subsidies under the Affordable Care Act so that more people could buy private insurance. Some would allow more people to opt in to Medicare — a measure shy of Medicare for All as Sanders defines it.

A recent Wall Street Journal poll tested public support for more than 30 policy ideas and found that the three most popular had to do with healthcare. The most popular ideas — drawing more than 80% support among both parties and independents — were banning surprise medical bills, capping drug prices, and requiring insurers to cover the services that doctors prescribe.

Medicare for All wasn't among them. The poll found that only 23% of Republicans supported the policy — a caution for any candidate who needs some measure of Republican support to win in November.
