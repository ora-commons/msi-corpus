## Investors seek guidance as a possible Huang return adds uncertainty

Colette Kress, Nvidia's finance chief, is scheduled to lead her first earnings call as interim chief executive this afternoon, days after the company elevated her following Jensen Huang's ouster. Investors will be looking for visibility into Nvidia's financial performance, whether the company is on track to meet its guidance and how the search for a successor to Huang is progressing.

Kress was already well-known to Nvidia investors as chief financial officer. Huang resigned last week following revelations of at least two extramarital affairs, one involving a Singapore data-center executive whom U.S. officials suspected of working with Chinese intelligence.

Gil Luria, head of technology research at D.A. Davidson, said investors will want as much visibility as possible into Nvidia's financial performance and succession planning. Investors will also want to see that the company is on track to meet its guidance, Luria said.

Luria said, “Investors do not like uncertainty,” adding that the prospect of a Huang comeback creates “considerable uncertainty.” A campaign to reinstate Huang has gathered support from his peers, employees and investors.

The Wall Street Journal reported Monday that Nvidia's board is not considering reinstating Huang. President Trump said Monday that he would support Huang's return, a position that differed from the board's reported stance.

Separately, the report noted that Trump held a stake in Nvidia in early October, shortly before the company's shares slid following Huang's admission of an affair with a business associate whom U.S. officials suspected of working with Chinese intelligence.

According to people familiar with the matter, a senior Nvidia employee sent a letter to the company's top executives and board arguing that failure to reinstate Huang could lead to the loss of “irreplaceable talent.” The letter was written by director of developer technology Nader Khalil and escalated the internal push to bring Huang back after Huang's resignation on Thursday.

The letter argued that Huang is uniquely qualified to lead Nvidia and deserves the benefit of the doubt as the board investigates his relationship with the Singapore data-center executive suspected of being a Chinese intelligence asset.

The call also comes amid scrutiny of Nvidia's recent dealmaking. The Wall Street Journal reported over the weekend that Kress is expected to rein in the recent spree of deals aimed at backstopping the AI economy. Kress has publicly defended the company's dealmaking, but internally she has raised questions about which deals to pursue, how to value guarantees on credit-support arrangements and how to calculate the return on investments, the report said.

Beyond the quarterly results, Nvidia faces long-term questions about whether it can sustain its pace of innovation without Huang and the impact of new restrictions on shipments of AI chips to China, analysts said.

The immediate agenda for Kress is financial performance, guidance and succession planning. The longer-term issues are innovation without Huang, restrictions on AI chip shipments to China and how Nvidia manages its AI-focused dealmaking under new leadership.

On the economic calendar, the Census Bureau is scheduled to report retail and food-services sales for October.

Separately, Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh said the central bank's staff has begun modeling how artificial intelligence could reshape the economy, including scenarios in which artificial intelligence displaces workers or disappointing stock returns drag down the broader market.

Warsh delivered opening remarks Monday at a half-day symposium that is studying how AI promises could ripple through the economy. He did not comment on the near-term outlook for interest rates or the economy.

Warsh stressed that the staff's scenarios were illustrative and not designed to offer predictions. “We can’t know the future and ought not to pretend otherwise,” Warsh said.
