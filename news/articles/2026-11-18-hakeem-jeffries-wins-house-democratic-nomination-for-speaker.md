## The choice would place a Black lawmaker in the top House post

House Democrats nominated Hakeem Jeffries on Wednesday, November 18, 2026, to serve as speaker of the House when the new Congress begins next year. The 56-year-old New York congressman, the Democratic minority leader since 2023, was selected by a recorded vote at a behind-closed-doors caucus meeting in which no other Democrat stood against him.

The nomination sets up a formal House election at the beginning of next year. With Democrats set to control both the Senate and House and undertake efforts to check President Donald Trump's powers, Jeffries is set to become one of Trump's most prominent opponents during the final two years of Trump's presidency.

Democrats also selected California congressman Ted Lieu to serve as caucus chair. The nomination came after what The Guardian described as a surprisingly good performance in the midterms, in which voters returned Democrats to power in both chambers with significant majorities. The party was looking to capitalize on the result, while some progressives had expressed concern that Jeffries represented a politically risky status quo.

Jeffries has been in office since 2013 and served as House Democratic caucus chair while Nancy Pelosi was speaker from 2019 to 2023. He became Democratic leader when Pelosi stepped down after Democrats lost their House majority in 2022. Pelosi is set to retire from Congress at the end of this year.

If the House elects him next year, Jeffries would become the first Black lawmaker to serve as House speaker and the first Democrat in the post since Pelosi. Democrats will begin the new Congress with at least 257 House seats, their largest majority in nearly two decades.

The Guardian reported that some progressives were rankled by Jeffries's refusal to endorse proposals to create a Medicare for All health care system and his failure to take a strong stance against aid to Israel amid accusations that Israel carried out a genocide in Gaza.

A poll released earlier this month by Indivisible, a nationwide progressive organizing group, found that only 28% of registered voters viewed Jeffries favorably.

Jeffries has recently sought to bridge those divides. On Tuesday, he announced support for lowering the age of eligibility for Medicare, expanding its coverage to include vision and dental treatments, and offering Medicare to children. He has also called for “dramatic change” in the US relationship with Israel, though in July he did not vote for an amendment that would have cut military aid to the Middle Eastern ally.

Don Leonard, a newly elected Democrat who represents an Ohio district Donald Trump won in the 2024 election, told CNN he would not vote for Jeffries unless Jeffries secured commitments to end aid to Israel and undo the Supreme Court's Citizens United ruling. The ruling allowed outside groups to spend unlimited amounts on elections.

Shortly before the caucus vote, Leonard confirmed to CNN that Jeffries had not met his demands and that he would not vote for him. The caucus nevertheless selected Jeffries by recorded vote.
