## Newsletter says Social Security is on the path to insolvency

Richard Rubin and Kara Dapena examine proposed fixes to Social Security's funding shortfall in "Ways to Narrow Social Security's Shortfall," a feature spotlighted in the Oct. 24, 2026 edition of The Wall Street Journal's Weekend Reads newsletter. The newsletter says the piece takes up "some of the ideas being discussed" and examines "how far they go to narrow the shortfall and how long they postpone the insolvency date, if at all."

The edition opens on what it treats as settled ground. "Everyone can agree that Social Security spends more on benefits than it receives in tax revenue," the newsletter writes. What people do not agree on, it says, is "how to shore up the system to keep it viable for future generations."

On the available approaches, the newsletter states: "The system is on the path to insolvency, but there are ideas out there to boost revenue or cut spending." The email accompanying the edition does not name the individual proposals or attach cost estimates to any of them; it directs subscribers to Rubin and Dapena's piece. The newsletter's own contribution is therefore a signpost, and the work of comparing how much each idea would close the gap and how much time each would buy sits in the feature it promotes.

The rest of the edition turns to the labor market and to consumer habits.
