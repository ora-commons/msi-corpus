## Lawsuit says Intel quarterly earnings fell 9% to $8.1bn

Shareholders have sued US chip giant Intel, accusing it of concealing how severe a slump in personal computer (PC) sales had become — a slowdown they say led to a nearly 29% one-day collapse in the company's stock.

Legal firm Robbins Geller Rudman & Dowd filed the proposed class action in San Jose, California, on behalf of owners of Intel common shares between 23 July and 29 October this year. The suit names the chipmaker, chief executive Tan Lip-Bu and other company leaders as defendants.

The plaintiffs contend that Intel was too late to alert shareholders about how slowing sales would affect its earnings, allegedly keeping the stock price artificially inflated in the meantime.

The complaint accused Intel of making "materially false and/or misleading" statements that left out both the slowdown in PC sales and the impact of its data centre projects.

According to the lawsuit, Intel's October announcement that computer hardware sales had dropped sharply in the three months ended in September undercut earlier suggestions from Intel that demand had been improving, sending Intel shares plunging.

The "depressed demand for PC sales" caused "a dramatic slowdown" in quarterly earnings, the lawsuit said — earnings fell 9%, or about $800m, to $8.1bn.

The disclosure claims follow a 30 October session in which Intel shares plunged, erasing tens of billions of dollars from the company's market value, one day after Intel forecast disappointing revenue in its coming earnings report. BBC News reported that it has contacted Intel for comment.

Intel has poured huge investments into manufacturing to meet the enormous demand for AI computing power. Chips for PCs and other electronics have also become more expensive as a growing share of semiconductors is channelled towards data centres.

Intel boss Tan Lip-Bu had told analysts that the industry was facing an "unprecedented memory crunch", resulting in a shortage of chip supplies for PCs. "We need to address these challenges with urgency," Tan said in October.

Over the past month Intel shares have fallen 37.5%, although the stock is still up 51% overall this year.
