## Associated Press found Francis received a Karadima victim's letter accusing Barros of covering up

In a 2023 Associated Press interview, Pope Francis said the 2018 news conference aboard the papal flight home from Chile was a turning point in his understanding of the clergy sexual abuse scandal. Associated Press reporter Nicole Winfield, who challenged him during that exchange, recounted Francis telling her, “I couldn’t believe it. You were the one on the plane who told me, ‘No, that’s not the way it is, Father,’” and then, “That’s when the bomb went off, when I saw the corruption of many bishops in this.” Francis made a gesture indicating that his head had exploded.

Winfield described the news conference as unusual. Turbulence had forced a pause in the questioning, but when Francis returned to the front of the economy section of the papal plane, she picked up where other journalists had left off. Like other journalists, she challenged Francis because, she said, he had discredited victims of Chile’s most notorious abuser and seemed completely indifferent to their pain.

Winfield wrote that Francis had bungled the Chile case by defending Juan Barros, a bishop implicated in the cover-up for Fernando Karadima, whom she described as Chile’s most notorious abuser. Francis had called the victims’ claims “calumny” and slander and, over their objections, appointed Barros to head a Chilean diocese.

Questioned by reporters on the plane, Francis insisted no victims had come forward to accuse Barros of protecting Karadima. Winfield and other journalists challenged him, saying they knew otherwise. “It’s the victims who are saying this,” Winfield told him. Francis replied, “I haven’t heard from any victim of Barros.” Winfield insisted, “There are! There are!” The pope interrupted, but she cut him off and said, “No! There are victims of Karadima who say that Barros was there!”

Francis replied, “But they didn’t come forward,” and “They didn’t give evidence for a judgment. You, with good intentions, tell me that there are victims, but I haven’t seen them because they didn’t present themselves.” Associated Press reporting subsequently showed that Francis had received a letter from one of Karadima’s victims accusing Barros of covering up for Karadima’s crimes.

Winfield said Francis later credited the Chile trip and his encounter with journalists with opening his eyes. Her account also says Francis commissioned a full Vatican investigation into the scandal, apologized to the victims in person and demanded that the entire Chilean hierarchy offer to resign en masse.

The Vatican released “The Private Diary of Pope Francis” on Tuesday and issued the diary globally in several languages. Its entries describe Francis’s growing recognition of the scandal’s severity after the journalists’ questioning. He wrote: “Devastated because of the situation in Chile and the dialogue with the journalists on the airplane, I became aware of how serious the problem was. … It was hard for me to admit what happened in Chile; I didn’t think it was so serious.”

The diary says Francis saw the “gates of hell” in the Chilean scandal after the Chile trip, describing it as “Pagan seduction in full force.” In another entry, he recalled that after returning from Chile, he saw the devil and heard him speak. “I can’t forget the powerful raspiness of his voice,” he wrote.

The diary also recounts a separate spiritual crisis Francis endured in 2021-22. The account says the crisis was apparently connected to his own temptations to exercise power omnipotently and suggests that he overcame it by entrusting himself entirely to God’s mercy.

Cardinal Víctor Manuel Fernández, the Argentine theologian who heads the Vatican’s doctrine office, authored the volume; his comments contextualize Francis’s entries in his journal. Winfield said she understood the diary’s significance only after reading it and realizing how much Francis had suffered when he understood the depths of the Chile problem.
