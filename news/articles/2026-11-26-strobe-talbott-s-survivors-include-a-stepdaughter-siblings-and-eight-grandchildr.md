## The Guardian calls Brookings one of the US's most prominent think tanks

The New York Times reported that Strobe Talbott was survived by two sons, Devin and Adrian; stepdaughter Rebecca Ascher-Walsh; siblings Kirk, Marjo and Page Talbott; and eight grandchildren. The Guardian characterized Talbott as the architect of the Clinton administration's approach to Russia and other formerly Soviet countries. The Guardian also described the Brookings Institution as one of the US's most prominent think tanks.

Strobe Talbott, a former Time reporter who became deputy secretary of state under Bill Clinton and president of the Brookings Institution, died Thursday at his home in East Hampton, New York. His son, Devin Talbott, said his father died peacefully after a long illness. Talbott was 80.

Talbott studied at Yale and Oxford, where he roomed with Clinton. After interning for Time in Moscow, the magazine asked him to help translate the leaked memoirs of Nikita Khrushchev, the former first secretary of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union. The assignment led to a job at the magazine.

During his 21 years at Time, Talbott focused on Soviet-American relations. He held positions including Washington bureau chief and foreign-affairs columnist before leaving the magazine for the Clinton administration.

Talbott joined the US state department in 1994 and quickly became deputy secretary of state. In that role, he conducted negotiations between the US and India after India's nuclear tests in 1998.

He served as president of the Brookings Institution from 2002 to 2017. During the same period, Talbott chaired the US state department's foreign affairs policy board.

Talbott authored over a dozen books. His final book, *Our Founders’ Warning: The Age of Reason Meets the Age of Trump*, was published in 2020.

Talbott was born in Dayton, Ohio, in 1946. His first wife, Brooke Shearer, died in 2009. He later married author Barbara Lazear Ascher.
