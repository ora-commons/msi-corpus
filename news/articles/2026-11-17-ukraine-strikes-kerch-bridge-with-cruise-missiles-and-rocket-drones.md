## Damage complicates resupply while one rail line remains open

Ukraine’s two-day series of strikes on the Kerch road bridge culminated overnight in the collapse of a roadway span and its supporting arch. The Guardian described the operation as the culmination of weeks and perhaps months of military operations and years of industrial development, during which Ukraine developed its own cruise missiles and long-range drones. Volodymyr Zelenskyy said the attack involved Ukrainian-made Neptune and Flamingo FP-5 cruise missiles, Palianytsia rocket drones and Ruta cruise missiles from Dutch manufacturer Destinus, the only foreign arms company confirmed as involved.

The demolition remained partial. The rail bridge still stood with one line in operation, while satellite imagery appeared to show road damage that Ben Hodges, a former commanding general of the US army in Europe, said “cannot be repaired any time soon.” The 19km (12 mile) road bridge cost $3.6bn (£2.7bn) and was completed in 2018.

The crossing connected Russia to eastern Crimea and was closely associated with Vladimir Putin’s efforts to secure the 2014 annexation of Crimea by opening a supply route to the peninsula. Workers cheered when Putin drove an orange truck into Crimea after construction was complete. Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov said at the time, “Putin initiated this project himself.” The full-scale invasion of Ukraine came four years later, in 2022. The Guardian said the invasion had, if anything, made Russia’s position in Crimea more precarious, though the change in the balance of power was still a while coming.

The strike relied on weapon systems Ukraine developed over several years. The Guardian contrasted Russia’s ability in 2022 to bomb anywhere in Ukraine with Kyiv’s lack of Western long-range missiles powerful enough for such a politically sensitive and strategic target. Ukraine tried to blow up the bridge with a truck bomb in 2022 and a sea drone attack a year later, but neither did much long-term damage. The Guardian said that only as Ukraine’s own long-range drones and cruise missiles came to fruition near the beginning of 2026 did “a knockout strike became possible.”

Zelenskyy said on Tuesday that the attack was the “seventh stage” of an operation “carried out over several weeks.” He said radars, jamming equipment and air-defense systems were progressively neutralized, opening corridors for the strikes.

Russia was striking bridges as well. In early October, Moscow attacked Kyiv’s busy Northern and Southern bridges as part of a scaled-up campaign against the capital and other large cities. The Guardian said Russian efforts were focused on knocking out electricity, heating and water supplies, and that attacks on bridges showed the Kremlin’s target list expanding at a time when Ukraine could strike back. NATO secretary general Mark Rutte said a record 50,000 Russian troops were killed or seriously wounded in October as Russia struggled to make progress on the frontline.

Military Informant, a pro-war Russian military blogger, said Ukraine had “parity in firepower” after bringing down the road bridge in two days of air raids. The blogger said it could not be ruled out that the railway section would be destroyed next.

The Guardian said that, if anything, further attacks on the bridge were almost certain both to prevent its repair and to make resupplying Crimea more difficult. If both the road and rail sections were cut, Moscow would have only the R-280 land highway from Rostov to Crimea, which Ukrainian drone attacks had targeted for months.

Hodges had long argued that Crimea could become a defining theater of the war because of what Crimea represented to Putin. He argued that Ukraine should try to make “Russian occupation untenable” by destroying the bridge and asserting fire control over the R-280. The Guardian said a supply squeeze would develop slowly, though its political effects could be felt sooner.

The strike came two days before Putin was due to meet Donald Trump and Xi Jinping in Shenzhen. The Guardian described the damage to a project closely associated with the Russian leader as an embarrassment ahead of the talks. Hodges said bringing down the road bridge “further kills the narrative that Russian victory is inevitable or that Crimea is theirs,” although Trump might not bring up the episode in his meeting with Putin and Xi on Thursday.

The Guardian warned that the danger was that both sides, in their desperation to end a deadlocked war, would escalate further.

Emily Ferris, a Russia expert with the Rusi thinktank, said Ukraine’s military-industrial complex had been ready for some months. In her view, the timing of the attack could be interpreted only as political. The Guardian said Zelenskyy, cut out of the talks in Shenzhen, had little choice but to remind Trump and Putin that Ukraine still had cards to play. “They were keeping it for a rainy day, and that day has come,” she said.
