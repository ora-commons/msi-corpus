## The notice is not a license and requires all requisite consents

Kenya's mining ministry issued a notice of its intention to award the coastal Mrima Hill project in Kwale County to the Frontier Minerals Consortium, which the government selected as preferred bidder to develop niobium and rare earth deposits. The consortium comprises Frontier Minerals LLC, Bunker Hill Mining Corp., ReElement Technologies and American Resources Corp.

The selection did not itself authorize mining. The ministry stressed that “the notice is not a license.” The consortium must now “go to the ground,” engage communities and obtain “all requisite consents” before applying for a prospecting license, according to the ministry.

Residents and farmers said they fear losing their land if the project proceeds. Justus Mwero, chairman of the Mrima Farm Owners Community-Based Organization, said: “Until now, we have received no information from the government on the content of the project,” adding that “It will be hard for us to express our consent or opinion to the company with no information.”

Elders from the local Digo community worry about damage to a sacred forest where, they said, their ancestors are buried. The Mrima Hill Sacred Grove was designated a national monument in 1992.

The selection comes as countries including the U.S. race to secure rare earth minerals used in electric vehicle motors, wind turbines and defense technologies, seeking alternatives to China's dominance.

Kenya's ministry said it had “deliberately” chosen investors before starting formal consent processes. Mining Minister Hassan Joho said in a Nov. 2 interview that the community was consulted before bids were sought.

Fakii Omar of the Kwale Mining Alliance said, “The government should make the proposal public, so that the company can be held accountable. The government should not distance themselves from their responsibilities and leave it to the investor.”

Omar also questioned the forest's legal status. “The notice does not mention if the protected status of the forest has been revoked,” he said. He asked, “Can the proposed mine coexist with the protected status under current laws?”

A previous attempt to mine the site ended in arbitration. In 2013, Kenya's environment regulator rejected an environmental assessment for a license held by Cortec Mining Kenya, citing the site's protected status, according to Extractives Daily. An arbitration tribunal of the International Centre for Settlement of Investment Disputes later ruled that the license should not have been issued without environmental approval, and Kenya won the case.

The Centre for Litigation Trust, a Kenyan civil society group, has asked the High Court in Kwale County to suspend a proposed Kenya-U.S. agreement on Mrima Hill's minerals until residents are consulted. Kenya's attorney general has challenged the court's jurisdiction.
