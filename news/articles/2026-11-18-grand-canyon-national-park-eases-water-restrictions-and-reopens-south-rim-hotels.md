## Late-August flooding killed three people and damaged key park infrastructure

Officials at Grand Canyon National Park in Arizona said Wednesday that the park was easing water restrictions, allowing hotels on the South Rim to accommodate overnight guests again.

The El Tovar Hotel and several other lodges were scheduled to reopen Friday. The park said it had reached a milestone in restoring water service after severe flooding earlier this year.

The late-August flooding caused three deaths and prompted rescues. The flooding damaged key infrastructure, including a water intake pump and a significant stretch of a decades-old pipeline.

The Transcanyon Waterline supplied water for millions of visitors to the South Rim and about 1,600 residents of Grand Canyon Village.

At the time, park officials said they were just over a month away from completing a more than $200 million upgrade to the 12.5-mile-long (20-kilometer) Transcanyon Waterline. More flooding in September damaged temporary repairs.

Food and beverage services were set to resume gradually, according to park officials.

The park said restoration would continue and urged visitors to conserve. “Simple actions, including taking shorter showers, turning off faucets when not in use and avoiding unnecessary water use, help support the park’s water system as operations resume,” the park said in its announcement.
