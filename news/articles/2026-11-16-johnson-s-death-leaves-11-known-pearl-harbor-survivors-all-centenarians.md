## USS St Louis was the only major ship to leave the attack unscathed

Only 11 known Pearl Harbor survivors are still living, all of them centenarians, following the death of Freeman K Johnson, the oldest known survivor, on Saturday at his home in Massachusetts at the age of 106, according to the Boston Globe.

At 21 years old, Johnson said he never saw the attacks from the Japanese fighters that Sunday morning in Hawaii on 7 December 1941. He was a fireman below the deck on the USS St Louis, his first deployment after basic training.

Johnson heard machine guns and felt vibrations while he was reading that morning. "That was the first sign we knew something was wrong," Johnson told the Boston Globe in 2018.

He was tasked with fixing parts of the ship's boilers as another member of the crew passed the boiler's pieces to Johnson. "While all the rigamarole was going on topside, I was inside a steam drum. Couldn't see anything, absolutely nothing," Johnson told the Associated Press in May.

The USS St Louis was the only major ship that left the attack "unscathed," according to the US Department of War. The ship graced the nickname "Lucky Lou."

Johnson said his work during Pearl Harbor didn't make him heroic, nor was it a source of his pride. "I wasn't brave, I was there," he said last year in a Pacific Historic Parks interview. "I just did what I was told. I did my job."

His pride, instead, came when he married his late wife Ruth Hills in 1950. They went on to have three daughters, served as foster parents to several others and eventually had two grandkids.

After leaving the navy, Johnson worked several jobs including at a machine shop for 40 years, a convenience store and delivering meals to seniors. He formally retired at 90 and eventually lived with his daughter Diane Johnson until he passed.

"It's going to be a hell of a Thanksgiving," Diane Johnson told the Associated Press. "He's going to be up there with all of his friends and family and my mom."
