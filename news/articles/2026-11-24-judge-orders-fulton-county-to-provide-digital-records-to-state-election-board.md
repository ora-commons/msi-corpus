## State Election Board may consult experts under limited access to unredacted records

Fulton County Superior Court Judge Kelly Lee Ellerbe ordered the county on Tuesday to turn over a digital copy of ballots and other election records seized by the FBI in January. The records must reach Georgia's State Election Board by 5 p.m. Wednesday. The order grants the board access while placing the unredacted records under a protective order that limits who may see them.

The State Election Board, whose majority is aligned with Donald Trump, has spent more than two years trying to obtain Fulton County records from the 2020 election in Georgia's most populous county. It has repeatedly sought to reopen a case alleging wrongdoing by the county. In November 2024, the board issued subpoenas for records, and county election officials asked a judge to quash them.

In December 2025, Fulton County Superior Court Judge Robert McBurney declined to quash the subpoenas, finding that the board's inquiries “fell squarely within the SEB’s authority.” McBurney ruled that the board had to pay the cost of producing the records. A hearing on the dispute was pending when the FBI seized the county's records, and McBurney put the case on hold.

Ellerbe took over the case this year and held a hearing in October before issuing Tuesday's order. Her protective order permits the State Election Board to use the records for its investigations. The board cannot disclose the records beyond people identified in the order, including board members and staff and experts they may consult.

The FBI served a search warrant at the Fulton County elections hub on Jan. 28, seeking records related to the 2020 election, and left with hundreds of boxes containing ballots and other records. Fulton County unsuccessfully asked a judge to order the records returned. The federal government provided the county with what it said was a digital copy of the records taken.

An FBI agent who supported the search warrant said the agency was examining possible “deficiencies or defects” in Fulton County's 2020 vote count. The Associated Press reported that no charges had been filed more than six years after the election and 10 months after the records seizure.

The investigation remained a priority for the FBI. In July, the agency instructed field offices across the country to dedicate more than 200 staffers to the Fulton County inquiry, describing it as a “priority investigation.” The New York Times reported last week that the FBI and federal prosecutors had interviewed the man who served as Fulton County's election director in 2020.

Trump and his allies have focused on the overwhelmingly Democratic county since Trump narrowly lost Georgia's 2020 presidential election to Democrat Joe Biden. The county includes most of Atlanta. Trump has consistently insisted without evidence that widespread voter fraud in the county cost him victory in the state. Multiple courts and state and federal officials have found no evidence of fraud that would have altered the outcome.

A Fulton County spokesperson declined to comment on the pending legal matter. The State Election Board did not immediately respond to an email seeking comment.
