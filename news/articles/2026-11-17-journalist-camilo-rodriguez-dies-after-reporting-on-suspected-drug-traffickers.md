## Attorney General Carlo Diaz says scene resembled a contract killing

Costa Rican authorities said Camilo Rodriguez, 51, was shot dead on Monday, 16 November 2026, on a main highway in Heredia, on the outskirts of the capital, San Jose. Rodriguez gained prominence for publishing allegations against suspected drug traffickers and politicians. The Red Cross reported that Rodriguez was attacked at about 3.20pm local time amid heavy traffic.

The Guardian reported that Rodriguez's killing was the first known killing of a journalist in Costa Rica in 25 years. The Guardian said the killing came as Costa Rica grappled with a surge in drug violence driven by international trafficking groups, despite the country having long been regarded as one of Central America's most peaceful and stable democracies.

President Laura Fernandez condemned the attack in a video message. “I am filled with indignation and outrage over the cowardly murder of communicator Camilo Rodriguez. Regardless of the content of his communications, what happened today in our country is a stain on freedom of expression,” Fernandez said.

Attorney General Carlo Diaz told local news outlet Extra Noticias that investigators found multiple bullet impacts and a crime scene resembling a contract killing. Diaz said the scene was likely linked to the allegations Rodriguez had published. He gave no further evidence and did not name suspects.

“We know he was a communicator who publicly identified people for alleged links to drug trafficking,” Diaz said. He added that Rodriguez had previously reported threats but had declined protection.

Diaz also said: “Silencing someone who reports on drug trafficking or corruption, in this case a communicator, is a signal we cannot tolerate in a democratic society.” He pledged efforts to hold those responsible for the murder accountable.

The Guardian reported that the last such attack on a media worker in Costa Rica occurred in July 2001, when radio producer Parmenio Medina was gunned down on a Santo Domingo highway, in roughly the same area where Rodriguez was attacked.

The Guardian also reported that Costa Rica's homicide rate doubled between 2013 and 2023 and had only eased slightly since then.
