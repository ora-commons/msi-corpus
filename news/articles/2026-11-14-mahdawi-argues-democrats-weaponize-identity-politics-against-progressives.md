## Jeffries expected to win speakership with no real challenger

Arwa Mahdawi, a columnist for The Guardian, argued in commentary published November 14, 2026 that corporate Democrats are using identity politics to ward off reasonable criticism. The column's standfirst read: "House members have responded to legitimate criticism with cynicism. Let's not have another 'Bernie bros' moment." Mahdawi opened by warning readers that disagreeing with her might mean they are "racist, homophobic and/or sexist" — an approach she said was "taking a page from the corporate Democrat playbook." "From Haley Stevens to Hakeem Jeffries, cynically weaponizing identity politics to ward off reasonable criticism seems to be all the rage," she wrote.

Mahdawi's first example is Stevens, the Michigan representative, whom she wrote attempted to torpedo Abdul El-Sayed's senate campaign by endorsing his Republican opponent, Mike Rogers, at the 11th hour. In an interview with conservative commentator Scott Jennings before the midterms, Stevens seemed to imply that sexism played a part in why she was not invited to an El-Sayed campaign event. "I gave [El-Sayed] an opportunity ... to unite around something that everyone could agree on, and then I got disinvited instead," Stevens told Jennings. "This is what happens to women."

After El-Sayed won, Stevens posted a lengthy essay on Twitter/X. "For me, living my beliefs was never measured by who would win at the ballot box," she wrote, adding: "As a woman, I have the right to make a decision and to change my mind."

Mahdawi turned next to Hakeem Jeffries, who is expected to be the next speaker of the House. She wrote that he is "very chummy with Aipac" — the American Israel Public Affairs Committee — noting that he has received money from the group and that Aipac "has publicly boasted about supporting him." On Thursday, Jeffries refused to say whether he supported or opposed controversial efforts to integrate the US and Israeli militaries. Jeffries also said this summer that he opposes Medicare for All, reversing his earlier position and putting him at odds with a number of progressive Democrats who campaigned on the platform.

Mahdawi wrote that Jeffries has been "consistently hostile to the progressive faction of his party," refusing for months to endorse Mamdani for New York City mayor, and that it was recently revealed Jeffries met with Jared Kushner in private before the midterms, which she wrote did not go down very well. At an event on Friday, a protester yelled at Jeffries: "The American people demanded impeachment!" and "Your version of accountability is shaking hands with the Trump family!"

Mahdawi wrote that many progressives view Jeffries as an establishment figure who offers no hope for meaningful change and want someone less beholden to corporate interests to be speaker. Jeffries and some of his allies, meanwhile, have suggested that denying him the opportunity to become the first Black speaker in US history would be, to quote Bloomberg, "racially insensitive."

During the June congressional primaries, a picture of Jeffries came up on screen at a victory party for Claire Valdez, a member of the Democratic Socialists of America, and the crowd yelled: "You're next!" Jeffries said in a CNN interview this week: "It was very interesting to me, as observed by others, that there was almost zero diversity in that crowd."

Mahdawi disputed the implication, writing that there is "plenty of diversity amongst DSA-affiliated politicians." She listed Zohran Mamdani, the first Muslim mayor of New York; Nithya Raman and Janeese Lewis George, who just got elected as the respective mayors of Los Angeles and Washington DC; and DSA members David Morales and Yousef Rabhi, who are going to be the next mayors of Providence, Rhode Island, and Ann Arbor, Michigan. She noted that all six of the DSA candidates elected to the House were "Black, brown, or from marginalized backgrounds." Mahdawi acknowledged that DSA supporters skew white, but wrote that painting it as some sort of racist organization, "as Jeffries seems to be doing, is intellectually dishonest."

Mahdawi wrote that this was "the Bernie bros playbook all over again." Despite the fact that some polls found that young women supported Bernie Sanders in greater numbers than young men in the 2016 primaries, Sanders' supporters were portrayed as misogynistic bros, she wrote. Hillary Clinton's campaign may not have coined the term, Mahdawi added, but "leaned heavily into this bad-faith narrative."

"Let's not have a Bernie bros 2.0 moment, please," Mahdawi wrote. A petition to make the progressive Massachusetts congresswoman Ayanna Pressley the speaker of the House reached around 100,000 signatures on Friday, she noted, while Jeffries faces no real challengers and is almost certainly going to be the next speaker. Her conclusion was addressed directly to him: "Jeffries has an opportunity to listen to reasonable concerns about his leadership and build a broad coalition that includes progressives instead of sidelining them."
