## Ruling covers Texas, Louisiana and Mississippi; appeal to Supreme Court likely

The 5th Circuit Court of Appeals issued its 9-8 decision on Friday, siding with Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton's claim that Congress exceeded its constitutional authority when it passed the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act in 2022. The law strengthens the rights of women to receive workplace accommodations for needs related to pregnancy and childbirth, such as time off for medical appointments and exemptions from heavy lifting, and it passed with bipartisan support.

The appeals court said the act was unconstitutional because a majority of U.S. House members were not physically present when it was approved as part of a spending package in December 2022. At the time, the House was operating under COVID-era rules that allowed members to vote by proxy.

"The (Quorum) Clause's text, context, original understanding and unbroken historical application all indicate that members of Congress must be present for quorum purposes — as the Supreme Court expressly held over 125 years ago and as Congress has done ever since the founding," the court said in its decision. "'Proxy presence' does not count."

Advocates of the law said the decision invalidates it in Texas, Louisiana and Mississippi, which are covered by the 5th Circuit, and could imperil the law elsewhere.

A Better Balance, the advocacy group that spearheaded the law's passage, said in a statement Friday that the circuit court ruling would likely be appealed to the U.S. Supreme Court. Inimai Chettiar, the group's president, called the ruling "legally unsound and short-sighted."

"The 5th Circuit has put the health, livelihoods, and economic security of millions of American women and their families at risk," Chettiar said in a statement.

Paxton sued to block the act in 2023 on grounds that it was unconstitutional. In 2024, a federal judge agreed and blocked enforcement of the law for Texas state employees.

In 2025, a three-judge panel on the 5th Circuit disagreed, finding that the law was properly passed under Congress's pandemic-era rules. That decision was appealed to the full 5th Circuit, which issued Friday's ruling.

In their dissent, the eight judges who would have upheld the law noted that no member of Congress objected that a quorum was lacking when the $1.7 trillion government funding bill was passed.

"The State of Texas, having accepted the vast majority of the funds that Congress appropriated to it, now contends that the portion of the bill providing protections for pregnant women and workers violates the Quorum Clause," they wrote.

While the law was passed during the Biden administration, the Department of Justice under the Trump administration has continued to fight Paxton's lawsuit, which — if successful — could open the door to legal challenges of other pandemic-era laws passed by proxy.

A message seeking comment on the appeals court decision was left Sunday with the Justice Department.
