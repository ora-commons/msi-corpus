## Bipartisan lawmakers press for tighter restrictions on China chip exports

Chinese leader Xi Jinping is preparing to host President Trump and Russian leader Vladimir Putin in the southern Chinese city of Shenzhen on Thursday for an unprecedented trilateral gathering. Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang’s resignation over an affair with a woman whom U.S. officials suspect was working for Chinese intelligence is set to overshadow the event. The Wall Street Journal reported that the case is undermining Xi’s message that he has successfully stabilized ties with the United States. The Journal characterized the situation as precarious for Xi’s global diplomatic offensive.

Trump is traveling to China to participate in the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, his third in-person meeting with Xi so far this year. Those meetings have produced relatively few tangible breakthroughs, but they have also helped prevent bilateral relations from worsening. Huang’s resignation will loom over the APEC gathering. The Journal said the trilateral meeting is intended to place China on an equal footing with Washington and Moscow.

“We’re operating in a very narrow lane in which these summits serve to maintain a stable floor in the relationship,” said Andrew Harding, a Heritage Foundation analyst who previously worked on Trump’s National Security Council. “There’s a lot of mistrust in the relationship.”

Analysts do not expect the Huang affair to dramatically alter Trump’s approach to China, according to The Wall Street Journal. The spying allegations have nevertheless bolstered congressional hawks who describe China as one of the U.S.’s gravest security threats. Some top Republicans and Democrats are already pushing to tighten restrictions on chip exports to China, potentially setting the stage for a new round of escalating trade tensions in the months ahead.

Even before the Huang affair, the trilateral summit carried risks for Xi. The gathering could fuel suspicion about China’s intentions among pro-Ukraine lawmakers in Washington. Two years out from a presidential election, a more aggressive posture toward China was viewed as conceivable regardless of whether Republicans or Democrats win the White House. “Beijing is already thinking about what comes after Trump,” said Sarah Beran, president of the National Committee on U.S.-China Relations. “And if hosting this meeting kicks up opposition from the Hill and from the Dems, in the long run that’s actually more challenging.”

On the economic side, The Journal said Beijing is somewhat less susceptible to the threat of U.S. tariffs than it was a few years ago, with China’s control of the world’s supply of rare earths giving it critical leverage against U.S. tariffs in a trade war. Separately, advances in China’s domestic technologies, including Chinese AI firms that rival the best in Silicon Valley, have blunted the effects of U.S. export restrictions.

Chips remain a major obstacle to China’s ambitions. Domestic companies remain unable to produce semiconductors for AI on par with those from Nvidia, and The Journal said that gap was part of the reason the American chipmaker would have been an attractive target for Chinese spying.

Diplomatically, The Journal reported that Xi is pressing ahead with a strategy showing growing confidence in China’s position against Russia and the United States. It characterized Russian leader Vladimir Putin as having taken on the role of junior partner as he became bogged down in Ukraine and came to rely on Beijing to buy Russian oil and gas.

The Journal said a trilateral summit with Putin and Trump would have been all but unthinkable for Beijing even just a few years ago. It also said China has historically resisted three-way diplomacy with the United States and Russia, pursuing its interests with each separately while staying outside the Washington-Moscow relationship. The Huang case has refocused attention on China’s efforts to close its technology gap with the West and its increasing alignment with Russia.

Even if the trilateral summit breaks little new ground, the optics legitimize “the idea that the big powers can decide over the heads of smaller powers,” said Alicja Bachulska, who researches China-EU relations at the European Council on Foreign Relations. The Journal reported that the meeting would also signal China’s rise as a global power and that Xi has been trying to project himself as a force for peace and stability in developing countries. It said hosting Trump and Putin helps reinforce that message.

While many Chinese are struggling economically, on foreign policy Beijing “can still tell the story about ‘China on the march,’” said Jacob Stokes, a senior fellow at the Center for a New American Security. “Xi Jinping wants to be seen as sitting at the high table of world politics.”

The broader diplomatic push seeks to use a potentially narrow window to reset U.S. ties in ways favorable to China, according to The Journal. Xi wants to weaken U.S. support for Taiwan during Trump’s final two years in office, the Journal reported. It said the Ukraine war, together with Trump’s war with Iran, had helped keep American attention focused on the security of Europe and the Middle East rather than the Asia-Pacific.

Any discussion about Ukraine without Kyiv’s participation could further damage Xi’s already testy ties with Europe, The Journal said. China relies on Europe as an export market to keep its weakened economy growing. The gathering has drawn comparisons to the Yalta conference in 1945, when Roosevelt, Churchill and Stalin met to settle the postwar European order. Excluding Kyiv and European leaders risks further damage to China’s relations with countries such as France and Germany, which are already moving more aggressively to counter Chinese exports to the European Union.

Domestically, The Journal reported that Beijing needs a positive story about Xi to tell its people as many Chinese grow poorer while the government prioritizes security over growth. It said China’s police apparatus is quick to suppress dissent and described widespread apathy among young people as one sign of weariness with Xi’s agenda after 14 years in power.

The trilateral meeting takes place Thursday in Shenzhen. In the wake of the Huang affair, some top Republicans and Democrats are already pushing to tighten restrictions on chip exports to China, according to The Journal. Separately, The Journal said China’s rare-earth supply provides leverage against U.S. tariffs in a trade war, while advances in domestic AI firms have blunted some effects of U.S. export restrictions.
