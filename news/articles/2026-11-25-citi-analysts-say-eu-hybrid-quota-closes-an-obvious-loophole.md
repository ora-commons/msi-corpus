## Citi sees quota as possible key driver of European automaker share recovery

Citi analysts said the European Union's quota on new hybrid passenger-vehicle imports closes an obvious loophole and could be a key driver of any recovery in European automaker shares. Citi's note linked that assessment to rapid growth in hybrid imports and Chinese automakers' move into the segment after EU battery-electric vehicle tariffs.

The measure is provisional and runs for 200 days. It covers imports of new hybrid passenger vehicles.

Imports above country-specific allocations will not be permitted.

Citi cited a 49% increase in total hybrid imports since 2023. The analysts also cited a more than tenfold increase in imports from China.

Chinese automakers responded to the EU's battery-electric vehicle tariffs by moving rapidly into the hybrid sector, Citi said. The analysts said the new quota closed that route.

Citi framed the measure as part of a wider change in the bloc's approach. “European trade protection is therefore broadening beyond BEVs to the wider electrified-car market,” the analysts wrote.

The investment consequence runs through how investors assess the region's manufacturers. Citi said that without the quota, investors would continue to view EU original equipment manufacturers as unattractive.

With the quota in place, the analysts said the measure could be a key driver of any recovery in European automaker shares. The claim remained conditional, while the restriction's provisional 200-day term defined the period it covers.
