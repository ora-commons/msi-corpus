## Truth Social video showed journalists jostling to get into the Xi-Putin meeting room

Trump announced the press conference while flying home from a three-day Asia trip, his first abroad since the midterms. The Wall Street Journal described the planned Friday-afternoon event as part of a White House strategy to show voters that the administration can break through gridlock in the nation's capital. During the trip, Trump negotiated face-to-face with world leaders as dignitaries vied for his attention and elaborate spectacles were staged in his honor.

From Air Force One, Trump posted a Truth Social video showing a throng of journalists jostling each other to get into an ornate room where he was meeting with Xi and Putin. The Wall Street Journal said the post was meant to signal to supporters that his Asia trip was “must-see TV.”

## Joint commitment on nuclear risks

The trip culminated in what The Wall Street Journal called an unprecedented three-way summit with the leaders of China and Russia. The Journal said the trip offered a glimpse into how Trump was expected to operate in the twilight of his presidency. According to people close to Trump, as his influence in Washington waned, he was increasingly determined to remain a dominant figure on the world stage.

Trump had long sought a three-way deal that would bring Beijing to the table and potentially curb China's rapid nuclear buildup, a breakthrough that had eluded previous presidents. He indicated that he hoped to use the Shenzhen summit to advance one of his most ambitious legacy projects: a nuclear arms agreement with Russia and China. The meeting did not produce that agreement. Trump, Xi and Putin emerged with a broad commitment to continue discussing nuclear risks, while their joint statement offered few details about what would happen next. The Journal wrote, “But even the high-profile trilateral didn’t seem to satisfy Trump’s appetite for a legacy-defining diplomatic breakthrough.”

## Domestic investigations and service outages

As Trump departed China, his administration was on day seven of a halting public response to outages that grounded flights, froze emails and delayed banking transactions. The trip included a military flyover, musical performances and other ceremonies honoring Trump. Some Trump advisers privately expressed frustration with Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin, who was sidelined from some television interviews after an uneven early response to the outages.

Rep. Robert Garcia (D., Calif.), the incoming chairman of the House Oversight Committee, issued document-preservation notices to Trump family members and administration members. The Journal said the notices set the stage for months of investigations surrounding Trump and his inner circle as Democrats took control of Congress.

Washington was also dealing with the ouster of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang amid allegations that Huang had an intimate relationship with a suspected Chinese intelligence asset. The Journal said the scandal exposed deep divisions among Trump's senior advisers.

## Remaining foreign-policy goals

The trip followed steep midterm losses for Trump's party and Democrats' control of Congress. With no more elections of his own and just over two years left in office, Trump was betting that foreign-policy breakthroughs could cement his legacy in ways that had eluded previous presidents. The Journal said Trump was making that bet and that the political headwinds had done little to constrain his ambitions abroad. Trump had signaled to advisers that he planned to chase the kinds of diplomatic breakthroughs that could secure his place in history, including a nuclear agreement with Russia and China.

The Journal wrote, “But his determination to make history could hand America’s rivals an advantage as they weigh how much the U.S. president might be willing to concede to secure deals before his time in office runs out.” The Journal added that foreign policy experts “note that he will face an uphill battle in securing deals on intractable global issues that have bedeviled U.S. presidents for decades.”

During the trip, Trump repeatedly floated the possibility of another meeting with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, hoping to revive nuclear negotiations that collapsed during his first term. His overtures prompted speculation about a surprise encounter, but Trump left Asia without seeing Kim.

The Journal reported that Trump was expected to turn to Latin America, where he had long promised “to topple more than six decades of Communist rule in Cuba.” Trump spends a lot of time in South Florida and with Cuban-American allies including Secretary of State Marco Rubio, and has long told advisers he wants to accomplish what eluded generations of American presidents, including John F. Kennedy. In a radio interview the week after the midterm losses, Trump said, “I want to get Cuba done before I leave.”

The Journal reported that Trump had struggled to end the pair of wars in Iran and Ukraine that had dogged his presidency. On the sidelines of the Shenzhen summit, he held a separate meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin. Trump warmly embraced the Russian leader, whom he called “my friend,” and predicted that “a lot of wonderful things” could emerge from their talks. The Kremlin said Putin emerged in an “excellent mood” from the meeting, but Putin offered no public concessions.

## Ceremonies across the Asia trip

In Manila, a marching band greeted Trump with a rendition of “YMCA,” his signature campaign song. Former Philippine first lady Imelda Marcos, 97, made a rare public appearance to meet the U.S. president. Trump told Marcos, “You always have been the most beautiful,” and the widow of the country’s former dictator blew him a kiss.

Chinese leader Xi Jinping personally greeted Trump at the airport in Shenzhen, where the president received a colorful military flyover and later another rendition of “YMCA.” The banquet seating arrangement placed Trump at the head of the table. Trump told Fox News, “The fanfare, the beauty, the pageantry, the thousands of people, the young children, the soldiers, the whole thing. It was a great respect,” and added, “It’s a big win for us. It’s a big win for China, too. We get along.”

At a banquet for Asia-Pacific leaders, Trump walked alone and slowly down the red carpet as an eight-piece ensemble serenaded him with “America the Beautiful.” He stopped to listen and turned toward the cameras, the only world leader who paused to pose for the press. White House communications director Steven Cheung said, “There is nobody who can command a room like President Trump, and all of his foreign trips show he is a true leader in every sense of the word.”
