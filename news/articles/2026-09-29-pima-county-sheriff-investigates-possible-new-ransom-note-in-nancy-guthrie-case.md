## Pima County sheriff investigates possible new ransom note in Nancy Guthrie case

**Subtype:** fact

- The Pima County sheriff's department and the FBI Guthrie taskforce are investigating a possible new ransom note in the case of Nancy Guthrie, nearly eight months after her disappearance from her Tucson-area home.
- Officials have not disclosed how they learned of the possible note or what it contains, and said investigators are working to determine its credibility.
- Two earlier ransom notes released in July gave conflicting messages — one said Guthrie was alive and would be released unharmed in exchange for $4 million in cryptocurrency, while a second said she died shortly after being kidnapped.
- No suspects have been arrested in the February 1 disappearance of the 84-year-old mother of Today show host Savannah Guthrie.

## No suspects have been arrested in the February 1 disappearance

The Pima County sheriff's department said Monday that the sheriff's office and the FBI Guthrie taskforce were recently made aware of the possible new ransom note as well as social media comments related to it. "Investigators are working to determine its credibility," the statement said. Officials did not immediately release details on how they found the note or what was in it.

In July, authorities released two earlier ransom notes sent to a Tucson television station in the days after Nancy Guthrie went missing from her home outside Tucson on 1 February. The first note, sent on 2 February, said Nancy Guthrie was safe but scared and would be released unharmed once her captors received $4 million in cryptocurrency. The second note, sent on 6 February, said Guthrie died shortly after she was kidnapped. Authorities have not said whether Guthrie is still alive.

The investigation continues to be marked by unanswered questions, including how many people are believed to have taken part in the abduction and whether she was taken by someone who knew her. No suspects have been arrested.

The FBI has released surveillance videos showing a masked person who authorities said appeared to tamper with a camera on Nancy Guthrie's front porch and block it with a plant. Nancy Guthrie's family has shared their pleas for information in videos posted to social media.
