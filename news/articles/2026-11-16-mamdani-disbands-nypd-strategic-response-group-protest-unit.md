## Protest duties shift to civilian-led group and de-escalation team

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani on Monday announced the disbanding of the Strategic Response Group, the New York Police Department unit that responds to protests, after he faced increasing pressure to fulfill a promise he had made during the campaign in the wake of anti-ICE protests this fall.

Known as the Strategic Response Group, the unit is in charge of making arrests at demonstrations, but protesters and civil liberties groups have long complained about its aggressive tactics.

Under the changes Mamdani announced, responsibility for responding to protests will pass to a civilian-led group operating out of the mayor's Office of Community Safety and to a newly formed specialized NYPD team trained in de-escalation. The mayor said a new patrol division, made up of officers trained in crowd management, will support protest responses. Mamdani, a Democratic socialist, also said the unit's counterterrorism work — which includes heavy weapons deployments at critical infrastructure and major events — will move to a separate bureau focused on intelligence and counterterrorism.

"New Yorkers exercising their constitutional right to protest should not be met with the same police response as a terrorism threat," the mayor said.

The announcement also narrows the tactics the NYPD can deploy against demonstrators. Officers will be barred from using tear gas and long guns in responding to legal demonstrations, according to the mayor. Pepper spray will also not be used as a crowd-management tool, he said.

NYPD Commissioner Jessica Tisch backed the changes, even as some law enforcement groups have said the Strategic Response Group plays a critical role at demonstrations.

"I would never support changes that diminish the NYPD's ability to protect New Yorkers, or that put our officers in a position where they lack the resources necessary to confront a serious threat," Tisch said. "These changes will make us a stronger, more effective department."

The disbanding follows through on a campaign pledge. During his mayoral run, Mamdani said he would dismantle the Strategic Response Group and promised to move some police responsibilities to a new department of community safety. Until Monday, he had been vague about the timeline for ending the unit, even as some progressive supporters grew impatient with his lack of action.

Pressure on the mayor has built from two directions. Calls to disband the group arose in response to its actions at anti-Israel demonstrations, and those calls intensified this fall after protests over a shooting in the Bronx during a U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement operation. Video footage from the protests showed NYPD officers knocking anti-ICE demonstrators to the ground. The officers involved were not part of the Strategic Response Group, but many of Mamdani's supporters renewed their calls to end the unit anyway.

Among them was Congresswoman-elect Darializa Avila Chevalier, a close Mamdani ally and Democratic socialist. Avila Chevalier released a statement calling for the abolition of the NYPD's protest unit and for the police commissioner to step down. "The NYPD spent more time protecting a militia that just shot someone than protecting New Yorkers," she said.
