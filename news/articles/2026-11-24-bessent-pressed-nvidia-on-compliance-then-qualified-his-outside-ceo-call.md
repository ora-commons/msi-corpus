## Nvidia still needed to identify responsibility for possible noncompliance

On CBS’s “Face the Nation” Sunday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent qualified the call he had made a week earlier for Nvidia to hire an outside CEO. He said Huang “is on the outside now,” declined to say whether the chief executive should return and said Nvidia still needed to establish who was responsible for possible noncompliance.

A week earlier, Bessent had sharply criticized Nvidia’s compliance culture in the wake of the scandal in which Huang admitted to affairs, including one with Alice Huang. The Wall Street Journal reported that some U.S. authorities suspected Alice Huang of working for Chinese intelligence.

Bessent also expressed frustration in a string of interviews that China was receiving Nvidia’s chips. During a Fox News interview on Nov. 15, he set a deadline of 10 days to receive answers from the board and outside counsel.

Bessent invoked banking oversight while arguing for executive removals. “If this were a bank, and they hadn’t complied like this, the OCC would be in there right now,” he said, referring to the Office of the Comptroller of the Currency, a key banking regulator. “And a lot of the executives would be removed.”

On Sunday night, after Bessent’s qualification, Trump posted that he hoped Nvidia’s board would reinstate Huang. On Monday, Nvidia announced a unanimous board vote to restore Huang, less than two weeks after his ouster and following a push from President Trump and other administration officials. The vote restored Nvidia’s co-founder and the only CEO the chipmaker had ever had and cited input from “senior levels of the U.S. government.”

Trump had also expressed sympathy for Huang on the day he was ousted and later suggested former Apple CEO Tim Cook should take the helm of the chip maker. On Monday, Trump called into CNBC for a nearly hourlong interview, welcomed Huang back and called him “brilliant” and “one of our great stars.”

Harvey Jones, a longtime Nvidia board member who became chair after Huang’s resignation, had served as the board’s main public voice. He strictly ruled out a return and, on Nvidia’s Nov. 17 earnings call, said the board “does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.” He maintained that position as Huang’s allies began calling for his reinstatement.

On Monday, however, Jones personally welcomed Huang back, called him the right person for the job and said the board had decided unanimously to restore him. “We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the Company,” Jones said. He will remain chair of Nvidia’s board.

CNBC’s “Mad Money” host Jim Cramer also changed his assessment. Earlier in the month, he said Huang’s resignation “had to happen” and there was “much evidence” to support it. On Monday, he called the move to oust the chief executive “hasty” and called Huang “a national treasure.” A CNBC spokesperson said Cramer’s commentary was based on extensive sourcing.

The Wall Street Journal said some people who had rebuked Huang were heralding his arrival.

Huang said he was honored to return to Nvidia and thanked Jones and the board. “I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment,” he said in a statement.

The questions over possible noncompliance had not been answered when Nvidia restored Huang. Bessent’s call for outside leadership had been qualified, but he still wanted the company to establish who was responsible. The board’s Monday vote restored Huang; it did not resolve the possible-noncompliance questions.
