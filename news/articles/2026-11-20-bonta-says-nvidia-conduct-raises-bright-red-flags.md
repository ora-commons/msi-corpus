## Nvidia did not immediately respond to a request for comment

California Attorney General Rob Bonta said in a statement, “The conduct of Nvidia and its former CEO raises bright red flags.” A Nvidia spokesperson did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

On Thursday, The Wall Street Journal reported that California Attorney General Rob Bonta issued an investigative subpoena to Nvidia, opening a state inquiry into the chipmaker's conduct after the exposure of former chief executive Jensen Huang's extramarital affairs.

The California Department of Justice is asking whether Nvidia's powerful chips or any confidential information were shared with China. The department is also seeking to determine whether Nvidia misled investors and the public.

Bonta's office said the information sought would be used to determine whether Nvidia violated state law. “Nvidia is in a messy, transnational situation but the underlying principle of our corporate securities law is clear: Companies cannot mislead their investors,” Bonta said.

Nvidia is a Santa Clara, California-based chipmaker in the artificial-intelligence industry. Jensen Huang resigned from Nvidia the prior week after extramarital affairs.

One of Huang's extramarital affairs involved Alice Huang, an executive at Singapore-based Megaspeed. U.S. authorities suspect Alice Huang of working for Chinese state intelligence.

The Securities and Exchange Commission is also investigating Nvidia, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Nvidia shares were down 3.30%.

Separately, lawmakers said Jensen Huang has agreed to testify before Congress in December.
