## Citi Bike annual membership fee remains $239 through May 2029

New York City and Lyft, the parent company of Citi Bike, reached an agreement that will cap member e-bike fares at $3.65 for rides of up to 45 minutes beginning Jan. 19, 2027, Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced Monday. According to the mayor's office, the current charge for a 45-minute member ride is $12.15 and will fall to $3.65 under the deal.

“This cap will significantly reduce the cost of roughly 5 million rides taken by New Yorkers annually,” Mamdani said at a news conference on Monday. “If this cap had been in place just last year alone, it would have saved New Yorkers $10,932,958.”

Lyft will also freeze the annual Citi Bike membership fee at $239 through May 2029, when the company's current contract with New York City expires. Mamdani described the fare and membership changes as an affordability measure. “Today's announcement is a critical step towards making biking affordable for every New Yorker,” he said. “And it's a part of our city hall's larger goal to make life more affordable in every way we can for those who call our city home.”

Patrick Knoth, general manager of Citi Bike, welcomed the changes in a Monday statement. “Every day, New Yorkers choose Citi Bike to get to work, to school, and to each other, and this cap gives even more of them the chance to get around their city by bike,” Knoth said.

Under the agreement, New York City will work with Lyft to upgrade and install on-site electric charging infrastructure at Citi Bike stations, beginning at 130 stations next year. The charging setup will allow e-bikes to recharge while docked rather than requiring workers to drive around the city swapping batteries.

The agreement will also allow Lyft to run advertising across all bike-station display panels starting on 1 January of next year, Mamdani said. Under the revenue-share terms, Lyft will begin paying New York City only the amount above $5.25 billion a year, and the company will no longer have to reimburse the city for lost parking-meter revenue.

Citi Bike averaged about 169,000 rides a day in September, according to data shared by Lyft and the city's Department of Transportation. The same data showed about 139,400 people with annual Citi Bike memberships.

The cost of Citi Bike trips has risen significantly over time. Members currently pay $0.27 per minute for e-bike rides, producing a $12.15 charge for 45 minutes. The New York Times reported that the same ride cost members $4.50 in 2021. Member e-bike rides of up to 45 minutes that enter or exit Manhattan are currently capped at $5.40, and the new agreement will replace that exception.

The change also moves New York City closer to fare parity among major forms of transit, with subways, buses, ferries and e-bikes now costing roughly the same per ride.

The cap will not extend to nonmembers, whose prices will remain unchanged. The New York Times reported that a 45-minute e-bike ride for someone without a membership can cost more than $18, plus a $4.99 unlock fee.

Asked whether the city had plans to make Citi Bike more affordable for nonmembers, Mamdani said around 82% of Citi Bike users are members and that New Yorkers who had not signed up had raised concerns about membership costs increasing year after year. He said, “one of the reasons that we focused on going beyond the 365 was to freeze membership costs,” adding that keeping the current price could provide stability in the years ahead.

Mamdani also noted that Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program recipients and residents of New York City Housing Authority public housing are eligible for Citi Bike memberships costing $5 a month.
