## Preliminary results give Peltola 50.1% of first-choice votes

Mary Peltola held a 12,458-vote advantage over Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan as of Wednesday morning, according to The Associated Press. Preliminary results published Tuesday evening by Alaska's Division of Elections showed Peltola received 50.1% of Alaskans' first-choice votes, more than enough to avoid a run-off in the state's ranked-choice voting system.

The Trump administration's appeal of the U.S. Justice Department's election challenge is pending and was amended to reflect The New York Times' report.

Former U.S. Rep. Mary Peltola's victory in Alaska's U.S. Senate race sealed Democrats' nine pickups in the 2026 midterms and left the party with control of 56 seats in the next Congress, including two independents who caucus with the party. The Associated Press called the contest late Tuesday night, two weeks after Nov. 3 polls closed, making Alaska the last Senate contest called.

In Mississippi, Democrat and district attorney Scott Colom narrowly defeated Republican incumbent Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith. The Associated Press called the race on Nov. 11, when Colom's advantage was just over 8,000 votes. It was another Senate contest settled days after the Nov. 3 polls closed.

Democrats also flipped Senate seats in the moderate-to-conservative states of Iowa, Kansas, North Carolina, South Carolina and Texas.

Former Democratic Sen. Sherrod Brown also won back his Ohio seat from Republican Sen. John Husted. Brown ran in a special election after Husted replaced Vice President JD Vance, and he will be sworn in about two weeks before most other senators elected this month.

“Mary Peltola's victory last night was an exclamation point on what has been a great election year,” Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, D-N.Y., told reporters on Capitol Hill Wednesday.

“We won because we had great candidates, like Peltola,” Schumer added. “We won because we focused on what the American people cared about: lowering their costs, ending corruption, ending the chaos. But now, you know the American people have given us their votes. Now we have to earn their trust. The real work begins today.”

The Peltola-Sullivan race drew scrutiny as Republicans alleged that Democrats recruited another candidate with the name Dan Sullivan to run as a spoiler candidate. The two were listed on the ballot as Dan S. Sullivan, the incumbent senator, and Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., a retired teacher.

The U.S. Justice Department launched a legal challenge in late October seeking to preemptively stop the state from certifying the results of the election if, under Alaska's ranked-choice voting system, Republican incumbent Dan S. Sullivan lost by a smaller margin than the votes earned by Daniel J. Sullivan Jr.

The New York Times published findings on Oct. 28 that Democratic operatives in Alaska, including in Peltola's campaign, sought a candidate with the same name. States Newsroom said it had not independently verified the report. Peltola denied any knowledge of the alleged scheme, according to Alaska Beacon.

Daniel J. Sullivan's candidacy also had been challenged earlier in the year in state court, and the Alaska Supreme Court ruled in June that he could remain on the ballot.
