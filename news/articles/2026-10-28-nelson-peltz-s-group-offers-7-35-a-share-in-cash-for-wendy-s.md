## Trian holds 16.3% stake amid Wendy’s sales decline

Nelson Peltz’s Trian Fund Management and several partners offered to buy Wendy’s for $7.35 a share in cash on Wednesday, a nearly 19% premium over Tuesday’s closing price. Shares of the Dublin, Ohio, chain rose 15% at the opening bell.

In a letter to Wendy’s board, Peltz wrote that going private “will deliver the best outcome for the broader stakeholder base.” He wrote that “Wendy’s sits at a critical juncture,” citing significant pressures on the U.S. consumer, economic uncertainty, persistent commodity inflation and intensifying competition, particularly within the QSR (quick service restaurant) burger category.

Wendy’s same-store sales, measured at restaurants open for at least a year, have declined for more than two years. Those sales fell during the first two quarters of 2026.

A regulatory document filed Wednesday reported that Trian’s stake in Wendy’s totaled 16.3%. Peltz has invested in companies including Procter & Gamble, Disney and DuPont.

Wendy’s declined to comment on the proposal. The company is scheduled to report its quarterly financial results next week.

Analysts expected Wendy’s to post earnings of 9 cents per share on average sales of $531 million.

The chain was founded in 1969 by Dave Thomas and named after his daughter. Wendy’s is known for its square burgers and Frosty desserts.

Shares rose 91 cents to $7.05 Wednesday, remaining below the $7.35 cash offer. The stock was down 25% over the past year following a years-long decline.
