## Congressional Democrats pledge to defend Smithsonian's independence

Rep. Chellie Pingree announced Tuesday that she will investigate the Trump White House's potential overhaul of leadership and curatorial decisions at the Smithsonian Institution, the independent institution that oversees 21 national museums. In a letter to the Smithsonian's Board of Regents, Pingree cited ProPublica's recent reporting on draft plans by a White House staffer and called the reporting "extremely concerning."

Pingree said the plans align with what she described as the administration's broader efforts to interfere with the Smithsonian, an institution that receives federal funding but is governed by a 17-person Board of Regents. Its members include the chief justice of the United States, members of Congress, the vice president and private citizens.

In an interview with ProPublica, Pingree said the letter was intended to put the governing board on notice that the incoming Democratic majority in Congress is monitoring the institution to make sure it upholds its responsibility to remain independent of political pressure. "No White House employee riding on the Metro gets to design a plan for what future exhibits look like or gets to veto staffers or exhibits at the Smithsonian," she said. "That's not how it works."

Pingree, who is slated to lead the subcommittee that controls the Smithsonian's funding in the next Congress, said she planned to use her powers as chair to look into what she called the Trump administration's meddling with the Smithsonian and to expose those efforts to the broader American public. "We fully intend to dig deeper than has been done in the past," she said.

The reporting behind Pingree's letter concerns a 25-item list of possible changes aimed at the National Museum of American History, which attracts nearly 2 million people a year. ProPublica reported that, taken together, the items would amount to an unprecedented attempt by the White House to control the content and personnel of the museum.

The list's proposed changes include firing many of the museum's top officials and rolling out a new permanent exhibit called "The Riches of Our Western Civilization Inheritance." It also calls for new K-12 learning materials, including a founders-focused curriculum with two possible titles: "Architects of Liberty: The Heroic Journey of America's Founders" or "Profiles in Liberty: Washington and America's Heroic Men of Courage." Another item would unveil a "Colossus Statue" of George Washington at the museum's entrance.

The draft was visible on the laptop and in handwritten notes of a White House staffer riding a D.C.-area Metro train in late October. A nearby commuter photographed the materials and sent the images to ProPublica reporters, who verified the information, ProPublica reported. The reception the plans received inside the Trump White House is unclear, and the staffer and the White House did not respond to requests for comment.

Also on Tuesday, Lonnie Bunch, the Smithsonian's outgoing leader, responded to ProPublica's reporting in a message to employees. "The intended use of this list is unclear," Bunch wrote. "What remains clear is that the Smithsonian is an independent institution with the responsibility and privilege to present accurate information to millions of visitors through our scholarship and educational programs."

Sen. Alex Padilla, D-Calif., said on social media that if he becomes chair of the Senate Rules Committee, which has jurisdiction over the Smithsonian, in the next Congress, "we will protect it from Trump's interference and overreach." Padilla also wrote: "The Smithsonian is NOT a federal agency, it is an independent, nonpartisan organization overseen by Congress – not the White House."

Incoming Rep. Fred Wellman, a Democrat elected this month to represent the suburbs of St. Louis, responded more forcefully to the reporting on social media. "Over my dead body," he wrote.

Both Pingree's planned oversight and Padilla's pledge rest on committee roles that begin in the next Congress, while the White House's internal response to the 25-item list remains undisclosed.
