## Los Angeles County will take over the agency’s homelessness portfolio

The Los Angeles Homeless Services Authority will return $150 million and be permanently severed from federal funding by the end of 2027 under a settlement announced Tuesday with the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development, according to a HUD statement. The agreement resolves a dispute over claims that the agency misused funding and gives Los Angeles County responsibility for LAHSA’s homelessness projects under HUD oversight.

The deal followed HUD’s move last year to cut off money going to LAHSA after audits found reckless spending and a lack of transparency. LAHSA, which had overseen hundreds of millions of dollars in spending on shelter and services for homeless residents, sued to overturn the suspension. The terms require LAHSA to return $150 million and become permanently separated from federal funding by the end of 2027.

Under the settlement, Los Angeles County will take over LAHSA’s portfolio of projects and responsibilities for homeless services, subject to HUD oversight. The transfer covers programs LAHSA coordinated, including shelter, food and other homelessness services.

LAHSA interim CEO Gita O’Neill disputed the federal findings. “From the outset, we have maintained that LAHSA did not engage in wrongdoing and that HUD’s suspension lacked a legal foundation,” Gita O’Neill, LAHSA’s interim CEO, said in a statement. “Our focus now is on working closely with our local partners to ensure continuity of services, support an orderly transition, and remain true to our mission of connecting our unhoused neighbors with the services, resources, and pathways to housing they need.”

LAHSA, jointly funded by the city of Los Angeles and Los Angeles County, was formed in 1993 as a result of a lawsuit settlement. It coordinated and managed money for shelter, food programs and a wide range of services, but had long faced accusations of misspending.

In April 2025, the Los Angeles County Board of Supervisors voted to shift more than $300 million in annual taxpayer money out of LAHSA to create a new department on homelessness. The city of Los Angeles also began exploring how it could sidestep LAHSA and directly contract with homeless service providers.

A 2025 survey found that more than 72,000 people were homeless on any given night across Los Angeles County, a slight decrease from the year before. California accounts for nearly a third of the homeless population in the United States.

The region’s homelessness crisis is especially visible downtown Los Angeles, where hundreds of people live in makeshift shanties that line entire blocks of the neighborhood known as Skid Row. Encampments are increasingly found in suburban areas under freeway overpasses.
