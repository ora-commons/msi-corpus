## Service outages disrupted travel, work, banking and classified communications

WASHINGTON — The White House named Kirsten Davies as the new national cyber director on Thursday, replacing Sean Cairncross as the administration’s top cybersecurity official. Davies is chief information officer at the Defense Department. Several members of Trump’s cabinet announced the selection in social media posts.

The change came after a series of widespread service outages disrupted daily life and unnerved many Americans. The outages disrupted travel, cut off work, limited bank access and disrupted classified communications.

Over the past week, Microsoft, Chase Bank, air traffic control and the National Security Agency were all affected.

President Donald Trump faced criticism as Americans dealt with the outages. The Associated Press reported that even staunch Trump allies accused the administration of lying to Americans or hiding information about the true cause of the disruptions.

On Tuesday, Secretary of State Marco Rubio said there was “zero” information that the technical failures were related to each other or to a malign actor.

The White House made the change as President Donald Trump returned from a three-day trip to Asia. During the trip, Trump said of the episodes, “things like that happen.”

In a post on X, Davies wrote that “Our nation stands in the crossroad of analog, digital, and quantum technologies, our American way of living increasingly interconnected.” She added: “Our opportunity is to dominate the cyber landscape for generations to come … but only if we modernize and drive resilience into the foundations our great nation runs on, as well as build cyber talent for our future.”

In a post on X congratulating Davies, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth wrote: “She has proven herself to be a dedicated patriot and professional, and we are confident she will advance President Trump’s America First agenda across the cybersecurity landscape.”
