## Three vaccine-related videos were no longer visible on Children’s Health Defense accounts

The Guardian described Children’s Health Defense as an anti-vaccine group once led by Robert F Kennedy Jr. The group said Meta had “taken down” posts from its Facebook and Instagram accounts after the Guardian reported that the organization was sharing false and misleading information about vaccines. Within hours of the article running, Children’s Health Defense wrote on X that “ALL of our accounts have been targeted.” The group accused Meta of censorship.

The Guardian reported that Children’s Health Defense had been allowed back on Facebook and Instagram earlier this year, four years after Meta removed the group from both platforms for repeatedly violating Meta’s misinformation policies. A Guardian review found that the organization had reopened or opened six accounts on Facebook and Instagram from May through September.

Three videos posted by Children’s Health Defense were no longer up by Wednesday evening. One said the measles, mumps and rubella (MMR) vaccine was “not so safe and effective.” Another was titled “Vaccines: Your child could die” and made the misleading claim that vaccines are deadly. A third said HPV vaccines were “very dangerous,” had “caused hundreds, thousands of deaths,” and did not prevent cervical cancer. The Guardian said all three had been visible on Children’s Health Defense accounts when its story was published on Wednesday.

Children’s Health Defense posted screenshots of two messages about Meta’s action. The messages indicated that two posts had been removed because of false information about vaccines and cited Meta’s community standards. One message said: “We don’t allow people to show false information that leading health organizations say could contribute to people refusing vaccines.”

On Wednesday night, the group wrote: “Our posts are being taken down AGAIN simply for sharing the truth about vaccines and the experiences of families whose children were injured by vaccines,” adding: “People have every right to discuss vaccine injuries and share their experiences without being silenced by big tech.”

The Guardian noted that the account openings came at a time when measles had come roaring back in the United States. Health officials reported 11 measles-associated deaths in the country this year, and the United States lost its measles elimination status this week.

A separate Guardian investigation this month found health and vaccine misinformation was rampant in a huge public Facebook group, Crunchy Moms. The investigation found Facebook was recommending the group to users despite apparent violations of its policies.

Crunchy Moms was started a few months after Meta founder Mark Zuckerberg announced a rollback of moderation and fact-checking last year. Zuckerberg defended the move by saying he wanted to restore “free expression on our platforms.”

Meta and Children’s Health Defense did not immediately return emails seeking comment. Children’s Health Defense had accused Meta of censorship, while the Meta messages in the screenshots invoked community standards and false information about vaccines.
