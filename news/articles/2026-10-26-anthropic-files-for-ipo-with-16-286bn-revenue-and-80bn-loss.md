## Top five customers supply a quarter of Anthropic's revenue

Anthropic publicly filed for a US stock-market listing under the symbol ANT, following a confidential initial public offering filing with US financial regulators in June. Analysts and investors have valued the San Francisco-based company at more than $1.5 trillion. Founded in 2021 by CEO Dario Amodei after he left OpenAI, the maker of ChatGPT, Anthropic offers the Claude chatbot as its flagship product, with text-generation features as well as advanced coding and cybersecurity capabilities. The Guardian described Anthropic as a leader in the race to create powerful artificial intelligence models and a bellwether of the American AI boom.

*Source photograph: Dario Amodei, the CEO of Anthropic, in Bengaluru, India, on 16 February 2026. Photograph: Priyanshu Singh/Reuters.*

The company's audited filings will provide the first public glimpse of its financial health. The prospectus details Anthropic's rapid revenue growth: $41 million in 2023, $386 million in 2024 and $4.585 billion in 2025, an increase of more than 1,000%. Revenue reached $16.286 billion in the first six months of 2026 alone. Anthropic said it had raised $141.1 billion as of the end of June. Despite the increase in revenue, the company disclosed a net loss of $80 billion for the first half of 2026.

As of September 30, Anthropic said it had more than 14,000 enterprise customers. Of those, 11,900 had generated at least $100,000 in revenue, more than 1,900 had generated at least $1 million, and about 250 had generated at least $10 million. “The speed and depth of our enterprise customer adoption are powerful evidence of Claude’s position at the frontier of AI,” Anthropic executives wrote.

The Guardian said the filing contains hyperbolic language characteristic of AI companies predicting the miraculous potential of their technology. Anthropic's model could revolutionize knowledge work across industries, according to the company. The Guardian said investing in Anthropic and the development of that model comes with a great deal of risk. The filing listed more than 90 pages of potential risks to the business.

Anthropic warned that a “substantial portion” of revenue comes from a limited number of customers and that losing any one of them could put a huge dent in revenue. A quarter of Anthropic's revenue comes from its top five customers, though no single customer represents more than 10% of the business.

The company also has incurred $6.2 billion in debt as it built out its infrastructure and grew its business, including convertible notes issued to Amazon Web Services in 2023.

The company's investor prospectus states: “We believe AI is one of the most consequential technologies in human history.” Anthropic researchers and executives have repeatedly warned of AI's potential to cause human extinction. Describing the potential of AI agents, semi-autonomous chatbots that can carry out tasks on behalf of users, Anthropic executives wrote: “The result is something without historical precedent: what we refer to as a country of geniuses in a datacenter, available to every organization and sector of the economy.”

The Guardian reported that the misuse of AI and Claude in particular also poses a considerable risk to Anthropic's business. Anthropic executives identified four categories of potential AI risk: misuse by bad actors, loss of control, authoritarian abuse and economic disruption. The company said its most capable models have “capabilities that could provide meaningful assistance to malicious actors seeking to develop chemical, biological, or other dangerous weapons.” It deploys technical safeguards, “but such safeguards are incomplete, may be outdated in light of evolving threat models, have been and may in the future again be circumvented, or may fail to operate as intended.”

In September 2025, Anthropic disclosed that staffers documented what they believe was the first large-scale cyber-attack that did not rely on significant human intervention. The company said it “detected and assessed with high confidence that a Chinese state-sponsored hacking group manipulated our Claude Code tool into attempting infiltration into roughly 30 global targets, including large technology companies, financial institutions, chemical manufacturing companies, and government agencies.”

Anthropic's dispute with the Trump administration over the use of its products in warfare culminated in the company's designation as a supply chain risk, a first for an American company. The designation prevents any military agency from contracting with Anthropic. The company has challenged the blacklisting.

The proposed debut would be one of the largest in US history. The Guardian described the listing as one of the largest debuts in history and said Anthropic's US market float was likely to set a high-water mark in the multitrillion-dollar AI boom and test its financial viability. Anthropic's filing pairs $16.286 billion in six-month revenue and more than 14,000 enterprise customers with an $80 billion loss, $6.2 billion in debt and a federal contracting restriction.
