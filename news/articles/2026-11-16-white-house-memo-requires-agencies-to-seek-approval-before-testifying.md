## Directive lands as Congress prepares for Democratic oversight

A memorandum obtained by PBS News on Monday shows Will Scharf, White House counsel, directing every federal agency and executive department to clear testimony with White House legal and legislative affairs offices before agreeing to appear before congressional committees.

A White House official said in an emailed statement to PBS News that the Trump administration "will not be extorted, intimidated, or threatened into submission by politically motivated baseless investigations." The official said the administration is ready to engage with Congress and that "this process ensures reasonable requests are respected, but baseless political attempts to undermine our mission will not be accepted as the status quo by Democrats' who want to slow the administration down from its historic work."

The official wrote that the approval requirement lets agencies handle "legitimate congressional oversight requests while keeping Cabinet officials focused on their foremost responsibility: serving the American people and delivering results for them."

The timing matters. Both chambers of Congress are preparing for incoming Democratic leadership, which has promised robust oversight of the Trump administration after sweeping victories in the midterms.

The testimony directive follows a separate letter Scharf sent last week to Rep. Robert Garcia, a California Democrat and the ranking Democrat on the House Oversight Committee. In that three-page letter, Scharf argued that Democratic pledges to investigate Trump and his administration "may cross the line into blatant criminality" and accused Democrats of making "abusive and capricious threats" against Trump and administration officials.

The White House official also pointed to four letters House Democrats have sent to federal agencies, including the Department of Homeland Security, asking that documents be preserved, describing them as "exorbitant demands Democrats have sent the agencies."

The broader backdrop includes the administration's own use of investigative power. The Trump administration, in particular the Department of Justice, has investigated and in some cases prosecuted some of the president's perceived political enemies, such as former FBI Director James Comey, during his second term.

Garcia has said voters gave Democrats a mandate to pursue accountability, according to PBS News. The incoming Democratic majority is expected to press the oversight agenda the White House memo now seeks to route through internal approval.
