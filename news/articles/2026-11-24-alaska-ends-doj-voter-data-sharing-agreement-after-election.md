## State directs DOJ to destroy confidential data; no voters removed

Alaska ended an agreement under which the state gave the U.S. Department of Justice its voter-registration list and allowed the agency to flag voters for possible removal, Republican Lt. Gov. Nancy Dahlstrom said Monday.

Dahlstrom, the outgoing lieutenant governor who oversees Alaska elections, said she notified the Justice Department that the agreement was ending as state officials announced that results from the Nov. 3 general election had been certified. Democrats flipped a U.S. Senate seat and the governor's office in that election.

The Alaska Division of Elections directed the Justice Department to destroy the confidential voter information Alaska provided, according to a statement released late Monday. A Justice Department spokesperson said Tuesday that the agency would send a formal response to the state.

Dahlstrom's office said no voters were removed as a result of the agreement. Since the agreement was signed last December, the Justice Department had not identified any potentially ineligible voters or raised concerns about Alaska's voter registration records, the office said.

Dahlstrom said the Division of Elections “entered the agreement in good faith to cooperate with our federal law enforcement partners as permitted under Alaska law. The circumstances have now changed, the election has concluded and the agreement no longer serves a continuing purpose.”

She said Alaska “has strong list-maintenance procedures and will continue to protect both the accuracy of its voter rolls and the rights of eligible Alaska voters.” The office added that the termination “does not constitute an acknowledgment of wrongdoing by the Division of Elections.”

Alaska was one of at least 16 states to provide voter lists containing information such as driver's license and partial Social Security numbers, dates of birth and addresses. Alaska and Texas also signed data-sharing agreements outlining plans for the Justice Department's own analysis of voter files, flagging voter-list issues and directions for removing voters deemed ineligible.

In the Associated Press account, the federal government's demand for detailed state voter information was part of a broader midterm effort to promote the idea that scores of noncitizens were on state voter rolls and potentially casting ballots illegally. The report said years of research, state investigations and the Justice Department's own record of arrests showed the issue was extremely rare.

The Justice Department sued 30 states and the District of Columbia for failing to comply with its demands for detailed voter registration data or other election-related records, according to the Brennan Center for Justice. The Brennan Center said courts dismissed 28 of those lawsuits. The Justice Department entered a settlement agreement with Oklahoma and dismissed its case with that state, the group said.

Voting and civil rights groups sued Alaska election officials earlier this year, alleging that sharing the state's full voter registration list violated the Alaska Constitution and that the agreement's terms violated due process rights. The case remains pending. The ACLU Voting Rights Project, one of the organizations representing the groups, did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

State Sen. Bill Wielechowski, a Democrat and critic of the state's decision to hand over voter information, said Tuesday he believed the motivation for ending the agreement was to try to “make the lawsuit go away” before the Kreiss-Tomkins administration could “settle or produce discovery.” Wielechowski said he wants the administration to examine the circumstances and questioned the impact of the demand to delete the records.

Democrat Jonathan Kreiss-Tomkins and his lieutenant governor, independent Zac Johnson, take office Dec. 7.

That case remains pending after Alaska ended the sharing arrangement and directed the Justice Department to destroy the confidential voter information.
