## DOJ seeks $53 million in Northern District of California lawsuit

The Justice Department filed a federal lawsuit against Lyft on Monday, accusing the ride-hailing company of discriminating against passengers with disabilities who seek rides through the Lyft platform. The complaint was filed in the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California and targets Lyft's handling of riders who need accommodations under the Americans with Disabilities Act.

The lawsuit alleges that Lyft "is aware of, but fails to prevent and remedy" several discriminatory practices against riders who need accommodations under the Americans with Disabilities Act. According to the suit, those practices include denying rides to people who have service animals or use stowable wheelchairs and refusing to let passengers with mobility issues sit in the front seat.

Jesus A. Osete, acting assistant attorney general, said in a statement: "Individuals with disabilities should be able to rely on rideshare services free of discrimination." Osete added: "We intend to uphold the ADA's promise that people with disabilities are entitled to equal access in every part of public life, including essential transportation services."

Lyft firmly rejected the accusations in a statement.

The lawsuit names several examples of people across the country who hailed a ride on the Lyft platform but had one driver — or, in some cases, several drivers — refuse to take them to their destination, including as recently as last month. Those individuals experienced delays in getting to medical appointments, cancellation fees and other issues as a result, the suit said.

Lyft said its policies require drivers to accommodate riders who travel with service animals or mobility devices and that "drivers who violate these policies face serious consequences, including permanent deactivation." The company also said: "We've cooperated with the DOJ throughout its review and remain committed to serving every rider, including riders with disabilities."

The suit is the latest from the federal government targeting ride-hailing companies over their treatment of would-be riders with disabilities. The administration filed a similar lawsuit against Uber last year, which remains ongoing. Uber settled a separate suit in 2022, and Lyft settled a different one in 2020.

Both companies stress that their policies and in-app tools require drivers to provide ADA accommodations and remind drivers of the consequences of refusing to do so.

The Justice Department said it is seeking $53 million for individuals who have been discriminated against and have submitted complaints to Lyft or the department.
