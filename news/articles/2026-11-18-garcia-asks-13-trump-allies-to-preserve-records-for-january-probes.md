## Garcia asks 13 Trump allies to preserve records for January probes

**Subtype:** fact

- Rep. Robert Garcia sent document-preservation letters to 13 Trump allies and family members to prepare for incoming House Oversight Committee investigations.
- Garcia's letters target Donald Trump Jr., Eric Trump, Jared Kushner, Kimberly Guilfoyle and Catherine Townsend over alleged financial and policy conflicts.
- Garcia's letters allege DHS misconduct involving Stephen Miller, Kristi Noem and Corey Lewandowski.
- Garcia's letters direct Epstein files allegations at J.D. Vance, Susie Wiles, Todd Blanche and federal prison officials.
- White House Counsel Will Scharf warned that Democratic probes may cross into criminality.
- White House spokeswoman Lauren Bis called the proposed investigations sham investigations.

## White House calls Garcia's planned inquiries sham investigations

Rep. Robert Garcia, the highest-ranking Democrat on the House Oversight Committee, sent document-preservation letters to 13 allies and family members of President Trump, laying the groundwork for incoming investigations that could begin in January. The California Democrat is poised to become the committee chair. Garcia said, "Oversight Democrats are fighting to put an end to this Administration's self-dealing, misconduct, and abuse."

Garcia's targets fell into four broad categories he described as "Trump family corruption, corporate corruption, DHS misconduct, and the White House Epstein cover-up."

The Trump administration had previously rejected accusations of misconduct and warned Garcia that overly zealous investigations could constitute a crime. In a letter dated Nov. 9, White House Counsel Will Scharf said the administration was "monitoring" the speech of Democrats in Congress as they mapped investigative targets, warning that efforts to investigate "may cross the line into blatant criminality." Legal experts broadly pushed back against the warning, citing Congress' oversight authorities. In a Wednesday statement, White House spokeswoman Lauren Bis said, "As the President has said, he's willing to work with anyone to continue delivering victories for hardworking Americans." She added, "If Democrats choose to pursue sham investigations and radical left policies, they are choosing to work against the American people."

The letters to Donald Trump Jr. and Eric Trump raised pay-to-play allegations and potential conflicts of interest tied to their stakes in World Liberty Financial, the Trump family's cryptocurrency venture. Garcia's letter to Donald Trump Jr. also raised the financing of his wedding after-party by a Russian businessman with ties to the Kremlin; President Trump later told reporters the wedding celebration's costs were paid back. The letter said, "You have engaged in business pursuits that appear to have allowed you to personally profit from your father's presidency on a massive scale and at the expense of the American people." For Eric Trump, the letter said, "Oversight Democrats have found that your roles as a co-founder of World Liberty Financial and a key player in the Trump family's pay-to-play schemes have raked in billions of dollars for the Trump family over the past several years."

Jared Kushner, Trump's son-in-law, received a letter raising allegations of conflicts of interest, his investment firm Affinity Partners and using his government role to enrich himself and his companies. The letter said, "Directly or indirectly, you have received billions of dollars in fees and investments with particularly troubling ties to Middle East states where you also have a government role." It continued, "This raises urgent questions about transparency, conflicts of interest, and the abuse of what appears to clearly be a government position—with influence over war, peace, and nuclear proliferation as well as direct access to President Trump—for personal profit."

Kimberly Guilfoyle, the former ambassador to Greece who was previously engaged to Donald Trump Jr., was contacted about alleged pay-to-play schemes. Garcia's letter said, "As ambassador, you appear to have been intent on leveraging your position to cash-in." Catherine Townsend, CEO of the Trust for the National Mall, was also a target, with Garcia's letter concerning construction and donations for the White House ballroom, financed in part through private donations to the Trust.

White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller received a letter alleging abuses by Immigration and Customs Enforcement under his direction. The letter said, "Members of the Trump Administration have described you as the 'linchpin' and 'architect' of the Trump Administration's most extreme policies, advocating for the widespread abuse of civil liberties," and added, "We have documented, and will continue to document, your role in orchestrating, overseeing, and defending these abuses." Former Department of Homeland Security Secretary Kristi Noem and former DHS senior adviser Corey Lewandowski also received letters alleging abuse of DHS funds, corruption and self-dealing.

Vice President J.D. Vance received a letter alleging secret meetings inside the White House Situation Room about the administration's handling of the Epstein files. The letter said Vance was reportedly the first to suggest that Ghislaine Maxwell be interviewed to distance President Trump from Epstein. It also invoked what it described as President Trump's decades-long friendship with Epstein and argued, "These discussions appear to be directly linked to an apparent quid pro quo in which Maxwell was moved to a minimum-security prison camp after she made favorable statements about President Trump in a highly unusual prison interview conducted by then-Deputy Attorney General Todd Blanche."

Former White House Chief of Staff Susie Wiles received a letter alleging secret White House meetings surrounding the Epstein investigation. It said, "Reporting describes you as refusing to release documents concerning allegations against President Trump to the public." Attorney General Todd Blanche received a letter over an alleged cover-up of the Epstein files, failure to comply with the Epstein Files Transparency Act and Maxwell's transfer to a minimum-security prison. Blanche's letter said, "You have refused to cooperate with our investigation, or to fulfill your legal responsibilities to be transparent with the Committee and the American people." It added, "This obstruction is even more concerning given your direct involvement in the broader cover-up, as well as your history as President Trump's personal attorney."

Federal Bureau of Prisons Director William K. Marshall III and Federal Prison Camp Bryan Warden Tanisha Hall received letters regarding Maxwell's transfer to a minimum-security prison camp days after she was questioned about President Trump and his relationship to Epstein. The letter said, "Committee Democrats will not tolerate the Administration's refusal to respond to our inquiries and, in the future, will not hesitate to compel information from individuals enabling the cover-up of Maxwell's and Epstein's crimes."

Garcia said, "These investigations will lay the groundwork for legislation which helps all Americans. This is just the beginning." Many of the letters said: "Americans should be secure in the knowledge that their government is focused on their best interests—not on making the President's family richer."
