## Commerce moves to add Megaspeed to its Entity List as scrutiny continues

Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia's chief executive has not settled the questions surrounding the chipmaker's leadership, William Blair analysts Sebastien Naji and Ana Bilbao said in a Monday research note on Nvidia. The analysts rate the company at market perform.

“While Huang’s return clears some of the uncertainty surrounding Nvidia, the events of recent weeks have exposed deeper questions around corporate governance, leadership succession, and regulatory oversight that could have lasting implications for the company’s competitive position,” Naji and Bilbao wrote in the note.

Public markets supplied the backdrop. After a powerful rally Monday, the Nasdaq opened lower Tuesday. The Wall Street Journal said volatility in public markets can affect adjacent markets, including venture capital and enterprise technology buyers, and that it remains to be seen how disruption in the stock markets may spill over into other areas of technology.

“Nvidia is signaling tighter standards for neoclouds—the specialized cloud providers that rent its chips to AI labs—putting a critical link in AI’s circular financing chain under the microscope,” PitchBook said in a note Tuesday morning.

PitchBook added: “Jensen Huang’s return as CEO hasn’t changed that…Tighter chip allocation and financing standards leave unfinanced neocloud builds most exposed.” The uncertainty around Nvidia is raising more questions for other companies in the AI ecosystem, especially those that depend on it for financing, The Wall Street Journal reported.

The Wall Street Journal also chronicled the push by the White House to get Huang back. President Trump told the Journal on Monday that he had wanted to call Nvidia's board a week and a half earlier but held off. “The climate became more and more right as the company stock began to tumble,” he said. Once he was told the government had not found that Huang had done anything illegal, “I began a push,” he said.

Some on Capitol Hill criticized the reinstatement. Monday had lawmakers in both parties speaking out against Huang's return, citing national-security risks, the Journal reported. Senate Intelligence Committee Chairman Tom Cotton (R., Ark.) said: “The controversy with Nvidia and Megaspeed is just the tip of the iceberg, and it’s not resolved because Jensen Huang has his job back.”

The scrutiny is not expected to end. In a filing Monday, the Commerce Department moved to formally add Megaspeed to its Entity List, Bloomberg reported. The designation would bar the company from buying AI chips and other advanced technology without a U.S. government license.
