## Conley-Lawler race among country's most expensive House contests

Competitive races in three of New York's wealthiest congressional districts — the 3rd, 4th and 17th — could help decide whether Democrats regain control of the U.S. House. Democrats need to gain three seats in the chamber to attain a majority, and outside groups have poured millions more into the races.

Among the top pickup opportunities for Democrats is the 17th District in the Lower Hudson Valley, where two-term Republican Rep. Mike Lawler is facing a difficult reelection battle in an unfavorable climate for Republicans. Lawler is defending his seat against Democrat Cait Conley, an Army combat veteran who served on the National Security Council. Lawler won the seat in 2022 by less than one percentage point and increased his margin of victory to over six percentage points in 2024. He was one of the few Republicans elected in a congressional district that also supported Democratic former Vice President Kamala Harris for president.

The district covers wealthy suburbs and working-class communities. Lawler has campaigned on his record in Congress, highlighting his work to cut taxes and decrease the cost of living. He has campaigned with President Donald Trump, but as the president's approval rating continues to plunge, Lawler has also sought to emphasize his bipartisan record and his willingness to disagree with the president on certain issues. Conley has emphasized her working-class roots and pledged to cut costs and ban congressional stock trading if elected.

The race has become one of the most expensive House races in the country this cycle. Conley's campaign had raised about $10.7 million as of mid-October, while Lawler had raised $10.3 million. Outside groups have poured millions more into the contest.

In the 3rd District, which spans northeast Queens and part of the North Shore of Long Island, Democratic Rep. Tom Suozzi is facing a challenge from Republican Michael LiPetri Jr., a former state assembly member endorsed by Trump. The two also faced each other in the 2024 election, when Suozzi won by less than four percentage points. Suozzi took office in early 2024 after winning a special election to take over the seat of Republican Rep. George Santos, who was expelled from the House after being indicted for wire fraud and money laundering. Suozzi previously represented the district from 2017 to 2023.

The wealthy 3rd District, which includes parts of Nassau, Suffolk and Queens counties, sided with Trump over Harris in the 2024 election but favored former President Joe Biden in the election prior. Suozzi has campaigned as a candidate willing to reach across the aisle and to fight Trump administration policies, especially those that have affected Americans' cost of living, like tariffs and the war in Iran. He has painted LiPetri as a Trump devotee, while LiPetri has countered by casting himself as a down-to-earth candidate, attacking Suozzi as a career politician and criticizing his stock trades. Suozzi has outraised LiPetri, amassing $7.6 million as of mid-October compared with LiPetri's $2.6 million, but outside groups have also spent millions to oppose Suozzi.

In the 4th District, on the South Shore of Long Island and entirely within Nassau County, Democratic Rep. Laura Gillen is being challenged by Republican Jeanine Driscoll, a local tax official. Gillen ousted then-Republican Rep. Anthony D'Esposito in the 2024 election after narrowly losing to him in 2022. D'Esposito declined to run for the seat again in 2026, a blow to Republicans' prospects in the district.

In the gubernatorial race, Gov. Kathy Hochul, the incumbent Democrat, is running for a second full term as New York's first female governor. Her Republican challenger is Nassau County Executive Bruce Blakeman.

Polls close at 9 p.m. ET. The Associated Press will provide vote results and declare winners in races for governor, U.S. House, attorney general, ballot measures in New York City, state Supreme Court, state Senate, state Assembly and state comptroller.

In New York, an automatic recount is triggered in races where more than 1 million votes are cast if the margin of victory is less than 5,000 votes.
