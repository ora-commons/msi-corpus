## Trump sold Nvidia after China trip; briefing covered Huang and suspected spy

CNBC reported that President Trump bought or sold Nvidia stock at least 103 times during his second term, according to PBS News. White House correspondent Liz Landers said Trump is the first president to have actively traded individual stocks while in office. She said the practice breaks a norm and has raised questions not only about Nvidia but also about other companies in which Trump owns stakes.

CNBC also pointed to the timing of some of those trades, PBS News reported. Landers said Trump sold between a half-million and a million dollars of Nvidia stock in May, right after returning from a visit to China. Before the trip, Trump was apparently briefed that Jensen Huang had a relationship with a suspected Chinese spy, Landers said.

An analysis cited by Landers showed that Trump bought and traded Nvidia stock fairly regularly. She also said Nvidia's sell-off at the end of the previous week could have left Trump with a large loss that he had not fully recovered.

After PBS asked the White House about the trading, spokesperson Davis Ingle said in a statement: "Neither President Trump nor any member of his family has had any ability to direct, influence, or provide input regarding how the portfolio is invested or when investments are bought or sold."

A Nov. 5 financial disclosure placed Trump's Nvidia holdings between 32,000 and 370,000 shares. PBS News estimated that the surge after Huang returned as chief executive may have increased the value of those holdings by between $1.3 million and $15 million. The estimate came a day after Trump intervened to reinstate Huang, and Landers said the broad range left the size of the benefit uncertain.

PBS also documented Nvidia's financial ties to the administration. Landers said Nvidia began making donations at the start of Trump's second term in January 2025. The company made a million-dollar contribution to the inaugural fund and, not long afterward, a $25 million donation to the White House ballroom project.

Landers said the administration's policies directly affect the regulations that apply to Nvidia. In the previous December, the Trump administration announced that Nvidia could sell more of its advanced chips to China, though not its most advanced chips. Landers called the change "a big coup for the Chinese." Nvidia agreed at the time to share 25 percent of the revenue from sales of those chips with the Trump administration.

The Commerce Department oversees and regulates Nvidia as well as financial institutions, Landers said. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick stepped down from Cantor Fitzgerald, which Landers described as a large investment bank. Lutnick's sons still run the firm, and Nvidia has been one of its largest stock holdings, Landers said.

Taken together, the reporting places Trump's stock trading alongside Nvidia's financial donations and the company's regulatory responsibilities to the same administration. The White House said neither Trump nor his family could influence when investments were bought or sold, but the Nov. 5 filing range and the sell-off leave the financial effect of Huang's return uncertain.


As [MSI reported on Nov. 23](/articles/2026-11-23-jensen-huang-returns-as-nvidia-ceo-after-trump-s-intervention/), Nvidia reinstated Huang after Trump's intervention. PBS News' new reporting details the stock trading, corporate donations and regulatory ties that accompanied the reversal.
