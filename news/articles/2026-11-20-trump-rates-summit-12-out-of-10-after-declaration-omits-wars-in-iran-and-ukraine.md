## The four-point text calls for nuclear dialogue, petroleum supply and UN reform

Donald Trump rated the first trilateral summit between the leaders of the United States, China and Russia 12 out of 10 on Friday after he, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin issued a four-point “Declaration Toward Peace” that made no mention of Russia’s invasion of Ukraine or the U.S. military conflict with Iran. The closed-door meeting lasted 70 minutes at the Kylin Villa in Shenzhen, China, during the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit on Thursday. The declaration called for nuclear-risk dialogue, increased refined-petroleum production and supply, United Nations reforms, and respect for state sovereignty and the immunity of state officials. The Guardian assessed that “China and Russia had the most to celebrate” from the summit.

The leaders convened at the Kylin Villa in Shenzhen for the first-ever trilateral summit between the leaders of the United States, China and Russia. The Guardian described the three countries as the world’s three biggest nuclear powers. The Guardian characterized the formal output as “a study in profound omissions and authoritarian wishlists.” Their “Declaration Toward Peace” pledged dialogue to reduce nuclear risks, committed the leaders to increase the production and supply of refined petroleum, called for “reforms” to the United Nations, and declared that state sovereignty and the immunity of state officials must be respected. It also warned that the International Criminal Court must not abuse its jurisdiction.

Putin currently faces an active International Criminal Court arrest warrant for war crimes in Ukraine, The Guardian reported. It said the Trump administration had sanctioned ICC officials “to shield itself and its ally Israel from judicial scrutiny.” Ned Price, a former deputy to the U.S. representative to the UN, said: “The joint statement was notable, not because Russia and China said it, but because the United States also co-signed.” He added: “The United Nations they want is not a United Nations that’s more effective but rather a United Nations that is less effective at upholding the rules of the road, the very principles that have been at the centre of the UN system since its founding: the idea that big countries can’t bully small countries, that borders can’t be redrawn by force.”

## Trump-Putin talks produced no immediate Ukraine commitment

In their first meeting since failed negotiations in Alaska last year, Trump held one-on-one talks with Putin that touched on Ukraine but secured no immediate commitments. The bilateral occurred against a backdrop of Trump recently rejecting new sanctions on Russia. Pointing toward Putin as he walked over, Trump asked rhetorically, “Who is he?” and said, “He’s my friend.” He separately told Xi, “You’re my friend, and we have a very special relationship.”

In the Trump-Xi bilateral, Trump raised the U.S.-led war in Iran, emphasizing a joint interest in fully reopening the Strait of Hormuz and preventing Iran from acquiring nuclear weapons. Xi focused on U.S.-China trade stability and asserted China’s sovereign position on Taiwan, treating it as an unalterable domestic issue. Trump told Xi, “I think as long as you and I are in charge of things, we’ll never have a problem.” He later told NBC News that the pair had agreed artificial intelligence needed some form of safety “guardrails,” though he offered few details.

## Reactions focused on summit optics and diplomatic limits

The defining image showed the three sitting around a huge circular wooden table. At its hollow center was an immaculate arrangement of pink flowers, an ancient Chinese motif meant to evoke harmony. U.S.-based critics said the optics sent a different signal. Steve Schmidt, a former adviser to President George W Bush and Senator John McCain, said: “The image of the grotesque large round table with a war criminal dictator, a stable dictator and an irrational, delusional wannabe dictator is an image of deep shame and weakness for the United States. It’s a repudiation of American values, American honour, common sense and the national security interests of the country, and a betrayal of America’s allies all across the world.”

The Guardian wrote, “Beijing understood how to satisfy the Trump ego.” When Air Force One landed at Shenzhen Bao’an International Airport on Wednesday for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, Xi made the unusual move of greeting Trump personally on the tarmac. An honor guard stood at attention, crowds waved miniature U.S. and Chinese flags, schoolchildren shook pompoms, and Xi drew Trump into a warm embrace. The Guardian wrote that “Trump took the bait with delight. For him, victory was not measured in negotiating concessions but in the lavish banquet and the seat reserved for him at the head table.” Trump said in a television interview, “That’s a big win for us” and “It’s a big win for China too. We get along.” Trump was treated to a performance of YMCA, the signature song of his campaign rallies.

Trump arrived after a midterm election defeat. The Guardian wrote: “Rebuked by millions of voters in a still resilient democracy, Donald Trump travelled to Asia this week and found comfort in the company of autocrats who still flatter him.” The Guardian described Trump and the meeting this way: “The US president, reduced to lame-duck status at home by a humiliating midterm election defeat, returned to the global stage in a meeting with two strongmen in Shenzhen, China, that critics described as a new low for America’s claim to moral authority.” Observers watched the body language closely for any clues about the dynamics between Xi, “relishing his role as host”; Putin, “whose war in Ukraine is faltering”; and Trump, “ostensibly the most powerful of the three but politically wounded at home.” The Guardian wrote: “To many, it offered little more than a glorified photo op.”

Brett Bruen, president of the public-affairs agency Global Situation Room and former global engagement director of the Barack Obama White House, said: “Brand Trump has been badly sullied by a historic defeat at the hands of American voters. However, this isn’t going to improve his standing internationally and certainly not domestically.” He added: “If anything, it put on display his desperation. It put on display his isolation internationally from the very people that he’s going to need to be able to secure trade deals or peace deals. Once again he’s returning from China with the luggage hold empty of any diplomatic accomplishments.”

The Guardian wrote: “The summit was a propaganda win for Putin, who went from international pariah status to equal billing with the world’s two biggest economies. It was also a victory for Xi.” China made no concessions on Taiwan or trade tariffs and made no concessions concerning its industrial lifeline to Moscow. The Guardian wrote that Beijing “positioned itself as the arbiter of 21st-century diplomacy.”

Ali Wyne, senior research and advocacy adviser for US-China relations at the International Crisis Group, said: “Xi was able to burnish China’s diplomatic sway and give Putin and Trump something that each leader respectively coveted.” He said: “He gave Putin the opportunity to demonstrate that despite Russia’s wartime losses, despite western sanctions, Russia remains a great power. He gave Trump the opportunity to demonstrate, at least in Trump’s own mind, that despite these bruising midterm elections, Trump is a singularly visionary and consequential peacemaker, thereby appealing to Trump’s ego.” The Guardian wrote: “While neither Beijing nor Moscow harbours any illusions about Trump’s volatile temperament, both recognise the ticking clock on his second term and know they are likely to outlast him.” Wyne added: “Both of them recognise that it is next to impossible that any of President Trump’s successors will view them in comparable terms as friends, adopt conciliatory dispositions, and on certain issues come close to aligning US positions with those of China and Russia.”

Although Trump was caught on camera apparently nodding off, The Guardian said he departed China “appearing politically rejuvenated” after his encounters with his two “friends.” He hailed the visit as “Highly Consequential” on social media and said later: “On a scale of zero to 10, I think it was a 12.”

## European leaders met in Berlin as the summit concluded

The Guardian wrote: “The talks have done little to dispel the impression that Trump regards Congress, the media and elections as pesky encumbrances while gazing with awe and some envy at Xi and Putin.” As the Shenzhen summit concluded, European leaders and NATO officials convened urgently in Berlin to reinforce their support for Ukraine and resist any high-level partitioning of European security.
