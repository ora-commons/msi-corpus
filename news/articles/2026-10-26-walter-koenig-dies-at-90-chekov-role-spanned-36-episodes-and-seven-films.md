## George Takei recalled a friendship that lasted nearly six decades

Walter Koenig, who played Ensign Pavel Chekov in the original Star Trek television series, died at 90, his representatives announced Monday. His spokesperson told The Hollywood Reporter and The New York Times that Koenig died at his home in Los Angeles.

Koenig was born Sept. 14, 1936, in Chicago to Russian Jewish immigrants from Lithuania. He began his career with a few bit television roles in the early 1960s before receiving the role of Ensign Pavel Chekov in Star Trek's original series, beginning with its second season in 1967.

Koenig played Chekov in 36 of the series' episodes. The Russian character served as the USS Enterprise's navigator, with Koenig delivering lines including, “Captain, I have the coordinates locked. We are ready to engage warp drive on your command.”

Koenig reprised the role in all seven of the original Star Trek movies, beginning with Star Trek: The Motion Picture in 1979. His work as Chekov extended the character's time aboard the Enterprise beyond the television series.

Koenig also played the villain Alfred Bester in a recurring role on Babylon 5 during the 1990s. He was listed as a “special guest star” for 12 episodes, and United Press International described Bester as a “quintessential scoundrel.”

Koenig's second marriage came in 1965 to actress Judy Levitt, who appeared in three of the original Star Trek films. Levitt died in 2022, according to The New York Times. Koenig was survived by their daughter, Danielle Koenig, and a grandson.

Fellow original Star Trek cast member George Takei remembered Koenig in a social media post on Monday. “My heart is heavy. Walter Koenig, our Ensign Pavel Chekov, passed away early today,” Takei wrote.

Takei said he and Koenig met on the bridge of the Enterprise in the 1960s and stayed friends for nearly six decades. Koenig served as Takei's best man when Takei and Brad married in 2008, Takei said.

Takei also recalled that Koenig turned 90 on Sept. 14, the same day Takei and Brad celebrated their 18th wedding anniversary. He described Koenig as “more than Chekov” and called him “a writer, a teacher, a thoughtful man, and a loyal friend.”

Takei thanked the fans who had loved Koenig for years and said Koenig loved them in return. He closed his remembrance with: “Rest well, my dear friend. Keep navigating a steady course.”
