## Trump faces FAA funding disputes and a Senate Democratic leadership contest

President Trump began a trip to Asia on November 16, ahead of a planned meeting with Xi and Putin later that week. In a PBS News “Politics Monday” discussion moderated by Geoff Bennett, Tamara Keith of NPR and Amy Walter of The Cook Political Report With Amy Walter said Trump was seeking economic deals and nuclear talks with China and Russia. China had said it would not participate in the denuclearization talks, and Ukraine's war might not be fully on the agenda.

Keith said Trump likes meeting face-to-face with his fellow leaders and would get the image he wants, with Trump, Xi and Putin together as “these three global powers.” In some ways, she said, that was all he wanted, and the president often came into such meetings without a very clear agenda. Trump was asked repeatedly about what he would raise, and he said he may talk about Nvidia, or about Ukraine and Iran and denuclearization and economic issues, but it was not clear there were any deliverables or specific end goals, because so much of the way he does foreign policy is about his gut and his feeling for the leaders. Keith said the goals of the people around Trump going into the meetings could produce different outcomes, or no notable outcomes at all.

Bennett said that in administrations past, if there were going to be no deliverables, a president would not take the meeting, and asked what the political value was for Trump of a summit like this. Walter said the value was “more about the optics than almost anything else.” She said there was something about “a lame-duck president who has just lost midterm elections, not particularly popular at home, going abroad to show strength.” Other presidents have done similar things, she said, and standing with those leaders shows that, whatever the actual outcome, “I'm still the president of the United States of America, I can still do things regardless of what the actual outcome is.”

The discussion then turned to the FAA. Trump blamed the Biden administration for the problems plaguing the agency. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said the agency needs more money. Democrats said Congress had already appropriated money the FAA had not spent, and Senator John Thune also noted the unspent funds. Keith said the lunch meeting with Republicans and Duffy will be important as Duffy tries to resolve concerns and move toward convincing Republicans to get on board with his requests, which ballooned from $30 billion to $50 billion basically overnight. She said she did not know what it would take to get them to work together. There are millions of inconvenienced travelers who are upset and want solutions, she said.

Keith expected hearings and a lot more investigating of exactly what happened and why the appropriated money has not been spent in a cohesive way. Walter said she and others had discussed over the previous two years the sidelining of Congress and an empowered president and executive branch. She linked the dispute to that issue, along with pocket rescissions that say, in effect, that Congress requires the money to be spent but the administration does not want to spend it. She said there is no trust between the branches even before the page turns in 2027 to a Democratic-controlled Congress, and that the breakdown matters not just for what members of Congress want to spend, but for whether the administration actually spends it on the things they said it should go to.

Bennett remarked that as the page turned to 2027, it was almost upon them. Walter replied that she almost said '28, so be careful, then added, “That's where our heads are already.”

Senator Chris Van Hollen is challenging Chuck Schumer, who has led Senate Democrats for nearly a decade, for the chamber's Democratic leadership. A number of progressive senators and new senators-elect are backing Van Hollen. Bennett said Van Hollen was presenting himself as the candidate of change, and asked how compelling his argument was that he represents a break from business as usual. In a CNN interview with Dana Bash yesterday, Van Hollen said, “This is not about one person or any one individual. This really is a response to the call of the voters.” He said voters had clearly rejected Donald Trump and his policies but had not fully hired the Democrats, and that the party had been given a chance to earn their trust and should not go back to business as usual.

Keith called it a challenge for a 67-year-old career politician who has been in leadership in the House and ran the Democratic efforts to get senators elected in the Senate. She said Van Hollen is someone who is fairly establishment, though in recent years he has been more progressive in his policy and language, particularly toward funding for Israel. She said he is not a fresh young person coming in to lead the Democrats but would be a different person coming in to lead them, and that in interviews he has not fully distinguished himself from Schumer on policy in a lot of areas, agreeing with him that impeachment is not the right path right now.

Bennett asked whether Democrats wanted a leader who would move more to the left or would be aggressive about trying to hold President Trump and the administration accountable. Walter said there was a split. She said exit polls showed many people who identified as Democrats wanted to see more effort to hold Trump accountable, while many incoming members who had to win with independents and some Trump voters wanted actual deliverables. She said the leadership fight is interesting for today but will not be consequential for very long, because the real fight will come in 2027 and early 2028 over the Democratic nomination. It is not who controls the Senate Democrats or the House Democrats, she said, but who the nominee will ultimately be, who sets the course for Democrats.

Bennett noted that right now there is no standard-bearer, which is why there has been so much focus on Schumer and Hakeem Jeffries. Keith said the two will be the spokespeople for the Democratic Party, adding that this would be the case “for now, for a relatively short period of time,” which Walter confirmed, saying “For now, yes.” Keith said what Van Hollen is saying is that voters are demanding a better spokesperson.

Across the discussion, no summit deliverable was identified, while the domestic segments focused on FAA spending and the Senate Democratic leadership contest.
