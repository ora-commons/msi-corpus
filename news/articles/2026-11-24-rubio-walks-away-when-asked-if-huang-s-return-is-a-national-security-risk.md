## Trump turned to JD Vance to bring Huang back as CEO

Secretary of State Marco Rubio did not answer a reporter's question about Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's return at an event Monday welcoming Qatar's leader. After the reporter asked, “Mr. Secretary, is Jensen Huang’s reinstatement a national-security risk? Are you concerned about that?” Rubio waited until the question was finished, turned and walked away, The Wall Street Journal reported.

Rubio's warning came earlier this year, when he told other government officials that Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang could be compromised, according to The Wall Street Journal. The newspaper said Rubio appeared to be playing the long game, flagging the risk before the situation spiraled out of control.

The report said government officials also had concerns that Huang was close to a woman who had ties to Chinese intelligence. Nvidia had ousted Huang two weeks ago following revelations of a second affair, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Over the weekend, President Donald Trump and others put enormous pressure on Nvidia's board to reinstate Huang, and the board ultimately relented. The Wall Street Journal's Politics newsletter said Trump turned to Vice President JD Vance to get it done when he decided he wanted Huang back as Nvidia's CEO. Nvidia's board said it was confident that Huang's reinstatement poses no national-security risk.

Trump offered multiple explanations for the decision. He said the personal indiscretions were not relevant, declaring, “Sex sells, fellas,” and said Huang might have gone to work for China, adding, “He’s got a little blood in him.” The report said Nvidia and its AI-powering chips are central to Trump's China policy. It also said Trump's China policy had surprised many people.

The Wall Street Journal said Rubio is among the people often mentioned to lead the GOP in a post-Trump era. The report said it appeared, so far, that the most outspoken lawmakers of both parties shared concerns about Huang and Trump's involvement. It said lawmakers could end up rallying behind Rubio whether he wanted their support or not.

Huang is scheduled to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 3. News of his reinstatement drew criticism on Capitol Hill, where lawmakers of both parties said the decision was wrong.
