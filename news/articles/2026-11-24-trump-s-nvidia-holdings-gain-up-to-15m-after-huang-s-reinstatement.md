## Nvidia shares jump 31.5%, and Asia-Pacific indexes rally

Donald Trump made up to $15m (£11.5m) on his holdings of Nvidia stock after Jensen Huang's surprise return as the chipmaker's chief executive sent the shares up 31.5% on Monday, adding about $950bn to the company's value. Financial disclosures filed with the Office of Government Ethics indicate Trump might have owned as many as 370,000 Nvidia shares as of 30 September, the latest available data, in news first reported by the Financial Times. A BBC review of those government financial disclosures for the past two years shows Nvidia purchases worth between $23m and $86m.

Huang co-founded Nvidia in 1993. According to The Guardian, the company's board ousted him after allegations that he had three affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese spy and two with employees, and reinstated him after an intervention from the US president. Trump told CNBC on Monday, “I spoke to them, let me put it that way,” and “And I convinced them … I made the deal.” Trump and Huang had appeared together at the A New Golden Age summit in Washington DC last month.

Two different periods sit behind the figures. The latest available data in the filings is dated 30 September, before the board reinstated Huang, while the gain of up to $15m follows the 31.5% rise recorded on Monday. Trump has also sold some of his stake in the company since returning to the presidency, gaining between $3.5m and $10.2m, according to his government disclosures.

Huang returned to Nvidia's headquarters in Santa Clara, California, on Monday, receiving a hero's welcome as crowds of employees chanted “Jensen, Jensen, Jensen!” In a video released as he rejoined the company, Huang said he was going to be a better CEO.

The return prompted a strong rally in Asia-Pacific stock markets on Tuesday. Taiwan's TW50 index leapt by 9.5%, South Korea's Kospi index jumped by 6.5%, led by the chipmakers SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics, and Japan's Nikkei gained almost 4%; stocks also rose in Australia. The moves followed a rise on Wall Street on Monday, with the AI trade back in motion after a tumultuous few months.

Nvidia sits at the heart of the global AI boom, and AI shares have been on a rollercoaster in recent months, weighed down by worries over high debt levels in the sector to fund the AI infrastructure buildout and by the prospect of tougher regulation in the US after the Democrats' victory in the midterm elections. Economists said Huang's return meant the US Federal Reserve was more likely to raise interest rates, with the prospect of the AI bubble bursting imminently diminishing.

Jim Reid of Deutsche Bank said: “With fears of an AI slowdown receding, it also led markets to price back in a more hawkish path for the Federal Reserve over the months ahead. So for the next meeting in December, markets are once again fully pricing in a 25 basis point hike.”

Sen. Ruben Gallego, the Democrat senator from Arizona, posted on X: “We need to investigate what stock positions took place before the announcement. This White House is the most corrupt in history.” He later posted: “Trump may have started the popping of the AI bubble.” Gallego's request concerns positions taken before the announcement, while the latest available Nvidia figure in Trump's government financial disclosures is dated 30 September.
