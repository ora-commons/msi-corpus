## Oversupply, worried consumers and seasonal trends contribute to a roughly month-long decline

GasBuddy, which tracks prices at the pump, projects that regular gasoline will average $3.62 a gallon nationwide on Thanksgiving Day. GasBuddy said prices fell in all 50 states over the previous week, while AAA reported Friday's national average at $3.69, down 18 cents from a week earlier and 58 cents from a month earlier. Prices have followed a steady glide down for roughly the past month, with oversupply, worried consumers and seasonal driving trends all contributing to the drop.

The price would still be high — in fact, a Thanksgiving record by most standards — and the highest Thanksgiving Day price average was reported in 2022, at $3.56 a gallon. In the United States, AAA projected that 72.9 million people would drive somewhere during the holiday, 200,000 fewer than last year's Thanksgiving season. An AAA analysis said, “For most holiday travelers, driving remains the cheapest and easiest way to get around” this Thanksgiving, and about 90% of holiday travelers were expected to drive.

GasBuddy found that average regular gasoline prices fell by more than 25 cents a gallon in a dozen states over the past week. The biggest week-to-week drops were in Montana, by 38.8 cents a gallon; Ohio, by 33.3 cents; Michigan, by 32.1 cents; Colorado, by 29.2 cents; and New Mexico, by 28.4 cents.

Patrick De Haan, head of petroleum analysis at GasBuddy, found that regular gasoline prices had fallen below $4 in 43 states. Friday averages included outliers at $5.60 in Hawaii, the highest in the country, and $5.50 in California, while North Carolina had the lowest average at $3.03. The report noted that infrastructure can make it harder to obtain products in some states and that statewide averages do not show the highest and lowest prices within each state.

Oil prices are down roughly $25 a barrel over the past month. Part of the change reflects hopes that the U.S.-Iran war will end or at least cool. “Markets bet on a de-escalation between the U.S. and Iran following the midterm elections,” said Grace Zwemmer, an economist at Oxford Economics. The report estimated that each $1 change in the barrel price means about 2.5 cents per gallon at retail.

Before the war, about 20% of the world's oil passed through the Strait of Hormuz, a narrow waterway between Oman and Iran. Despite occasional skirmishes, that oil is mostly proceeding through the strait. The changed outlook partly reflects reduced demand and partly an expectation that the war's impact will wane.

De Haan said, “Expectations of a coming supply surplus outweighed fresh security incidents around the Strait of Hormuz.” The International Energy Agency's November report described “tentative signs of rebalancing, with global oil supplies surpassing demand for the first time since the outbreak of the war in Iran.” The IEA also forecast that the global oil market could return to a surplus in the quarter, “with a more substantial build in 2027.”

The U.S. Energy Information Administration had a similar forecast, predicting that oil prices would average $84 a barrel next year, about the same as the current level, and retail gasoline prices would average $3.56 a gallon. That prediction assumes that 2027 will proceed “as oil flows recover and inventories rebuild.” Goldman Sachs echoed the forecast in an analysis, saying “oil is forecast to ease gradually in 2027 as suppliers in the Middle East adapt to disruption from the war in Iran.”

Some of the decline is seasonal. Americans tend to drive less in colder months, reducing demand. In an X post, De Haan said, “After Sept. 15, stations can sell winter blend, which is cheaper to make. Summer gas has to meet tighter evaporation rules, and that costs refiners more.”

Consumers have also become more cautious about spending. The cost of living “continues to be at the forefront of consumers' minds across the population,” said Joanne Hsu, director of the University of Michigan Survey of Consumers. The survey's November report found that about 57% of consumers “spontaneously mentioned that their living standards have been eroded by high prices.” That was the highest level since May, compared with 46% in February before the war began.

The forecasts remain vulnerable because oil prices are difficult to predict. A year earlier, no one saw the price spike that gripped the country this year, the report said. The IEA cautioned: “As always, downside risks to the forecast persist, as attacks on shipping undermine maritime security, which is pivotal to a sustained recovery.”

Gasoline prices are down but remain historically high. De Haan said, “Americans aren't letting the pump keep them from the table,” but “that doesn't mean drivers aren't feeling it.”

By Friday, AAA's average had fallen to $3.69, and GasBuddy projected $3.62 for Thanksgiving Day, still above the 2022 holiday average of $3.56.
