## Upper East Side home hosted talking circles for more than 50 years

The Park Avenue Armory hosted the late-afternoon Gloria Steinem memorial in Manhattan on Wednesday, a few blocks from Steinem's Upper East Side home. Political figures, pop stars and poets were gathering for the joyful, music-filled tribute. Steinem, who died in September at 92, was an activist and prolific author whom the Associated Press described as the most visible symbol of American feminism.

About 1,000 guests had received the invitation, which declared, "It's Steinemite!" The Associated Press described the response as an indication of the buoyant mood organizers said Steinem wanted for her memorial.

Former Secretary of State Hillary Clinton was expected to be one of the main speakers at the late-afternoon memorial. Performances by pop star Cyndi Lauper and actor-singer Cynthia Erivo were planned, along with poetry and choral presentations. The Park Avenue Armory is a few blocks from Steinem's home in Manhattan's Upper East Side.

"Gloria wanted a true celebration filled with laughter and friends across generations," said Jill Heller, director of programs at Gloria's Foundation. Heller said the presenters "reflect how expansive a life Gloria lived but also just the spirit of community, celebration and hopefulness that she wanted for this moment." Gloria's Foundation is working to preserve Steinem's home as a center to further her work.

Close friend Amy Richards, a chief organizer of the event, said Steinem "definitely wanted people to have a good time. She hoped that new people will meet each other, and there will be laughter."

The celebration was expected to mirror the spirit of Steinem's talking circles, which she hosted at her Upper East Side home for more than 50 years. The Associated Press described the gathering as a vibrant mix of people from near and far, reflecting Steinem's extensive travels and eclectic tastes in art and music.

Steinem became an activist after an early career as a journalist. She has said she might have remained a journalist had editors been willing to let her write about social issues, including the nascent women's movement, but they were not interested. At a meeting where women spoke out about illegal abortions, she decided to change paths.

In a 2022 interview with The Associated Press, Steinem said, "That speak-out was the beginning of activism for me." She added, "Either our control over our own bodies is equal, regardless of sex or race, or we're not living in a democracy."

Steinem went on to co-found Ms. Magazine and the National Women's Political Caucus. She traveled constantly to campuses, rallies and protests despite an early fear of public speaking.

When she was at home in New York, her living room was a constant gathering place. Groups and movements were born there as women gathered from across the country and around the globe.

Steinem, a prolific author, died just weeks before the release of her final memoir, *An Unexpected Life*. In its introduction, she wrote, "As I write this, I'm both older than I ever imagined I would become and happier than I ever imagined I could be."

She added, "I've been rewarded with friends who are my chosen family. I have trusted friends in other countries, and we strive to be safe and free, and share hopes for a peaceful democracy in the world." Steinem also wrote that she planned to live to 100.
