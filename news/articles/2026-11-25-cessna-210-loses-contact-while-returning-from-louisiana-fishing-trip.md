## Cmdr Trevor Blount calls situation heartbreaking; FAA and NTSB will investigate

The Cessna 210 was en route from New Orleans to Fort Myers, Florida, according to FlightAware. WVUE reported that the people aboard were traveling back home after a fishing trip in Venice, Louisiana.

The air traffic control regional tower in Mobile, Alabama, lost contact with the aircraft at about 3.45pm Tuesday, according to the US Coast Guard. The Cessna went down in the Gulf of Mexico between Pascagoula, Mississippi, and Horn Island, part of the Gulf Islands national seashore.

Four adults and two boys, ages eight and 10, were aboard, Coast Guard spokesperson Juan Ros said. Divers recovered the bodies of all six people from the plane's fuselage, according to the US Coast Guard.

Responders searched for hours and initially found only three lifejackets, a life raft and the plane's cabin floating on the water, Ros said.

A WLOX/AP photograph showed crew vehicles parked at the search site for missing people after the small plane crashed off the coast of Mississippi on Tuesday.

Cmdr Trevor Blount, the Coast Guard coordinator for search and rescue in the Mobile area, called the situation heartbreaking. He said in a statement: “While this is not the outcome any of us had hoped for, we are grateful for the strong interagency coordination that enabled us to swiftly locate those aboard the aircraft and provide closure to their families.”

The Federal Aviation Administration and National Transportation Safety Board will investigate the Gulf of Mexico crash.
