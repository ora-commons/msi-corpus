## Colby says attacks may harden Russian public resolve and encourage Kremlin escalation

A regular meeting of countries supporting Ukraine’s defense at NATO headquarters in Brussels, intended to strengthen unity, became the setting for a pointed disagreement on Tuesday over how to put pressure on Russia. German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius called U.S. Under Secretary for Defense Elbridge Colby’s criticism of Kyiv’s strikes inside Russia “stupid.”

Colby drew raised eyebrows among allies by criticizing Kyiv for attacking certain targets deep inside Russia, including logistics depots of an online retailer. The Wall Street Journal identified the company as Wildberries and reported that it ships parts Russia uses in military drones.

Colby said such strikes “have actually probably hardened the Russian public’s resolve and the Kremlin’s own willingness to escalate.” His objection focused on attacks against logistics infrastructure and other targets deep inside Russian territory.

Asked later about the remark, Pistorius questioned why Colby was taking Ukraine to task for the strikes while Moscow was conducting a campaign to target Ukraine’s energy infrastructure. He said Ukraine was striking legitimate, strategic military targets in Russia.

“We should rather criticize and talk about the terroristic strikes every night and every day on civil energy, water and heater infrastructure in Ukraine. This is kind of terrorism,” Pistorius said. The Wall Street Journal noted that Pistorius is not a native English speaker.

The exchange underscored tensions between the U.S. and its European allies over Ukraine as Washington pushed to broker an end to the war. With the talks in a stalemate, Ukraine and Russia had ramped up strikes in recent days.

On Tuesday, Ukraine destroyed part of a bridge connecting Russia and the Crimean Peninsula, retaliating in part for repeated Russian strikes on Ukrainian bridges. The bridge has been a major artery for military supplies and a symbol of Russian President Vladimir Putin’s annexation of Ukrainian territory in 2014.

Last week, Russia and Ukraine emerged from two days of U.S.-mediated peace talks in Abu Dhabi with no breakthrough toward ending the war. Negotiators described the exchanges as among the toughest yet.
