## Shell expected to book loss on Monaca ethylene plant, Citi says

Crude futures moved lower on Monday, Nov. 16, 2026, as the market absorbed estimates that global oil supply is poised to shift into a surplus. The pressure came alongside a Middle East conflict that continues to simmer, with attacks on tankers and other targets.

WTI was off 1.1% at $87.56 a barrel, while Brent was down 1.8% at $94.13 a barrel, according to The Wall Street Journal's Energy & Utilities Roundup: Market Talk, published on Dow Jones Newswires.

Razan Hilal, a market analyst at Forex.com, laid out two key scenarios for oil prices in a note cited by the roundup. One is a steep decline toward pre-war levels if geopolitical risks ease. The other is a short-term bullish rebound that could remain in place while those risks persist.

Hilal argued that the risk premium in crude has support from a wider set of concerns than energy infrastructure alone. "Geopolitical risks extend beyond energy infrastructure and tanker attacks to worldwide cybersecurity concerns, helping sustain a risk premium in crude oil," she said.

In company news, Citi analysts wrote that Shell is expected to realize a loss of value on its Monaca ethylene plant in the United States. Shell has said it is trying to dispose of its U.S. chemicals business in a "capital markets or asset transaction."

The Citi analysts said Shell would ideally place the assets into a joint venture with another company and later monetize the stake when the outlook for chemicals improves. They noted that attracting an industrial partner willing to carve out its own assets into the joint venture is challenging, and that any transaction will ultimately crystallize significant value impairment. Shell shares rose 0.3% to 3,733 pence.

Maybank Securities' Jarick Seet said in a note that CSE Global's 2026 and 2027 earnings could be weighed by new expansion costs. The Singapore technology solutions provider signed a new production facility lease, likely for new data center clients in 2027, which could incur near-term start-up costs, he said.

Seet cut his 2026 and 2027 earnings projections for CSE Global by 7.1% and 6.9%, respectively, and trimmed Maybank's target price to 1.40 Singapore dollar from S$2.25, while retaining the firm's buy rating. He added that CSE Global expects to more than triple its capacity by 2027-2028, which could contribute to a multiyear growth story. CSE Global shares declined 4.1% to 82 Singapore cents.
