## Raskin cites a Signal exchange he said he obtained, saying it discussed a potentially ongoing healthcare fraud investigation

Rep. Jamie Raskin asked top officials at multiple federal agencies to preserve communications created on nongovernment messaging apps and investigate whether any records related to official business have been deleted. The nearly identical letters, made public Friday, went to the FBI, Justice Department, Department of Homeland Security, Bureau of Prisons, and Immigration and Customs Enforcement. Raskin, a Maryland Democrat, is the House Judiciary Committee's top Democrat and the panel's incoming chairman.

Raskin said in the letters that he was concerned President Donald Trump's administration was circumventing record-keeping requirements by using apps like Signal that can be configured to automatically delete messages after a certain time period. Signal supports direct messaging and group chats, phone calls, and video calls. It uses end-to-end encryption for its messaging and calling services, which prevents third parties from viewing conversation content or listening to calls.

Raskin said in the letters that the agencies were legally required to preserve all records related to their official business, policies, and decisions. He wrote:

> “There is significant evidence that Trump Administration officials have engaged in the widespread destruction, mutilation, and concealment of government records, seemingly with the express purpose of circumventing federal record preservation laws. This attempt to evade transparency extends to the highest levels of federal agencies, including top-ranking law enforcement officials.”

Raskin cited as an example a Signal exchange that he said he had obtained between the FBI's top lawyer and an associate of the Republican president's oldest son, Donald Trump Jr. Raskin said the exchange discussed a potentially ongoing healthcare fraud investigation.

The Associated Press made multiple attempts to get comment from the agencies cited by Raskin but did not receive any immediate responses.

The White House and the Pentagon have said that disgruntled former employees were spreading false claims about the use of Signal. Signal is a commercially available app not authorized to be used to communicate sensitive or classified national defense information.

An Associated Press review from last year found that encrypted messaging apps were increasingly popular with government officials.

The prevalence of encrypted messaging apps among government officials was demonstrated last year when journalist Jeffrey Goldberg revealed that he had been inadvertently added to a Signal group chat of U.S. national security officials coordinating plans for airstrikes.
