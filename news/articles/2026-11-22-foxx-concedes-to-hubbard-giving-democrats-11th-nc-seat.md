## Machine recount netted Foxx one vote, leaving Hubbard ahead by 330

U.S. Rep. Virginia Foxx, the 83-year-old Republican who has represented northwestern North Carolina since 2005, conceded the 5th Congressional District seat to Democratic challenger Chuck Hubbard on Sunday, two days after a machine recount left her 330 votes behind.

"The voters of the Fifth Congressional District exercised their Constitutional rights and made their voices heard," Foxx wrote on Sunday as she conceded the seat.

Foxx said she was saddened not to emerge victorious but continues to have faith in the election process.

"The peaceful transition of elected office from one person to another is one of the true hallmarks of our system of government, and I am proud to be a citizen of a nation that prizes fair and free elections," Foxx said.

Foxx faced a Sunday deadline to request a partial hand recount. The machine recount completed Saturday netted her only one vote. Hubbard said the congresswoman conceded graciously after the recount results made it clear she could not prevail.

"I deeply appreciate her commitment to a smooth transition of the office and constituent casework," Hubbard said on social media. "We may not always agree, but we both love North Carolina and the people of the Fifth District."

Hubbard's win officially gives Democrats an 11th seat in the North Carolina congressional delegation, with three Republicans — a switch from the current delegation of 10 Republicans and four Democrats.

North Carolina's 5th District covers the northwestern part of the state, stretching from Caldwell and Watauga counties in the west to Rockingham and Guilford counties in the east.

Foxx, an ally of President Donald Trump, served most recently as chairwoman of the House Committee on Rules. As a member of the U.S. House Oversight and Reform Committee, she skipped the vast majority of closed-door impeachment depositions against Trump in his first term.

In October 2023, Foxx drew headlines for telling a reporter to "shut up" after the reporter asked House Speaker nominee Mike Johnson about attempts to overturn the results of the 2020 presidential election.

Foxx served two stints as chair of the House Committee on Education and the Workforce and used the role to push back against the Biden-Harris administration's student loan forgiveness program. She joined her Republican colleagues in promising to protect women's sports from those who would equate "perceived sex with biological sex."

Last January, Foxx slipped and fell down the stairs outside the U.S. House chamber. Hours after the ordeal, Foxx told her constituents she was fine and would return to work a day later.

"I'm a mountain woman, and we're tougher than a $2 steak," she said.
