## Ukrainian officials say speed cap allowed Russian missiles to evade defenses

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky's public complaints — namely that Ukrainians could die if unrestricted Starlink service was not restored — led to widespread confusion over who had ordered a speed cap and to an informal investigation inside the White House. Ukrainian officials said the restrictions, which began Oct. 14, allowed Russian missiles to evade their defenses. U.S. officials and people familiar with the matter said the decision "sparked panic in Kyiv and confusion inside the Trump administration."

Some U.S. officials said Elon Musk acted unilaterally and that President Donald Trump did not specifically order any retribution against Ukraine. One person familiar with the situation said Musk talked with multiple senior administration officials, including Trump, before imposing the cap. U.S. officials and people familiar said Musk's order slowed connections for Ukrainian hardware using a military-grade version of Starlink, leaving some offensive drones and interceptor drones "virtually useless."

Two of the people said Musk and Trump discussed the Starlink decision in a call soon after the slowdown became public, while the president was campaigning last week. One of those people separately claimed Musk and Trump had discussed the matter before the decision to slow Ukrainian Starlink connections.

Musk posted on X hours after the slowdown became public: "Any limits on speed or usage are decided by the US military and ultimately POTUS," using the acronym for president of the United States. Trump told reporters Monday that he did not know who was responsible for the slowdown, "but I would like to find out."

During the uproar, SpaceX representatives called White House counterparts seeking a unified understanding, people familiar said. They then sought a subsequent message that SpaceX and the Trump administration had coordinated before Musk's directive, the people said.

The Oct. 14 slowdown followed several announcements and statements by the Trump administration that Ukrainian officials saw as hostile to Ukraine, including criticism of Zelensky from Trump and Musk. Ukrainian officials said those statements led Kyiv to immediately suspect the slowdown had been ordered by either Trump or Musk.

The slowdown followed recent public criticism by Trump and Musk of Ukraine's attacks on Russian diesel refineries. The Wall Street Journal said Trump and Musk's criticism of Zelensky had grown in recent weeks. Trump blames those attacks for high energy prices, and The Wall Street Journal said he was looking to lower diesel prices ahead of November's midterm elections. Most analysts said the Iran war had a greater impact on prices.

On Oct. 10, days before Starlink speeds slowed, Trump said he had told Zelensky, "You can do whatever you want to Russia, but don't hit the refineries because that's a world problem. And what does he do? He goes and hits the diesel fuel. He better damn well stop." Musk responded to a White House video of the remarks on X: "Zelensky has gone too far." Four days later, Ukrainian drone operators reported that they were losing unrestricted access to the Starlink network for unexplained reasons, leading to urgent requests from Kyiv for an explanation.

Michael Kofman, an expert on the war at the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace in Washington, said: "Ukrainian forces continue to be throttled in their ability to employ strike drones and interceptor drones to 160 kilometers per hour, but the current connection is below 130 kilometers per hour, and the limits continue to hamper Ukrainian strike operations and air defenses."

The Wall Street Journal said the episode had pushed U.S.-Ukraine tensions to their highest level since Trump publicly criticized Zelensky in the Oval Office last year. The paper described Starlink as a "lifeline" for Ukrainian forces on front lines attacking Russian troops and defending themselves from strikes, and said the episode highlighted Musk's "singular influence" over the war. It wrote that Musk "has the power to alter the course of a deadly conflict."

Zelensky also accused the Trump administration of temporarily cutting intelligence support that helped Ukraine's forces track Russian missile launches and strike deep into Russian territory. Trump and others denied that happened, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio told reporters Tuesday that the claims were "not true."

Rubio, who spoke with Zelensky by phone earlier Tuesday, later acknowledged "there's no doubt" about rising tensions between Washington and Kyiv. Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said Tuesday that "we haven't pulled anything back," but "Starlink has the option to make decisions concerning its terms of service." He added: "We haven't made any directives or anything like that."

Ukraine's top diplomat in Washington, Denys Sienik, said Tuesday that Ukraine was still "waiting for the official response from contacts with the Pentagon and SpaceX at the leaders level to understand what has happened, but also to understand whether the connection has been restored or will be restored." SpaceX did not respond to a request for comment.

Zelensky traveled to Norway on Tuesday for discussions with Trump's envoy Steve Witkoff and two other leaders about ending the war.
