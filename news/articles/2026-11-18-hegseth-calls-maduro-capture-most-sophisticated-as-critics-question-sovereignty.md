## Rodríguez cites cooperation; Reyes says White House directs Venezuelan affairs

US Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth described the 3 January operation that captured former Venezuelan president Nicolás Maduro as "the single most sophisticated military operation in the history of Latin America" during his first visit to Venezuela after US special forces seized him. The remarks included an anniversary exchange in which Hegseth recalled Maduro's mock "Come and get me!" challenge and answered, "Well, we answered that call."

The Guardian reported that critics of acting president Delcy Rodríguez's political movement cast the visit as a humiliating act of genuflection for a president with little choice but to receive the official whose troops captured her boss. Venezuelan journalist Luz Mely Reyes wrote on X that the trip was designed to underscore Washington's "direct administration" of Venezuela. She wrote: "Ten months after Nicolás Maduro's capture, Washington no longer needs to fire shots to remind everyone who is in charge: the mere presence on Venezuelan soil of the architect of the January incursion reaffirms that the country's operational sovereignty is subject to the White House's directives."

The Guardian reported that Delta Force operatives controversially seized Maduro in the early hours of 3 January, setting in motion what the outlet called an improbable rapprochement between Washington and Venezuela's anti-imperialist "Chavista" regime, which has ruled since 1999. Rodríguez, who had been Maduro's vice president, became acting president with Donald Trump's blessing after the abduction. In the aftermath of what the outlet described as a deadly US attack, Hegseth was among the administration's bluntest voices in celebrating the invasion. The Guardian quoted him saying, "Maduro had his chance … he effed around and he found out."

At the recently reopened US embassy in Caracas, Hegseth said it was "a great honor to be here as the first United States secretary of war this century to set foot in Venezuela." He added, "Just one year ago my presence here … would have been deemed impossible, almost unimaginable." Hegseth recalled: "In fact, exactly one year ago today … [Maduro] stood on a stage not too far from here and yelled mockingly, ‘Come and get me!’" He added, "Well, we answered that call." Hegseth then claimed: "The reality is that … January 3 is the day that will go down in history as a day of liberation for Venezuela." The Guardian noted that Maduro's allies continued to hold power with US support.

Earlier in the day, Hegseth was welcomed at Miraflores Palace by Rodríguez and met senior cabinet members, including defense minister Gustavo González López and foreign minister Oliver Blanco. The Guardian said his palace remarks, broadcast by state broadcaster VTV, struck a more conciliatory tone as he vowed to deepen cooperation with Venezuela's new leaders. Hegseth told Rodríguez and the cabinet: "I stand here on behalf of President Trump, who sends his greeting and looks forward to the opportunities, not just militarily … but also economically: the incredible resource capabilities that your country has, that we look forward to unlocking together with you."

Hegseth said his visit to Rodríguez's "beautiful country" was designed to re-establish and increase "defense cooperation" between "two great militaries" that, less than a year earlier, had been briefly at war. The Guardian said the New York Times had reported earlier this month that Trump officials were trying to negotiate a long-term US troop presence in Venezuela as part of their push for regional hegemony. Hegseth told Rodríguez, "I appreciate your leadership and commitment to your country." The Guardian described Rodríguez as a once outspoken anti-imperialist leftist who had overseen an unlikely realignment with Washington since her ally was grabbed.

During her five-minute address, Rodríguez, whose oil-rich country The Guardian said was widely seen to have become a US protectorate after January's invasion, made no mention of Maduro, whom The Guardian reported was behind bars in New York City. Referring indirectly to his capture and the subsequent reconciliation with Washington, she told Hegseth: "Thanks to the political and geopolitical maturity of our governments, we have – in a very short period of time, just a few months – turned what were very difficult and very tough times for our people, into an extraordinary space for cooperation and mutual benefit."

Rodríguez hailed the "historic" energy deal her government recently struck with Trump. Trump has called it "the biggest oil deal in world history" and "the spoils" of his "conquest" of Venezuela. Hegseth claimed the agreement, which reportedly involves 65bn barrels of reserves, "makes the strait of Hormuz a non-factor for the western hemisphere".

Reyes suspected the visit also carried a message for Venezuela's armed forces. She wrote, "Domestically, the trip forces the military high command to legitimize the man who directed the extraction of their former commander in chief." She added, "In this regard, I wonder whether it might be a message of discipline intended to prevent any pockets of resistance in the barracks."

The Guardian reported that Trump's attempts to expand his Shield of the Americas anti-cartel coalition of Latin American governments suffered a major blow last month with the defeat of his "far-right ally," Flávio Bolsonaro, in Brazil's presidential election. The outlet said "the leftwing winner," Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, has indicated that his country will not join the initiative.

The Guardian assessed that Venezuela's interim government was in little position to resist. During Hegseth's surprise visit, he said Venezuela had gone from being "our most aggressive regional threat to [being] a compliant security partner." He praised Rodríguez for working "closely, pragmatically, and diligently with the United States to stabilize Venezuela." Hegseth said US and Venezuelan armed forces would work together to attack "narco-terrorists" operating on Venezuelan soil, vowing: "We will hunt you down. We will find you. And we will kill you."

Rodríguez closed her palace speech by thanking her foe-turned-friend: "Thank you, secretary, and I wish you a lifetime of happiness."
