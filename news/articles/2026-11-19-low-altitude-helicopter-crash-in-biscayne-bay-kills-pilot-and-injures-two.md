## Rotor blades hit water during a turn near a filming boat

A Robinson R44 II helicopter crashed in Biscayne Bay near Bill Baggs State Park off Key Biscayne on Nov. 4, the Miami-Dade Sheriff’s Office said. The sheriff’s office said the aircraft was on a trip to film a powerboating event offshore.

The National Transportation Safety Board said one person was killed and two others were critically injured. The Miami-Dade Sheriff’s Office said the pilot died and that a photographer and videographer on board were injured.

The National Transportation Safety Board said on Wednesday that the helicopter was making low-altitude turns “extremely” close to the surface of the water, according to its preliminary report. On one turn, the rotor blades hit the water and brought down the aircraft.

Some doors had been removed from the helicopter to facilitate photography and videography. The photographer told investigators the helicopter flew low and parallel to a boat’s right side, then turned and got ahead of it for a “good shot” as the boat passed underneath, according to the report.

The videographer then observed the rotor blades strike the water, according to the preliminary report.

Television news video of the crash showed the helicopter upside down underwater with two yellow floats attached to the bottom of its landing skids. The emergency floats are designed to help keep a helicopter afloat in a water landing.

Witnesses in nearby boats made distress calls, and a good Samaritan tried to help one of the victims, Sheriff Rosie Cordero-Stutz said.

Rescue workers from several local agencies, the U.S. Coast Guard and U.S. Customs and Border Protection responded to the crash.
