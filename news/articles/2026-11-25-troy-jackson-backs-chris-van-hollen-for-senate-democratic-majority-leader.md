## Jackson cited the filibuster, Medicare for All and minimum wage

Troy Jackson announced Wednesday that he will support Chris Van Hollen when Senate Democrats meet on Dec. 1 for a closed-door vote for majority leader. The Maryland senator is challenging Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer for the post.

Jackson’s decision changes his position after a Senate campaign in which he said he would not support the current minority leader. He said he had held back his endorsement of Van Hollen because he wanted “to make damn sure that my vote counts.”

“Mainers voted for change in November, and they ought to get it,” Jackson said. He said he had talked with Mainers about what they need from the Senate and believed it was time for a change in leadership.

When he spoke to Van Hollen, Jackson “told him Maine needs a seat at the table when the caucus decides what gets funded and what we’re going to fight for,” he added.

Jackson said he and Van Hollen agreed on working to end the filibuster, pass Medicare for All and increase the minimum wage.

“I want a leader who’ll bring those fights to the floor,” he added. Jackson described the leadership choice in terms of what the caucus would fund and which fights it would take up.

Several grassroots groups that turned out Democratic voters for the midterm elections have been pressuring independent Sen. Angus King to rescind his support for Schumer and back Van Hollen.

Nearly 2,000 people have signed a petition delivered to King’s office earlier this week, urging him to rescind his support for Schumer and back Van Hollen.


Jackson’s endorsement advances [MSI’s Nov. 23 report on incoming senators withholding backing in the Schumer-Van Hollen contest](/articles/2026-11-23-some-incoming-senators-withhold-backing-in-schumer-van-hollen-contest/): Jackson had withheld his support for Van Hollen during the campaign but has now committed to back him before the Dec. 1 caucus vote.
