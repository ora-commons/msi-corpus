## Court orders the 82-year-old to home detention at his Florida assisted living facility

Thomas Markle, the father of Meghan, Duchess of Sussex, appeared in court on Friday after being charged with one count of possessing indecent images of children, the US Department of Justice said. Markle, 82, was arrested on a federal complaint alleging he held images that show child sex abuse. No plea was taken during the hearing, and an arraignment date in California will be determined in due course, according to a joint statement from the Justice Department and the US Attorney's Office Central California.

The hearing lasted 30 minutes, and federal prosecutor Courtenay Derry laid out the charges against Markle, according to the BBC's US partner CBS News. Markle was brought into court in a wheelchair, escorted by two US Marshals, and wore black trousers and a black t-shirt. The court ordered him to submit to home detention at his assisted living facility in Florida and to electronic monitoring.

Markle was arrested in Florida after the FBI, acting on warrants, searched two Los Angeles-area storage units registered to him. He had rented the units since 2008 — almost 20 years — and made the last payment in October this year.

Inside the units, FBI agents seized about 78 items, including film negatives, slides and books that they allege contained child sex abuse material, the FBI said. Witnesses "positively identified" Markle in two of the images believed to contain the material. In those images, the man pictured "appears to be in the age range of 30s to 40s" — which suggests the alleged abuse dates back decades, as Markle is currently 82.

FBI Director Kash Patel said authorities "moved quickly" when arresting Markle. "Mr Markle allegedly targeted innocent children and sought to steal their innocence through his criminal actions," Patel said. He added that the FBI would "use every single resource at our disposal to protect innocent kids – any predator who targets children will have zero safe haven in this country or anywhere they try to hide".

Markle has previously denied allegations of possessing images of child sex abuse, calling them "sick lies".

The federal case is one of several legal threads involving Markle. Last month, police in the Philippines obtained a warrant to examine devices allegedly removed from his former home in the country. Four boxes of material — including computers, phones, hard drives and old video tapes — were handed to officers by Meghan's half brother, Thomas Markle Jr.

Markle moved to the Philippines last year after a series of strokes and lived in Cebu until May. He was then reportedly living in a Florida rehabilitation centre, learning to walk again after having his leg amputated.

In October, London's Metropolitan Police confirmed that it received a letter in 2019 making a "number of claims" relating to Markle. The force said the information in the letter, which related to allegations against him outside the UK, was passed on to the office of the Duke and Duchess of Sussex but was not shared with other law enforcement agencies. The Met has since voluntarily referred itself to the Independent Office for Police Conduct (IPOC) over its handling of the letter.

In a statement released in October, after the allegations against her father emerged, the Duchess of Sussex said: "I am deeply distressed at the news that has come to light in the last 24 hours, and my thoughts are with any children who may have been harmed."

The Duke and Duchess of Sussex have not commented on Markle's arrest. Meghan has been estranged from her father since 2018; the BBC understands she broke contact with him last year.
