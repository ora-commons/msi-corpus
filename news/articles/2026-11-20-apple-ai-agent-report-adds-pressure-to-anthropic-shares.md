## Analysts question Anthropic revenue against $900.9 billion commitments

Bloomberg first reported that Apple is developing a new autonomous AI agent. The consumer-focused product could offer fresh competition to Anthropic’s models, The Wall Street Journal said, adding to pressure on the stock as Anthropic came off its record-breaking IPO Thursday. Anthropic fell more than 6.5% in U.S. premarket trading to around $90.80 a share. The Wall Street Journal updated its report at 7:32 a.m. ET.

Analysts said Anthropic’s revenue growth would not be enough to significantly outpace its spending commitments. Anthropic’s IPO filing with the SEC lists spending commitments totaling $900.9 billion, around half of which are due over the next five years.

Seaport Research senior analyst Jay Goldberg wrote in a note, “We are concerned about Anthropic’s revenue growth.” He added, “To merit its valuation, it will need to see much higher growth rates over the next two years.”

Jefferies’ global head of equity strategy Christopher Wood said investors were “ditching AI stocks” over fears that the sector’s spending boom had passed its peak, “in spite of a lack of any compelling evidence as yet of any such development on the ground.” Wood said the fear was driven by concerns that Nvidia would step back from its role as financier-in-chief for the wider AI ecosystem. He added that investors worried Nvidia would pull its backing from chip-hungry cloud-computing startups.

Wood said, “This suggests a real risk that the most speculative part of the demand for compute will disappear which should lower compute prices for others.”

Broader investor concerns included high financing costs, excessive AI spending and potential U.S. legislation that could hurt the technology. “So-called Animal spirits are taking over the market,” XTB research director Kathleen Brooks said. The Wall Street Journal said those factors together threatened a pullback in the AI build-out that could lead to lower revenues for related companies.

Nasdaq futures were 1.3% lower as the index headed toward its worst one-week performance since President Trump’s April 2025 imposition of sweeping tariffs. Artificial-intelligence stocks fell globally as sentiment around the AI trade deteriorated further, despite Anthropic’s record-breaking IPO Thursday.

Chip makers and cloud-computing stocks sold off in U.S. premarket trading. Intel fell 4.5% and Micron Technology fell 5.2%. Advanced Micro Devices fell 6.2%, extending a sharp decline from the previous session after a short-seller report into the company was published Thursday. Caterpillar, a data-center power supplier, fell 2.5%. Meta Platforms and Microsoft, which had served as havens in previous AI wobbles, each fell more than 1%.

Asian stocks were hit hard Friday. South Korean memory-chip makers Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix fell 7% and 5.9%, respectively. In Japan, SoftBank Group, a major backer of OpenAI, fell 14.5%.

ASML, described by The Wall Street Journal as Europe’s most valuable company and a Dutch supplier of chip-making equipment, fell close to 6%. The decline put ASML on track to lose around 28 billion euros of market value ($30.71 billion). A gauge of Europe’s technology sector fell 3.6%.
