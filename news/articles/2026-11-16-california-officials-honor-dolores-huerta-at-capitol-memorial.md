## Rivas calls Huerta the mother of the Latino civil rights movement

California officials paid tribute to Dolores Huerta during an invitation-only memorial ceremony Monday in the state Capitol rotunda in Sacramento. Gov.-elect Xavier Becerra, Assembly Speaker Robert Rivas, Senate President pro tempore Monique Limón and former U.S. House Speaker Nancy Pelosi spoke about Huerta's work as a labor leader, civil rights advocate and feminist activist. Performances of “We Shall Overcome” and “Put a Little Love in Your Heart” took place before the ceremony opened to the public.

Huerta, 96, died of cancer at her home in Bakersfield, California, last month. Assembly Speaker Robert Rivas, a Democrat, called her a family friend and mentor. “She is the mother of our Latino civil rights movement,” Rivas said. “Her place in the history of our state and nation is enduring.”

Rivas said his grandfather, Servando Flores, moved from Mexico to California in the 1960s and went on to organize fellow farmworkers alongside Huerta.

“As we honor one of the towering figures of our time, we make her this solemn promise: The march goes on,” said Limón, who was serving as acting governor while Gov. Gavin Newsom was out of state.

Before the event began, a hearse adorned with flags reading “Sí se puede” carried Huerta's casket to the Capitol. Hundreds of people, including union leaders, politicians and members of the public, followed behind, many with marigolds in hand.

Huerta was born in New Mexico in 1930 and raised in California's farm-rich San Joaquin Valley. She became an elementary school teacher before turning her focus to the plight of farmworkers and teaming up with César Chavez to create the National Farm Workers Association in 1962, which became the United Farm Workers.

Huerta made famous the rallying cry “Sí, se puede!” — which translates to “Yes, it can be done!” Then-President Barack Obama borrowed her rallying cry for his campaign slogan, “Yes we can,” and awarded her the nation's highest civilian honor, the Presidential Medal of Freedom, in 2012.

Well into her 90s, Huerta was a familiar face at news conferences and rallies in Sacramento, where she advocated for the rights of workers, women, voters and others.

This year, Huerta said she had been sexually abused by Chavez, leading to the birth of two children. She kept the secret for 60 years.
