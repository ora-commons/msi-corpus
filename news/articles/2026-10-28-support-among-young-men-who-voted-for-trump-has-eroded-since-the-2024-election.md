## Analysts heavily favor House Democrats; retaking the Senate remains possible

Two years after the 2024 election, support among young men who voted for President Trump has eroded, according to The Wall Street Journal. Trump was ushered into a second term in office in part by winning over young men, a majority of whom supported him. The paper reported that the backing had weakened since then.

The Wall Street Journal's latest project revisits that group directly. Last year, the paper brought together men under 30 who had all voted for Trump and asked them to grade his first six months in office. Just before the 2026 midterm elections, it invited them back to provide their perspectives.

National political reporter Eliza Collins wrote the report. She described the video as the second in a Wall Street Journal series that brings together groups of voters from across the country to hear their perspectives. Another installment featured a group of Democratic voters debating the future of their party.

According to analysts, Democrats are heavily favored to win the House and have a chance at retaking the Senate. Republicans headed into the November elections facing low approval ratings for Trump among young men and other parts of his 2024 coalition.

The report places those reassessments within the broader struggle over Trump's 2024 coalition. The returning voters offer a focused view of the change: the project follows men who had already given Trump grades and asks how they assess him shortly before the midterms. The direction The Wall Street Journal reported was an erosion of support among the young men who backed him in 2024.

The timing puts the project just before the November elections. According to analysts, Democrats are heavily favored to win the House and have a chance to retake the Senate. Republicans face that test with low approval for Trump among young men and other parts of the coalition that helped return him to office.

For Republicans, the report identifies erosion among young men as one part of the broader approval challenge. For Democrats, the analysts' House and Senate assessments leave both chambers within reach, with the House heavily favored and the Senate more uncertain.
