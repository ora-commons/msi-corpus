## Ukraine support plans hinge on unresolved contributions and loan terms

BRUSSELS — European Union leaders failed to agree on the bloc's 2028-2034 budget Friday after more than 12 hours of negotiations, leaving a proposed €45 billion loan for Ukraine without a final decision. European Council President António Costa, a former Portuguese prime minister, told reporters, "We didn't reach an agreement." European Union leaders will try again Dec. 17-18, in three weeks, but Costa said a final agreement must be reached by the end of the year for the budget to take effect Jan. 1, 2028.

The talks failed to settle either the size of the spending package or how it would be used. Although the package is small compared with national budgets and amounts to around 1% to 1.2% of gross national income, the Associated Press assessed that it encapsulates the ambitions of the world's biggest trading bloc and how the bloc sees itself on the international stage. Costa said some member states asked for further reductions in how much they would have to pay, according to his latest proposals, while most were opposed to further cuts and some even proposed reversing some cuts.

The Associated Press described the session as a day of brinkmanship in which leaders played tough for national audiences. The summit included two plenary sessions, several group meetings and the corridor conversations typical of EU budget negotiations, and Costa will draw conclusions from the discussions and consult again with national capitals. Some leaders, notably from countries that pay more into the budget than they get back in allocations, skipped certain meetings. The Associated Press reported that Costa was unruffled by those absences. He said, "This is a negotiation process. It's normal that the member states try to defend their own national interests."

Despite the failure to reach agreement, Costa said the discussions had been useful. "For me, it was very useful to figure out the parameters of a possible landing zone in December," he said. He insisted the new budget must be in place by Jan. 1, 2028. "In order to do this, we need to reach a final agreement by the end of this year," he said.

National elections make the timetable more difficult. French President Emmanuel Macron said France's presidential election in April meant he was not willing to negotiate further in 2027. "I won't be politically and democratically in a position after December 31 to be able to relaunch a discussion on the budget and to conclude it," he said, adding that it would be "a period much too close to the presidential elections." The Associated Press identified Marine Le Pen, a hard-right figurehead, as the favorite to win. Spain holds elections this weekend, while Italy and Poland vote next year. The report said new hardline governments or shaky coalitions could further complicate negotiations.

Macron nevertheless said he was willing to sign up to "a good agreement" at the Dec. 17-18 summit. He warned that Europe would make "a strategic error" if it narrowed its ambitions and said, "France will not accept more cuts."

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said two days had been set aside for the next talks but that "it could be longer. I want to be home by Christmas." Hungarian Prime Minister Péter Magyar, a newcomer to EU budget negotiations, said he had seen "zero results" and that a deal by "Christmas looks unlikely."

If leaders could not bridge their differences, the EU would need to find money elsewhere for traditional policies, future ambitions and other obligations. Funds also are needed to pay down debt on the €850 billion ($932 billion) loan agreed after the last long-term budget negotiations to help Europe recover from its worst-ever recession, which was sparked by the COVID-19 pandemic.

Costa proposed an EU-wide tax on digital services, drawing revenue from online advertising and money companies make from user data, that could amount to €5 billion ($5.5 billion) a year. Irish Prime Minister Micheál Martin voiced "strong opposition" to the levy. "Europe is reducing competitiveness and we feel, in principle, a digital tax is sending out all the wrong messages," he said. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said that, inside the room, the tax had "gathered broad support, and I see good prospects for a solid package of new own resources."

The EU already raises its "own resources" through customs duties and value-added tax. The European Commission is exploring other sources through the carbon emissions trading system and a tobacco duty.

Recent budget proposals foresee a €100 billion ($110 billion) envelope for Ukraine over seven years. The leaders also must decide on a new €45 billion ($49.4 billion) loan to help meet Kyiv's economic and military needs next year. A major issue is whether interest paid by European countries on that loan should be deducted from the budget envelope or whether more money should be put in to cover it. Ukraine would reimburse the loan only once Russia pays war reparation costs.

A year ago, EU leaders endorsed a €90 billion ($99 billion) loan to cover most of Ukraine's needs for two years. Russia's intensified attacks on energy and civilian infrastructure, along with disruptions to Black Sea trade, have drained resources.

Costa said 24 of the 27 member countries support the new loan plan, while Bulgaria insists that its share should pay only for economic needs. Costa must find a way to bring the Czech Republic, Hungary and Slovakia on board by next month.

After the talks, the bloc still faces unresolved questions over member-state contributions, new revenue, the treatment of loan interest and the amount of assistance Ukraine can count on for next year. The Dec. 17-18 summit is the next scheduled decision point, but Costa said the final agreement must come by the end of the year.

The stalled talks leave that December objective unresolved and make a final agreement by year-end necessary for the budget to take effect Jan. 1, 2028.
