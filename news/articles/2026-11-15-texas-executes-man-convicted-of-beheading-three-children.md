## Oklahoma puts to death James Pavatt as US executions reach 37

John Allen Rubio, 46, was pronounced dead at 6.47 pm on Thursday after a lethal injection at the state prison in Huntsville, Texas. He was convicted of fatally stabbing and beheading his three young children in a squalid, windowless apartment in Brownsville, on the US border with Mexico, in 2003.

The killings so rocked Brownsville that the apartment building was later demolished and the land turned into a park and community garden.

After a spiritual adviser prayed over him briefly, Rubio thanked friends and family for their "love and understanding" and said he was "not the same person I once was". He thanked prison staff for teaching him how to find God and also expressed love for Angela Camacho, his common-law wife, who is serving life in prison for her role in the slayings.

"I want to say I'm sorry to my wife's family," Rubio said. "I can't change the past but I can tell you my life is going to be over. But that's not going to make you happy." He added: "I'm going to be with my kids in a little bit. I'm ready to go to heaven to be with my lord and savior."

As the lethal dose of the sedative pentobarbital began taking effect, Rubio took three breaths and began what sounded like snoring. All movement ceased afterward, and a physician pronounced him dead 21 minutes after the injection began.

Rubio and Camacho were accused of killing three-year-old Julissa Quesada, 14-month-old John E Rubio and two-month-old Mary Jane Rubio in the apartment they shared in Brownsville. Camacho received a life sentence after pleading guilty. According to court records, Rubio was not the biological father of the two older children but raised them as his own.

An appeals court overturned Rubio's first capital murder conviction, ruling that Camacho's statements had been improperly admitted as evidence at his trial. At his second trial, Rubio again pleaded not guilty by reason of insanity but was again convicted and sentenced to death.

At various points, Rubio claimed the children were possessed and that he was the "chosen one" intended to save the world. Defense experts diagnosed him with paranoid schizophrenia, a finding prosecution experts rejected. The defense experts also testified that his childhood — marked by violence at home, "toxic" parents, drug use and prostitution — damaged him developmentally.

Hours before Rubio's execution, 73-year-old James Pavatt received a lethal injection at an Oklahoma prison for the fatal shooting of his girlfriend's estranged husband in 2001. Pavatt was convicted of first-degree murder and conspiracy to commit murder in the killing. The victim, Rob Andrew, a 39-year-old advertising executive, was killed as he arrived to pick up his two young children before Thanksgiving.

Pavatt attended the same church as the Andrew family and had been having an affair with Rob Andrew's wife, Brenda Andrew. She was convicted at a separate trial and is the only woman currently on Oklahoma's death row.

Weeks before the shooting, Rob Andrew told police someone was trying to kill him after the brake lines on his car were cut. Brenda Andrew told police after her husband was killed that masked intruders shot her in the arm and fatally shot him. Pavatt and Brenda Andrew fled to Mexico with her two children after the shooting and were arrested three months later while re-entering the US.

When Pavatt was asked on Thursday if he had any last words before his execution, he shook his head no. As a chaplain read from the Bible, Pavatt appeared to mouth some words that could not be heard.

Thursday's executions were the 36th and 37th this year, with the US on track to surpass 40 executions for a second consecutive year — a pace driven by Florida. The death penalty has drawn renewed attention since the failed execution of Christa Pike in Tennessee in September.

On 5 November, California's governor, Gavin Newsom, announced he would commute hundreds of death sentences before leaving office in January — what experts say would be the largest mass clemency in American history.

Also on Thursday, former Oklahoma death row inmate Richard Glossip pleaded guilty to a lesser charge of being an accessory in a 1997 murder and awaits sentencing in December. Glossip, who had been on the brink of execution three times, was released on bond in May after nearly 30 years in prison. He had been awaiting a retrial after the US supreme court overturned his conviction.
