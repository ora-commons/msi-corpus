## Meza Flores was first indicted by the U.S. more than 14 years ago

Mexican authorities captured Fausto Isidro Meza Flores in an operation that ran Friday night into Saturday, aided by intelligence from the Trump administration. The 44-year-old, known widely as “El Chapo Isidro,” had been one of the FBI’s ten most wanted fugitives, with a $5 million reward out for information leading to his arrest.

His rise in the Mexican drug trade ran for more than two decades. Authorities in the United States and Mexico have said Meza Flores was once a small-time drug trafficker and scrap metal dealer who grew in power and influence while evading capture by either country for years.

The FBI and Mexico’s government say he led the violent Beltrán Leyva organization, which the Treasury Department said split from the Sinaloa Cartel in 2008. The split caused a violent power struggle in the region, similar to the fighting between cartel factions seen in Sinaloa in recent years.

He later headed his own group, the Meza-Flores Transnational Criminal Organization — also known as Los Mazatlecos — for over 20 years. Beyond drug trafficking, the FBI says the group is accused of kidnapping, extortion, torture and murder. The State Department said the organization was responsible for numerous deaths of Mexican police and military forces and of other criminals operating in Mexico.

“The Meza-Flores TCO has been responsible for smuggling multiton quantities of heroin, methamphetamine, cocaine, and marijuana into the United States,” the State Department said in a 2025 statement announcing a reward of up to $5 million for information leading to his arrest.

The apprehension came more than 14 years after Meza Flores was first indicted by the United States. A federal superseding indictment and federal arrest warrant were issued for him seven years later in the United States District Court for the District of Columbia, after he was charged with multiple drug-trafficking violations and use and possession of a firearm, according to the FBI.

Capturing him proved difficult. His organization controls key drug transportation routes and drug manufacturing laboratories, and Meza Flores himself kept a low profile. Known by the nickname “Chapito Isidro,” he is relatively small of stature, standing 5 feet 6 inches tall and weighing about 160 pounds.

Meza Flores is allied with a faction of the Sinaloa Cartel known as “Los Mayos,” currently warring for control of the Mexican state. The Sinaloa Cartel was designated as a foreign terrorist organization by the Trump administration last year.

His organization forms part of a patchwork of warring criminal groups across Mexico that have terrorized civilians, alongside the police and military casualties the State Department attributes to his network. The FBI had listed him among the ten fugitives it most wanted taken off the street; Mexican authorities took him into custody in the operation that began Friday night.
