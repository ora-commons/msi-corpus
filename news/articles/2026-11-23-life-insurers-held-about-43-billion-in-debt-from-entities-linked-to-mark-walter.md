## Four of five Walter-linked firms are part of the federal loan investigation

In an analysis reported by Shane Shifflett on Nov. 23, 2026, The Wall Street Journal found that EquiTrust, Heritage Life and Group 1001 insurers held about $43 billion in debt issued by entities linked to Mark Walter. Most of those entities were set up by five firms with ties to Walter’s business empire. The Journal also examined private debt instruments held by no other U.S. life insurers.

The Journal began by compiling a list of affiliated-party instruments held by Group 1001 insurers. It drew the list from short-term loans on Delaware Life’s 2025 Schedule DA and from any related party appearing on the company’s Schedule Y through the second quarter of 2026.

The Journal also searched for instruments flagged as private placements, based on special characters appearing in their CUSIPs, that were held only by insurers that were part of Amistad and Group 1001. The review included instruments in which one of those insurance companies said the issuer was a related party in regulatory filings between 2022 and 2025.

To determine the value of EquiTrust’s debt investments as of the end of 2025, the Journal analyzed filings submitted to the National Association of Insurance Commissioners, corporate registration documents and public records. It matched the names of the affiliated entities to instruments listed on life insurers’ bond and loan holding schedules in annual and quarterly financial statements. That allowed the Journal to connect the affiliated names to the debt held by insurers.

The Journal requested hundreds of corporate registration documents from Delaware and examined who signed them. Public-record searches identified the people who signed for Global Securitization Services, ABS Capital, Hudson Trading, Bradford Allen, Amistad and Mark Walter’s TWG Global and family.

The Journal then matched the companies those people created or managed to the debt instruments held by life insurers. That step linked the corporate signers and managers to the instruments in insurers’ holdings.

Taken together, the filings, registration records and company links formed the basis for the Journal’s conclusion that Walter’s total borrowing was likely much higher than the currently reported amount under scrutiny by investigators. Four of the five firms with ties to Walter’s business empire are part of a federal investigation into the loans.
