## Pacific Archer attack leaves civilian crew unharmed amid falling oil flows

U.S. Central Command said Friday that a U.S. military aircraft had fired “precision munitions” at the stern of the Palau-flagged merchant ship M/V Pacific Archer in the Gulf of Oman. The command said in a statement that “the ship’s propulsion was ultimately disabled and the civilian crew was unharmed.” The command said the ship was trying to break the U.S. blockade of Iranian ports.

According to U.S. Central Command, the Pacific Archer left the Iranian port of Bandar Abbas before it attempted to break the blockade. Ship-tracking websites listed Singapore as the vessel’s destination.

The U.S. blockade of Iranian ports began in April, was lifted during the summer in talks with Tehran and resumed in July. Since the resumption, the U.S. military says it has redirected 158 merchant ships and disabled vessels that refused to comply.

The Pacific Archer was at least the 15th vessel publicly disclosed as disabled since the blockade began in the spring and the sixth since it resumed this summer. The previous vessel was disabled on Nov. 7.

U.S. Central Command has consistently said American forces issue multiple warnings before firing on merchant ships. The Associated Press report said a June strike on a Guinea-Bissau-flagged tanker attempting to evade the blockade drew condemnation after three deaths. It also said Indian officials reported that three Indian sailors were killed when American forces struck the Palau-flagged M/T Settebello, and that the leader of the International Maritime Organization, a United Nations agency, condemned the attack.

The action comes as the Trump administration weighs its options in the war with Iran, which began at the end of February and has settled into what The Associated Press described as a quiet stalemate in the past several months.

Earlier in November, officials in the region told The Associated Press that Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu was pushing the United States to restart the war or allow Israel to launch airstrikes on Iran’s nuclear facilities and missile capabilities. Government and security officials from Persian Gulf countries, as well as Pakistan and Turkey, were engaged in a diplomatic campaign that included phone calls with senior U.S. officials to stave off a renewed regional war.

President Donald Trump has alternated between threatening additional large-scale strikes and insisting the war “militarily is effectively over.” The U.S. military maintains a sizable naval force in the region, including a constant presence of two aircraft carriers and a host of other ships.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guard said Friday that two tankers had tried to transit the Strait of Hormuz through an “unauthorized” route, claiming one struck a naval mine while the other was forced to turn back. The Revolutionary Guard’s account could not be independently verified. In a statement carried by Iran’s state broadcaster, the organization also said the number of oil tankers attempting unauthorized routes had sharply decreased in recent days. Iran continues to warn ships not to pass through the strategic shipping lane without approval from Tehran.

Oil flows in the first two weeks of November fell by around 20% to around 13 million barrels per day, according to Rystad Energy. October exports roughly equaled the prewar level of 15 million to 16 million barrels per day, Rystad Energy said. Reasons for the November decline included Iranian attacks that discouraged tankers from attempting the Strait of Hormuz crossing and reduced flows through a key Saudi pipeline to the Red Sea port of Yanbu, which has also been attacked several times.

A report by U.N. Trade and Development said disruptions in the Strait of Hormuz, the Red Sea, the Black Sea and the Panama Canal were lengthening voyages, raising fuel costs, driving port congestion and adding uncertainty. Alternative ports, inland transport and logistics arrangements kept trade moving, but at a higher cost.
