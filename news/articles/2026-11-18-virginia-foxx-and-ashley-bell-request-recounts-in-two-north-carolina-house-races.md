## Unofficial results point to 11 Democrats and three Republicans in Congress

State Republican leaders said they expect to regain the seven U.S. House seats Democrats appeared to flip in 2026, a result that would oust six incumbents. If the unofficial results hold, North Carolina will send 11 Democrats and three Republicans to Washington in January, but Republican U.S. Rep. Virginia Foxx and Democratic challenger Ashley Bell have requested recounts in close races. Foxx trails Democrat Chuck Hubbard by 331 votes in the 5th District, and Republican Pat Harrigan leads Bell by 2,510 votes in the 10th.

Foxx has represented North Carolina’s 5th Congressional District since 2005 and chairs the House Rules Committee, a position of seniority and influence. The incumbent requested a recount Tuesday. She trails Hubbard by 331 votes after provisional, absentee and overseas ballots were counted, according to unofficial results from the State Board of Elections, a margin of 0.1% out of more than 286,000 votes cast. Hubbard told NC Newsline, “We certainly are willing to go through the process as laid out by law, and that won’t really change anything.”

On election night, Foxx and Hubbard alternated taking the lead as votes trickled in across the district. A Greensboro precinct took longer to publish its results, and Hubbard, a former journalist and publisher, said he wondered why. “Turns out it was N.C. A&T and UNCG students who were basically wrapped around the building trying to cast their votes,” he said. Hubbard and his wife decided to leave their watch party and go to bed, and the final precinct’s results arrived while they were in the parking lot. “The place where we were having the watch party just exploded,” Hubbard said. “People came running out the door and said, ‘It’s over. You’re up by 99 votes.’” A photograph from freshman orientation in Washington on Nov. 8, 2026, showed Reps.-elect Chuck Hubbard, Jamie Ager and Richard Ojeda.

The next day, Foxx said the counting process wasn’t over. She claimed overseas and provisional ballots could play in her favor. But Hubbard’s lead increased after those votes were counted, widening his election-night edge from 99 votes to 331.

In the 10th District, freshman Republican U.S. Rep. Pat Harrigan leads Democratic challenger Ashley Bell by 2,510 votes, or 0.85 points, according to unofficial results. Bell, a physician associate, requested a recount Friday.

Under North Carolina law, candidates can request a recount if the difference between the top candidates is less than 1% of total votes cast. Adam Newmark, a political science professor at Appalachian State University, said, “The first thing to do would be to see if the machines retabulating the votes produce the same or very close to the same results.” He said, “That might produce a couple votes difference here and there. Maybe two ballots got stuck together or something, little things like that.” Newmark said a recount was unlikely to change a margin of a few thousand votes, or even Foxx’s deficit of a few hundred votes.

The 5th District covers northwestern North Carolina, stretching from Caldwell and Watauga counties in the west to Rockingham and Guilford counties in the east. Over her two decades of U.S. House elections, Foxx typically received about 55% to 65% of the vote.

Republican lawmakers redrew North Carolina’s congressional districts in 2023 to elect more Republicans by splitting up or “cracking” Democratic-held seats in the Triad and Triangle. Under the new map, North Carolina sent 10 Republicans and four Democrats to Washington in 2024. Foxx held on that year, when President Donald Trump’s name was on the ballot to draw Republican voters, but 2026 appeared to be different.

Newmark said the 2023 redistricting made Foxx more vulnerable because Republicans moved some Republican voters out of the 5th District to help capture another district. “The only way Republicans could grab another district was to take away some of those Republican voters, and in doing so, you made Virginia Foxx a lot more vulnerable,” he said. “If you gerrymander too much, you’ve created a thinner advantage, and in a lopsided Democratic victory, that was too much for her to retain the majority in that district.”

Following Trump’s call, Republican lawmakers drew another map in 2025, targeting Democratic U.S. Rep. Don Davis in the 1st District, which spans eastern North Carolina, by adding more Republican voters from the neighboring 3rd District. The change left Republican incumbent Greg Murphy slightly more vulnerable. The move backfired in what is often called a “dummymander”: Davis won reelection, while Murphy was ousted. Murphy initially held a slim lead but conceded after provisional and absentee ballots placed Democratic challenger and former state lawmaker Raymond Smith ahead.

North Carolina Senate Democratic Leader Sydney Batch of Wake County said, “Be careful what you wish for, because you might just get it.” She added, “They wanted an 11-3 map. We got exactly that. It just didn’t go in their favor.” Batch’s caucus picked up four seats on Nov. 3. She said she wanted to focus on issues rather than politics, particularly affordability, as Democrats work to retain the seats they gained. “If we continue to keep an eye on affordability and also on the voters, and we pass legislation that is bipartisan and helps the many rather than the few, then I’m not worried about our chances in 2028,” she said.

News From The States reported that Democrats benefited this year from Trump’s unpopularity and voters’ concerns about rising costs, and now face the challenge of holding gerrymandered seats if those conditions do not repeat.

Foxx told supporters in a fundraising email that she planned to file for the 2028 election. “Regardless of the outcome of this race, I’m not going away,” Foxx said. “It’s going to be the toughest fight of my life.”

Former North Carolina House Speaker Tim Moore led the General Assembly’s redistricting efforts in 2023, drew the 14th District for himself, won it in 2024 and lost this year to Democrat LaKesha Womack. Politico reported that Moore intends to make another congressional bid in 2028.

Republican North Carolina House Speaker Destin Hall of Caldwell County said he does not expect the legislature to take up redistricting again. “The reality is with the wave election that we just saw, the maps are almost beside the point,” Hall told reporters Tuesday. “I’m confident that my party will be able to get back all those congressional seats they lost this time around in the upcoming election.”
