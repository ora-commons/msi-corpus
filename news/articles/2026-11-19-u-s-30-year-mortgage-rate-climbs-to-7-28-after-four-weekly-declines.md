## Pending home sales fall 16.7% from a year earlier

Freddie Mac reported Thursday that the average 30-year fixed-rate mortgage rose to 7.28% this week from 7.11% last week, the first weekly increase since early October. The average 15-year fixed rate, often sought by borrowers refinancing an existing loan, climbed to 6.66% from 6.45%.

The move ends a run of four weekly declines. The 30-year rate had last risen on Oct. 8, when it reached 7.40%, its highest level in nearly three years. FRED's raw weekly [MORTGAGE30US series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MORTGAGE30US) records 7.28% for Nov. 19.

A year ago, the 30-year average stood at 6.26% and the 15-year rate at 5.54%, leaving both benchmarks well above their year-earlier levels. Higher mortgage rates can add hundreds of dollars a month to a payment and reduce buyers' purchasing power, and affordability challenges have continued to sideline many aspiring homeowners.

Going by Freddie Mac's latest average rate, the Associated Press reported that a home shopper financing a $400,000 home loan is now paying roughly $343 more a month than when the 30-year rate was just below 6%. FRED's raw [median sales price series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/MSPUS) placed the median sales price of houses sold at $393,300 on Nov. 19, 2026, close to the loan amount in that comparison.

Mortgage rates generally follow the 10-year Treasury yield, which lenders use as a guide to pricing home loans, and they also reflect inflation, Federal Reserve policy and bond-market expectations for the economy, among other factors. The Associated Press reported the 10-year yield at 5.05% at midday Thursday. FRED's raw daily [DGS10 series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS10), a reading for the same date, lists 5.1%.

That yield had mostly been trending lower since early October, after reaching its highest level since 2002, before turning higher again following new data showing hotter inflation. The reversal in long-term borrowing costs moved with it.

The signs of accelerating inflation have raised the odds that the Federal Reserve will raise its key interest rate at its next meeting in December. The central bank kept rates unchanged late last month after raising them in September. The Fed does not set mortgage rates, but bond investors watch its decisions closely, and those expectations can ultimately influence rates on home loans.

Anthony Smith, a senior economist at Realtor.com, described the changed rate outlook. "The debate has shifted from whether the Fed hikes in December to how aggressively it moves," Smith said. "That is a tough backdrop for mortgage rates heading into what is already a seasonally slow stretch for the housing market."

The broader climb in borrowing costs predates this week's increase. The Associated Press linked the rise in mortgage rates since late February to the start of the U.S. war with Iran, which pushed oil prices higher and added to inflation. In late February, the average 30-year rate briefly dipped to 5.98%, its lowest level going back to late 2022, before beginning the advance that has carried it to 7.28%.

Elevated home-loan borrowing costs have weighed on home sales this year and deepened a national housing slump that dates to 2022, when mortgage rates began climbing from pandemic-era lows. Sales of previously occupied U.S. homes were essentially flat last year, stuck at a 30-year low, and declined last month to their slowest annual pace in more than 16 years as mortgage rates surged.

Pending home sales point to potentially more sluggish activity in the months ahead. The National Association of Realtors reported Wednesday that pending U.S. home sales fell 4.7% last month from September and sank 16.7% from October a year earlier. Contracts usually take a month or two to finalize, which makes the pending-sales tally a near-term bellwether for the housing market.
