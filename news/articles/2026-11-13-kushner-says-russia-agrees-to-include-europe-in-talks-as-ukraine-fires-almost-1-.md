## Zelenskyy says Starshield speed limits cap Ukrainian drones at 161 kph

Ukraine fired almost 1,200 drones at Russian targets overnight in one of its biggest aerial attacks since Moscow's invasion nearly five years ago. Russia's Defense Ministry said air defenses downed 1,198 Ukrainian long-range drones between late Thursday and early Friday. Strikes on three Russian regions killed seven people, including a 12-year-old, and wounded 17, local officials said.

The massive Ukrainian barrage appeared designed to show that Kyiv is holding its own in the war, countering Moscow's insistence that a Russian victory is inevitable.

Meanwhile, U.S. and Russian negotiators broadened the talks to stop the fighting by including for discussion wider European security arrangements that have been in place since the end of the Cold War. That change, long sought by Moscow, has alarmed European leaders and, the AP reported, placed a question mark over NATO's future role.

"Russia wants to talk about questions of European security architecture. We don't need to talk about that. There is one," German Chancellor Friedrich Merz said, referring to the NATO military alliance.

After two days of talks with Russian and Ukrainian delegations in Abu Dhabi this week, U.S. envoys went in person to brief Russian President Vladimir Putin in the Kremlin and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy, who was meeting with European leaders in Dublin, Ireland. Further talks are planned as negotiators seek a breakthrough in peace efforts, according to U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, who is also President Donald Trump's son-in-law.

Kushner said Russia has agreed to include Europe in upcoming talks, after Moscow's long insistence that the continent's governments should be excluded because they were part of the problem by supporting Ukraine. Europe's presence at the negotiating table will allow negotiators "to address the broader postwar European security arrangements alongside the territorial issues between Ukraine and Russia," Kushner said on X.

As European leaders scrambled to find an envoy to the talks, they were also wary of Russian maneuvering as well as Trump's praise for Putin and his administration's sharp criticism of Europe.

Kaja Kallas, the EU's top diplomat, said Friday that Europe must insist on a ceasefire before taking the negotiations further — the opposite of what Putin demands. "If we start to talk about what Russia is asking regarding the security architecture, we actually walk into the traps that Russia is setting us," she said in Dublin.

Luxembourg Prime Minister Luc Frieden insisted that Europe must take its fate into its own hands. "We cannot let neither the Americans nor the Russians decide about our future," Frieden said.

Moscow is aiming "to dismantle NATO and the post-Cold War European security architecture," the Institute for the Study of War said in an assessment late Thursday. Kremlin spokesperson Dmitry Peskov reaffirmed that Russia is open for contacts with the Europeans. "Once we understand (Europe's) true intentions and their readiness to facilitate the peace process, then it will be possible to make a decision on holding a dialogue regarding a settlement in Ukraine in an expanded format," Peskov said.

Zelenskyy on Friday repeated his complaints about drone speed limits on the U.S. Starshield system — SpaceX's military-grade version of the Starlink satellite internet service — that Ukraine needs for its defense. The speed restrictions are limiting Ukrainian drones to 161 kph (100 mph) on the entire front line and in Russian-occupied territory, he said, making it impossible for Ukraine to deploy its interceptors effectively against Russia's fast-moving jet-powered drones.

"We still have no clear explanations," Zelenskyy said of the unannounced restrictions, after first raising the issue on Thursday. Ukrainian officials are asking the U.S. Department of State, the Pentagon and SpaceX to clarify and resolve the situation, he said.

Last month, similar SpaceX limits on Ukrainian drone speeds severely disrupted operations, alarming Kyiv officials who fear a drop in U.S. support. Elon Musk acknowledged in October that his company SpaceX had throttled the Starlink internet service in Ukraine for about a week, saying the system couldn't be used for offensive strikes against Russia.

On the battlefield, Kyiv's forces are using drones and ground robots to help defend against Russia's army along the about 1,200-kilometer (750-mile) front line snaking down eastern and southeastern Ukraine. With its troops largely bogged down on the battlefield, Moscow has used strategic bombardment of civilian areas to hurt Ukraine, the AP reported.

Russia launched 117 drones against Ukraine overnight, killing at least two people, including a 17-year-old, authorities said. A single Russian attack on southern Zaporizhzhia on Friday hit two five-story apartment blocks and wounded 25 people, including six children, officials said.

The war has killed hundreds of thousands of soldiers and thousands of civilians, analysts and humanitarian organizations say.

Russian attacks against Ukraine's energy infrastructure caused widespread power outages in 12 Ukrainian regions on Friday. Those outages covered about half of the country's regions, First Deputy Minister of Energy Artem Nekrasov said. Moscow has battered Ukraine's power grid each winter since the war began, hoping to weaken public morale, the AP reported.
