## Undercover agent exposed scheme before an attack on Pannun

Nikhil Gupta received nearly 20 years in prison Friday for his role in a foiled murder-for-hire scheme targeting New York-based lawyer Gurpatwant Singh Pannun, a Sikh separatist activist and critic of India. Gupta, an Indian national, pleaded guilty in February to charges including murder-for-hire and conspiracy to commit murder-for-hire.

In court, Pannun said, “By the Guru’s blessings, I am alive today.” He added, “I thank U.S. law enforcement for foiling the plot and saving my life.”

## How an undercover agent exposed the scheme

U.S. prosecutors said Gupta, who was operating from India, unwittingly hired an undercover drug enforcement agent as his hit man and organized for $15,000 to be delivered in cash to the agent as an advance. The arrangement exposed the scheme before an attack on Pannun.

Gupta was later held in the Czech Republic and extradited to the United States.

## Alleged orchestration and evidence cited by prosecutors

U.S. prosecutors said the scheme was orchestrated by Vikash Yadav, an Indian intelligence agent who was working at the time out of India's cabinet secretariat. The secretariat reports to Prime Minister Narendra Modi and houses India's foreign intelligence agency.

The case links the foiled murder-for-hire plot to the 2023 killing of Hardeep Singh Nijjar, a Sikh activist in Canada, days ahead of Modi's state visit to the United States. The conspiracy against Pannun began to unfold in the weeks before Nijjar was gunned down on June 18, 2023, in the parking lot of the Sikh temple he led near Vancouver.

According to communications between Gupta and the undercover drug agent, Yadav initially urged Gupta not to target Pannun during Modi's state visit, which started June 20. Following Nijjar's killing, Yadav shared with Gupta a photo of Nijjar slumped in his car and told Gupta there was “now no need to wait,” according to the indictment against Gupta.

Pannun and Nijjar were friends and fellow organizers of an unofficial referendum movement advocating a separate Sikh homeland called Khalistan. Both men were designated terrorists by the Modi government in 2020.

## Government responses and the Indian inquiry

The Biden administration raised the plot against Pannun privately with the Modi government numerous times, warning of consequences for the U.S.-India relationship. The Indian government said in November 2023 that it had established a high-level inquiry as U.S. prosecutors unsealed an indictment of Gupta.

Before the U.S. allegations came to light, Canada publicly aired concerns that agents of India were linked to the Nijjar killing. India asked Canada to withdraw more than 40 diplomats from the country.

Indian officials traveled to the United States in October 2024 to brief the U.S. on the Indian inquiry's findings, shortly before the United States unsealed charges against Yadav. Last year, India's home ministry said the committee looking into information provided by the United States had recommended legal action against an individual, but did not name Yadav.

Yadav's whereabouts are unknown, and he could not be reached for comment.

## Sikh advocacy and security context

Pannun and Nijjar were among the many Sikhs who fled India for the United States and Canada as Indian authorities pursued the movement seeking a Sikh homeland to be carved out of India. Rights groups say police used extrajudicial killings, disappearances and torture to subdue the movement, which was at its height in the 1980s and early 1990s.

Pannun has said he and other Sikhs living outside India have been targeted by the Modi government for peaceful activism. He has vowed to continue his advocacy for a Sikh homeland.
