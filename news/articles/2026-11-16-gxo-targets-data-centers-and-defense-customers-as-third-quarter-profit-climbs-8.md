## GXO says quarterly organic revenue grew at fastest rate in three years

GXO Logistics grew into one of the world's largest third-party logistics companies by handling warehousing for consumer brands such as Hasbro, Apple and Nike. The contract-logistics provider is now turning to data centers, aerospace companies and defense manufacturers to speed up growth.

"Those are significantly more complex supply chains," Kelleher said. "They come with higher risk and reward, and therefore command higher margins" than industries such as e-commerce and consumer products.

Kelleher said he plans to increase the company's margin by targeting customers in sectors such as aerospace, defense and technology. GXO has signed more data-center operators and suppliers of the components needed to run the high-tech buildings.

GXO also offers maintenance and repair services for data centers, which Kelleher said is a more reliable income stream amid questions about whether the rapid build-out will slow down.

"We certainly participate in the front-end build of data centers, but our long-term revenue stream is not dependent on the build," he said. "Should the industry reach a peak in terms of the build-out of data centers and the services in those areas slow, that's fine. We're really focused on the maintenance, sustainment side of it."

GXO's profit climbed 8% year over year to $65 million in the third quarter. The company's organic revenue, which accounts for foreign-exchange rates, rose 6.4% in the quarter, which GXO said was the fastest rate of expansion in three years.

The company is the third-largest global warehousing-services provider by square footage, behind Amazon.com and DHL Supply Chain, with 221 million square feet of warehouse space, according to research group Armstrong & Associates.

Kelleher, who joined GXO last year from DHL Supply Chain, is looking to accelerate growth at the company after several years of postpandemic whiplash in the warehousing business. He also aims to expand GXO's business in North America and Asia and to rein in the company's labor costs, which make up two-thirds of its expenses.

GXO, which spun off from logistics company XPO in August 2021, bought U.K.-based rival Wincanton in 2024 for about $965 million. The deal extended its reach in the U.K. and Ireland and added to its capabilities in the aerospace, industrial and healthcare sectors. In the third quarter, GXO earned about half its revenue in the U.K. compared with roughly a quarter in the U.S.

The company is working to reduce labor costs by using more technology and automation in its buildings. GXO said it would roll out a new labor-management system in some locations over the next year that will measure the productivity of each employee with the goal of reducing downtime and bottlenecks in its warehouses.

GXO has been testing humanoid robots in its buildings and is working with robotics companies Apptronik and Agility Robotics on pilot programs. On Thursday, GXO announced a deal to use up to 1,000 Apptronik humanoids across its buildings over the next four years, depending on the results of a trial program.

Those robots are so far largely tasked with picking and packing e-commerce orders. Kelleher said the technology won't be replacing human workers in its warehouses anytime soon.

"I see humanoids complementing the work that humans are doing," Kelleher said. "Our associates are going to be performing work that requires much higher degrees of dexterity and especially higher degrees of intellectual discernment."

Citi analyst Ari Rosa said GXO's shift toward serving customers with more complicated supply chains aligns with a broader industry trend.

"There was a long time that in the supply chain and logistics world, everyone was very excited about e-commerce growth," Rosa said. "Now, obviously, everyone's very excited about the data-center build-out."
