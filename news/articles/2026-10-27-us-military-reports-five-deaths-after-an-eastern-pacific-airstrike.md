## Media tallies put combined deaths above 240 during Trump's second presidency

The US military said on Tuesday that it carried out an airstrike on an alleged drug-trafficking vessel in the eastern Pacific and that the attack killed five people. A screenshot from a video published with the report showed the vessel in the Pacific on October 27, credited to US Southern Command via X.

The October 27 report described Tuesday's strike as the latest attack in the region as of publication.

According to media tallies, Tuesday's strike was among more than 70 such attacks since the beginning of Donald Trump's second presidency. Collectively, those attacks killed more than 240 people.

The Trump administration has maintained that the people aboard and boats targeted by the strikes were involved in trafficking. That assertion is part of the administration's public rationale for the attacks.

Trump has said the United States is locked in armed conflict with Latin American cartels. He has used that position to justify the strikes as necessary to stem the flow of drugs into the United States.

The Trump administration has not provided definitive evidence to support its contentions that the targeted people and boats were engaged in drug trafficking. Tuesday's military announcement provided a casualty figure but did not resolve that separate factual allegation.

Legal experts and rights groups have argued that the strikes could amount to extrajudicial killings. Their concern centers on the status of the people aboard: the groups said the operations apparently targeted civilians who did not pose an immediate threat to the United States.

The distinction matters because a casualty count does not establish either the target's alleged conduct or the lawfulness of the strike. Tuesday's statement gives, on the military's account, a death toll of five; it does not answer whether the vessel was engaged in trafficking or whether striking people who did not pose an immediate threat was lawful.

The initial report did not provide further operational details. It recorded the military's account of five deaths, the administration's trafficking allegation without definitive evidence and legal objections based on the apparent targeting of civilians.
