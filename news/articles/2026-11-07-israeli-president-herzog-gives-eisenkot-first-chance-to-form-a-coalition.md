## Netanyahu's video statement called for a national unity government

Israeli President Isaac Herzog said Saturday night that he is giving opposition candidate Gadi Eisenkot the first opportunity to form a governing coalition following Israel's Oct. 27 election. The decision hands the task to the former military chief who ran as the main challenger to Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.

Eisenkot led a bloc of parties seeking to oust Netanyahu, Israel's longest-serving premier. Neither Eisenkot nor Netanyahu won a clear majority in the vote.

In marathon discussions with Herzog this week, representatives from most of Israel's main parties recommended that Eisenkot be tasked with assembling a new government. Herzog's decision followed that recommendation.

Herzog's decision gave Eisenkot procedural priority to form a coalition.

The announcement had been expected on Sunday. It came shortly after Netanyahu called for a "national unity government" in a video statement and warned against "hastily swearing in a government with terrorist supporters" — an apparent reference to the Arab-led parties.

Yashar criticized Netanyahu's video in a statement posted on X. The statement urged members of Netanyahu's Likud party to disavow their alliance with "radical parties" — an apparent reference to the more extreme parties in Netanyahu's religious-nationalist base.

The statement argued that Eisenkot would include only parties and parliament members who support its principles, including recognizing Hamas as a terrorist organization.

The tasking leaves Eisenkot with the first opportunity to form a government following the recommendation, while Netanyahu has called for a national unity government. The competing positions center on the Arab-led parties: Netanyahu and his allies have repeatedly portrayed them as a national security threat, while Eisenkot has indicated a conditional willingness to partner with them.
