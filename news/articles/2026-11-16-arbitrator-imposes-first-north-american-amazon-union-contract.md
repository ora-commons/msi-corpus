## Amazon challenges a contract imposed without a worker vote

Hundreds of Amazon workers at a warehouse in Delta, British Columbia, are now covered by a collective bargaining agreement, the company's first North American site under a collective bargaining agreement. An independent arbitrator imposed the terms between Canadian union Unifor and Amazon, and the agreement is set to run until Nov. 13, 2029. Amazon said the facility employs more than 700 workers.

In a blog posted late Friday, Amazon said the agreement sets terms for wages, scheduling, layoffs, and benefits that are largely similar to what the workers had already. Amazon also said the contract requires every employee in the unit to join the union, whether they want to or not, and to have union dues of up to C$1,064 ($744.03 US) deducted from their pay in the first year. That means they could actually take home less money than they did before, Amazon said.

"We believe that this process was improper, unfair, and defies common sense," J. Ofori Agboka, Amazon's vice president of people, wrote in the blog post.

Unifor National President Lana Payne praised the workers who "stood up to one of the most powerful companies on earth and won rights in writing that Amazon cannot take away," in a statement on Friday.

Unifor and Amazon have been locked in a fight since 2024, when employees at the facility started an organizing drive. In July 2025, the British Columbia Labour Relations Board certified the union for the warehouse through a rare process called "remedial certification" after it found that Amazon committed unfair labor practices, including what it described as an unnecessary hiring spree to stop the certification process. The board said in its decision that Amazon pursued a "lengthy and pervasive anti-union campaign." Amazon had filed an appeal.

Remedial certification is used when an employer pursues unfair labor practices during an organizing drive that makes a fair vote unlikely.

Amazon contested the process that produced the contract. The Seattle-based company argued that the arbitrator set the terms without giving its workers "the chance to ratify or vote on it," and said the terms were imposed despite a pending effort by some of Amazon's employees to decertify the union.

Amazon said it has filed a request for judicial review in the British Columbia Supreme Court, asking to halt implementation of the collective agreement until its decertification application is resolved and to overturn the board's order imposing a contract through arbitration.

"We think the best workplace is one where associates have direct input and we work together to make progress quickly, and where nobody should have to pay dues or go through a third party to be heard," Amazon's Agboka said.

The contract contrasts with the status of Amazon's union effort in the United States. In 2022, Amazon workers in Staten Island, New York, voted to unionize, marking the first successful U.S. organizing effort in the retail giant's history. The workers were represented by Amazon Labor Union, which affiliated with the International Brotherhood of Teamsters two years later.

Those workers don't have a contract yet, with the National Labor Relations Board ordering Amazon to negotiate and Amazon continuing to fight the union certification in court.
