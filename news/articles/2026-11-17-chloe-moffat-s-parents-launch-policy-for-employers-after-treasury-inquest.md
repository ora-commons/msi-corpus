## Chloe Moffat's parents launch policy for employers after Treasury inquest

**Subtype:** fact

- Chloe Moffat’s parents launched “Chloe’s policy” to change how employers handle disciplinary processes after the Treasury employee’s death.
- “Chloe’s policy” asks human-resources departments to minimize discipline, prioritize psychological safety, support affected staff and clarify whether workers can keep their roles.
- The civil service has added supporter access, advance notice and notice when dismissal is not likely for disciplinary meetings.
- The Treasury plans to plant a memorial garden at its Horse Guards Road offices to honor Moffat.
- The Treasury is hiring an on-site occupational therapist to support staff wellbeing.

## Treasury memorial garden planned; civil service changes meeting rules

Anne and David Moffat have been working with the Global Kindness Institute, an NGO, on “Chloe’s policy,” which they are asking organizations across the country to help develop and adopt. The initiative follows the death of their daughter, Chloe Moffat, a 26-year-old personal assistant at the Treasury who took her own life last year, the day after a surprise disciplinary meeting about anonymous complaints alleging that she had disclosed sensitive information, which she denied.

The inquest heard that Chloe Moffat had worked at the Treasury for almost three years, loved her job and had an exemplary employment record. She had been convinced she would lose her job even though that outcome was not likely, according to evidence presented to the coroner. Her managers did not allow her to bring a colleague to the “informal” meeting, gave no advance notice, refused to say whether dismissal was likely, removed her access to her director’s email and changed her work responsibilities.

In August, the coroner found that the Treasury had “materially contributed” to Moffat’s death. The coroner’s prevention of future deaths report, issued last month, said there was still a risk at the civil service because of its disciplinary procedures. The Treasury’s response to that report acknowledged the department’s role in Moffat’s death and committed to further safeguards.

Chloe’s policy calls on human-resources departments to consider the harm disciplinary processes can cause, particularly when complaints are anonymous or unverified. It prioritizes psychological safety and support for staff going through discipline, seeks to minimize such processes by considering alternatives, and asks employers to provide clarity, including whether a worker’s role is safe.

Anne Moffat said: “If you think about what happened to Chloe, if HR had thought ‘we know that she was very well thought of, nothing else had ever happened, these were the first ever allegations, they were anonymous,’ was this process necessary?”

She said, “All we want is for nobody else to go through this,” adding that the policy could prompt HR departments to ask whether discipline was necessary when people were considering making allegations. “So many people are so policy driven and so into disciplinaries and making people accountable when it may not be necessary. People aren’t being treated in a commonsense, human way,” she said.

David Moffat said: “It’s a powerful leadership standard. If it raises the profile of the process that Chloe went through and avoids it happening to anyone else, that is good enough for me.”

A review of the civil service by the Cabinet Office found that the conditions Moffat experienced had been replicated in other disciplinary meetings. The civil service has put in place guidance allowing staff to have a supporter in disciplinary meetings, providing proper notice of meetings and informing staff when dismissal is not likely. The Treasury is also hiring an on-site occupational therapist to support staff wellbeing.

The Moffats met James Bowler, the permanent secretary at the Treasury, at the end of last month. David Moffat said: “We felt listened to, and as part of their response to the coroner as well we felt reassured that change was being implemented. If the changes they have put forward to the coroner had already been in place, we certainly feel that Chloe would be with us today. It was a very emotional meeting.”

The parents also said the Treasury plans to plant a memorial garden in Moffat’s honor at its Horse Guards Road offices. Anne Moffat added: “It is the first time we felt that the Treasury had been compassionate to us and it wasn’t defensive or legal speak. They are looking at how to remember Chloe, and looking at the possibility of a memorial garden within the building and marking the garden with a plaque.”

David Moffat said: “We feel that Chloe is finally being acknowledged and remembered.”

A Treasury spokesperson said: “The Treasury’s deepest sympathies continue to be with Chloe’s friends and family, and the department continues to mourn our colleague.

“Our response to the coroner’s prevention of future deaths report sets out the action we have taken following this tragic case. An independent review found staff acted in good faith and with a duty of care throughout.”
