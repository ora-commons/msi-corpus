## House panels set Dec. 1-2 interviews; Senate hearing set for Dec. 3

Nvidia's board reinstated co-founder and longtime chief executive Jensen Huang on Monday, less than two weeks after pushing Huang out over issues that included extramarital affairs, one with a woman whom U.S. authorities suspected of working with Chinese intelligence. Nvidia shares were up 31.48%. The return did not close the related reviews: the board committee is examining whether Huang shared corporate or national security information, the Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating Nvidia, California's attorney general has issued an investigative subpoena, and Huang and Nvidia's general counsel are scheduled to testify before Congress.

Nvidia's board said on Monday that its investigation would "proceed to conclusion" and that Huang "fully supports the investigation and is committed to cooperating with it." The inquiry began days before Huang's resignation, when the board formed a committee of independent directors to determine whether Huang shared corporate or national security information during his romantic relationship with Alice Huang.

At the time of that relationship, Alice Huang was an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud-service provider suspected of funneling Nvidia chips to China. Before Huang's ouster, the board committee hired the law firm Wachtell Lipton to conduct the inquiry, led by former U.S. federal prosecutor Joon Kim.

The Securities and Exchange Commission was investigating Nvidia as recently as last week, The Wall Street Journal reported. The focus of that investigation could not be learned, the Journal said, though the inquiry included whether the company's disclosures misled investors. It also could not be determined whether the investigation covers questions around Nvidia's reporting of international chip sales. Nvidia eliminated a disclosure that showed its sales to Singapore, where Megaspeed is based, shortly after placing Megaspeed on an internal block list in June 2025.

Lawmakers on several congressional committees have been looking into the potential national security implications of Huang's conduct. Last week, three House committees said Huang and Nvidia's general counsel would sit for transcribed interviews after the Thanksgiving holiday, on Dec. 1 and Dec. 2, respectively. The interviews will be conducted behind closed doors, with the committees deciding what information to release. "The American people need answers on Mr. Huang and Nvidia's role in allowing dangerous PRC actors to access our most advanced chips, as well as the role of a potential Chinese intelligence asset in this scandal," the committees said, referring to the People's Republic of China.

Sen. Tim Scott (R., S.C.) said Huang will testify before the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 3. "The national security concerns and adherence to export controls will be central to the committee's oversight and the congressional hearing," Scott said Monday. "Our markets demand transparency. Americans deserve the truth. Our committee will deliver answers."

Democratic congressional leaders have also vowed to increase scrutiny of Nvidia and Huang when they take control of Congress next year. "Democrats will wield their full power to investigate any wrongdoing," Sen. Chuck Schumer (D., N.Y.) said, pointing to Huang, Nvidia and the Trump administration as potential targets.

Officials from several U.S. agencies, including the Commerce Department and Justice Department, have investigated Megaspeed and its potential ties to the Chinese government for more than a year, the Journal has reported. The two departments said last week that they would work together to "bring the full extent of the law against Megaspeed for any criminal conduct that may have occurred." In May 2025, Commerce investigators requested information from Nvidia about direct or indirect sales to Megaspeed, and the Justice Department took the lead in the criminal investigation the next month.

California's attorney general also issued an investigative subpoena to Nvidia last week, seeking information on whether the company shared its AI chips or confidential information with China. The attorney general's office said the state is also looking at whether Nvidia misled investors. "The conduct of Nvidia and its former CEO raises bright red flags," said Rob Bonta, California's attorney general.

The investigations described remain open. The board's committee is proceeding with Huang's cooperation, the SEC's focus has not been publicly confirmed, and the House and Senate proceedings are scheduled after the Thanksgiving holiday.
