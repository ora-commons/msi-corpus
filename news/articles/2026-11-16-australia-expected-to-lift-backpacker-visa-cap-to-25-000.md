## Regional work stay for second-year backpackers may rise to four months

The federal government is expected to lift key elements of its September plan to cut backpacker numbers, following criticism from regional stakeholder groups that rely on the travellers for labour. The Albanese government is expected on Tuesday to lift the proposed cap on third-year working holiday visas, boost regional work requirements and delay the start of a new ballot system.

The home affairs minister, Tony Burke, was due to hold a press conference in Cairns on Tuesday morning to set out the changes.

Burke announced major reforms to working holiday visas in September, including creating a ballot for backpackers seeking to stay a second or third year, which would require them to work in the regions. Under that proposal, 45,000 backpackers would have been allowed to remain for a second year and would have been required to work at least three months in a regional area, while 5,000 places would have been allocated for workers seeking to remain in Australia for a third year.

Burke previously announced in September plans to cap the number of third-year backpackers at 5,000, from the current level of about 31,000. Industry sources said they expected the government to set a cap at 25,000 places instead.

Industry sources also expected second-year backpackers will be required to work in regional areas for four months, instead of three, and that the ballot will be delayed.

The changes follow months of consultation with industry, regional and agricultural groups who warned of worker shortages under the September proposal.

Workers from the UK are exempt from the caps under the free trade agreement signed in 2021. Sources said the prime minister, Anthony Albanese, is in discussions with his UK counterpart, Andy Burnham, to require British backpackers to also do regional work going forward.

One industry source said they welcomed the anticipated announcement, which showed the government was "listening to the concerns from our sector".
