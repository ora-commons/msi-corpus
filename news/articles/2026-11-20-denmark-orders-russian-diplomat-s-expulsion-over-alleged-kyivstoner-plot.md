## Denmark orders Russian diplomat's expulsion over alleged Kyivstoner plot

## Police arrested three suspects five minutes before concert north of Copenhagen

According to prosecutors, the three suspects were driving toward the concert venue north of Copenhagen with two loaded pistols and two knives when police arrested them at about 9:55 p.m., five minutes before the concert was due to begin. A fourth suspect was arrested later that evening, police said. One of the suspects had bought a ticket to the concert, according to the charges.

Danish police said they thwarted the alleged plot when they arrested three men with links to the Danish criminal underworld shortly before Kyivstoner was due to perform at a concert on September 25. The concert was then canceled.

Kyivstoner, whose real name is Albert Viktorovich Vasiliev, holds US and Ukrainian nationality. Danish police said they had averted an attempt by Russian intelligence services to kill the rapper in September before his planned performance in Denmark.

Danish prosecutors charged the four suspects under Section 108 of Denmark’s criminal code, which covers assisting a foreign intelligence service, and with attempted murder. The suspects denied wrongdoing in court, broadcaster DR reported.

Rasmus Kieffer-Kristensen, the head of Denmark’s national security and intelligence service, said: “It is our clear assessment that the assassination attempt was planned by the Russian intelligence services.”

Kieffer-Kristensen said: “A Russian assassination attempt is not something we’ve seen in Denmark before, and that gives me an impression of an increasingly aggressive and risk-taking Russia.” He added: “We believe that the case is important for understanding the threat that Denmark faces from Russia.”

## Expulsion and European visa measures

Denmark has decided to expel the Russian defence attache in Copenhagen after the country suspected Moscow was behind an attempt to kill US-Ukrainian rapper Kyivstoner before a September performance.

Foreign Minister Lars Løekke Rasmussen said at a news conference: “We have just summoned the Russian ambassador to the ministry of foreign affairs and notified him of the government’s decision to expel the Russian defence attache from Denmark.” The expulsion will happen within seven days, he said.

“We are saying this very clearly: we have exposed you,” Rasmussen added.

Rasmussen said Denmark was considering suspending Schengen visas for Russians. “We have launched work at the European level to further tighten the rules governing access of Russian nationals to Europe,” he said. “We are now stepping up our efforts towards a halt to all non-essential visas.”

“The strongest response Denmark can give is not to waver and to support Ukraine. And we fully intend to continue supporting Ukraine,” Rasmussen said.
