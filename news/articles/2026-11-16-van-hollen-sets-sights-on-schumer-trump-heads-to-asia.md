## Sanders and incoming senators back Van Hollen, McDaniel says

Eric McDaniel told NPR that Van Hollen needs 30 Democrats to secure a simple majority in the Senate. McDaniel said Van Hollen has the support he needs from Bernie Sanders and incoming senators Abdul El-Sayed and Peggy Flanagan.

A wave of incoming senators has spent months hearing voters demand change in Washington, McDaniel said. He described Van Hollen's bid as a real opportunity and Schumer's position as a potential risk after the recent election results. Votes will be cast by secret ballot when lawmakers convene in a few weeks, he said, making it uncertain how many senators will back each leader. McDaniel said anything could happen.

As of NPR's report, no one had challenged Rep. Hakeem Jeffries for House speaker as Democrats prepared for substantial majorities in Congress.

## Trump travels to Philippines and China for Xi-Putin summit

He traveled to China with Trump earlier this year; this time, Musk is accompanying Trump to Shenzhen.

Mara Liasson said it is not unusual for presidents who face midterm election defeats to shift their focus to foreign policy. Trump's recent foreign policy efforts, including tariffs and the war in Iran, have been widely unpopular and seen as reasons his party lost the midterms, she said.

## Sleep research links short sleep with weight gain

Sleep plays a crucial role in weight management. Studies have found that poor sleep can increase appetite and lead to overeating, making it harder to shed pounds, while quality sleep might enhance weight-loss efforts. In one year-long weight-loss program, participants who enjoyed better sleep lost more weight and fat.

Researchers said evidence linking more sleep to weight loss is limited, but there is consistent science connecting insufficient sleep to weight gain. Lack of sleep increases activity in brain regions associated with pleasure and reward, making good food choices harder. It also can disrupt circadian rhythms and reduce insulin sensitivity, encouraging fat storage and weight gain. The relationship works both ways: eating added sugars and saturated fats disrupts sleep and reduces deep sleep, while consuming fruits, vegetables and fiber improves sleep quality and decreases nighttime awakenings.
