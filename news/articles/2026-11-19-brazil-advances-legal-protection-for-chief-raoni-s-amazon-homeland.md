## About 200 rural properties overlap the territory, Lula says

Brazil's Justice Ministry issued an order formally recognizing the Mebengokre-Kayapo and Yudja-Juruna peoples' permanent possession of the Kapot Nhinore Indigenous Territory. President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva advanced the legal protection during a visit to the territory in the Brazilian rainforest. Chief Raoni Metuktire was born there.

Lula said the government still faces legal hurdles to complete the demarcation because about 200 rural properties overlap the territory. At a public event streamed on the government's official YouTube channel, Lula said the government would work to resolve the overlap. “But we’re going to work very hard to resolve this once and for all,” he said.

The territory covers 362,000 hectares (895,000 acres) and is home to 60 people. The land's size for that population has been a sticking point in land-recognition discussions. “It doesn’t matter if it’s one person. We cannot decide whether to recognize Indigenous land based on how much land people will have,” Lula said. “We must never forget that Raoni’s ancestors were here before there were any Brazilians. We are simply restoring the rights and dignity you should never have lost.”

Kapot Nhinore lies along the Xingu River, a major Amazon tributary, on the border between the states of Para and Mato Grosso. Raoni has fought for decades for the territory's permanent protection. The Brazilian government formally defined its boundaries in 2023 through an administrative process that began in 2004.

“ This is a victory that should be celebrated by all Indigenous peoples in Brazil and around the world, especially because this is the ancestral territory of the most celebrated defender of forests,” Danicley Aguiar, a spokesperson for Greenpeace Brazil, said.

Márcio Santilli, a founding member of the nonprofit Socioenvironmental Institute and former head of Brazil's Indigenous affairs agency, said the recognition was significant both as the traditional homeland where Chief Raoni was born and as a key safeguard for the rainforest along the Xingu River. He said: “This is one of the few stretches of the Xingu’s banks that remained outside protected areas. Land grabbers and loggers have occupied the region and cleared parts of this corridor.”

Indigenous lands are the best-preserved areas in Brazil. Protecting Indigenous territories is widely seen as one of the most effective ways to curb Amazon deforestation, which researchers warn could accelerate global warming. A 2022 analysis by MapBiomas, a network tracking land use, found that Brazil's Indigenous territories lost just 1% of their native vegetation over three decades, compared with 20% on private land nationwide.

Without formal protection, Kapot Nhinore has faced intense pressure from soybean farmers and cattle ranchers. A Greenpeace report released Wednesday found that about 17% of its forest has been cleared since 1988. Greenpeace has advocated that Brazil's banking authorities change rural lending rules to reduce agribusiness pressure on occupied Indigenous territories.

Raoni, one of the world's best-known advocates for the Amazon rainforest and Indigenous rights, has received palliative care at home in central Brazil since September after being diagnosed with cancer at age 94. Family members, community members and specialized medical teams are surrounding him.

During a private visit before the public event, Lula posted a photo on Instagram showing the two holding hands. “I thank God for giving me the chance to speak with him, to find him alive and tell him: We are legally recognizing your land,” Lula said.

At one of his most recent public appearances in April, at the Free Land encampment in Brasilia, Brazil's largest annual Indigenous mobilization, Raoni reaffirmed his support for Lula. Lula won a fourth term in October. Raoni's activism helped secure protection for numerous Indigenous territories, and an Oscar-nominated documentary brought him international recognition in the late 1970s.

Raoni later toured with British musician Sting to promote Indigenous causes, traveling from communities in Canada to presidential offices and royal palaces in Europe. In October 2025, King Charles III welcomed him to St. James’s Palace.

The order formally recognizes permanent possession, but final demarcation remains incomplete because of the overlapping rural properties. Lula said the government would keep working to resolve that overlap.
