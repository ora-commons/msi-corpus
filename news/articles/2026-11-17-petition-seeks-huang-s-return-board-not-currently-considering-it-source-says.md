## Trump and Musk back reinstatement as three House chairmen seek answers

A person familiar with Nvidia's board thinking who insisted on anonymity told The Associated Press on Monday that the board was not currently considering Huang's return. The account came as organizers of the "Stand with Jensen" petition, set up last week, called on Nvidia's board to bring the former CEO back. Nvidia did not immediately respond to an Associated Press request for comment on Huang's Tuesday statement.

The petition has gathered momentum, drawing signatures from current and former Nvidia staff, investors and other Silicon Valley supporters. It calls on the board to bring Huang back while an independent investigation proceeds or until there is "compelling evidence" that his departure is in the company's best interests. In an open letter, the organizers said, "We are concerned that Jensen was removed without meaningful due process." They added, "We have not been shown evidence substantiating the allegation that the woman at the center of this controversy was a spy."

Also Tuesday, the chairmen of three House committees with oversight of China and intelligence issues said Huang had voluntarily agreed to appear for transcribed interviews. Huang is scheduled to appear before congressional committees on Dec. 1, followed by Nvidia General Counsel Tim Teter on Dec. 2. The chairmen said, "We look forward to hearing from Jensen Huang and Tim Teter on their roles in a potential breach of our national security." They also said, "The American people need answers on Mr. Huang and Nvidia's role in allowing dangerous PRC actors to access our most advanced chips, as well as the role of a potential Chinese intelligence asset in this scandal," referring to the People's Republic of China.

Some technology industry figures, including SpaceX and Tesla CEO Elon Musk, called for Huang's reinstatement. President Donald Trump, who has spoken with Huang frequently, also endorsed the comeback bid. Trump suggested Monday that the former CEO could return, saying Huang "learned a big lesson" and that he "would certainly not mind" if he were reinstated.

Nvidia said it was creating a new board committee to oversee national security and global trade compliance following the revelations about Huang. Trump, speaking shortly after arriving Tuesday evening in the Philippine capital of Manila, where he participated in a photo opportunity with the leaders of Australia, India and Japan, called the committee a good idea. "Yes, that's a good idea. It's a great company. It's a great idea," Trump said.

Huang's Tuesday social media post was his first public statement since resigning last week following revelations of an extramarital affair with an alleged Chinese spy. He wrote, "If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO." The statement made his return conditional on a board decision.

Huang also acknowledged making "serious mistakes" in his personal life and said he had received an "outpouring" of support from employees, investors, business partners and customers for his return. Before resigning, Huang oversaw Nvidia's development as the key provider of chips driving advanced AI models.

Huang resigned last week, apparently ousted by the board after revelations that he had extramarital affairs both with a Chinese businesswoman suspected of being an intelligence asset for Beijing and with a senior executive who reported to him. The businesswoman reportedly had links to Chinese intelligence and was an executive at Singapore-based cloud computing firm Megaspeed International.

Megaspeed is being investigated by the United States for allegedly violating U.S. export controls by illegally diverting advanced Nvidia AI chips to Chinese companies. Nvidia's advanced chips are in high demand because they are essential to cloud computing and data centers that underpin the AI boom, but they face restrictions on their sale to China.

The Santa Clara, California-based company was scheduled to release quarterly earnings later Tuesday. Nvidia Chief Financial Officer Colette Kress was expected to speak with Wall Street analysts on the earnings call for the first time since being named interim CEO.
