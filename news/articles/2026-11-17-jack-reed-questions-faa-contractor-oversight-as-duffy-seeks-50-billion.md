## FAA blames equipment issue during telecoms upgrade for Friday's ground stop

Sen. Jack Reed spent part of Tuesday pressing the Federal Aviation Administration on how it supervised a contractor whose work coincided with Friday's four-hour nationwide ground stop. Speaking with NPR's Michel Martin on Morning Edition, the senator said the administration's explanation of an apparent contractor error made sense but called for a closer examination of its cause.

"Was the contractor properly supervised? I have questions too about whether FAA, after DOGE, is capable of supervising these types of activities," Reed said.

Friday's ground stop occurred as a federal contractor was replacing copper wiring with fiber optic cables at an FAA facility in Arkansas. The FAA has blamed the disruption on an equipment issue during a telecommunications network upgrade.

The exact cause remains under investigation, and federal officials say there is no indication of a cyberattack.

The disruption has renewed debate in Congress over funding for the aging system. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy initially requested $30 billion in additional funding before increasing the figure to $50 billion.

Congress has already provided $14.5 billion for FAA modernization, and roughly $9 billion of that money remains unspent.

Reed, who sits on the Senate Appropriations Committee, criticized the administration for seeking more money without presenting a detailed plan. "They're just trying to throw money at a problem," he said.

Reed paired that objection with the condition of the system itself. "The top priority in my book is getting this system back up to speed," he said.

On how the money would be spent, Reed said he agreed with Texas Republican Sen. Ted Cruz, who has questioned whether some of the proposed funding would go toward airport improvements rather than safety.

Two of Reed's questions remained open as of Tuesday's interview: what produced the equipment failure that triggered the nationwide ground stop, and whether the FAA can supervise contractor work of that kind after DOGE. Federal officials say the cause is still under investigation and that there is no indication of a cyberattack.
