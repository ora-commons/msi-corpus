## Alaska cast vote records favor Peltola in a ranked-choice count

Mary Peltola moved above 50% of the counted vote in Alaska's race for the U.S. Senate on Tuesday, prompting the Associated Press to declare her the winner. The threshold allowed Peltola, a Democrat, to win outright and bypass the state's ranked-choice voting system.

The Tuesday update put Peltola 12,458 votes ahead among 287,896 ballots counted, a margin of about 4.3 percentage points over Republican incumbent Dan Sullivan. The Associated Press said the updated count provided the basis for its decision.

Only a small number of ballots remained uncounted, most of them absentee mail ballots. AP reported that Peltola had substantially led Sullivan among the ballots counted last, making it unlikely that her share would fall below 50% once the remaining ballots were added.

Had Peltola fallen below 50%, the count would have triggered Alaska's ranked-choice voting system. Ballots and data released by state election officials showed that Peltola would win comfortably in that process, according to AP.

For Sullivan to prevail in a ranked-choice count, he would need to be the second choice of essentially all voters who backed every other candidate. Those candidates included Republican Gerald Heikes, independent Dan J. Sullivan Jr. and write-in candidates. AP said the released data showed that support was not present.

Alaska officials provide cast vote records that cannot be linked to individual voters but show how each candidate was ranked on individual ballots. According to AP, many voters who did not rank Peltola or Sullivan first also did not rank anyone second. Of those who selected a second candidate, only some ranked Sullivan ahead of Peltola.

The records showed that Peltola would gain more votes than Sullivan after first-choice votes for write-in candidates and Dan J. Sullivan Jr. were redistributed. AP said that result would be enough to return Peltola above 50% and send her to Washington as the 56th Democrat in the Senate.
