## Chip stocks lead declines in South Korea and Japan as Taiwan's Taiex sinks 4.1%

Asian shares mostly declined Monday as worries about the direction of spending on artificial intelligence carried into another week of trading, following the upheaval at Nvidia. U.S. equity futures traded lower. Investors and traders were watching for signals about the next phase of the global AI industry, with Nvidia's earnings report due Tuesday and Anthropic's stock market debut set for later this week.

Jensen Huang's resignation as Nvidia chief executive last week, which came after his extramarital affair sparked U.S. national security concerns, shook global markets. Huang has been one of the biggest advocates for AI, and his downfall left investors questioning how the global AI boom would be affected. The concern has spread to Asia, where AI hardware companies hold prominent positions on exchanges in South Korea and Japan.

South Korea's benchmark Kospi index lost 2.8% to 5,142.84. Memory chipmaker SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics, two of South Korea's most valuable companies, both traded lower. SK Hynix plummeted 5.5%, while Samsung Electronics gave up 3.2%.

Japan's Nikkei 225 fell 1.6% to 60,182.50 after official data Monday showed the economy contracted in the latest quarter. Several of Japan's big AI stock names also traded lower. SoftBank Group, an OpenAI investor, slipped 4.2%, memory maker Kioxia Holdings dropped 5.5% and chip equipment maker Tokyo Electron dipped 5.2%.

Taiwan's Taiex, another market shaped by the global AI boom, sank 4.1%. Shares of its leading chipmaker, TSMC — Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. — fell 3.4%.

Analysts have pointed to increasing concerns that the robust growth of AI capital spending may slow, even before the Nvidia episode. Uncertainties surrounding developments of AI data centers in the U.S. were also growing following the U.S. midterm elections.

"The market was already worried about this risk before what happened at Nvidia," Zhiwei Zhang, president and chief economist at Pinpoint Asset Management, said in a commentary Monday. "Now the risk has become even more serious."

Elsewhere in Asia, Hong Kong's Hang Seng index rose 0.9% to 23,852.96. The Shanghai Composite index declined 0.4% to 3,621.46 following Chinese official economic data released Monday, including on retail sales and industrial production, that disappointed analysts. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 edged down 0.2% to 9,064.30, while India's Sensex was up 0.6%.

Wall Street's benchmark S&P 500 fell 0.4% Friday. The Dow Jones Industrial Average edged 0.1% higher, while the technology-heavy Nasdaq composite dropped 0.9%. Nvidia lost 2% Friday after falling 4.3% Thursday as the company announced Huang's resignation. Broadcom declined 4.8% Friday and AMD, or Advanced Micro Devices, gave up 8.8%.

In commodities trading, oil prices declined as tensions in the Middle East dragged on while oil flows in the Gulf were recovering from prewar levels. The International Energy Agency said last week that Gulf exports "recovered much faster than global oil demand." Brent crude, the international standard, slid 1% to $94.91 per barrel, though that is still much higher than the approximately $72 a barrel in late February before the Iran war began.

Investors are also looking for updates this week as U.S. President Donald Trump, Chinese leader Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin are expected to meet Thursday on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, China. The minutes from the Federal Reserve's latest policy meeting are set to be released Wednesday, which could offer more detail on its decision-making on rates as it attempts to cool inflation in the U.S.

The U.S. dollar was trading at 159.64 Japanese yen and was little changed. The euro was trading at $1.1017, down from $1.1029.
