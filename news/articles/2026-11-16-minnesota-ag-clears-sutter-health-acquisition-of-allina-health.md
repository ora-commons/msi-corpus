## Minnesota AG clears Sutter Health acquisition of Allina Health

**Subtype:** fact

- Minnesota Attorney General Keith Ellison concluded that Sutter Health's acquisition of Allina Health meets the public interest standard and Minnesota's health care transaction law.
- The consolidation, first announced in March and set to take place in January, will create a $26 billion system with 39 hospitals serving more than 5 million patients in California, Minnesota and Wisconsin.
- A 10-year oversight agreement binds Sutter to maintain Allina's core services for five years and commit $2.5 billion in capital investments.
- The agreement includes labor protections and workforce and rural health investments but no explicit guardrails limiting price growth.

## Oversight pact lacks explicit guardrails limiting price growth

Minnesota-based nonprofit hospital system Allina Health is set to be acquired by the larger California-based Sutter Health, after the two nonprofit systems signed a 10-year oversight agreement with the Minnesota Attorney General's Office. The consolidation, first announced in March, is set to take place in January.

At a Monday press conference at Allina Health's Bandana Square clinic in St. Paul, Ellison said the review had reached its conclusion. "After this thorough review, we have concluded that the integration of Allina and Sutter has met the public interest standard and Minnesota's health care transaction law," Ellison said. The signed agreement nonetheless contains no explicit guardrails to limit price growth — the central concern of many health economists when it comes to hospital consolidation — though Minnesota state law forbids health systems from entering into a transaction that substantially lessens competition.

## The scale of the combined system

The deal will create a combined $26 billion system with 39 hospitals serving more than 5 million patients in California, Minnesota and Wisconsin. Allina would maintain its Minneapolis headquarters under the combined system and become the Upper Midwest Division of Sutter Health, with Allina CEO Lisa Shannon remaining in leadership of the division.

At the press conference, Shannon said that challenges in the health care landscape — rising input costs, reduced reimbursement for care and widening gaps in the safety net — have led to financial losses for the health system, though Allina officials previously told the Star Tribune that the merger was not a financial rescue.

## What the oversight agreement requires

The signed oversight agreement binds Sutter to maintain Allina Health's core services for five years, which refers to primary care, family medicine and internal medicine; mental health; cardiovascular services; emergency room services; ICU services; and stroke services.

Sutter committed $2.5 billion to complete Allina's overhaul of its Abbott Northwestern campus; modernize Allina's IT systems; and build out an outpatient care network, among other capital investments. The agreement, summarized on the AG's website, also includes labor protections, including a ban on noncompetes reflecting state law; and explicit investments into workforce training and rural health care.

## Consolidation evidence and the attorney general's 2023 powers

The agreement arrives amid a broader pattern of health care consolidation, as hospitals and clinics combine into large multi-site systems. In Minnesota this year, consolidation transactions are being proposed at what Erin Conti of the Minnesota Attorney General's Office called a "breakneck pace" during a state Senate committee hearing.

Substantial evidence has shown that health care consolidation has led to higher prices, mainly because larger health systems have more power when negotiating their prices with insurers. Evidence on the effects on quality of care is mixed "at best," according to a brief from KFF, a health policy research organization.

The Minnesota Attorney General's Office received expanded regulatory powers over health care mergers from the Legislature in 2023. It's now able to analyze and sue to block hospital mergers if they are against the public's interest — for example if they increase health care costs for patients.

## Sutter's litigation history

Sutter, the fourth biggest health system in California by number of staffed patient beds, has previously been accused of driving up costs for Californians through anti-competitive behavior in two lawsuits brought by unions, employers, individuals and California's attorney general. Sutter denied wrongdoing in the lawsuits' settlements.

## Other Minnesota reviews

The Minnesota Attorney General's Office approved the acquisition of North Memorial Health by South Dakota-based Sanford Health under another 10-year oversight agreement, and is currently in the process of reviewing a proposed merger between HealthPartners and Essentia, both Minnesota-based health systems.
