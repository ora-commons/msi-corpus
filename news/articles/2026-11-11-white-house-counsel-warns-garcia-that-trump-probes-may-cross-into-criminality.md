## Garcia calls the letter baseless and says Democrats will not be deterred

White House counsel William Scharf sent Rep. Robert Garcia, D-Calif., a three-page letter of warning on Wednesday, telling the incoming House Oversight Committee chair that the Democrats' promise to investigate the Trump administration "may cross the line into blatant criminality."

Scharf wrote the letter, which PBS News obtained and published, to "remind" Garcia of the constitutional limits of congressional investigations, "which you and members of the new Congress have evinced a clear intent to transgress."

The letter accuses Democrats of "abusive and capricious threats" against President Donald Trump and members of his administration.

Garcia dismissed the warning in a statement Wednesday. "It appears Donald Trump has something to hide," Garcia said. "We are a co-equal branch of government and will not be deterred by baseless threats from a weaponized DOJ or from the President's lawyers."

The clash has been building since election night, when projections showed Democrats would control the House of Representatives in the incoming Congress. Garcia marked the projections with a two-word message to the Trump administration: "Lawyer up."

In a PBS News Hour interview last week, White House correspondent Liz Landers asked Garcia whether he felt voters gave Democrats a mandate to investigate the Trump administration. "I think they have," Garcia said.

"The American public are looking at their own bills and seeing costs go up when they go to the supermarket, yet they're seeing the Trumps get richer," Garcia added later in the interview. "And so that's a real contrast, and that's a real issue for us to take on."

The News Hour paired the interview with a companion segment on Trump's $2 billion income in 2025 and fresh questions about profiting off the presidency.

The letter and Garcia's response set out the two sides' positions heading into the new Congress: the White House counsel's office arguing that Democrats' promised investigations already run up against constitutional limits, and the incoming Oversight chair saying voters gave Democrats a mandate to pursue accountability.
