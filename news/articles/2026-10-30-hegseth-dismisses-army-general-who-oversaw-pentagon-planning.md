## VA loan program shutdown raised costs for over 15,000 veterans

An NPR investigation found that thousands of military veterans with VA-backed mortgages have faced higher interest rates and monthly payments since last year, after the Trump administration shut down a key part of the VA's home loan program. More than 15,000 veterans experienced monthly payment increases of at least $150 after their loans were modified due to financial hardship, and more than 700 veterans saw their payments rise by $1,000 or more.

Before it was shut down, the Veterans Affairs Servicing Purchase (VASP) program — created under the Biden administration — helped tens of thousands of veterans who had fallen behind on their payments by rolling missed payments into a new, low-interest mortgage, according to NPR. The mortgage industry and housing groups warned the administration not to kill the program without first creating a viable replacement; without one, veterans had to modify loans at higher interest rates.
