## Sentence leaves Pannun’s India claim and Kirshner’s Canada denial unresolved

**Court-paper disclosure**

According to court papers, Nikhil Gupta told an undercover Drug Enforcement Administration officer whom Gupta thought could carry out Gurpatwant Singh Pannun’s killing that the June 2023 killing of Hardeep Singh Nijjar outside a Sikh temple in British Columbia, Canada, was the work of the same individuals planning Pannun’s assassination. The papers say Gupta told the officer that Nijjar “was also the target” and “we have so many targets,” then instructed the officer to proceed with Pannun’s killing now that Nijjar was dead.

**Pannun’s allegation and Kirshner’s denial**

The case targeted Pannun, a prominent Sikh leader and advocate for independence for Punjab who was living in New York City. Pannun was also a U.S. citizen and lawyer, and Punjab is the northwest Indian state where Sikhism was founded. In a lengthy speech to the court, Pannun described Gupta as “just a foot soldier acting at the behest” of Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi. Pannun said Modi’s administration wanted him dead “because I lead the campaign openly, defy threats and intimidation.”

Speaking in court Friday, Gupta’s lawyer Isabelle Kirshner denied he was involved in the Canada plot involving Nijjar’s killing. She regarded Gupta’s discussions, captured on government recordings, about assassination attempts in Nepal and Pakistan as “pure puffery.” She said, “There is no question he was not the brains behind this operation.”

**Credible-threat warning**

Pannun said, “I do not stand before you for my personal safety because my life belongs entirely to my people,” adding that he was “willing to take India’s bullet if that is the price for attaining freedom for my people.” In August, Pannun said the FBI and the Royal Canadian Mounted Police warned him that he faced a new credible threat to his life.

**Sentencing consequences**

At Friday’s sentencing in Manhattan federal court, Judge Victor Marrero imposed a 235-month term — just over 19½ years — on Gupta, 55, from India. Gupta had pleaded guilty in February to three conspiracy charges for conspiring to hire a hit man to assassinate Pannun. Gupta faces deportation to India once he completes the 235-month sentence, Marrero said, and his plea agreement called for him to serve at least two decades in prison.

Gupta was in India when he paid $15,000 online in 2023 to someone he thought could carry out Pannun’s killing. Court papers identify the person as an undercover Drug Enforcement Administration officer posing as a hit man. Gupta was arrested in Prague and has been held without bail since his extradition to the U.S. in June 2023.

Gupta’s family, whom he had not seen since before his arrest three years earlier, listened to the sentence through an internet hookup from India. In court, Gupta said, “I’m sorry to Mr. Pannun.” He added, “I’m sorry to the communities I hurt. I’m sorry to my own family, which always loved me, even when I did not deserve it.”

**Defense account**

Kirshner described Gupta as a hardworking husband and father who volunteered at an orphanage and animal shelter before he became involved in the plot to kill Pannun. She said, “This is a human being sitting here,” and urged Marrero to craft a sentence that punished Gupta only for what he did, “not as a rebuke to India.”

**Courthouse protest**

Outside the courthouse, protesters gathered with yellow and blue flags representing an independent Punjab, which they hoped to rename as the Democratic Republic of Khalistan. They marched around a makeshift prison cell bearing photo illustrations of Modi and his Minister of Home Affairs, Amit Shah, in handcuffs and orange prison jumpsuits.

Marrero imposed the sentence on Gupta for the three conspiracy charges. Pannun’s allegation that Modi’s administration wanted him dead and Kirshner’s denial of Gupta’s involvement in the Canada plot remained separate from those charges.
