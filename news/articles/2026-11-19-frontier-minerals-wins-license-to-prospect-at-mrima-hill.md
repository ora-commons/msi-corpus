## Exploration award leaves the mining decision unresolved

The Wall Street Journal reported that Frontier Minerals, a U.S.-led consortium, won the right to explore a major rare-earth deposit at Mrima Hill on Kenya's Indian Ocean coast. The three-year prospecting license advances the Trump administration's push to reduce reliance on China for minerals used in jet fighters, EVs and high-tech ammunition. The award does not give Frontier Minerals the right to mine any deposits it finds.

The winning consortium includes two Indiana-based companies, American Resources Corporation and ReElement Technologies, and Idaho-based Bunker Hill Mining Group. Frontier beat the other applicants after a competition lasting more than six months.

Two Chinese companies withdrew before the final bids, with no public explanation. U.S.-linked consortium Mrima Earth and Australian miners RareX and Iluka Resources also competed for the license.

The rare-earth deposits lie beneath Mrima Hill, a forested mound in Kenya's coastal Kwale county. A previous license holder estimated the deposits at around $62 billion. The area also contains niobium used in jet engines.

The Kenyan government insisted that bidders process the minerals locally, a stance increasingly adopted by African countries trying to secure more wealth from their minerals. Frontier promised to develop the country's processing and refining capacity, create jobs and bring community-development projects.

The International Energy Agency said China refines around 85% of the world's rare earths. China also controls exports of technology used to process rare-earth minerals.

Residents and environmentalists near the site fear mining activity could damage the forest. The forest has been a protected national monument since 1992. Local communities have sacred shrines, ancestral graves and medicinal trees in the forest.

Another company, Canadian-owned Cortec Mining Kenya, won a license to develop the site in 2013. Kenyan authorities later revoked the permit, citing irregularities. An international tribunal found that the company had not obtained the required environmental approvals.

Frontier said it recognized “the environmental, cultural and social significance of Mrima Hill and the importance of ensuring that local communities are meaningfully involved” in the plans.

The prospecting license runs for three years and does not guarantee Frontier the right to mine any deposits it discovers. It could give the consortium an edge in securing a mining license later.
