## Jeffries lays out affordability, healthcare and immigration agenda

**Subtype:** fact

- Incoming House Speaker Hakeem Jeffries said Democrats are focused on affordability, expanding healthcare and fixing the country's "broken" immigration system in an interview with CBS News's Face the Nation.
- Jeffries said he is willing to "explore whether we can work together with the president and our colleagues on the other side of the aisle" on those areas, including a bipartisan healthcare plan.
- Jeffries reiterated his support for legislation banning members of Congress from trading stock, describing it as an anti-corruption measure.
- Democrats seized control of Congress in the 3 November midterms, giving them oversight power over Donald Trump's final two years in office.

## Incoming speaker says Republicans could join on bipartisan healthcare plan

Representative Hakeem Jeffries of New York, whom Democrats nominated for House speaker four days before the interview, told Face the Nation host Margaret Brennan that his party's priorities are affordability, healthcare and immigration.

"We need to drive down the high cost of living and deal with the affordability crisis," Jeffries said.

On healthcare, Jeffries said Democrats intend to go beyond restoring existing programs. "We have a broken healthcare system. We need to fix it, and then make sure that we actually don't simply return to the status quo, but we do much better than what currently exists," he said. "And we need to fix our broken immigration system as well."

Jeffries said those three areas were among the ones where Democrats could possibly "explore whether we can work together with the president and our colleagues on the other side of the aisle."

He told Brennan that he envisions Democrats and Republicans working together particularly on a bipartisan healthcare plan.

Jeffries also restated his support for legislation barring members of Congress from trading stocks, which he described as an anti-corruption measure.

"No one is going to get a pass because there's a cost to corruption that adversely [affects] everyday Americans," Jeffries said. "And we should begin by dealing with our own House."

The interview came less than three weeks after the 3 November midterms, in which Democrats seized control of Congress for the latter half of Trump's second presidency. Trump's Republican party resoundingly lost the midterms amid dissatisfaction with high living costs worsened by the Iran war, as well as his aggressive but unpopular mass deportation campaign.

The outcome gave Democrats the power to conduct tough oversight of Trump over the final two years of his presidency, after his party had controlled Congress since the second half of Joe Biden's Oval Office term. Jeffries said Democratic lawmakers were also compelled to meet more practical everyday needs for Americans.

"Can you imagine how angry the American people will be with Washington if over the next two years, we fail to do anything about affordability, fail to do anything about healthcare, and fail to do anything about immigration?" Jeffries said.

"We're committed to bringing about the type of dramatic change the American people are demanding," he said.

Democrats nominated Jeffries for speaker on the understanding that the new Congress is sworn in, a step that put him in line to become the first Black lawmaker to lead the US House beginning in early January.
