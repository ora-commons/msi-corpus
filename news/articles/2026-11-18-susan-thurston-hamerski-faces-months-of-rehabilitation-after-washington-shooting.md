## Fundraiser tops $210,000 for Hamerski’s medical expenses

Susan Thurston Hamerski is recovering after a gunman shot her in the face during the 12 November mass shooting at a Washington DC condominium building in Logan Circle, near the White House. According to her friends and family, the gunman killed one person, injured five others and evidently died by suicide. Hamerski survived after being brought to a hospital in critical condition.

Hamerski had traveled from her home in Minnesota’s Twin Cities for her daughter’s wedding. Family friend and spokesperson Tina Wilcox said Hamerski stayed back to care for her daughter’s dog while newlywed Madeleine and her husband went on their honeymoon. Hamerski was walking Wrigley when the shooting erupted in and around the condo building, and the gunman shot her. The Guardian reported that the attacker was locked in an ongoing legal dispute with the complex’s management.

On Friday, the day after the deadly shooting, two people hugged at a makeshift memorial in Logan Circle in a photograph credited to Tom Brenner/AP.

Hamerski was left needing to undergo multiple surgeries to restore her jaw and tongue’s function and repair her face. The procedures require months of rehabilitative therapy, Madeleine and her family wrote in a statement online after Madeleine cut her honeymoon short to be at her mother’s side.

Hamerski spent six years voluntarily managing social media in her community for Moms Demand Action for Gun Sense in America, a nonprofit. In that role, she shared news and research about gun safety in the US, The Guardian said. Hamerski made clear that her advocacy remained at the forefront as she faced a looming recovery, and she wrote from her hospital bed because the injury left her unable to speak.

As of Tuesday, more than 415 mass shootings had been reported nationwide, The Guardian said. The outlet described mass shootings colloquially as incidents in which four or more victims are either wounded or killed. The high numbers of such cases reported perennially in the US have prompted many, including those aligned with Moms Demand Action, to call for more substantial federal gun control.

Wilcox said Congress in Washington DC had not implemented such measures over the years. She said the resulting environment could leave people feeling that “you can’t go anywhere [outside your home] and feel safe.” She said it feels as if “it can happen to any of us.” “And it’s happening so frequently that more and more innocent people are losing their lives or suffering massive injury.”

Hamerski put that concern in writing because she could not speak after being shot. Her son, Sam Hamerski, shared a picture of the handwritten note with Minnesota news station KARE. Sam Hamerski told KARE, “It’s ironic that … the people [who] do the most and care the most are often the people that are affected”.

In a social media post, Moms Demand Action said gun violence does more than change lives; it “reverberates throughout families and communities, leaving survivors and their loved ones to navigate unimaginable physical, emotional, and financial hardships”. The group also said, “We are in awe of Susan who, through her pain and suffering, found a way to make her voice heard”.

Hamerski is a novelist and poet who has worked as a Minnesota state public health department communications specialist and has served as a governing board member of the Illusion Theater in Minneapolis. As word of her shooting circulated, The Guardian reported substantial public support for her.

An online campaign meant to help Hamerski cover her medical expenses had raised more than $210,000 from about 2,600 individual donations as of Tuesday. The campaign’s page said Wrigley was back home and safe. It also said the money would pay costs described as “enormous,” including getting Hamerski back to Minnesota from Washington DC, ongoing medical care and financial support while she recovered and was unable to work.

Wilcox said the fundraising had been a boon for Hamerski and her loved ones as she mustered all the strength she could to navigate her recovery. She said, “Susan is the type of person who you say, ‘If it had to happen to anyone.’” She added, “Because she’s doing the best she can, and she’s a really strong individual – and she’s going to need to be to survive this.”

Moms Demand Action wrote: “We hold Susan and her loved ones in our hearts as they come to terms with their new reality as survivors of gun violence. We will keep fighting in her honor, working toward a future where no family has to endure this kind of horror and devastation.”
