## Pledge opposes EU, single market and customs union membership

Reform UK leader Nigel Farage helped launch Keep Britain Out, a campaign against the United Kingdom rejoining the European Union. Farage was the first MP to sign the campaign's “Pledge for Britain” and said he expected other Reform UK MPs and candidates to sign. The launch reunited Farage with Arron Banks, his Brexit-supporting ally, who co-founded the Leave.EU campaign during the 2016 referendum.

The pledge contains “10 commitments” for elected officials to follow, including opposition to rejoining the European Union, its single market or a customs union. The pledge card lists 10 issues, including asking signatories to “oppose mass migration” and support for Britain's farming and fishing industries.

Keep Britain Out launched as Prime Minister Andy Burnham explored closer ties with the European Union after agreeing a deal with the bloc covering British food exports, emissions trading and a youth experience scheme. Burnham has argued that Brexit has done “more harm than good.” He has suggested that future options could include rejoining the customs union, the single market or holding a fresh referendum on full EU membership.

Burnham has ruled out major changes to the United Kingdom's relationship with the European Union before the next general election. He said he would stick to Labour's 2024 manifesto pledge not to return to the single market, customs union or freedom of movement. On Monday, Burnham said there was “no fixed destination” for the talks and that rejoining the bloc without a referendum would be “unthinkable.”

At a press conference on Wednesday, Farage said the 2016 referendum was the “most divisive thing I have seen in families, in communities.” He said he hoped the next general election would “kills this stone dead.”

Farage told reporters: “I didn't pick this fight, the prime minister has picked this fight. As far as I was concerned it was done and dusted.” He added: “But if battle has to be joined I will join it gleefully. What he's doing is madness, it's not necessary.”

Farage described Keep Britain Out as a “forward-looking, positive campaign about how Britain can be richer, more productive” outside the European Union. He suggested he expected other Reform UK MPs and candidates to sign the pledges.

Banks said he wanted pro-leave MPs to support the pledge. He added: “We hope some Labour MPs will see sense and sign it and even Conservatives.”

Asked whether he was funding the campaign and to what extent, Banks said a “whole series of donors” had “back in touch.” He added: “The last campaign was also funded by the British public, so I'm not going to go into who's donating what at this stage.”

Keep Britain Out follows the formation of another anti-EU campaign called Stay Out, which has received support from Lord Gove, the chairman of the Vote Leave campaign in 2016. Meanwhile, the European Movement is helping launch a rival campaign to rejoin the European Union, called Get In.


The [UK-EU reset agreement](/articles/2026-11-20-uk-and-eu-agree-reset-deal-burnham-seeks-options-for-closer-ties/) covered British food exports, emissions trading and a youth experience scheme, while [MSI previously reported](/articles/2026-11-19-uk-brexit-debate-returns-as-burnham-prepares-eu-options/) that Burnham was preparing options for Britain's long-term relationship with the European Union. Farage's campaign launch then advanced that debate by organizing opposition to rejoining the EU, the single market or a customs union.
