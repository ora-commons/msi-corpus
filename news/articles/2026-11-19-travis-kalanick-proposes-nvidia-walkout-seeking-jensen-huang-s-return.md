## Supporters say Huang is vital to a thriving U.S. open-source AI ecosystem

Travis Kalanick said Wednesday at the TomorrowX Summit, an investor conference in Austin, Texas, that he would appear at Nvidia’s offices with a “wafer encrusted megaphone” and lead employees in a walkout supporting Jensen Huang. The proposal came as Nvidia’s board said Huang would not return as chief executive and its board chairman said the company had no intention of bringing him back, while Silicon Valley technology figures continued to press for his reinstatement.

Kalanick compared Huang’s situation with his own fight to remain Uber’s chief executive before resigning under pressure in 2017. He said, “Let’s fight for what’s right and give it a best shot,” then added, “We don’t have control of the outcome, but let’s f—ing fight.” Kalanick could not immediately be reached for comment.

Kalanick walked on stage wearing a black leather jacket. Antonio Gracias, a close friend and longtime investor of Elon Musk, stood next to him and also wore a black leather jacket. “Yours looks a lot like Jensen’s,” Gracias said, referring to Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang. The report said Huang had made a black leather jacket his uniform and characterized him as having been ousted last week as chief executive of the chip maker. A market quote accompanying the report showed Nvidia shares down 2.89%. “Yeah, we gotta bring Jensen back,” Kalanick said.

Nvidia’s board said Huang would not return as chief executive, and Nvidia’s board chairman said the company had no intention of bringing him back. The Wall Street Journal reported that Huang had been ousted last week as chief executive following reports of affairs with two women, one of whom U.S. officials suspected was passing information to Chinese intelligence during their relationship.

After what The Wall Street Journal described as his ouster last week, Huang made his first public remarks on Tuesday, saying he would be willing to return as Nvidia chief executive if Nvidia’s board believed “my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America.”

Huang’s supporters argued that the former Nvidia chief executive was pro-American and essential to ensuring that the U.S. had a thriving open-source AI ecosystem. They have also questioned why Huang was forced out before an investigation announced by the board delivered its findings.

At the New York Times DealBook Summit, also on Wednesday, technology figures took turns praising Huang. Billionaire Elon Musk compared Huang’s ouster to “kicking Michael Jordan off your team when you’re on a winning streak.” Appearing remotely from China, Musk also reiterated past comments he wrote on X that he would hire Huang.

Greg Brockman, president of OpenAI, described Huang as “singular,” while OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman said it was “hard to imagine the AI industry without Jensen.” Palmer Luckey, founder of Anduril Industries, said that “nobody can take Jensen’s place in this critical juncture.”

Ron Conway, a venture capitalist and founder of SV Angels, wrote on X, “Has NVIDIA’s Board lost perspective?” Conway continued: “The Board prejudged him even before their own internal investigation was completed. Why push out a visionary American founder?”

Bill Ackman, founder of Pershing Square, also criticized Nvidia’s directors on X. “The board’s conduct here is shocking for a company of this scale and importance to our country and the world at large, particularly at this moment in history,” he wrote on X.
