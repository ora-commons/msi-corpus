## Researchers estimate Cuba could lose a full quarter over five years

A Wall Street Journal team traveled to Cuba to document how the island's rapidly shrinking population is changing daily life amid an economic crisis. The report says mounting U.S. sanctions, a fuel embargo and a failing state apparatus are worsening conditions, and it presents the population decline as a potentially even more catastrophic problem.

Some researchers cited by the report estimate that Cuba's population loss could amount to a full quarter of the island's inhabitants over a five-year stretch. The report presents that figure as a possibility rather than a completed loss.

The Wall Street Journal calls the potential scale virtually unprecedented for a country that is not at war. It says the demographic change has enormous implications for both Cuba's present and future.

The team said it traveled to the island to see firsthand how the mass exodus was reshaping daily life. According to the report, the exodus also complicates any hopes for an economic turnaround.

The report places the population decline alongside deteriorating conditions and describes the exodus as making the economic crisis worse. That makes the demographic shift part of the central account of Cuba's economic future rather than a separate measure of migration.

The immediate pressures described in the report remain specific: U.S. sanctions are mounting, a fuel embargo is in place and the state apparatus is failing. Against that setting, the report documents a rapid population decline and says the exodus is changing everyday conditions while complicating Cuba's prospects for an economic turnaround.

The new report advances that account by focusing on how population loss adds another pressure while hopes for an economic turnaround remain complicated.
