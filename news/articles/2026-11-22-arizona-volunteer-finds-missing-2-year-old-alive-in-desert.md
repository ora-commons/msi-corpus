## Sheriff presents searcher with plaque and honorary posse badge

A two-year-old girl who walked away from the front yard of her family's home in the desert community of Eloy, Arizona, was found alive hours later curled up under a bush, roughly two miles (3.2km) from where she had last been seen, after a volunteer joined the search and spotted her by flashlight.

Jay Stewart, described as both a father and a grandfather, was among the community members who volunteered to comb the remote desert after Vella Campillo disappeared at about 5pm on 17 November wearing only a diaper, according to authorities who spoke to local media. Vella's parents, Joseph Campillo and Deanna Fritz, later told news station KSAZ that Vella was playing with her siblings and being cared for by another family member when she ran off.

The search drew dozens of law enforcement officers and community volunteers.

At about 12.15am on 18 November, with temperatures cold at about 50F (10C), Stewart was shining a flashlight through the darkness along a ditch lined with bushes. Vella suddenly "popped out" of one of the bushes, Stewart later told reporters. She was shivering. Stewart said she came up to him and jumped into his arms, and he called first responders to alert them to his discovery.

"I'll never forget it as long as I live," Stewart said at a news conference, recalling the moment he found her. "I've never wanted to find something so bad in my life – or somebody to find her, anybody to find her."

A deputy with the Pinal County Sheriff's Office soon reached Stewart and Vella, and the deputy's body-worn camera recorded the scene. "That's where she was at, right in that spot – I was just walking the ditch," a visibly relieved Stewart told the deputy, pointing to a nearby bush with the shirtless girl in his arms.

"Are you OK? Are you so cold?" the deputy could be heard saying on the video as he covered her in a blanket. Stewart tried to hand Vella to the deputy, saying, "She's got a hold of me." He managed it after the deputy gently asked her, "Can you let go? We're going to move you over here and sit you in [a] chair." Stewart reassured her: "I'll go with you."

Paramedics brought Vella to a hospital for a medical evaluation, and she was soon back with her family.

At a news conference later that day, Sheriff Ross Teeple said the timeliness of Stewart's intervention was vital. Prolonged exposure to the temperatures Vella experienced while wearing only a diaper during her night alone in the desert could lead to hypothermia, which is life-threatening.

"Very remote areas like this can get extremely cold very fast," Teeple said. "And she's a small body producing very little heat."

Teeple's office later published a video on social media showing the sheriff presenting Stewart with a plaque recognizing him as an outstanding citizen. He also gave Stewart a badge distinguishing him as an honorary member of the office's search and rescue posse.

In the video, Teeple said he had heard Stewart express discomfort at being labeled a hero. Even so, the sheriff said, "I'm here to tell you that when you watch the body camera footage, it's obvious you're that little girl's hero. And today you're Pinal county's hero."

Fritz, Vella's mother, told KSAZ the disappearance marked "the worst day of our entire lives." She said the family was "beyond blessed for the man [who] found her," and thanked everyone else involved in the search.

Stewart said he would treasure the tokens of appreciation the sheriff presented, and that Vella's parents had no need to thank him. "It's what we do," he said at the news conference.

"The scariest thing for parents is to lose a child," Stewart said. "I can't imagine what they were going through."

He said everyone searching for Vella was "doing it for the same reason."

"We loved her and wanted her back – she may not be our child, but she could be," he said.
