## NTSB investigating Copa 737 and UPS 767 ground collision

Federal aviation safety officials said on Sunday that they were investigating a minor ground collision at Miami International Airport, where the wingtip of a Copa Airlines Boeing 737 made contact with the nose of a UPS Boeing 767 cargo aircraft at a taxiway intersection. Two people reported minor injuries.

The incident took place at about 9 p.m. Saturday, according to the National Transportation Safety Board and Copa Airlines. The Copa jet is a Boeing 737 configured to carry passengers, and it had 172 people aboard at the time; the UPS aircraft is a Boeing 767 freighter.

Photographs taken at the scene depicted the UPS jet's nose with a horizontal gash along one side and a larger hole in its center.

Audio recorded by ATC.com captured a pilot on the UPS plane telling air traffic control that he had gone too far at the intersection and that the Copa flight had clipped his aircraft. Controllers told the UPS pilot that he was supposed to yield to the Copa plane.

The NTSB said it was gathering information about the incident and might issue an update on Sunday.

Officials said the Copa aircraft had just flown in from Panama City, while the UPS plane was bound for Bogota, Colombia.

Copa Airlines addressed the event in a statement posted on social media, describing the collision as minor and reporting that two of its passengers received treatment at the scene for minor injuries.

UPS said it was cooperating with authorities to establish what occurred. "We cannot speculate on the details of the incident," UPS said in a statement. "The National Transportation Safety Board is responsible for investigating incidents like this and will control the flow of information."

The mishap came two months after an Amazon cargo plane careened off a runway at Miami International and smashed into a van carrying workers for a company that cleans airplanes, killing five people.
