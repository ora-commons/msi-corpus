## McIver is the only elected representative facing charges

The Third Circuit Court of Appeals on Thursday declined to rehear its August decision allowing Rep. LaMonica McIver to face charges over the May 2025 confrontation outside the Delaney Hall immigration detention center in New Jersey. The action leaves the Democratic representative likely to face trial and keeps the three-judge panel’s ruling in place.

McIver had sought dismissal on various grounds, including her argument that the Constitution protects her from prosecution. The court rejected her arguments in August, and Thursday’s action rejected her request that the full bench of active judges reconsider the panel’s decision. McIver has described the prosecution as a political attack by the Trump administration.

“Because of a weaponized Department of Justice, and because I dared to do my job and conduct oversight of ICE, I am facing 17 years in prison,” McIver said in a Thursday statement. She continued: “Now, due to the disappointing decision by the third circuit, we are all one step closer to a world in which the president of the United States can try to imprison people simply because he does not like what they stand for.” McIver also said: “I hope that everyone who feels that way will stand with me in my fight against these unjust charges, because while I am the first lawmaker they have tried to jail, I will surely not be the last.”

McIver was charged last year with assaulting, resisting, impeding and interfering with federal officers. The prosecution has produced a lengthy legal battle as McIver has attempted to have the case dismissed on several grounds.

The case stems from a congressional oversight visit to Delaney Hall in May 2025. McIver arrived with two other members of Congress and Newark mayor Ras Baraka, but the officials were barred from entering the detention center. Outside the facility, a federal agent attempted to arrest Baraka because he was refusing to leave the premises.

According to a homeland security agent’s declaration, McIver intervened to prevent Baraka’s arrest. Federal officials provided a more specific account, claiming that McIver “slammed her forearm” into the homeland security agent and “forcibly” struck another deportation officer with her forearms.

Delaney Hall has drawn repeated scrutiny since it returned to detaining immigrants arrested by Immigration and Customs Enforcement in 2025. At least three people detained at the facility have died. The facility also was the site of major clashes with ICE earlier this year during protests outside in support of a hunger strike by detained immigrants.

McIver raised conditions inside Delaney Hall and the number of people who had died in ICE custody during a House committee hearing on Wednesday with Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin. “As you know, I am facing 17 years in prison, from this Trump administration, for my work at Delaney,” McIver said on Wednesday, then asked Mullin: “Do you even understand why I have been sounding the alarm about Delaney for so long?” Mullin said he had visited the facility and that it was in “very good condition.” McIver told him, “History is going to judge you for what you are doing.” She then said, “You are killing people. You are killing people.” Mullin said those claims were false.

McIver is the only elected representative facing charges from last year’s Delaney Hall confrontation, while charges against Newark mayor Ras Baraka were dropped. “This result is frustrating, but my lawyers and I are looking at every possible option for what comes next,” McIver said.
