## Warren cites blockade and Bahrain damage figures

Pentagon budget official Arthur Miller gave the Senate Armed Services Committee the latest overall estimate of the Iran war: $48.3 billion as of Nov. 13. The committee was considering Miller’s nomination to be Navy comptroller when he presented the estimate Thursday. The figure includes military operations and the cost of replacing equipment and munitions, but not damage to U.S. bases attacked in the Middle East.

Democratic Sen. Richard Blumenthal of Connecticut pressed Miller for an estimate of damage to U.S. installations. Miller said he did not have that estimate. Republican Sen. Roger Wicker of Mississippi, the committee’s chair, directed Miller to try to provide an answer as soon as possible.

An Associated Press analysis found that Iran attacked aircraft hangars, systems that detect incoming strikes, warehouses, troop housing and more, causing billions of dollars in equipment damage. Democratic lawmakers are pressing the Pentagon to release estimates of the damage Iran has done to American bases in the Middle East through missile and drone attacks.

Congressional debates over the war’s cost have intensified as the Trump administration asks lawmakers to pass a $1.5 trillion defense budget. The Associated Press reported that the unpopular war and its economic effects contributed to severe Republican losses in the midterm elections. The AP said financial battles over the conflict will intensify once Democrats take control of Congress in January for the remainder of President Donald Trump’s term.

Sen. Elizabeth Warren, a Democrat from Massachusetts, questioned acting Navy Secretary Hung Cao on the costs of the war at his confirmation hearing Tuesday for the permanent Navy secretary job. Citing independent estimates and media reports, Warren said the U.S. naval blockade on Iranian shipping costs about $32.5 million a day. Cao confirmed the blockade figure.

Warren also said an Iranian attack on the Navy’s regional headquarters in Bahrain did at least $400 million in damage. Cao said he could not offer a full damage assessment in Bahrain because “it’s a nonpermissive area for us to go.”

Months earlier, Cao told a reporter that Iran “blew the hell out of Bahrain.”

After the hearing, the committee still lacked a Pentagon estimate of damage to U.S. installations. The unresolved figure was the cost of the missile and drone attacks on American bases, and Wicker had directed Miller to try to provide an answer as soon as possible.
