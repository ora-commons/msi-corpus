## California sends 1,300 hospital beds and millions of bandages to Ukraine

**Subtype:** fact

- Gov. Gavin Newsom announced Monday that California is donating 1,300 hospital beds, more than 11 million bandages and other surplus medical supplies to Ukraine after traveling to the country.
- Newsom said he visited the Ukrainian city of Lviv on Sunday, toured a rehabilitation center for people injured in the war, visited defense technology companies and saw the graves of Ukrainian soldiers.
- The California shipment includes a 50-bed field hospital with oxygen concentrators, wheelchairs and wound-care materials, plus 20 portable generators and thousands of wound-care kits.
- European Union countries accused Russia on Monday of trying to drag out talks on ending the war while it bombs power plants and other infrastructure as winter nears.

## Newsom visited Lviv before announcing the surplus-equipment donation

The governor said the donated equipment comes from surplus supplies in California and builds upon previous donations made earlier in the war. Earlier in the war, California provided five field medical stations, more than 4,300 ballistic vests and nearly 1,600 helmets, the governor's office said.

"The people of Ukraine did not choose this war," Newsom said in a statement, adding: "No people fighting for their freedom should have to wonder whether they stand alone. California stands with Ukraine."

The supplies from California include a 50-bed field hospital, which includes oxygen concentrators, wheelchairs and wound-care materials. The state also is providing more than 1,300 additional hospital beds, more than 11 million bandages, thousands of wound-care kits and 20 portable generators.

Newsom said he also visited the graves of Ukrainian soldiers, toured a rehabilitation center for people injured in the war and visited defense technology companies.

Newsom, the Democratic governor from the largest U.S. state, is preparing to leave office after eight years and is considering a run for U.S. president in 2028.

The donation lands as the diplomatic picture around the war remains unsettled. Republican President Donald Trump has recently played up chances for a settlement to Russia's war in Ukraine. But European Union countries on Monday accused Russia of trying to drag out talks on ending the war while it bombs power plants and other infrastructure as winter nears.

In October, the White House said first lady Melania Trump had brokered a ceasefire to help with the delivery of humanitarian aid and to allow civilians to evacuate from a war-torn area.
