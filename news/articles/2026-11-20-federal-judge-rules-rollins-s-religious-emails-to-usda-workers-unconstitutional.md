## Ruling permits faith discussions with interested colleagues but requires Rollins to stop disseminating religious messages if a colleague asks her to stop

U.S. District Judge Trina Thompson in San Francisco ruled Thursday that religious messages Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins emailed to the Department of Agriculture's 100,000 employees from her official email account violated the First Amendment's establishment clause. She said Rollins must be careful to avoid proselytizing when communicating with workers. The ruling came in a case filed in May.

On 3 November, a photograph by Andrew Harnik/Getty Images showed Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins speaking to reporters on the North Lawn of the White House.

Thompson distinguished between broad emails circulated through the department and religious discussions with interested colleagues. Her ruling identifies the discussions as permissible while restricting the broad distribution of religious messages.

Thompson found that employees could not realistically opt out of reading emails from their agency director. She said the messages could coerce workers to hide their own faith or feel pressure to adopt Rollins's Christian faith. Her reasoning cited Rollins's position of authority, her use of an official email account and the messages' suggestion that Christianity was the shared faith of USDA workers.

Thompson also drew on a Department of Agriculture policy on workplace religious expression that Rollins had adopted. The policy's commands were: “Cannot create a hostile work environment through persistent unwelcome advances” and “cannot use official authority to pressure subordinates”. She ruled that when disseminating religious messages, Rollins must stop if a colleague asks her to.

Thompson also said Rollins may discuss her faith with interested colleagues, share why her faith is meaningful to her, politely invite co-workers to religious events and express her views in appropriate conversations, again quoting directly from Rollins' policy.

Rollins sent the messages from her official account on holidays including Independence Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas and Easter. The emails contained Christian messages and images, and she did not send messages commemorating holidays from other religions.

In an email sent on 5 April to mark Easter, Rollins wrote: “And so, like the very first disciples to encounter our risen Lord in the Upper Room almost two thousand years ago, this Easter let us too be alive with hope, full of Paschal joy, and confident in the mission each of us has been called for.”

Six Department of Agriculture employees and the National Federation of Federal Employees, a labor union representing 19,000 Department of Agriculture workers, brought the suit in May.

The Department of Agriculture did not respond to a request for comment.
