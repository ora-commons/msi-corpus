## Fischer heirs and Blaquier-Arrieta family settle dispute before auction

Sotheby's sold Vincent van Gogh's oil painting "Châtaigniers en fleurs" ("Chestnut Trees in Blossom") for a record $190.4 million (£144.8 million) at an auction in New York on Wednesday. The result surpassed Van Gogh's previous auction record of $117.2 million, set in 2022.

The record sale followed an agreement between the Blaquier-Arrieta family and heirs of the Fischer family, the prior German owners, over a claim that the painting may have been sold under duress during the rise of the Nazis. Sotheby's announced in the hours before the auction that the dispute had been resolved.

Representatives for the Fischer heirs had claimed that the painting may have been sold under duress. A lawyer for the heirs reportedly wrote to Sotheby's, saying the work had been sold in Amsterdam before World War Two because of financial losses suffered by the Fischer family's publishing company under the Nazis. The BBC contacted the lawyer for comment.

"In recognition of the painting's complex Nazi-era history and the suffering endured by the Fischer family during the Holocaust, the two families have reached a mutually satisfactory resolution and the work will be sold pursuant to their agreement," Sotheby's said on Wednesday.

Sotheby's website details the painting's provenance and the auction house's research into how the artwork changed hands since 1890. It says German publisher Samuel Fischer acquired the painting in 1909, after which it passed down through his family following his death in 1934.

Before going to Henriette and Marcel Kapferer in Paris in 1936, the painting was deposited with Paul Cassirer in Amsterdam in 1932 or 1933. Four other owners held it afterward.

The Blaquier family acquired the painting in 1975 and owned it for more than half a century before the latest sale, according to Sotheby's.

Van Gogh painted the work in 1890 during the final two months of his life. Sotheby's said the painting is considered one of the most important works of art from that period to remain in private hands.

The oil painting was offered as part of a set called the Blaquier-Arrieta Collection. Before the sale, Sotheby's estimated its value at $180 million (£136 million).

The buyer was not immediately known. The BBC contacted Sotheby's for comment.

At an auction preview in Hong Kong last month, Sotheby's staff handled the painting, according to a BBC image caption.
