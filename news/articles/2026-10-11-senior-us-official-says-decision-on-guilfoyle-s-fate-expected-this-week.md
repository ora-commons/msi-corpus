## Second US official says inspector general is looking into allegations against envoy

President Donald Trump and Secretary of State Marco Rubio are considering firing the US ambassador to Greece, Kimberly Guilfoyle, after a series of reports alleging improper and possibly illegal conduct before and during her tenure in Athens. A senior US official said Sunday that Trump and Rubio are “extremely pissed off” by Guilfoyle's reported conduct and would likely decide what action to take this week, after Monday's federal holiday.

The official, who spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss internal deliberations, said Rubio had been particularly angry, in part because he visited Athens last week and Guilfoyle attended most, if not all, of his events, greeting him on arrival with a bear hug as the allegations swirled. The same official said Rubio had grown increasingly frustrated as the reports multiplied.

Since late September, the Wall Street Journal has detailed numerous accounts of questionable conduct by Guilfoyle, including an alleged threat to a host country official, accepting improper gifts and services, and offering to use her influence inside the administration in return for the payment of a large credit card bill. The Journal's first report said Guilfoyle told guests at a reception that the US could undermine the Greek government if it chose to, promoted the business interests of one particular Greek construction and energy company, and allowed its lobbyist into private diplomatic meetings.

While Rubio was in Athens, the Journal reported — citing text messages — that Guilfoyle had asked a Trump donor and supporter to pay a $100,000 credit card bill just before her confirmation hearing, promising him access to administration officials if he did.

The Journal then reported that Guilfoyle had been enraged about not being invited to a Greek-themed event at the World Economic Forum in Davos, Switzerland, earlier this year, and demanded to attend while boasting about luxury travel to France with energy company executives. A separate report from the British tabloid The Daily Mail said Guilfoyle had alienated staff at the US embassy in Athens and had pushed the bounds of behavior appropriate for the top representative of the US government to Greece.

Guilfoyle has not addressed the reports directly. Her lawyer has said she did nothing wrong, and the US embassy in Athens has tacitly expressed support for her. After defending Guilfoyle following the first report, Rubio has declined to comment publicly on the growing allegations. On Wednesday he said: “Our ambassador has been a strong ambassador here.”

But after another report appeared late Wednesday, Rubio on 8 October berated journalists for asking about the allegations as he prepared to leave Athens for Portugal, saying he did not know whether they were true and that he would not respond if he could not confirm. At the foot of his plane, with Guilfoyle, who was there to see him depart, looking on, Rubio said: “You guys walk around thinking that because some source told you something and you reported it, it's the gospel. It's not the gospel. It's not the gospel for me. That's not the way we do things.”

A second US official, who also spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss personnel matters, said the State Department's inspector general is looking into the allegations, which first began surfacing in the Journal late last month and have since multiplied. The second official could not say whether a formal investigation had begun. Inspectors general often conduct informal inquiries into complaints against staffers, and many times those inquiries do not result in a full-on investigation, which can result in disciplinary action up to and including dismissal. The State Department has declined to comment on a potential inspector general investigation.

The top two Democrats on the House Foreign Affairs and Senate Foreign Relations committees — Representative Gregory Meeks of New York and Senator Jeanne Shaheen of New Hampshire — along with Robert Garcia, Democrat of California, the ranking member on the House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform, have called for an investigation into Guilfoyle.

Guilfoyle is a former Fox News Channel host and California prosecutor who was once engaged to Trump's son Donald Jr.
