## Romanch's Law is named for Romanch Mahajan

New York City's Mamdani administration announced the transition package on Tuesday, saying the city will establish a Horse Carriage Worker Industry Program offering job training, career counseling, entrepreneurship support, employer connections and tuition assistance. The package is aimed at about 200 affected workers, including medallion owners, carriage drivers and stable hands, and New York City says it will release the full transition plan by April 2027.

New York City plans to facilitate medallion buy-backs, provide stipends and offer career assistance. City officials also said they would engage with the Transport Workers Union, which represents carriage industry workers.

New York City also said it would commission a feasibility study examining electric carriages and electric pedal-assist bicycles as potential alternatives in Central Park. The study would also examine electric carriages along the park's perimeter.

On Thursday, Mayor Zohran Mamdani signed the bill. Known as Romanch's Law, the legislation will end the city's horse-drawn carriage industry by 1 June 2028 and mark the end of an industry that operated in New York City for more than a century. The law is named for Romanch Mahajan, an 18-year-old Indian tourist who was killed in June after being thrown from a carriage in Central Park when a horse became startled.

A photograph credited to Yuki Iwamura/AP shows a horse-drawn carriage taking passengers on a loop through Central Park on 14 October 2026 in New York.

Workers' unions opposed the legislation over concerns about lost livelihoods, while animal-rights activists backed the move, citing longstanding concerns over horse welfare, including cases in which horses have died from exhaustion.

During Thursday's signing, Mamdani said: “For 150 years, horse-drawn carriages have been a fixture of Central Park. Today, we are bringing that chapter to a close. This industry will come to an end, and we have a responsibility to the workers whose livelihoods depend on it. We will work alongside them to ensure they have the financial support and career opportunities they need to build their next chapter.”

New York City Council Speaker Julie Menin also described the legislation as a historic milestone. She said: “This is a truly historic day for New York City, nearly two decades after the council first considered ending the horse-drawn carriage industry.”

Menin connected the law to Mahajan's death and support for workers affected by the ban. She said: “Today, I’m thinking of Romanch Mahajan, who should still be with us and whose tragic, untimely death reminds us why this legislation was so urgently needed. This law will protect the welfare of horses while ensuring that the hard-working New Yorkers who depend on this industry have the support they need to transition to new employment opportunities.”
