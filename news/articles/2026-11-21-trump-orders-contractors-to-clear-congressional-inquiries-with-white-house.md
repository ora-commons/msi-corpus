## Firms that refuse could lose federal contracts and future work

Donald Trump is directing federal contractors and subcontractors to check with his administration over any inquiries they receive from Congress, and to withhold any communications the White House considers privileged, according to an executive order issued late Friday.

The order requires the companies to coordinate with the White House and US agencies on "appropriate responses" to such requests, according to the order as described by The Guardian.

Compliance carries an explicit price. The White House warns that contractors who fail to follow the order could see their contracts terminated and their future work with the federal government suspended.

The order applies to all contractors and subcontractors — tens of thousands of businesses that hold federal agreements.

It was signed as Democrats prepare to take over Congress in January. Democrats have made government contracts one of their top investigative targets, noting that federal money has been flowing to companies linked with Trump's family, particularly at the Pentagon.

The administration has moved on other fronts as well. On Monday, Will Scharf, the White House counsel, told US agencies that they must seek approval from his office and the White House's office of legislative affairs before agreeing to appear before Congress.

Scharf also suggested in a recent letter to Representative Robert Garcia, the top Democrat on the House oversight committee, that some congressional investigations may amount to "blatant criminality."

Garcia earlier this week sent letters directing members of Trump's family and top administration officials to preserve documents.

Scharf sought to dismiss those requests, noting in a letter to Democratic lawmakers that they are not yet in the majority and cannot compel compliance.

In a fact sheet, the White House framed the new requirements as "protecting executive privileges from congressional overreach," arguing that some congressional leaders have tried to intrude on executive branch privileges by "harassing private companies that contract with the federal government."

Presidents of both parties have long used executive privilege to resist congressional inquiries. The protection allows the president to keep information from the courts, Congress and the public in order to protect the confidentiality of White House deliberations, but it is far from absolute, and claims to it can set up messy, protracted legal fights as the parties dispute whether it applies.

Friday's order extends that fight beyond the government itself, to the private companies that do business with it.
