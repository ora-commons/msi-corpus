## Vance cites Trump’s Iran stance; Rubio defends meetings with China and Russia

At Friday’s 90-minute White House press briefing, Vance went first before Trump and a room crowded with dozens of journalists. He explained that staying in Washington was part of his job while Trump, Rubio and others attended international summits in the Philippines and China. Vance said he had to “stay back and hold down the fort,” like the movie “Home Alone,” but “with better food and more gold” at the White House, then told Trump, “So, I missed you, sir,” before praising his economic leadership.

Trump said Vance’s statement distancing himself from the war in Iran and blaming it for GOP midterm losses was “fine” and “accurate.” Vance passed on an opportunity to say the Iran war was worth the political costs. “I think the president is the one who makes the decision, and the president has said he would do it again,” Vance said.

When Rubio took the microphone, he spoke in his typical rapid-fire clip as he recapped Trump’s Asia trip and forcefully defended Trump’s decision to meet the leaders of China and Russia. Rubio’s tone suggested irritation at a question no one had asked. He argued that good results for the country and global safety required positive relationships between the leaders: “If these leaders can’t get along and can’t engage with one another and don’t have a positive relationship, it is impossible to get anything good done for the country and the safety of the world,” he said.

Trump has not settled the succession question. The race to succeed him as the GOP nominee is quietly drawing attention, with Vance and Rubio widely seen as some of the likeliest contenders even though both have demurred about whether they will seek the White House in the next presidential race. The two maintain that they are good friends, and Rubio has said he will not run if Vance does.

Even after the GOP was defeated in the midterm elections, a result widely seen as a repudiation of Trump, the president continues to enjoy deep support from the Republican base. His endorsement is likely to carry considerable sway among 2028 primary voters. Trump has praised Vance and Rubio and suggested they might be running mates but has not selected one over the other.

When a journalist asked which of the two men would make a better president, Trump responded deadpan, “Oh, let me give you the answer to that.” The reporter moved on to his next question.

In the same room months earlier, Rubio and Vance each took a turn filling in as White House press secretary, defending the president and promoting his agenda. The Associated Press characterized Friday as the latest installment in a “public audition” that Trump, a former reality television star, appeared to delight in arranging for his potential successors as he neared the final two years of his presidency. Trump has also been known to quiz public and private audiences about their opinions of the two men.

Trump called Vance and Rubio to the lectern one at a time, shifting his gaze between each man and the audience as he surveyed how their remarks landed with the dozens of journalists in the room. Vance and Rubio stood nearly shoulder to shoulder and about an arm’s length from Trump for the 90-minute event, wearing matching outfits. As Trump spoke, Vance laughed at his jokes but stood mostly still, hands clasped in front of him and a serious expression on his face. Vance smiled and furrowed his brow at points. Rubio fidgeted and sighed, turned from side to side, adjusted his tie and pulled a note from his breast pocket to review.

Vance and Rubio stood behind Trump for the entire hour and a half. During the State of the Union address each winter, the House speaker and vice president flank Trump, their faces on camera the entire time, but at least get to sit. Friday’s appearance lasted longer than nearly every State of the Union speech on record. The Associated Press said there was no doubt both men were keenly aware they remained on camera throughout.

Their navy blue suits with white shirts and red ties created, intentionally or not, a symmetrical backdrop for Trump, aside from Vance’s height advantage. Rubio’s rectangular lapel pin bore the stars and stripes, while Vance’s displayed a flag swaying on a pole.

At the end of the briefing, Trump called up his new chief of staff, Stephen Miller, who praised the president and ran through a list of accomplishments. Trump then joked about Vance and Rubio: “We have breaking news — he was better than these two guys.” Turning to Miller, he asked, “Do you want to run for president?”

Friday left the two men’s relative position unresolved: both defended Trump, and the president did not choose one over the other.

Jonathan J. Cooper reported from Phoenix. Associated Press reporters Darlene Superville, Bill Barrow and Ali Swenson contributed.
