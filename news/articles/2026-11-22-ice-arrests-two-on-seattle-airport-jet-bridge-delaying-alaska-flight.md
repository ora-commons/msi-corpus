## Airline says officers gave no advance notice before boarding began

Masked federal immigration officers detained two people on the jet bridge of a Los Angeles-bound Alaska Airlines flight at Seattle-Tacoma International Airport on Saturday, causing what the airline called a distressing scene for passengers and employees and delaying the departure.

Video of the incident shows officers dragging away at least one person on the jet bridge as a woman screams that her daughter is being taken and that she has a work permit. The officers wore unmarked jackets and khaki pants, and the video showed them struggling with someone on the ground as passengers surrounded by rolling suitcases looked on.

Immigration and Customs Enforcement said in a statement that its officers conducted a targeted enforcement operation to arrest Ricardo Larrondo Mendoza, an immigrant from Mexico who "overstayed his visa in violation of our nation's laws."

During the confrontation, another passenger "hurled hot coffee on the officers," the ICE statement said. The agency said the woman, Sofia Hernandez Mejia, was determined to be in the U.S. illegally from Mexico and to have also overstayed her visa. She could be charged with assaulting a federal officer, according to ICE.

Online flight data showed Alaska flight 1306, which had been scheduled to depart at 8:10 a.m., was delayed. The arrest occurred during a busy travel weekend ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday.

Alaska Airlines said ICE officers gave no advance notice of the enforcement action.

"We are concerned by this morning's law enforcement action during the boarding of Alaska Flight 1306," the airline said. "We recognize how distressing this incident has been for affected employees and guests."

The airline said it is committed to "treating every guest with dignity and respect." Alaska said it was working with the Port of Seattle, which manages the airport, to determine whether protocols were followed, and that it had raised its concerns "directly with the appropriate authorities."

The Port of Seattle said in a statement that the arrest was distressing not only for the affected family but for other passengers, and that local authorities would "engage federal officials to understand more about what occurred."

ICE has stepped up arrests at airports since the summer, especially targeting people who overstayed visas in the past.

Washington Gov. Bob Ferguson, a Democrat, said ICE's tactics were "deeply un-American" and called for the agency's abolition.

"The video is horrific and it is chilling," Ferguson said at an afternoon news conference with labor leaders and local elected officials. "We need a system of immigration enforcement and border security that is effective, ethical and humane. And ICE is not it. It is beyond reform."

Ferguson also said he was looking into how ICE agents accessed the jet bridge, and that he believes they violated a state law he signed in March limiting facial coverings on law enforcement officers.

April Sims, president of the Washington State Labor Council, AFL-CIO, which represents more than 600,000 workers, said union members were aboard the flight. She said the action disrupted travel during one of the busiest times of the year and only days after flights were grounded nationwide because of a communications problem.

"Airport workers are already managing extraordinary stress, passengers are already anxious and instead of restoring calm and confidence, federal agents introduced more fear and more chaos," she said.
