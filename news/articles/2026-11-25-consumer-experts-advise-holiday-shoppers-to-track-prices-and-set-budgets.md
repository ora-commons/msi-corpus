## Adobe forecasts $275 billion in online holiday sales and a record 63% mobile shopping share

Holiday shopping season is officially here, and consumer experts are offering practical advice for shoppers navigating major sales forecasts. The National Retail Federation forecasts this year's sales could top a record $1 trillion, even as many Americans report feeling the pain of rising prices and inflation.

Woroch and Pandya offered three tips for getting the best deals during the holiday shopping season. Woroch, a consumer saving and spending expert, said many Americans are still trying to make ends meet without abandoning holiday purchases. “Despite the fact that Americans are struggling to make ends meet, they are not willing to give up the holiday season,” Woroch said.

Adobe forecasts online holiday sales will hit $275 billion and predicts a record 63% of those sales will happen on mobile devices. Vivek Pandya, Adobe's lead insight analyst, said another change is emerging: consumers are using Cyber Week to stock up on everyday essentials. Adobe's data shows personal hygiene product sales are expected to jump 150% this year.

This year's doorbuster selection will be narrower than in past years. “We won't see as wide a selection of doorbusters like we have in the past,” Woroch said. Woroch identified products that have plugs as Black Friday deal candidates, including small personal gadgets, small kitchen appliances, smart home devices, beauty tools such as blow dryers and household cleaning products such as vacuums.

Deep discounts on electronics and apparel peak during Cyber Week. This year, sales of everyday items will peak during that period as well. Pandya said consumers will “see these different types of categories being marked down, especially personal care products, cosmetics, and they're really using these moments as an opportunity to stock up for the year ahead.”

Her budgeting advice starts before shoppers encounter a sale. Woroch advises consumers to list the people they need to buy for, identify specific gift ideas and set a realistic budget that does not rely on credit cards or money taken from essential bills.

Woroch said a written plan can prevent overspending: “You're not shopping aimlessly and then leading to impulse purchases or overbuying. So that's gonna be a really important tool as you approach sales events like Black Friday and Cyber Monday.”

Price history can help shoppers distinguish a meaningful discount from a regular price. Woroch recommends checking current prices and historical pricing data from the last six months before the big shopping days. “See what things cost before the deal drops. This will just give you better information to make better buying decisions so that you can spot the really good deals when they do become available,” she said.

Woroch also offered a rule of thumb: if an item a shopper has been seeking is 30% off, the shopper should buy it. “Chances are it's not going to get much cheaper than that,” she said.

AI tools could play a larger role in shoppers' searches. Pandya expects traffic from AI platforms to retail sites to rise 140% from last year, partly because AI-powered chat and browser services can supercharge the hunt for deals.

With an AI shopping tool, Woroch suggested entering a product name and a request such as “compare prices across every retailer selling this item, apply any working coupon codes or free shipping offers to get me the lowest possible deal.” She also recommended asking, “Can you find me a cheaper option for a similar item?”

Those strategies helped Woroch save $20 on a gift from her own list this year. “Imagine you multiply that across all the gifts on your list, that can add up to big savings,” she said.
