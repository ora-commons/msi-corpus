## High wind and coastal flood warnings cover Thursday into Friday

The Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade has been moved to Saturday after NBC said Wednesday that New York City, Macy's and the broadcaster had postponed the annual event over extreme weather conditions. In a statement, NBC said the decision followed conversations with the city and concerns over the safety of participants and spectators.

"Due to the extreme weather conditions, the city of New York, Macy's and NBC have decided, in the interest of safety for both the participants and spectators, to move the Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade to Saturday," the statement said.

New York City emergency management officials expect the city to see wind gusts between 50 and 60 mph, or 80 to 96 kph, on Thursday, along with steady, sustained winds of 30 to 38 mph, or 48 to 61 kph. Moderate to heavy rain is also expected.

Separately, the National Weather Service issued a high wind warning and a coastal flood warning for Thursday into Friday.

NBC, which airs the parade, will instead broadcast a rerun of the 2025 edition on Thursday.

Wind has long been a specific safety concern for the parade's balloons. The city has long had measures in place that dictate whether to lower balloons or cancel their flights entirely if the wind gets too strong, because strong winds can knock them off course and into spectators along the parade route.

Wednesday's announcement sets the parade for Saturday and replaces NBC's Thursday broadcast with a rerun of the 2025 edition. The Saturday date also falls after the Thursday-into-Friday window covered by the National Weather Service's high wind and coastal flood warnings.
