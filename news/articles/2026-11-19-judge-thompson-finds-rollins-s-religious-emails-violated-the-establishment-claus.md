## Workplace limits bar official pressure and allow discussions with interested colleagues

A federal judge ruled Thursday that religious messages sent by U.S. Agriculture Secretary Brooke Rollins to the agency's 100,000 workers were unconstitutional and that she must be careful to avoid proselytizing when communicating with employees. U.S. District Judge Trina Thompson, who issued the ruling in San Francisco, said the emails violated the First Amendment's establishment clause, pointing to Rollins's position of authority, her use of an official email account and messages suggesting that Christianity was the shared faith of USDA workers.

Thompson said employees cannot realistically opt out of reading emails from their agency director and that the messages could coerce them to hide their own faith or feel pressure to adopt Rollins's Christian faith.

Thompson also placed explicit limits on future communications. When disseminating religious messages, Rollins must stop if a colleague asks her to stop. The judge ordered that Rollins “cannot create a hostile work environment through persistent unwelcome advances” and “cannot use official authority to pressure subordinates.”

The judge said those commands came directly from a USDA policy on workplace religious expression that Rollins herself adopted.

Rollins may discuss her faith with interested colleagues, share why her faith is meaningful to her, politely invite coworkers to religious events and express her views in appropriate conversations, Thompson ruled, again quoting directly from Rollins's policy. The judge said the stop requirement still applies when Rollins is disseminating religious messages.

Rollins sent Christian messages and images from her official email account on holidays such as Independence Day, Thanksgiving, Christmas and Easter. She did not send messages commemorating holidays from other religions.

In an April 5 email marking Easter, Rollins wrote: “And so, like the very first disciples to encounter our risen Lord in the Upper Room almost two thousand years ago, this Easter let us too be alive with hope, full of Paschal joy, and confident in the mission each of us has been called for.”

Six USDA employees and the National Federation of Federal Employees, a labor union representing 19,000 USDA workers, brought the suit in May. The USDA did not respond to a request for comment.
