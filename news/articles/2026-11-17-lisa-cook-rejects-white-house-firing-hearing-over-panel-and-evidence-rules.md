## Scharf had warned the hearing could proceed without Cook

Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook will not participate in a White House hearing on whether to fire her unless the administration changes the panel and the way evidence is shared, her lawyer said in a Monday letter. Abbe Lowell said Cook would be prepared to appear before an unbiased panel using fair procedures. The hearing is scheduled for Dec. 14.

The disagreement centers on whether the White House hearing can satisfy the Supreme Court's procedural requirements while giving Cook a fair opportunity to respond. Cook remains a Federal Reserve governor as her removal case proceeds before U.S. District Judge Jia Cobb.

In the letter to White House counsel William Scharf, Lowell said Cook “has no legal obligation to participate in an unfair hearing.” Lowell accused the White House of seeking “only to manufacture ‘cause’ for a predetermined removal outcome.”

Lowell said Kevin Hassett, director of the National Economic Council, had prejudged the case and should come off the three-member committee. Her objections also covered the schedule for evidence. The White House demanded Cook's documents immediately but promised to share its own evidence only seven days before the hearing, she said.

Pressure had increased before Monday's letter. In an Oct. 19 letter, Scharf told Cook that her obligation to follow the president's directive to attend outweighed her Federal Reserve duties, according to Lowell's account. Scharf warned that the hearing would proceed without Cook if she did not appear and that the committee could draw an adverse inference from her absence.

On Nov. 5, Scharf wrote that Cook was “not entitled to veto” the president's choice of committee members.

Trump had also criticized Cook's position on interest rates. On Oct. 10, he said to reporters that Cook was “an automatic no vote” on lowering interest rates, “and that’s not a good thing.” Cook voted against the Federal Reserve's decision to hold rates steady last month, joining Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack in supporting a rate increase.

The committee is the White House's attempt to meet the Supreme Court's conditions for firing Cook. U.S. District Judge Jia Cobb blocked Trump's first attempt to remove her in September 2025, ruling that Cook could keep her seat while her lawsuit proceeds.

On June 29, the Supreme Court upheld that order, finding that Cook could not be removed without at least some minimal procedural protections, including a chance to respond to the allegations.

Through written responses from her lawyers in August, Cook denied mortgage-fraud allegations raised by the White House. The case has since returned to Cobb, where Cook's original lawsuit is pending.

A White House spokesman did not immediately respond to a request for comment. Cook's refusal leaves the Dec. 14 hearing without her unless the administration meets her conditions, while the removal case remains before Cobb.
