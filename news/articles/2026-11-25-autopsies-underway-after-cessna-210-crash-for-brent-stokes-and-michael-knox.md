## Flight data showed rapid right-hand descent from around 9,000 feet

Jackson County Coroner Bruce Lynd Jr. said autopsies were being performed on pilot Brent Stokes and Michael Knox, who was also a licensed pilot. Lynd identified Stokes as the 50-year-old pilot and president of a Fort Myers-based marine contracting company. The six victims were Stokes; his 16-year-old son, Hunter Stokes; his sister, Arlyn Knox, 43; her husband, Michael Knox, 43; and their sons, Charles Holden Knox, 12, and Truett Knox, 10. The FAA said Stokes and Knox had received private pilot certificates in 2023.

Relatives said the six family members were returning home after a fishing trip. The Knox and Stokes families confirmed the deaths in an emailed statement provided to The Associated Press. “They died doing what they loved: celebrating the start of a wonderful holiday season with a fishing trip together, before heading home to be with the rest of our family,” the statement said. “In the middle of our grief, we are holding on to that, and to how much love they gave us and how much love surrounds us now. We will carry them with us always.”

Stokes' company's website described him as an “avid fisherman and life time boater.” Arlyn and Michael Knox ran Knox Technologies, a Fort Myers-based IT company they founded in 2012, according to the company's website.

FlightAware tracking data analyzed by Steven Arroyo, a retired airline captain with 50 years of flying experience, showed the Cessna flying at around 9,000 feet (2,743 meters), above the bottom of the cloud layers, when it began descending rapidly in a right-hand turn. Arroyo said, “It's highly, highly irregular,” and added, “I think it's safe to say this aircraft had some serious problems.”

Publicly available air traffic recordings on LiveATC.net showed that the pilot did not appear to report any problems when the plane took off from an airport in New Orleans or to make a distress call during the flight. Shortly before losing contact, an air traffic controller at Gulfport–Biloxi International Airport advised the pilot of precipitation and adverse weather in the area. According to the Coast Guard, the regional air traffic control tower in Mobile, Alabama, reported at about 3:45 p.m. that it had lost contact with the aircraft. FlightAware said the Cessna was en route from New Orleans to Fort Myers, Florida.

The closest National Weather Service station, in Pascagoula, showed light winds, no precipitation and clouds blanketing the sky at the time of the crash. Todd Curtis, an aviation expert and former Boeing safety engineer, said nothing appeared out of the ordinary based on the publicly available audio recordings. He noted that precipitation in below-freezing temperatures can cause dangerous icing, potentially leading to inaccurate instrument readings or a loss of lift. It was unclear whether the plane had a de-icing system.

Authorities are investigating why the Cessna 210, carrying six members of a Florida family, crashed off the Mississippi coast on Tuesday. Divers found the six bodies in the fuselage after the aircraft went down in the Gulf of Mexico between Pascagoula, Mississippi, and Horn Island, which is part of the Gulf Islands National Seashore. Responders searched for hours and initially found only three life jackets, a life raft and the plane's cabin floating on the water, Coast Guard spokesperson Juan Ros said.

Page Field airport in Fort Myers said in a statement that its community was “shocked and devastated.” The airport added, “We have lost great friends and outstanding aviators.” Cmdr. Trevor Blount, the Coast Guard coordinator for search and rescue in the Mobile area, called the situation heartbreaking. “While this is not the outcome any of us had hoped for, we are grateful for the strong interagency coordination that enabled us to swiftly locate those aboard the aircraft and provide closure to their families,” Blount said in a statement.

The Associated Press reported earlier this month that a Learjet 60 business jet nosedived into the ground in South Florida, killing all nine people on board, and that people inside their cars captured the crash on video.

The FAA and the National Transportation Safety Board will investigate the crash.
