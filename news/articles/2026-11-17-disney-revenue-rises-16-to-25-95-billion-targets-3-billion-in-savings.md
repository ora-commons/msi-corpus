## Streaming revenue climbs 19% as "Avengers: Doomsday" and the Super Bowl approach

The Walt Disney Co. reported fiscal fourth-quarter results on Tuesday that beat Wall Street expectations, with streaming services and theme parks driving revenue growth. Revenue rose 16% to $25.95 billion, above the $25.2 billion Wall Street expected. For the three months ended Oct. 3, the Burbank, California, company earned $2.61 billion, or $1.51 per share, compared with $1.31 billion, or 73 cents per share, a year earlier. Stripping out one-time charges and benefits, earnings were $1.77 per share, better than the $1.67 per share analysts surveyed by FactSet were calling for.

Disney also announced that it is looking for $3 billion in cost savings by fiscal 2029. The company said the cost cuts include layoffs it has enacted throughout the year.

Disney continues to anticipate double-digit growth in earnings per share in fiscal 2027. The company said the bulk of those earnings is expected in the first half of the year, with the release of "Avengers: Doomsday" in December and the Super Bowl in February, which Disney will broadcast on ESPN and ABC.

Disney's Experiences division, which includes the company's six theme park resorts worldwide, its cruise line, merchandise and video game licensing, reported operating income of $2.25 billion in the fourth quarter, up 20%, on revenue of $10.11 billion.

Overall attendance rose 11%, or 4% excluding an extra week in the quarter, driven by strength at Walt Disney World in Orlando, Florida, and Disneyland Paris. Attendance at domestic parks rose 11%, or 3% when the extra week is removed.

Disney said it is still seeing lower attendance by international visitors at its domestic parks, though the company said the impact is smaller than it was in the third quarter. The Walt Disney Co. cautioned earlier this year that its theme parks division would likely see modest growth, due in part to declining tourism from abroad.

Park spending remained solid despite those attendance issues. Per capita guest spending increased 4% across domestic parks and 5% across international parks in the quarter, with strength at Disneyland Paris more than offsetting incremental weakness relative to the third quarter at the company's Hong Kong and Shanghai parks.

Revenue for the entertainment streaming business, which includes Disney+ and Hulu, rose 17% to $11.89 billion in the quarter. Streaming revenue climbed 19% to $6.1 billion, with subscription revenue growing 22%. Disney said "Toy Story 5" attracted 20 million views on Disney+ in its first five days following its September premiere on the platform, and Marvel viewership on Disney+ more than doubled in the quarter. Disney's shift from cable toward streaming entertainment continued as viewers cut the cord.

Chief executive Josh D'Amaro, who took over from Bob Iger earlier this year, is the ninth chief executive of the company, which is more than 100 years old. D'Amaro spent about six years overseeing Disney's theme parks, cruises and resorts before taking the top job.

In a letter to shareholders, D'Amaro and chief financial officer Hugh Johnston wrote: "By continuing to invest in our existing franchises while innovating to build new ones, we can reach new audiences and deepen our relationship with consumers for generations to come."
