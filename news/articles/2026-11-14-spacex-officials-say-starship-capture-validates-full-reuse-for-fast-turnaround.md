## Musk congratulates his 'increíble equipo' as arms take hold of ship

SpaceX officials said Saturday's capture of the returning Starship by the launch tower's mechanical arms demonstrates full reuse of the ship, which they described as crucial for fast, low-cost turnaround. The capture was the first time a Starship flying back from space has been caught in flight — the outcome Elon Musk and his team had been waiting for — and it happened at the same Texas launch site where the vessel had begun its journey 22 hours earlier. Musk congratulated his “increíble equipo” shortly afterward.

The triumph was incomplete. The first-stage booster exploded shortly after Friday's launch from Texas, so the company did not achieve the double catch it had sought — an objective SpaceX says remains in place to accelerate trips to the moon and Mars. The booster's remains stayed in the Gulf of Mexico, cordoned off with buoys, as the bullet-shaped stainless-steel ship descended in a vertical attitude onto the launch platform. The dramatic capture helped eclipse the failed and messy booster attempt.

Cameras panned the suspended ship from top to bottom as it hung well above the ground, held by the tower's arms. SpaceX commentator Dan Huot shouted “¿Pueden creerlo?” — can you believe it? — drawing out each word. “¡Mírenla! ¡Cariño, está de vuelta!” — Look at her! Baby, she's back!

Although the Friday booster recovery failed, the company had already made three previous booster captures, which SpaceX said validates the technique.

The world's largest and most powerful rocket — a 124-meter (407-foot) colossus — lifted off late Friday afternoon from SpaceX's Starship base near the U.S.-Mexico border. After separating cleanly from its booster before things went wrong, the upper-stage ship climbed to orbit and released 26 advanced Starlink satellites. Starship then circled the globe at 265 kilometers (165 miles), beaming live views of itself and Earth via Starlink, and completed 14 orbits — far exceeding the previous mission, which managed one or two. SpaceX said this was its 15th full-scale Starship flight since 2023 and only its second orbital journey, and that every element of the ship, including its critical heat shield, appeared to have worked well.

On the booster, Kate Tice of SpaceX said its engines stopped firing too early during descent. The booster's guidance system detected that it was not in the correct position and automatically diverted toward the water and self-destructed. Engineers are still investigating the cause.

NASA marked Saturday's success. The space agency pays billions of dollars for Starship to land Artemis astronauts later in the decade and help build a permanent lunar base, while Musk's ambitions run to a fleet of Starships building a city on Mars. Administrator Jared Isaacman wrote on X that “Fue casi magia” — it was almost magic — adding that more believers were coming each day and that the flight brought the program one step closer to the moon and the American flag.

SpaceX expects to begin launching Starships from Kennedy Space Center in Florida in the coming months. That site is the starting point for NASA's next Artemis mission, which will not land and will instead stay in Earth orbit to practice with the lander module. The four Artemis III astronauts will attempt to dock their capsule not only with Starship but also with Blue Moon, of Blue Origin, with the two companies alternating crews to the lunar surface. NASA is aiming for Artemis IV to make humanity's first moon landing since the Apollo era as soon as 2028.
