## OPEC+ meets November 29 to discuss next year's output policy

Front-month Brent crude futures fell 0.7% to $91.84 a barrel and front-month West Texas Intermediate crude futures declined 0.8% to $85.02 a barrel in early European trading Tuesday. The declines extended the previous session as recovering Middle Eastern exports and expectations of a global supply surplus outweighed concerns about attacks on oil infrastructure and shipping in the region.

Both benchmarks had fallen more than 3% Monday as traders assessed the prospect of excess supply following the recovery in Gulf exports. Brent settled at $92.44 a barrel, while WTI closed at $85.72, its lowest settlement since Aug. 28.

The global oil market recorded its first surplus since February in October as supply conditions improved, according to David Oxley at Capital Economics. The recovery in Middle Eastern production has eased some of the tightness caused by disruptions to shipments through the Strait of Hormuz.

Ole Hansen, head of commodity strategy at Saxo Bank, said in a note last week that the recovery in Middle Eastern crude production had increased the prospect of oversupply. He also said stalled U.S.-Iran negotiations and continued tanker attacks left prices vulnerable to a sharp reversal.

OPEC said Monday it had completed an independent assessment of the maximum sustainable production capacity of its members and other producers participating in the OPEC+ alliance.

OPEC+ is scheduled to meet Nov. 29 to discuss its output policy for next year.

Security risks remained elevated across the region. U.K. Maritime Trade Operations said Monday that a tanker was struck by an unknown projectile while leaving the Strait of Hormuz, causing a fire. No casualties were reported.

Separately, Saudi-led coalition forces said Monday they had intercepted and destroyed two Houthi drones targeting the Mecca and Riyadh regions. The Saudi Press Agency carried the statement.
