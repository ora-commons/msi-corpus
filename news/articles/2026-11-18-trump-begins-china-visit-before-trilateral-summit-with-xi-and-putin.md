## War and peace, Iran, Ukraine, trade and nuclear weapons are expected topics

President Donald Trump arrived in southern China on November 18, 2026, after signing a trade deal and pledging increased defense cooperation with the Philippines. His trilateral summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin was due to begin on November 19 in Shenzhen, within hours of Trump's arrival, on the sidelines of the APEC summit. PBS NewsHour said it would be the first ever formal meeting among the leaders of China, Russia and the United States.

PBS reported that at the airport, Xi personally greeted Trump, the first time in Xi's 14 years in power that he had greeted a foreign leader there. Putin joined hands with Trump beside Xi after the Russian president met the Chinese leader. PBS called Xi “China's most powerful leader since Mao” and described the start of Trump's trip as “an unusual show of respect.” The report said Xi and Trump “leaned in to their promise to stabilize the U.S.-China relationship.”

There was no official agenda, and PBS said the expected topics were war and peace, Iran and Ukraine, trade and nuclear weapons. Trump said, “So we're going to talk about denuclearization, tough subject.” Schifrin said Trump wanted to talk about reducing nuclear weapons. Trump also said, “we discussed the possibility of a three-way deal, instead of a two-way deal.”

China has joined the United States, Russia, France and Britain as countries possessing nuclear weapons. For years after China's 1964 nuclear test, Beijing limited its stockpile to around 300 weapons. PBS reported that Beijing was testing nuclear-capable missiles and that China's nuclear arsenal was the fastest growing in the world. The Pentagon says that by 2030, Beijing will have more than 1,000 nuclear warheads, still a fraction of the U.S. and Russian totals, PBS reported. The report also said Xi had long refused to discuss arms control until China achieved parity, while trilateral arms control had long been a Trump priority.

A senior U.S. official said before the summit: “We do think it's time for China to step up to the adult table and have adult conversations about adult things. They can't just take their ball and go home.” PBS reported that China's Foreign Ministry “suggested the ball is in their court” this week. The ministry said the trilateral would have “groundbreaking significance.”

## Li Cheng on China's rationale and regional context

Li Cheng, a Hong Kong University professor who founded the Center on Contemporary China and the World, said, “Well, first of all, it makes China look good. China is the country to host these two major powers.” He said Beijing saw the meeting in the context of convening the region's leaders, not as a sequel to the 1945 Yalta summit, when Roosevelt, Churchill and Stalin divided Germany and Europe. Li said, “China did not see Russia as, like, a three kingdom. It's a multipolar world. And the multilateralism is the emphasis.” PBS wrote, “For Putin, the meeting will allow him to play global statesman after meeting Xi today.”

Xi Jinping said through an interpreter: “China-Russia relations have remained as solid as a mountain, undaunted by wind and rain, providing greater stability to an increasingly complex world.” PBS wrote, “Of course, the complexity has in part been created by Putin himself.” The war in Ukraine was expected to be discussed despite Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy's fears. Earlier in the month, Zelenskyy said: “But I hope that these big guys will not -- will not divide the world and, of course, our country.”

Iran was the other expected topic. On the day of Trump's arrival, the United States targeted an Iranian missile site. Li Cheng said, “I think the answer is yes,” adding that China wanted the strait open and wanted Iran not to develop a nuclear weapon. Describing China as a transactional leader, he said, “you should also understand China's core interests. So, that expands China's position.”

## Trade controls and rare-earth restrictions

PBS reported that Taiwan, trade and technology were at the top of China's core interests. Xi and Trump were due to meet again on November 19 for their third bilateral that year after the United States increased restrictions that week to prevent Beijing from skirting U.S. laws to access high-end chips.

Beijing countered those export controls with its own restrictions on the sale of rare earth magnets, which PBS said were essential for everything from electric vehicles to American fighter jets. Li Cheng said China had leverage and that rare earth controls made Chinese leaders more confident to demand mutual respect.

## Shenzhen economy and residents' concerns

Shenzhen is a city of 18 million people and a former fishing village whose skyline is now crowded with cranes. Xi once called the city “a miracle of world development.” PBS reported that consumer spending was down, real estate was slumping and the middle class faced economic headwinds.

Jiangyuan Luo recently lost his job selling to overseas customers. Luo said most residents were in hard circumstances, with lower salaries and heavier workloads. He said, “We should have cooperation, instead of conflict. Yes, people need cooperation. Otherwise, this is a lose-lose situation.”

Li Cheng said the private sector was not doing so well at the moment and that this was creating anxiety. PBS said Xi had invited world leaders to Shenzhen for the meetings. The report wrote: “But for the world, Xi Jinping presents himself as a source of stability, and he invited world leaders to this gleaming city for meetings that he hopes shape the world in China's image.”
