## FBI says investigation remains ongoing after evacuation lifted

Peoria Mayor Rita Ali told the Guardian on Monday that leadership at the BioUrja Renewables plant "have informed me that a suspect is in custody." Ali said the FBI, which is leading the investigation into the bomb, is expected to release more details later.

The FBI's division in nearby Springfield, Illinois, announced in a separate statement on Monday that its agents "were conducting law enforcement activity … in connection with the Biourja Renewables explosives device incident." "The investigation remains ongoing," the FBI said.

The FBI added that it was receiving assistance from the federal Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives, Illinois state police, the local sheriff's office, the Peoria police department and the police department of adjacent East Peoria. The Guardian said it had contacted the FBI for additional comment.

Ali's comments followed her administration's order that thousands of residents in parts of Peoria evacuate their homes and businesses after authorities discovered an explosive device at BioUrja Renewables. The evacuation zone encompassed part of East Peoria, a separate municipality. Local events were canceled and streets in the affected area were closed to the public.

Peoria police chief Brad Dixon said a potential explosion could have affected an area within a one-mile radius, but that technicians had rendered the bomb safe. Officials lifted the evacuation order at about 1.25pm on Saturday after they determined that no additional explosives had been found. They did not immediately announce any arrests but made clear they were working to identify any suspects in connection with the bomb.

The plant produces ethanol for fuel and alcohol, which is highly flammable, and the riverfront facility spans an area roughly equivalent to 44.5 US football fields, the Guardian reported.

Bomb threats and suspicious package calls are relatively common for law enforcement, and officials take those kinds of calls seriously as they investigate them. But they often are not verified as credible or substantial, making the case in Peoria — a city with about 110,000 residents — somewhat rare.
