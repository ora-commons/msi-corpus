## Republicans reel from unexpected losses, the Associated Press reports

The Associated Press framed the central question after the 2026 midterms as whether the results represent a durable tectonic shift — one that could reshape the path to the presidency — or a fluke stemming from Donald Trump's deep unpopularity and voters' unhappiness with the rising cost of living. AP assessed that, either way, Republicans are reeling from losses they never expected at the start of the election year.

AP described the outcome as voters taking a "wrecking ball to the traditional map of political battlegrounds," electing Democrats in states that were long considered reliably Republican. Kansas, South Carolina, Texas, Ohio and Iowa — states Republican Donald Trump easily won two years ago in the presidential election — backed Democrats for the U.S. Senate and, in some cases, governor, on Tuesday.

Of the five states, Texas alone carried the governor, lieutenant governor and attorney general offices alongside a Senate seat. Iowa and Ohio backed Democrats for governor and the Senate, and in Kansas and South Carolina the reported Democratic pickups were Senate seats.

In Kansas, Adam Hamilton, a Methodist megachurch pastor, defeated Republican Sen. Roger Marshall, giving the state its first Democratic senator since 1932.

South Carolina rejected Republican Sen. Darline Graham in favor of pediatrician Annie Andrews, the first Democrat to win a Senate seat in the state since 1998. Democratic Senate Leader Chuck Schumer of New York called the results a "generational remake of the Senate map."

The Democratic victories went deeper in Texas. James Talarico beat Ken Paxton for Senate, Gina Hinojosa ousted Gov. Greg Abbott, Vikki Goodwin dislodged Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick, and Nathan Johnson defeated Mayes Middleton for attorney general. Until Tuesday, no Democrat had won a statewide race in Texas since 1994.

In a statement, Republican Lt. Gov. Dan Patrick acknowledged: "This was a wave election in Texas and across the country."

The Associated Press said Democrats also swept to power in states that were once battlegrounds but were more recently viewed as out of reach. Iowa chose Rob Sand for governor and Josh Turek for senator, and Ohio backed Amy Acton for governor and Sherrod Brown for senator.

Brown is a former senator who lost his seat just two years ago, defeated by Republican Bernie Moreno. This time, he beat Republican Sen. Jon Husted.

Democrats will start the next campaign holding Senate seats in Kansas, South Carolina, Texas, Ohio and Iowa, the governor's offices in Texas, Ohio and Iowa, and the lieutenant governor and attorney general posts in Texas. How durable that alignment proves, and whether it reshapes the path to the presidency, was not settled by the results.
