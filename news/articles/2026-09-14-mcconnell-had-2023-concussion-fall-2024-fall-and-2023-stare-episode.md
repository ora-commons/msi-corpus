## Childhood polio disclosure accompanied McConnell's Monday return to Senate floor

Reporting on Senator Mitch McConnell's return to the U.S. Senate on Monday after a three-month absence, BBC News documented three prior health incidents that preceded the June fall which sent him to hospital. The 84-year-old Kentucky Republican "suffered minor injuries after a fall in 2024," according to BBC News. "A year earlier he had another fall, leading to a concussion and a hospital stay." In a separate 2023 episode, BBC reported, McConnell "seemed to stare into space while speaking to reporters, raising questions about his mental acuity."

McConnell's Monday return came after weeks of unanswered questions about his condition. His office said about a month after the June 14 fall that he had fallen at home and been treated in hospital for mild pneumonia; the delayed disclosure fueled Democratic demands for transparency about his health.

"I'm really looking forward to being back on the Senate floor and seeing my colleagues," the Kentucky Republican said Monday, before casting a vote on a judicial nomination. Video of the senator leaving his home in the U.S. capital showed him being taken in a wheelchair into a vehicle. He appeared a short time later on the Senate floor, where he was greeted by several senators.

In a written statement released Monday, McConnell described his recovery as "a long and often frustrating process," adding that "the lingering effects of childhood polio haven't made it any easier." "I'm still not quite back to 100%," he continued, saying he would "do my best to be present for tough votes." His statement concluded: "I'm glad to get back to more of business as usual on their [fellow Kentuckians'] behalf this month."

McConnell last voted on June 11. His long absence "provoked rampant speculation about his health, and demands from Democrats for transparency," according to BBC News.

His return on Monday coincided with the first day of the Senate reconvening after a five-week break. Senators are embarking on a final stretch of legislative work before November's midterm elections, which could reshape the balance of power in Congress.

McConnell, the longest-serving party leader in Senate history, has served in the chamber for 41 years. His term expires in January, and he is not seeking re-election.
