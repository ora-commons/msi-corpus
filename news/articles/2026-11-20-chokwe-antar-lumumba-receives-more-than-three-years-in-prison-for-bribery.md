## 2024 indictment: Two FBI workers posed as real estate developers and paid officials $50,000 for Lumumba's reelection campaign

Chokwe Antar Lumumba, the former mayor of Jackson, the capital of Mississippi, was sentenced Friday to more than three years in prison after pleading guilty to federal bribery charges. Lumumba will also forfeit $50,000 in property and serve one year of supervised release. The bribery scheme included several other high-ranking Democratic county and city officials.

The charges against Lumumba stem from a 2024 indictment that described two people working for the FBI posing as real estate developers. The indictment said the two FBI workers provided payments to officials, including $50,000 for Lumumba's reelection campaign, in exchange for assistance greenlighting a development project.

Lumumba was charged alongside former Hinds County District Attorney Jody Owens and former Jackson City Council President Aaron Banks. Owens, who was charged on accusations that he solicited $250,000 in bribes, pleaded guilty and was sentenced to more than three years in prison earlier this month. Banks also pleaded guilty and was sentenced to nearly four years in prison.

Two other participants in the scheme pleaded guilty to federal bribery charges in 2024: former Jackson City Council Vice President Angelique Lee and Sherik Marve Smith, a businessman and a relative of Owens.

Lumumba was the son of human rights activist and attorney Chokwe Lumumba, who died shortly after being elected Jackson mayor in 2013. The younger Lumumba began his career as an activist, was first elected mayor in 2017 and served two terms. He presided over Jackson during a water crisis that drew national attention.

Lumumba lost his reelection bid in 2025, shortly after he was charged in the scheme.

Lumumba's lawyers did not immediately return an email from The Associated Press requesting comment on his sentencing.
