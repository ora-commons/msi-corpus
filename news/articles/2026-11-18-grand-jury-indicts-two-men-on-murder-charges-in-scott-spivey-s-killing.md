## Judge had already denied the men's self-defense claim

A South Carolina grand jury in Horry County indicted Weldon Boyd and Bradley Williams on murder charges Wednesday in the shooting death of Scott Spivey, according to lawyers involved in the case. Boyd and Williams are expected to be formally charged with murder and a related firearms charge in a hearing Thursday afternoon, the lawyers said. The two men fatally shot Spivey, 33, during an encounter on Sept. 9, 2023.

Spivey's killing had drawn little notice until a Wall Street Journal investigation last year aired conversations recorded on Boyd's phone. The recordings were included in police files but were not brought to light in the initial police investigation or in a review by the state attorney general, the newspaper reported.

Some of the calls indicated that Boyd was intent on pursuing Spivey and had anticipated a shootout while still en route, the newspaper reported. After the killing, Boyd called the deputy police chief of Horry County, a friend who promised to help steer the investigation in Boyd's favor, according to The Wall Street Journal.

Boyd and Williams initially avoided arrest by claiming self-defense, saying they feared for their lives when they shot Spivey. A South Carolina judge denied that claim earlier this year. The ruling raised the possibility of charges. The law at issue, South Carolina's "stand your ground" law, allows people to defend their lives when faced with a lethal threat.

Special prosecutor Barry Barnette presented evidence to the grand jury in Horry County, where the fatal shooting occurred. Jennifer Foley, who found Boyd's recorded calls buried in a police file, credited Barnette with a yearlong investigation that included his review of that file.

Foley has long advocated for criminal charges in the case. “Someone finally listened,” Foley said. “It may have taken 3½ years but I’m so thankful that people who were eager to do the right thing finally got hold of Scott’s case.”

Defense lawyers responded to the indictment on Wednesday. Shaun Kent, Boyd's lawyer, declined to comment, saying he had not reviewed the indictments. O'Bryan Martin, Williams's lawyer, said his client is not guilty of the charges. “The facts haven’t changed,” Martin said. “The only thing that has ever changed is the noise, the narrative around this case.”

The charges follow the judge's denial of the self-defense claim, a ruling that raised the possibility of prosecution. On Thursday afternoon, the lawyers said, Boyd and Williams are expected to be formally charged with murder and a related firearms charge.
