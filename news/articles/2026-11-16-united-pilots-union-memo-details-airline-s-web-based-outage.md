## United says it has no indication outage was cyber-related

An internal memo from United Airlines' pilots' union, seen by Reuters, said a company-wide web-based outage was affecting multiple applications used by pilots, including crew communications. The memo said that United was unable to provide final aircraft weights for departing flights and advised pilots to follow airport station guidance on whether to leave their gates.

United addressed customers directly about the disruption on Monday. "We're aware of the widespread system outage, and our teams are working hard to get things back up and running as soon as possible," United said in a post on X, in which the airline also "sincerely apologized" to customers.

The Guardian contacted United for comment. A statement United sent to other outlets said the company is "troubleshooting a technology issue affecting departing flights". United added that there was no impact on flights in progress, or on United Express flights, and no indication that the system outage was a cyber-related issue.

United's Monday outage came three days after the Federal Aviation Administration issued a rare nationwide ground stop on all US flights. On Friday, that stop temporarily grounded all commercial airplanes in the US for more than four hours, and thousands of flights were delayed or canceled.

The Guardian attributed Friday's grounding to ageing equipment. The Trump administration said the rare ground stop stemmed from a fiber upgrade during routine maintenance; that upgrade led to data flooding, which crashed a critical communication system.

United's Monday disruption was one of several technology failures affecting Americans over the same stretch of days. Earlier on Monday, customers at several major US banks and at Zelle, the money transfer service, reported service outages and difficulty accessing their online accounts. A technical issue at JPMorgan Chase caused ripple effects throughout the financial system, The Guardian reported.

Microsoft also experienced a major outage beginning Thursday and continuing into Friday, disrupting a host of services including Outlook, Teams and the Copilot AI assistant. The outage's serious repercussions reached millions of people and organizations all over the world, including schools, hospitals, businesses, courts, banks, prisons, police forces and governments.

Microsoft said it had resolved the issue and restored service by Saturday and had also issued an apology. It did not attribute the disruption to a cybersecurity breach. In an initial report on Monday, Microsoft said the outage resulted from a combination of a months-old bug, malfunctioning internal connections and server capacity.
