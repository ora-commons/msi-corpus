## Matt Canavan says the defection "smacks of self-interest"

Federal Liberal senator for South Australia Leah Blyth has announced that she is defecting from the Liberal Party to One Nation. The move makes Blyth the party's first upper-house member for South Australia and brings a sitting senator into its ranks.

Blyth, a member of Angus Taylor's shadow cabinet, said she did not believe the Coalition was capable of opposing the Albanese government, which she described as "running the country into the ground". In her statement, she said, "I can no longer in good conscience tell Australians that the Liberal Party can save this country," and wrote, "Australia is a country worth fighting for … I want our children to grow up proud of their country, with the freedom and opportunity to build a better life."

Blyth also said, "One Nation has never shied away from confronting the uncomfortable truths facing our nation. They are the only party with the bold ideas needed to turn this country around."

One Nation leader Pauline Hanson welcomed the South Australian senator and said she would "build on One Nation's expanding support within the state". Hanson posted a video to social media on Wednesday night showing her cooking a steak in a sandwich press and asking viewers to "guess who this is for". The post referred back to the same meal she had shared with then-Nationals MP Barnaby Joyce ahead of his defection.

On Thursday morning, Nationals leader Matt Canavan called Blyth's defection "disappointing on a number of levels" and condemned the move as part of a broader pattern of behavior from One Nation.

Speaking to Radio National, Canavan said the move took him by surprise. "I try and be friends with everyone … and I thought I was friends with Leah, but there was no warning from her," he said.

Speaking to Blyth's reasoning that her values aligned more closely with One Nation, Canavan said the move "smacks of self-interest to me". He questioned her stated principles, saying: "Good luck to her. Leah went to the election last year supporting net zero. She didn't oppose the hate speech laws earlier this year, like we did - the Nationals."

He continued: "To turn around now a few months later and say, 'my principles and policies are more aligned with One Nation' – I mean, give me a break."

Deputy Liberal leader Jane Hume similarly described the move as a "disappointing decision" but said Blyth "wasn't a big contributor to the party room".

The defection gives One Nation its first federal upper-house member from South Australia. Blyth framed the move around conscience and political alignment, Canavan questioned her principles and the absence of warning, Hume called the decision disappointing, and Hanson said One Nation would build on its expanding support in the state.
