## OPEC+ producers increasingly use alternative routes amid higher freight and insurance costs

The seven core members met Sunday in a virtual session and agreed to leave December oil production unchanged. The participants were Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iraq, Kuwait, Algeria, Kazakhstan and Oman. The Wall Street Journal reported that the decision came as concerns that the U.S. could resume strikes on Iran after the midterm elections kept geopolitical risks elevated and continued threats to shipping in the Persian Gulf remained a concern.

Oil prices remained above $100 a barrel. Brent crude for January delivery settled above $113 on Friday, while West Texas Intermediate, the U.S. oil gauge, closed the session at around $104. Continued threats to shipping in the Persian Gulf pushed freight and insurance costs sharply higher, while global onshore oil inventories were near their lowest levels in at least a decade.

The conflict had disrupted global oil flows through key shipping routes for months, limiting OPEC+'s ability to add barrels to the market and influence the supply balance. Most members continued to produce below their official targets. Producers were also increasingly relying on alternative export routes to bypass the Strait of Hormuz.

Preliminary data from Kpler showed terminal loadings at the United Arab Emirates' port of Fujairah and Omani terminals, outside the Strait of Hormuz, running 36% above their prewar levels. Red Sea shipments were 8% above baseline, while verified transits through Hormuz were at half their prewar level. Saudi Arabia's east coast loadings were well below the 5 million to 6 million barrels a day range seen in March, averaging 3.49 million barrels a day.

According to OPEC's latest data, crude production among OPEC members rose by 920,000 barrels a day to around 25.1 million barrels a day in September. Output remained more than 3 million barrels a day below prewar levels in February, the cartel said.

Eight months into the conflict, diplomatic efforts to revive peace negotiations between Washington and Tehran showed no sign of a breakthrough, The Wall Street Journal reported. The lack of progress raised the prospect that supply chains could remain vulnerable for longer and add to inflationary pressures worldwide.

The Journal reported that U.S. Vice President JD Vance sent a message to Mohsen Rezaei, secretary of Iran's Supreme National Security Council, in an effort to restart peace talks. Separately, it reported that the U.S. told several Gulf Arab allies to prepare for a possible short but intensive military campaign against Iran following the midterms. The risk of renewed military action remained.

OPEC+ had also held production unchanged for November. That pause followed the completion in September of a phased unwinding of 1.65 million barrels a day in voluntary production cuts agreed in 2023. Separate production cuts of around 2 million barrels a day, introduced by OPEC+ in 2022, remain in place through the end of the year.

The group was also reviewing members' production capacity after announcing last year that it would establish a new system for setting production baselines in 2027. Those baselines would determine future output quotas.

The seven OPEC+ producers are scheduled to meet again on Nov. 29 to decide production levels for January.
