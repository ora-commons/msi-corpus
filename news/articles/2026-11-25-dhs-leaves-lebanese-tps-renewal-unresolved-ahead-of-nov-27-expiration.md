## DHS leaves Lebanese TPS renewal unresolved ahead of Nov. 27 expiration

**Subtype:** fact

- The Department of Homeland Security has not announced a renewal of Temporary Protected Status for an estimated 11,000 Lebanese nationals whose protections are set to expire by Nov. 27.
- A DHS spokesperson said Lebanese TPS recipients retain protection until DHS makes an announcement.

## Lebanese recipients retain protection until DHS makes an announcement

Thousands of Lebanese immigrants living in the United States are scheduled to have their Temporary Protected Status protections expire by Nov. 27. DHS has not said whether it will renew the status, and an estimated 11,000 Lebanese nationals received a six-month renewal in May.

TPS is a form of temporary immigration protection for nationals from countries deemed too dangerous for return because of war, violence or other extreme instability. The program provides six-month to 18-month cycles of protection that allow recipients to remain and work in the United States.

Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin renewed TPS for an estimated 11,000 Lebanese nationals in May for six months. Mullin cited instability in the region due to the U.S.-Israel-backed war in Iran, which the source said remains active.

News From The States reported that the Trump administration has aimed to wind down TPS, a program Congress created in the 1990s. At the start of the second Trump administration, 17 countries had TPS designations; Lebanon is now the only country with the status in effect, through Nov. 27.

DHS has made the same kind of announcement for nationals with TPS from El Salvador, Sudan and Ukraine. TPS for people from all three countries expired this year, but DHS said it will allow them to keep their work authorization and deportation protections for now.

The report counted roughly 200,000 Salvadorans, 100,000 Ukrainians and 2,000 Sudanese among the remaining TPS holders whose protections have expired but who maintain deportation protections. DHS also allows those immigrants to keep their work authorization for now.

The administration's broader moves have stripped TPS from more than 1 million immigrants, according to News From The States, exposing them to deportation.

Congressional Democrats have tried to pass legislation extending TPS broadly and extending protections for certain nationals, including Haitians and Afghans. They have been unable to do so with Republicans controlling both chambers.

In January, Democrats will hold the majority in the House and Senate, and members of both chambers have expressed immigration reform as a top priority. Any legal protections for immigrants would likely face a veto from President Donald Trump and would need a two-thirds majority to override.

President Donald Trump has expressed interest in a deal with Democrats that would provide legal status for some immigrants in industries including hospitality and agriculture. News From The States said new White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller has often taken a hard-line approach to immigration, including legal immigrant workers.

A DHS spokesperson did not answer State Newsroom's questions about the renewal status for Lebanese immigrants with TPS. In a statement, the spokesperson said, “An announcement on Lebanon’s TPS will be made at the appropriate time,” and stated, “Until such an announcement is made, Lebanese individuals present in the U.S. under TPS retain protection.”
