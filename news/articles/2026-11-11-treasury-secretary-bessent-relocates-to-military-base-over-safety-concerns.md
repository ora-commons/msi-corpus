## Iran steps up targeting of US personnel amid Treasury's sanctions campaign

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was recently forced to relocate to a military base over concerns for his safety, The Associated Press reported Wednesday. A person familiar with the matter, who spoke to the AP on the condition of anonymity to discuss sensitive details, said the relocation was partly due to media reports that helped identify Bessent's home address. The relocation was earlier reported by The New York Times.

The move comes as Bessent has spent the last several months executing a massive economic campaign against Iran aimed at severing all of Tehran's remaining financial ties around the world.

Recent U.S. intelligence showed that Iran has stepped up efforts to target American personnel in the Middle East and elsewhere, according to the AP report. That intelligence contributed to the State Department's decision last month to reduce staffing at four U.S. embassies in the region.

President Donald Trump said this week that the more than 8-month-old Iran war "militarily is effectively over." The administration has nevertheless kept up the financial warfare in a bid to get Iran to capitulate to U.S. demands over Tehran's nuclear program.

The Treasury Department in August pledged an "economic D-Day" against Tehran, and the effort has since become a massive sanctions and influence campaign.

U.S. officials have spent the past few months traveling from Europe to the Middle East and meeting behind closed doors on the sidelines of the U.N. General Assembly. According to the report, they have made the case to some of Tehran's closest and most vital trading partners: sever the financial ties or face retaliation from the United States.

It appears some have followed suit. The United Arab Emirates and Turkey — key trading partners and regional neighbors of Iran — halted the country's top commercial bank from issuing transactions in their countries. Some of Iran's top airlines were also cut off from critical regional routes from Istanbul to Muscat, Oman.

Bessent is not alone in the living arrangement. Other top Trump administration officials, including Secretary of State Marco Rubio and Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, also live on military bases.
