## FAA investigates while airport operations face hourslong flight delays

The FedEx McDonnell Douglas MD-11 overran a runway at Miami International Airport at about 8:40 p.m. Wednesday, according to airport officials. No injuries were reported after the plane ran off the runway, and the airport temporarily closed all but one runway.

The airport advised passengers scheduled to fly Wednesday night to check with their airlines for the latest flight updates before traveling to the airport. “We appreciate our passengers’ patience as we work to safely resume normal operations,” the airport said in a statement.

By early Thursday, the Federal Aviation Administration was reporting hourslong flight delays. The agency said it was investigating and that the aircraft was a McDonnell Douglas MD-11 coming from Memphis.

FedEx said the plane had “an issue upon landing” and was coordinating with investigating officials. “We are grateful that our crew is safe and there are no reported injuries,” the company said in a statement.

FedEx had resumed MD-11 service in May to deliver packages after that type of aircraft crashed in Kentucky last year during takeoff. The carrier had just started flying the aircraft again when the Miami incident occurred.

In the Kentucky crash, the UPS plane’s left engine flew off, and three pilots along with 12 people on the ground were killed. The FAA had approved Boeing’s proposed fix for the MD-11s after an extensive review.

The FedEx incident came almost three months after an Amazon cargo jet overshot another runway in Miami, killing five people on the ground. The runways involved were different, but three of Miami’s runways converge at the airport’s west end, leaving the FedEx plane close to where the Amazon plane careened across a road and struck a van filled with a plane cleaning crew.

The FedEx incident also came less than two weeks after a minor collision between two planes at a taxiway intersection at the Miami airport. The wing of a Copa flight and the nose of a UPS plane collided.

Miami-Dade Fire Rescue units responded to the FedEx incident Wednesday. The National Transportation Safety Board said it had no information about the situation. The FAA investigation was continuing as the airport worked to resume normal operations and directed passengers to check with airlines for updates.
