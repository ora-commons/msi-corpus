## Mullin defends government-plane trip to LSU-Texas game; Clayton warns of Iranian threats

Jay Clayton, the director of national intelligence, told the House Homeland Security Committee on Capitol Hill on Wednesday that a series of nationwide network outages was not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack. He appeared with Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin and FBI Deputy Director Christopher Raia as lawmakers examined the disruptions, Mullin’s use of a government plane to attend the LSU-Texas college football game on Saturday, less than a day after the nationwide flight ground stop was lifted, and other national-security matters.

The hearing on worldwide threats turned into a broad discussion of the outages and intelligence about Iranian threats to the homeland. The session came after the Trump administration ordered a ground stop on all flights in the United States, a classified intelligence network temporarily went down and Microsoft 365 products stopped working, among other problems. The disruptions affected millions of Americans and prompted speculation that the incidents might have been part of an attack on the United States. The administration has come under intense criticism in recent days, including from former administration officials, over its inability to clearly explain what was behind the outages, while some of President Trump’s political allies were frustrated by the slow public-relations response. Clayton was added to the hearing at the last minute, a day before the session, to address the nation’s growing concerns.

Clayton said repeatedly that there was no coordinated attack on the United States. “I understand the timing of these incidents can raise questions,” he said, adding that the service disruptions were “associated with maintenance and technical changes. They are not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack.” “We have no indication that these events are related,” he said. Clayton also said the U.S. intelligence community continued to investigate the incidents and other issues.

Rep. Jim Himes, the top Democrat on the House Intelligence Committee, released a statement echoing the administration’s explanations. “I have seen no evidence to suggest any of the outages were the result of a malicious actor,” he said. He said the “breakneck pace of patching” outdated systems “could cause more frequent outages,” but the work was necessary to get ahead of new cyber and AI-related threats.

Mullin faced criticism for using a government plane to attend the LSU-Texas college football game on Saturday, less than a day after the nationwide flight ground stop was lifted. He said the journey was a normal family trip, planned for weeks to take his niece to the game, and that cancelling it would have raised concerns. In his opening statement, Mullin said: “We had deep conversations about it.” He continued: “And it came in that if we canceled something when the airspace would be completely open…it would show distrust that we aren’t even willing to fly.”

Mullin called members of Congress who inquired about the trip hypocritical. “You would have done the same thing, except you would have called it a campaign event,” he said.

Clayton also used the hearing to warn about Iran, saying the United States faced a “heightened threat from the Iranian lethal plotting against our citizens at home and abroad.” “These threats have substantially increased,” he said. “Our women and men abroad, our women and men here in the United States, are under greater threat.”

Lawmakers repeatedly asked Clayton about a CNN story that the United States was collecting intelligence about an Iranian plot to detonate a “dirty bomb” in Washington. Clayton declined to address the intelligence directly. Instead, he cited the indictment of an imprisoned man in New York accused of orchestrating plots against U.S. citizens and an attack on U.S. aircraft and pilots at a British military base as evidence of the danger Iran poses. “That demonstrates a network capability that is very sophisticated, global, and one that we need to combat seriously with all the tools we have available,” he said.

Rep. Seth Magaziner (D., R.I.) asked Mullin when he learned of an affair involving Jensen Huang, who stepped down as Nvidia CEO the previous week, and Alice Huang, who is alleged to have been working with Chinese intelligence. Magaziner cited months of reporting that other top administration officials, including Secretary of State Marco Rubio, had known of the affair. Mullin replied: “We weren’t privileged to that information at all until it broke.”

The Wall Street Journal reported that Mullin had been “on thin ice” with White House officials for months following repeated verbal blunders, including remarks that broke with the administration on immigration enforcement and his suggestion, during Trump’s Fourth of July celebration on the National Mall, to cancel a fireworks display due to dangerous extreme heat that day.

Magaziner then asked why the homeland security secretary, who oversees the nation’s premier cybersecurity agency, had not been informed. Mullin answered: “If it were a direct threat to the homeland, I would have been read in on it.”

Though the reclassification initiative was not the hearing’s primary subject, Rep. Bennie Thompson (D., Miss.), the committee’s top Democrat, asked Raia which left-wing groups should be designated terrorist organizations. Raia answered: “I would say 764. I would also say antifa.” Thompson asked whether any right-wing organizations would meet the test. Raia repeatedly declined to name any, saying, “I don’t want to paint a broad brush.” He added: “There are people inside of right-wing organizations just like there are people inside of left-wing organizations.”

Clayton reiterated that the outages did not represent a coordinated or state-sponsored attack, while acknowledging that the intelligence community continued to investigate them. The hearing also placed the administration’s Iran threat warning, Mullin’s government-plane travel explanation and Raia’s answers about group designations before lawmakers in the same session.
