## Task force would develop rules for sharing national oil stockpiles

On Tuesday, Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi proposed an Asian energy agency to help countries in the region manage oil crises, including shocks tied to the Iran war. She presented the proposal at a meeting on the sidelines of the annual Association of Southeast Asian Nations summit in Manila, the Philippine capital.

Takaichi called for two institutional arrangements: an oil security task force and a Japan-based version of the International Energy Agency for the region. “We would like to position this as an Asian version of the IEA,” Takaichi said.

The International Energy Agency is an independent, multigovernmental agency established in 1974 in response to a severe oil crisis in the early 1970s. It provides policy recommendations and helps coordinate oil reserves among its members.

The urgency surrounding Takaichi’s proposal has increased since the Iran war began in late February, particularly in Asia. Oil and gas prices rose, shipping of oil from the Persian Gulf through the Strait of Hormuz was disrupted, and some Asian countries took emergency measures to conserve energy.

Japan would help lead the proposed oil security task force with the Indonesia-based Economic Research Institute for ASEAN and East Asia, or ERIA, an energy security research organization. Takaichi said the task force would develop rules for emergency releases and the sharing of oil from national stockpiles. She made the proposal at a meeting of the Asia Zero Emissions Community.

Takaichi said the region also should cooperate more broadly in stockpiling oil and establish a joint reserve system for Southeast Asia. Idled oil storage tanks in Japan could be used for those reserves, she said.

Japan is one of many Asian countries that depend heavily on imports of oil and gas. Takaichi’s proposals follow a Japanese-led initiative announced in April to strengthen energy supply chains and cooperation across the region.

The price increases and supply disruptions have also prompted a faster regional shift toward renewable energy, including rooftop solar and electric vehicles.

Taken together, Takaichi’s proposals would link emergency oil releases, shared national stockpiles and joint Southeast Asian reserves under regional coordination. The plan comes as Asian countries confront higher oil and gas prices, disrupted Persian Gulf shipping and a faster transition toward renewable power.
