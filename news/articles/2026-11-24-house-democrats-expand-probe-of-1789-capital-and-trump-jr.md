## House Democrats expand probe of 1789 Capital and Trump Jr.

**Subtype:** fact

- House Democrats led by Rep. Jamie Raskin are expanding an investigation into 1789 Capital and Donald Trump Jr.'s role in the firm's growth.
- Investigators say 1789 Capital's managed assets grew from $150 million to $3 billion after Donald Trump Jr. joined as a partner.

## Document requests target Hadrian, Juul, Polymarket and GrabAGun

House Democrats, led by Rep. Jamie Raskin of Maryland, are expanding their investigation into 1789 Capital, the venture capital firm that brought Donald Trump Jr. on as a partner days after President Donald Trump's reelection to the White House. Rep. Raskin is the likely House Judiciary Committee chair, and the inquiry is examining whether Trump Jr.'s political influence or government information helped companies connected to the firm secure federal contracts and regulatory changes, NPR reported.

Investigators described 1789 Capital as "a middling venture capital firm" with $150 million in managed assets before Trump Jr. joined. They said the firm had grown into "the darling of the venture capital world" with $3 billion in managed assets. The committee is examining whether that expansion was aided by political influence or government information available to the president's son.

In an Axios interview last week, Trump Jr. said the timing of his hiring was coincidental and that he had already worked with 1789 Capital founder Omeed Malik for years. He said he draws a clear line when marketing and raising money for the firm. He said in the Axios interview: "I make it very clear [to foreign investors] that I have nothing to do with government."

In the same interview, Trump Jr. said: "[The president is] my father, but he's also busy and I probably haven't spoken to him in three weeks." He also suggested that he did not use or need government connections to make smart investments. He said: "Trying to do the things that we're alleged to be doing every day, I think that would last for about two seconds before it was out there very publicly." NPR said it reached out to Trump Jr. for comment but did not hear back.

Trump Jr. said 1789 Capital relies on teams of analysts to invest in industries with obvious public and national security needs but relatively few companies able to meet those demands.

Bill Burck, an attorney representing 1789 Capital, rejected the premise of the inquiry. He told NPR: "We responded to Congressman Raskin back in September pointing out in detail the many flaws in his understanding of how 1789 has operated and a number of basic factual errors. He never bothered to reply. Facts are facts and Congressman Raskin has them wrong here but persists in his tall tales against 1789. These are personal attacks untethered to actual evidence."

On Monday, congressional investigators sent letters to four companies — military contractor Hadrian, electronic-cigarette company Juul, the prediction market and betting site Polymarket, and online weapons dealer GrabAGun — asking each company to preserve records and turn over a broad array of documents to the committee. The companies represent four lines of inquiry: Hadrian's armed-forces contracts, Juul's product authorizations, Polymarket's federal investigations and GrabAGun's proposed firearms-shipping rule.

Hadrian's armed-forces contracts, signed in March of this year, are worth close to $1 billion. Investigators alleged that in 2024 the company had a single manufacturing facility and no history of fulfilling government contracts. They also said CEO Chris Power donated tens of thousands of dollars to Trump-aligned political organizations in advance of the 2024 election. NPR noted that political donations are constitutionally protected speech and are not necessarily evidence of wrongdoing.

1789 Capital first invested in Hadrian in July 2025. Hadrian is also backed by a number of bluechip investors, including Morgan Stanley, T. Rowe Price, Andreessen Horowitz and JPMorganChase. The company did not answer NPR's request for comment.

Investigators said 1789 Capital took a stake in Juul in March 2025, when the e-cigarette company was teetering near insolvency following first-term Trump administration rulings that limited sales of various flavor cartridges. Four months after the investment, but before it was publicly disclosed, investigators said Juul's fortunes rebounded after the Trump administration authorized five new Juul e-cigarette products. Juul has announced plans to seek more flavor authorizations. Juul did not answer NPR's request for comment.

Investigators said 1789 Capital invested in Polymarket after company leadership met with Trump Jr. at the 2024 Republican National Convention, while Polymarket was banned in the United States. In July 2025, after President Trump took office, the Justice Department and Commodity Futures Trading Commission notified Polymarket that they had closed investigations launched during the Biden administration, according to Bloomberg News reporting. The CFTC later authorized a U.S. version of the platform to launch. Investigators allege that 1789 Capital's investment produced a 70-fold return.

Investigators said GrabAGun became a publicly traded company with help from 1789 Capital's leadership, including Trump Jr., who serves on its board. The company says people should be able to use their computers, phones and tablets to shop for firearms the same way they purchase other products. In May 2026, the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives proposed a draft rule that would make it easier to ship guns directly to people's homes. GrabAGun claimed that it was uniquely positioned to capitalize on the opportunity, and investigators asked what Trump Jr. knew about the proposal and how involved he was.

GrabAGun rejected the investigators' account. In a statement to NPR, the company said the letter was "a political document, and its allegations are baseless." GrabAGun said the ATF's regulatory review was well known across the firearms industry and that the agency had published numerous proposed rules for public comment. "GrabAGun's only input came through public comments on several of those proposals," the company said.

The document requests target the records needed to examine investigators' account: Hadrian's armed-forces contracts and CEO donations, Juul's product authorizations, Polymarket's federal investigation closures and ATF's proposed gun-shipping rule. NPR's received responses were Burck's factual challenge and GrabAGun's statement; Trump Jr.'s denials came through the Axios interview. Hadrian and Juul did not answer requests for comment.
