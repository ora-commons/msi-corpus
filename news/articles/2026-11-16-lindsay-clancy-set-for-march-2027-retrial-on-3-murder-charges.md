## Lindsay Clancy set for March 2027 retrial on 3 murder charges

**Subtype:** fact

- Plymouth County prosecutors are seeking a new trial for Lindsay Clancy in the deaths of her three children, with proceedings scheduled to begin on March 29, 2027.
- Judge William Sullivan denied the Clancy defense's request to dismiss the criminal charges on double jeopardy grounds and left the first-degree murder counts unchanged.
- Clancy's defense argues she cannot be held criminally responsible because she was suffering from severe postpartum psychosis when her children were killed in January 2023.
- Clancy, 36, remains in the custody of a state psychiatric hospital where she has been held for more than three years.

## Prosecutors keep first-degree murder charges unchanged at hearing

There had been speculation that the mistrial of Clancy's first trial might lead prosecutors to consider lesser charges that wouldn't require a jury, such as manslaughter or second-degree murder. No changes to the charges were made at Monday's court hearing.

"Given what the charges are, it requires a jury trial," Assistant District Attorney Jennifer Sprague told Judge William Sullivan.

Clancy's defense attorney, Kevin Reddington, read the statement as a signal about what is coming. "I think you can read between the lines that when they say that the case is going to require a jury, that it's an indirect comment from the DA that they intend to proceed on first-degree murder indictments," Reddington said.

Clancy was initially tried on three charges of first-degree murder, one for each of her children. Her case has drawn intense media interest, due to the killings of her young children. And as WBUR has reported, the trial has also sparked conspiracy theories online.

Sullivan is expected to preside over the new trial. He oversaw Clancy's murder trial, which ended in a mistrial two months ago. The decision to retry Clancy comes after the judge denied a request from her defense team to dismiss the criminal charges on double jeopardy grounds.

Sullivan declared a mistrial on Sept. 4, after five weeks of testimony and seven days of jury deliberations. The jurors, nine women and three men, repeatedly told Sullivan they were deadlocked and unable to reach a unanimous decision. Jurors said a lone juror refused to join 11 others in finding Clancy not guilty of murder.

Clancy's defense filed several motions after the mistrial. The first asked the court to issue a "required finding of not guilty," a Massachusetts state law term for instances where judges determine there is insufficient evidence to sustain a conviction. Another focused on the holdout juror, asking the court for an inquiry and to examine that juror's responses to a juror questionnaire and other records. Sullivan denied both motions.

When deliberations were dragging out at the end of the trial, Reddington had asked the judge to remove the holdout juror, saying the jury foreperson reported one juror refused to follow the law in regards to reasonable doubt. Sullivan declined to dismiss the juror, saying he reaffirmed his instructions with all the jurors about the law.

Clancy, 36, did not testify during the trial. Her attorneys did not deny that she killed her three children in January 2023. Cora, 5, Dawson, 3, and Callan, 8 months, were found strangled in the basement of the home Clancy shared with her then-husband Patrick in Duxbury, Mass., south of Boston.

In court, Clancy's defense argued that she cannot be held criminally liable because of her mental state at the time of the killings. Her attorneys said Clancy was suffering from severe postpartum psychosis and did not get adequate mental health help when she sought it out. "I'm appalled and disgusted," Reddington said of Clancy's medical care, as NPR and WBUR reported. "It's repulsive what happened here."

Prosecutors argued Clancy was mentally capable of committing a premeditated crime, accusing her of sending her husband out on errands so she would be alone with their children. They also raised doubts about Clancy's claims that a voice told her to kill them.

"This case is not about our mental health system or how it treats women," Sprague said in her closing statement. "That's a distraction to get you angry and passionate about an important issue, but an issue that's not on trial here."

During the trial, the jury visited the house where the Clancy children died. Their bus route also took them past two places Patrick Clancy visited that evening — a CVS and a takeout restaurant — before returning home to the scene. Sullivan told the jurors that the visit, called a "view" under Massachusetts state law, was meant to "help you better understand the evidence" by seeing the locations and the house's layout.

Clancy remains in the custody of a state psychiatric hospital where she has been held for more than three years. She was severely injured by attempts to end her own life, and she used a wheelchair during the trial.
