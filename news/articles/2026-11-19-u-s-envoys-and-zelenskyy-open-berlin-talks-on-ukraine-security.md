## Kushner says Russia is comfortable with NATO-like guarantees for Ukraine

U.S. envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff opened talks in Berlin on Thursday with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy and more than 40 European leaders, gathering officials who support Ukraine's war effort but fear being sidelined by President Donald Trump's direct diplomacy with Russian President Vladimir Putin. Kushner said Russia was "comfortable" with NATO-like security guarantees for Ukraine. Trump called into the session by phone after meeting Putin in China, Kushner said.

Steve Witkoff said Trump discussed Ukraine and a peaceful end to the war during trilateral talks with the Russian and Chinese leaders earlier Thursday. Kushner said on X that Washington's priority for the Berlin meeting was to "further strengthen our close coordination with Ukraine and Europe, work toward a unified position on the key elements of a lasting peace, and to jointly prepare the agenda and proposed format for the next round of multilateral negotiations." Kushner and Witkoff have led the Trump administration's efforts to end the nearly five-year war following Russia's all-out invasion, and their recent mediation has produced no major breakthrough.

The meeting will explore how Ukraine's European backers can fit into postwar security arrangements. Kyiv wants to ensure that Russia does not invade again, while Moscow wants to keep the NATO military alliance away from its borders. Putin said in Shenzhen that the Kremlin was not against European participation but "we need to understand with what Europe will come to these negotiations." He said the Kremlin had proposed to European officials, through diplomatic channels, that they come to Moscow "to determine their position," without elaborating. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov declined to say which European officials had been invited or whether Moscow had received a response, Russian state news agency Tass reported. Putin said no date had been set for the next round of talks.

The Berlin session came hours after a third Ukrainian aerial attack in four days on the Kerch Bridge halted rail traffic while authorities assessed the damage, according to officials. Earlier strikes had shut down the road section, and the rail service resumed Thursday afternoon, RIA Novosti reported. The Associated Press assessed that the strikes seemed calculated to embarrass Putin as he met in China with President Xi Jinping and Trump, to demonstrate that Kyiv still holds cards in the war and to undermine Russia's argument that its victory is inevitable.

The strikes were part of an operation against the 19-kilometer (12-mile) bridge, a key route for military and civilian supplies to Crimea. Moscow seized Crimea in defiance of international law in 2014 and uses it to support front-line troops, making the bridge a potent reminder of the annexation and a prime target for Ukraine. Zelenskyy said a large section of the road deck and an arch collapsed in the culmination of weeks of preparatory strikes over seven stages of the operation. Ukraine's Defense Ministry said the train track was damaged by domestically produced Neptune missiles.

Putin insisted the bridge is a civilian asset and said he had ordered it closed to the transport of military hardware or ammunition. He said the road side had been "seriously damaged" but vowed to rebuild it. He also warned: "We will respond to these attacks, and I want everyone to be clear about that." The rail section is believed to be stronger than the road side and is intended to bear the greater weight of cargo wagons.

Ukraine's Defense Ministry said Russia would need to reroute fuel and ammunition supplies over land, where "our drones are waiting for the cargo." Russia's Defense Ministry said air defenses intercepted 53 Ukrainian drones over 10 Russian regions overnight. A Ukrainian attack on the Saratov region killed three people and wounded two, local Gov. Roman Busargin said. A drone killed a woman traveling by bicycle with her 8-year-old grandson in the Bryansk region, Gov. Egor Kovalchuk said.

Ukrainian authorities said Russia launched anti-ship missiles and 98 drones at Ukraine, with hits recorded at 18 locations. Explosions and sirens rang out across Kyiv during the day, and the Dutch and Austrian embassies sustained minor damage, officials said. Two people, including a 44-year-old woman, were killed and at least 23 people, including a 12-year-old boy, were reported wounded as Russian strikes caused damage in 12 regions.

The Associated Press reported that Russia's larger army hopes to exhaust Ukraine on the front line through sheer weight of numbers in soldiers, armor and weapons, while Kyiv's defenses are largely holding the Russians back, sapping Russian momentum with the help of drones that quickly choke off troop advances. Russia has resorted to strategic bombing to wear down Ukraine's resistance, with an emphasis on knocking out power ahead of winter. Kyiv's domestically developed long-range drones and missiles have struck deep inside Russia, often targeting its oil refineries.

In China, Trump called Putin "my friend." The Associated Press reported that Trump's relationship with Zelenskyy has soured. Ukrainian officials fear their country's fate could be determined by agreements between Trump and Putin, and European governments are alarmed by Moscow's aggression. European leaders have tried to isolate Putin diplomatically and have heavily sanctioned the Russian economy.

The question of U.S. support for Ukraine, severely curtailed under Trump, could be raised by Democrats in Congress after their wins in the midterm elections. In an interview released late Wednesday, Britain's Prince Harry said he believes there is a "big separation" between the Trump administration's stance and that of the American people, in what the Associated Press characterized as a veiled criticism of the U.S. government's policy toward Ukraine.

Berlin's gathering brought together governments with different stakes in the outcome: European leaders want a role in any postwar security framework, Kyiv wants protection against another invasion, and Moscow wants NATO kept away from its borders. Kushner said the meeting would prepare the agenda and proposed format for another round of multilateral negotiations, but neither the security guarantees nor the date for further talks had been settled. Russian and Ukrainian strikes continued across the front and along supply routes while the delegations met. Barry Hatton reported from Lisbon, Portugal.
