## Trip leads to planned China nuclear talks with Xi and Putin

President Donald Trump arrived in Manila on Tuesday, the first stop on a three-day Asia trip. As he descended from Air Force One, Trump pumped his fist and accepted small American and Philippine flags from a child. He waved the flags in large circular motions while smiling, then walked to a waiting helicopter as a marching band played "YMCA."

After the Philippines, Trump was scheduled to travel to China for a planned three-way meeting with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin on nuclear issues. Trump said a meeting with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un was also possible.

The trip marked Trump's first travel abroad since his party suffered losses in the midterm elections. The Associated Press report described the White House and stock market as rattled by Jensen Huang's departure from Nvidia last week, after Huang admitted an affair with an alleged Chinese government agent. Beijing said it was not involved. The report also described the trip as a break from a major disruption to the U.S. aviation industry and a scandal that had shaken the global AI industry.

Trump's first agenda item Tuesday was a meeting with the leaders of the Quad nations, comprising the United States, Australia, India and Japan. It was their first meeting since Trump returned to the White House last year. The group has aimed to counter China's influence in the Asia-Pacific.

He was then scheduled to sign a trade agreement with Thailand. The trade war Trump launched last year had been supported by steep tariff threats. Under a framework announced last October by the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative, Thailand would remove tariffs on most American goods and barriers for importing U.S. autos, food products and pharmaceutical drugs. The Trump administration said Thailand would also spend more than $18 billion on U.S. aircraft and buy U.S. farm goods and liquefied natural gas.

The U.S. Supreme Court overturned in February the 19% tariff Trump placed on goods from Thailand. The administration sought replacement tariffs in July by levying a 12.5% tax on imports from Thailand for not taking steps to oppose the use of forced labor.

Later Tuesday, Trump was to be feted as the "guest of honor" at the annual summit of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations. His first hours in Manila were set to include a cocktail reception and dinner with other world leaders, which the report framed as an intermission from some of the thornier issues at home.

Before the gala, Trump was expected to participate in a "special summit" with the ASEAN leaders. The meeting was expected to address efforts to deepen trade and security ties and the region's longstanding conflicts, including yearslong violence in Myanmar and escalating disputes in the South China Sea.

ASEAN leaders were also working on a non-aggression pact with China in the contested waters of the South China Sea, a key global trade route. Confrontations have escalated in recent years, particularly between Chinese and Philippine coast guard and naval forces at disputed shoals. Beijing claims virtually the entire waterway, while Washington rejects the claim and has repeatedly stated that it is obligated to defend the Philippines if Filipino forces come under armed attack.

About 1,500 left-wing protesters held a noisy rally in a downtown Manila square before Trump's arrival, calling on Philippine leaders not to take their foreign policy cues from the United States. They waved red flags, held up a large streamer reading "Resist U.S. war and plunder!" and planned to burn an effigy of Trump. Riot police with shields and wooden truncheons stood nearby.

Protest leader Teddy Casiño told The Associated Press at the Liwasang Bonifacio square, referring to President Ferdinand Marcos Jr.: "The Philippine government should not deal with such a loser so our call to the Marcos administration is to stop being a puppet of Donald Trump and to uphold an independent foreign policy for the Philippines."

On Wednesday, Trump was set to receive a red-carpet welcome and full military honors from Marcos Jr., followed by a state banquet during his visit to the Malacanang presidential palace. Trump and Marcos were expected to affirm their commitment to the countries' longstanding treaty alliance. The report described the Philippines as Washington's oldest treaty ally in Asia.

Officials were arranging a possible brief reunion in Malacanang with an old acquaintance, Imelda Marcos, if she was well enough and received her doctors' approval. Now 97 and using a wheelchair, the former jet-set Philippine first lady is the mother of the incumbent president. The report said she had partied in New York decades ago in her heyday and described her as the controversial wife of a strongman leader who was ousted in the pro-democracy 1986 "People Power" public uprising.

The friendship between Trump and Marcos endured, Philippine Ambassador to Washington Jose Manuel Romualdez said. "Personal connections are very important to him," Romualdez said. Trump often asked Marcos Jr. and other Philippine officials, "How's Imelda?" When Marcos Jr. met Trump in the White House last year, he carried a note from his mother thanking the U.S. president for his strong backing of the Philippines. Romualdez said: "President Trump was very happy and told Marcos, 'please say hello to your mother,'"

Trump was also expected to announce a series of commercial deals and investments in the aviation, defense, energy and technology sectors before heading to Shenzhen, China, for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. The planned China visit included the three-way nuclear talks with Xi and Putin that the report described as historic.
