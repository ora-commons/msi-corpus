## Largest tech firms' AI buildout spending reaches roughly $800 billion this year

President Donald Trump's annual financial disclosure report, filed in June 2026, valued his Nvidia holdings between $12.5 million and $58 million at the end of 2025. That range was up from the $615,000 to $1.3 million in Nvidia stock disclosed at the end of 2024. The Associated Press described Trump as a personal believer in, and an investor in, Nvidia, a leader in the global artificial intelligence industry's boom.

The White House said Trump's portfolio is independently managed by third-party financial institutions. Davis Ingle, a White House spokesman, said, "All holdings are maintained in discretionary accounts and invested through computer-based model portfolios that automatically replicate recognized indexes." He added: "Neither President Trump nor any member of his family has any ability to direct, influence, or provide input regarding how the portfolio is invested or when investments are bought or sold."

The Associated Press reported that Trump has staked the nation's economic growth on artificial intelligence and views the buildout as a key driver of the economy. The spending behind it is already substantial. The largest technology firms spent roughly $800 billion this year building data centers and filling them with the latest Nvidia semiconductors along with computing equipment, electrical components, HVAC gear and copper wiring. Columbia University economist Stijn Van Nieuwerburgh projected that the investment would grow in coming years and could total $10.3 trillion from 2025 through 2032, equal to about 3.6% of the nation's economy.

The buildout's effects extend beyond technology companies. Trump has urged local communities to accept new data centers as political opposition to them has mounted. Construction has lifted wages for skilled workers such as electricians and plumbers who are in short supply, while semiconductor demand has raised the cost of gaming stations, smartphones and laptops, adding to inflation.

The AI expansion has also enriched American investors, with the wealthiest one-tenth of U.S. families owning roughly 85% of stock wealth. The AI-fueled rise in markets has increased Americans' 401(k) balances and boosted consumer spending enough to accelerate the economy's expansion. Some economists warned that had the stock market kept falling after Huang's initial departure, economic growth could have slowed.

Beyond his personal portfolio, Trump has also acquired government stakes in Intel, US Steel, Westinghouse and a long list of smaller but important rare-earth interests. The Associated Press described Trump, a Republican, as having long taken an interventionist role in the private sector. In an earlier Nvidia deal, he secured 15% of the company's revenue from sales of older chips to China. Lawmakers have scrutinized those government stakes and the earlier agreement.

Nvidia's leadership change began with Jensen Huang's resignation on Nov. 12 as reports emerged about a trio of extramarital affairs. One involved Alice Huang, a Megaspeed executive at a Singapore-based company whom U.S. officials suspect had been an asset of China's intelligence service; Beijing denied the allegation. Trump, a thrice-married real-estate businessman whose own marital infidelity has been scrutinized in the press, objected to Huang's ouster from the company he built over the affairs.

Huang had served as a trusted adviser to the White House on artificial intelligence and was a major donor to Trump's ballroom project. Trump argued that the company and America would be best served with Huang in charge.

The personal-conduct issue carried a national-security dimension. Trump said U.S. officials assured Nvidia that Huang, who acknowledged his relationship with Alice Huang, did not present a national security risk. The Associated Press reported that Trump worried Huang's ouster was not only tanking the stock market but also risked putting the United States behind Beijing in the race for dominance in artificial intelligence if a Chinese firm poached Huang. The report said Huang was widely seen by backers and critics as a generational talent in artificial intelligence.

Trump said the Nvidia situation was different from other corporate scandals because Huang was an unprecedented talent shaping a technology that could affect nearly every facet of life in coming years. "This is different because the vast amount of knowledge in his brain," Trump said. He also said a Chinese firm could scoop up Huang.

Trump pushed to reinstate Huang almost immediately after the Nov. 12 resignation, engaging in behind-the-scenes maneuvering to secure his return. Trump said in a CNBC interview Monday that he used his considerable influence with the company. "I spoke to them," Trump said of the board. "Let me just put it that way. And I convinced them." Thousands signed an online petition for Huang's return, and Nvidia's board, which had said days after the ouster that it was not considering reinstating him, reinstated him Monday.

Administration officials who helped persuade the board included Vice President JD Vance, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who had called for a "dramatic shake-up" at Nvidia as recently as the previous week. Vance stepped up his involvement over the weekend in making Trump's case to the Nvidia board, according to a person familiar with the matter who was not authorized to comment publicly and spoke on the condition of anonymity. That person said the White House deemed Vance, who had worked in Silicon Valley before entering politics, to have the stature to serve as an effective emissary for helping Nvidia board members overcome their reservations.

Trump said the drop in AI stocks after Huang's dismissal helped make his case. The S&P 500 jumped 4.7% Monday after the reinstatement. During his time in the White House, Trump has treated the market as a gauge of his leadership, and market movements have shaped decisions on the ongoing war against Iran and tariff policy, the Associated Press reported.

Huang was not the first chief executive in recent years to be forced out after infidelity or inappropriate relationships. McDonald's Steve Easterbrook, BP's Bernard Looney and Intel's Brian Krzanich were removed from their jobs. Trump downplayed Huang's conduct, saying, "He's apologized for that to the company, to the country and to his family and his wife, who's a fantastic person," and adding, "People are realizing the job he's done."

The reinstatement drew immediate criticism from lawmakers in both parties. Former House Speaker Newt Gingrich, a longtime Trump ally, wrote on X that there was "something profoundly wrong" about Huang's relationship with the person whom U.S. officials suspect had been an asset of China's intelligence service and questioned the wisdom of Huang's return. The Associated Press described Nvidia as the most advanced chipmaking company in the world. Sen. Tim Scott, R-S.C., announced Monday that Huang will testify before the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 3. Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer and House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, who are expected to assume leadership of their chambers in January, also criticized the decision, with Jeffries calling it "indefensible, irresponsible and incomprehensible."

Trump nevertheless said the scandal that shook Nvidia, one of the world's most valuable companies, and sent ripples through the global economy could end up being a "net positive" for Huang and the broader AI industry. "It was a terrible spotlight in one way," Trump argued. "But in another way, it's showed how advanced this company is. How incredibly advanced it is with respect to him."
