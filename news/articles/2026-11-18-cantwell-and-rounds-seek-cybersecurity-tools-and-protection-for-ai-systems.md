## Patch deployment is the official explanation, but the full cause is unclear

Bipartisan U.S. senators Maria Cantwell, the top Democrat on the Senate commerce, science, and transportation committee, and Mike Rounds, the Republican chair of the Senate subcommittee on cybersecurity, asked Jay Clayton, the director of national intelligence and White House AI czar, to identify and fix vulnerabilities across U.S. critical infrastructure after nationwide outages at major banks, workplace communication systems and federal agencies. The Tuesday letter came as the cause of the disruptions and any role for artificial intelligence remained unclear. Cantwell and Rounds also asked that U.S. cybersecurity teams secure the AI systems used to find software weaknesses.

A photograph accompanying the report showed Clayton arriving to testify before a Senate intelligence committee confirmation hearing on Capitol Hill in Washington DC on 15 July 2026.

The senators said their concerns were prompted by reports about AI accessing major banks, cyberattacks in Japan and other developments. Cantwell and Rounds wrote: “The fundamental problem is that AI can discover software weaknesses and help attackers exploit them faster than many organizations can respond.” Their warning focused on the pace of AI-assisted vulnerability discovery and the limits of organizations trying to address it.

The senators also described a deployment problem. “Even when fixes are available, testing and installing patches across interconnected systems can take weeks or months, potentially disrupting essential services,” they wrote.

Over the preceding week, outages hit major banks, workplace communication systems and federal infrastructure, including the Federal Aviation Administration. The FAA disruption triggered a four-hour nationwide flight stoppage that grounded or delayed thousands of flights. The sequence of outages raised alarms about possible cyberattacks, although no single cause had been established for the broader disruptions.

Top officials said the disruptions stemmed from software patch updates intended to address potential AI harms. The broader cause of the outages and the extent of any AI role remained unclear.

Clayton said aging infrastructure required upgrades when speaking to CNBC on Tuesday. “Many people are patching their systems. They know that they’re legacy systems, have vulnerabilities, they’re outdated,” he said. “It is time for an upgrade across our digital infrastructure and as you patch and upgrade your systems, taking them down and bringing them back up, there are things like this that happen.”

Mark Warner, the top Democrat on the Senate intelligence committee, echoed Clayton. “AI is making it possible to identify vulnerabilities across enormous numbers of systems at unprecedented speed, creating enormous pressure on companies and government agencies to patch those vulnerabilities immediately, often all at once, putting tremendous strain on systems that weren’t built to handle that pace of change,” he wrote on X.

Secretary of State Marco Rubio said the tech outages were not linked to one another or the work of malicious actors. The outages hit a public that included voters already skeptical of rapidly integrating and developing AI. That assurance did not resolve uncertainty about what caused the disruptions or whether AI played a role.

JD Vance canceled a scheduled interview at the New York Times’ DealBook summit on Wednesday, saying he needed to attend meetings addressing the recent tech outages.

The letter asks Clayton to provide cybersecurity tools to critical U.S. infrastructure, including hospitals, utilities, small governments and federal agencies, to prevent further disruptions.

Cantwell and Rounds also asked U.S. cybersecurity teams to secure the same AI systems deployed to find software weaknesses, citing concern that those systems could “gain unauthorized access to sensitive systems.” The request pairs continued use of AI-assisted discovery with safeguards for the tools doing the search, while the cause of the broader outages remains unresolved.
