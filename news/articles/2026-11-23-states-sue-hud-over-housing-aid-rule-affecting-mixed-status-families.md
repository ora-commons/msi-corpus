## Nearly 20,000 households nationwide would be cut off from assistance

The attorneys general of 22 states, the District of Columbia and Pennsylvania's governor have sued the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development in federal court to block a rule that would cut mixed-status families off from housing assistance. The complaint, filed in the U.S. District Court for the District of Massachusetts, argues that HUD lacks authority to rewrite federal law requiring subsidies to be prorated to eligible household members. The rule is scheduled to take effect Dec. 14 unless a court blocks it.

Mixed-status families include households with U.S. citizens and people who lack legal immigration status. Under the HUD rule, any household that includes one member without lawful status would be cut off from housing assistance. Subsidy payments are currently prorated to cover eligible recipients only, a provision enacted by Congress.

HUD's figures show that mixed-status families account for less than 1% of the households receiving federal housing assistance. HUD has reintroduced the rule as a proposal targeting rental aid for mixed-status immigrant households, and the rule is set to take effect Dec. 14 unless blocked.

HUD Secretary Scott Turner said the rule "ends the exploitation of taxpayer dollars." "This rule guarantees ONLY AMERICAN citizens and certain eligible noncitizens will reside in HUD-funded housing," Turner said in a statement to Stateline. He added: "This change represents a reallocation of up to $218 million which will now go to eligible Americans that are currently on a waitlist."

HUD argues the rule would allow the department to redistribute tenant subsidies from mixed-status families to households classified as eligible under the new rules.

HUD estimated that nearly 20,000 mixed-status families nationwide would be affected. Most would leave assisted housing voluntarily, the department said, while nearly 1,000 households would require formal eviction.

Advocates and plaintiffs said the rule would affect 78,000 people in total, of whom 55,000 are fully eligible for federal housing assistance. That group skews toward the youngest of renters, and HUD figures listed in the complaint show that 65% of eligible people in mixed-status households are children.

California accounts for the largest share of affected families, followed by Texas and New York. In those states, rental housing advocates warn that thousands of households now receiving prorated rental assistance could lose eligibility entirely if the rule is finalized.

The lawsuit says "HUD lacks authority to rewrite federal law to exclude families this Administration disfavors from federal affordable housing programs." The states argue the change violates federal law.

The plaintiffs also argue the rule would shift the costs of housing displaced people onto state and local governments. Requiring eligible people to prove their legal status would make it harder for them to keep their housing, the states say.

Plaintiffs include the attorneys general of Arizona, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Delaware, Hawaii, Illinois, Maine, Maryland, Massachusetts, Michigan, Minnesota, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, Oregon, Rhode Island, Virginia, Vermont, Washington and Wisconsin, as well as the District of Columbia and the governor of Pennsylvania.

"For decades Congress has recognized the importance of keeping families together even if not every member of a family qualifies for assistance," Arizona Attorney General Kris Mayes, a Democrat, said in a statement. "The Trump administration does not get to unilaterally change those protections," Mayes said.

Tenant and immigrant rights groups, as well as a coalition of cities from across the country, have also sued over the HUD rule.
