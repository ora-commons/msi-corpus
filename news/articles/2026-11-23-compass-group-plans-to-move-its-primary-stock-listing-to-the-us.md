## North America accounts for two-thirds of Compass Group's business

Catering company Compass Group is planning to move its primary stock-market listing from the UK to the US, according to a person close to the company. The person told BBC News that an official announcement was expected in Compass Group's full-year results on Tuesday.

The Financial Times first reported the proposed switch. It is understood that the number of investors and Compass Group's increasing business across the Atlantic are driving the proposal. North America accounts for two-thirds of the group's business and helped drive its total 2025 revenues to $46.1bn.

Compass Group supplies food and drink through several brands to football stadiums, visitor attractions, schools, universities and large corporations. The group operates brands including Levy, Bon Appétit, Eurest, Morrison Healthcare and Chartwells. Compass describes itself as "possibly the biggest business you've never heard of" because of the breadth of its catering and hospitality portfolio.

The company was created during the Second World War after the Factories Canteen Order required large manufacturing or munitions firms employing 250 people or more to provide hot meals for workers. Today, Compass Group operates in more than 25 countries and employs more than 600,000 people.

The proposed move is understood to have no impact on the number of Compass Group's UK employees. A consultation process with investors could take more than a year, and a secondary listing in London is expected. In a sign its listing could change, Compass Group changed the currency in which its standard shares trade on the London Stock Exchange from sterling to US dollars in April this year.

For the London Stock Exchange, the proposal would represent another company moving its primary listing to the US. The London Stock Exchange has seen a growing number of firms move their UK listings to the US in recent years. Other businesses that have left the UK to list shares in the US include Arm Holdings, Just Eat, Deliveroo and Flutter, the parent company of Paddy Power.

Long-running speculation also remains over the future of Shell and AstraZeneca. AstraZeneca began listing shares in New York in February.

London Stock Exchange chief Dame Julia Hoggett has called on the UK government to do more to support homegrown companies and make the country more attractive for investment in its domestic stock market. In the BBC's Big Boss Interview podcast, Hoggett said: "If we want Britain to back Britain, which is what I hear the chancellor and the prime minister saying, then let's make sure that we're creating structural incentives to do so. We need to take the handbrake off."

The contrast between the two markets is substantial. Last year, the London market hosted 23 IPOs that raised £2.1bn. The much larger US market hosted 354 IPOs that raised $44bn, or £33bn.

The immediate next step for Compass Group is the expected announcement in Tuesday's full-year results. Even then, the switch would remain subject to an investor consultation that could take more than a year, while the company is expected to maintain a secondary listing in London.
