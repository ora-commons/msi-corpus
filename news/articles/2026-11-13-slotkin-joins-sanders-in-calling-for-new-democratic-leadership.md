## Abdul El-Sayed backs Van Hollen's bid to lead the Senate majority

Sen. Elissa Slotkin of Michigan joined Sen. Bernie Sanders of Vermont on Tuesday in calling for new Democratic leadership, speaking at a press conference at which neither senator endorsed a specific candidate.

The call came as Democratic Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland said Thursday that he will run for Senate Majority Leader in the next Congress, setting up a challenge to Democratic Leader Chuck Schumer after the party swept to power in the midterm elections.

Van Hollen said in a statement that "the American people called for change" and that is why he is running. "It's now on us to meet this moment, and to do that we need new leadership," Van Hollen said. "This is not about any one person — it's about answering the call of the American people. That is why I'm running for Senate Majority Leader."

Schumer has said he has the necessary support to win the race. The closed-door, secret-ballot election will be held in early December, and it will be the first contested election for Democratic leader in decades. The question of whether to reelect Schumer has already divided the caucus, and many senators will not say publicly how they will vote. Senators have privately deliberated about what message to take from the election, and Schumer recruited many of the party's winning candidates and raised money for them.

Schumer has been talking to Democratic senators since the election and forming an agenda for 2027. In private caucus meetings this week, Schumer laid out a broad plan for the caucus's first 100 days in power.

"I'm going to win it," Schumer told The Associated Press in an interview last week. In a statement after Van Hollen's announcement, Schumer said he welcomes any discussion about the future of the caucus. "I'm proud of what we've accomplished together — 55 seats and counting is an amazing result — and I have the votes to continue serving as Democratic Leader," Schumer said. "I look forward to leading our new Democratic majority and delivering for the American people."

A rift in the party opened in the spring of 2025, when Schumer sided with Republicans on a spending vote that put him on the defensive, The Associated Press reported. Schumer has since led the caucus through a government shutdown last year, and the party won big in the midterm elections. The AP reported that Schumer is the favorite to win the leadership vote and has redeemed himself within his caucus since that spending vote, even as many in the Democratic base are agitating for generational change.

Van Hollen, 67, is not that much younger than Schumer, 75. But he has positioned himself as a new face in the party. He has also said he is considering a run for president.

While most Democratic senators have either said they are supporting Schumer or are undecided, Van Hollen has at least one backer: Sen.-elect Abdul El-Sayed of Michigan, who won a competitive election in his state last week. El-Sayed posted on X on Thursday night that their majority will need "bold leadership."

"I called on Sen. @ChrisVanHollen to lead our caucus as a candidate back in April because he leads with courage and experience," El-Sayed wrote. "We need both right now. Which is why I'm proud to stand with him in his bid to lead our Senate majority."

Several senators have said they are backing Schumer, including incoming Ohio Sen. Sherrod Brown, who beat Republican Sen. Jon Husted last week. Maine Sen. Angus King, an independent who caucuses with Democrats and votes in the leadership election, issued a statement after Van Hollen's announcement: "Why would you fire the manager when you just won the World Series?"

"Chuck Schumer is one of the principal architects of the most successful election in modern history, and I see no reason to change the leadership that helped make that success possible," King wrote.
