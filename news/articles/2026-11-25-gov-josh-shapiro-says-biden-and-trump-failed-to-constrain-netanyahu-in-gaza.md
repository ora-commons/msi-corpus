## Shapiro backs the alliance while urging clear guidance on Israeli defensive weapons

Pennsylvania Gov. Josh Shapiro said former President Joe Biden and President Donald Trump did not take sufficient steps to constrain Benjamin Netanyahu in Gaza. In a Wall Street Journal interview, Shapiro defended the U.S.-Israel alliance while saying there needed to be accountability for Netanyahu’s actions in Gaza.

“Netanyahu is a failed leader,” Shapiro said. “He’s led Israel down a dangerous, dark, isolated path.”

Shapiro has not announced a presidential run for 2028, and he and his family have yet to make a decision on his political future. He is signaling what is likely to be a fractious debate over foreign policy in the Democratic primaries, and his recent forays into foreign policy are fueling speculation that he will plunge into presidential politics. His stance on Israel will face particular scrutiny if he enters what is likely to be a crowded Democratic presidential primary, as the Democratic Party is growing more divided over its relationship with Israel.

Shapiro has garnered significant presidential buzz in recent months, particularly after a midterm election cycle in which Democrats made big gains nationwide. Shapiro this month won his re-election in Pennsylvania, a key battleground state that swung for Trump in 2024, by 22 points.

Shapiro is an observant Jew who keeps kosher and, like his four children, attended Jewish day school. He wrote in his memoir that he lives his faith “out loud.” Shapiro has long advocated for a two-state solution and pushed back against those who say Israel has no right to exist, saying the criticism is a recipe for permanent war.

Shapiro said Israel had a right to defend itself after the Oct. 7, 2023, attacks that triggered the Gaza war. But he said the way Israel carried out its response undermined both the United States and Israel in the region. On defensive weapons, Shapiro said, “Israel should have the ability, as our other allies do, to purchase defensive weapons, weapons that come with very clear guidance on how and when they can be used.”

The issue has been tested in Congress. In July, more than 100 House Democrats voted for an amendment that sought to cut off military financing for Israel. The vote failed and was seen as a key barometer of where the party stands amid growing concern about Israel’s military actions in Gaza. “I don’t think that either the current president or President Biden used the leverage of the United States of America effectively to make sure that the weapons were being used in ways that comport with American values,” Shapiro said.

Shapiro also said Trump needs to end the war in Iran and fire Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. “Trump decided to go it alone. Didn’t explain to the American people why he was doing it, and clearly, he and Hegseth and Vance have no idea how to get out,” he said.

His criticism extended to potential 2028 Republican candidates Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio. Shapiro said Vance and Rubio “both have the stink of Donald Trump on them. It’s real hard to wash clean of that.”

Shapiro called Trump’s negotiations with Russia “an embarrassment to our country.” He also said the United States needs to clamp down on chip exports to China in the wake of the Nvidia scandal.

Ukraine was another area of Shapiro’s criticism. Pennsylvania, his home state, has one of the country’s largest Ukrainian-American diasporas, and Shapiro said the United States needs to rapidly boost support for Ukraine while rebuilding trust with foreign allies. “Share intelligence. Share the weaponry, and help negotiate an end to this war that doesn’t give Russia what they want,” he said.

Trump has maintained a pipeline of U.S. military sales to Ukraine through European allies and enabled U.S. intelligence cooperation with Kyiv to continue. He has also criticized Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky and courted Russian President Vladimir Putin in his bid to broker an end to the war. On Saturday, Trump posted an image of himself embracing the Russian leader with the caption “My Friend.”

Shapiro also spoke Friday at a public event in Philadelphia sponsored by the Atlantic Council think tank and the North Atlantic Treaty Organization. There, he said Trump “actively worked to undermine Zelensky, and he has actively worked to coddle Putin.”

“So much damage has been done that simply stating ‘America is back’ is not going to cut it,” Shapiro said, referring to the slogan Biden used during his presidency. “I’m not convinced that the next president alone can repair the damage Trump has done, and I think the reality is that it’s going to take multiple generations to earn the trust of our allies again.”

The interview also illustrates a divide among potential Democratic contenders. Shapiro and other would-be Democratic presidential candidates largely agree on Iran and Russia, but the party’s progressive flank is pressing to overhaul — or even cast aside entirely — close U.S.-Israeli security cooperation. Biden’s support for Israel fractured the party in the 2024 elections, leaving Shapiro’s position on Gaza under particular scrutiny if he enters what is likely to be a crowded primary.
