## A gamble led Sánchez to call the pivotal election

Spain's pivotal election this Sunday could replace Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez with a conservative coalition that includes the far right. Sam Jones, The Guardian's Madrid correspondent, and Ashifa Kassam, its European community affairs correspondent, reported that Sánchez might be replaced after eight years in power by a conservative government in league with the far right.

The Guardian presents the vote as a turning point for Sánchez and for Spain's left, asking where the result would leave the left both at home and across Europe. The possible shift to a conservative government makes the domestic contest part of a wider European political question.

Sánchez has been in power for eight years, and The Guardian describes him as an icon of the European left and a political survivor. Its report places his tenure against a period in which the right has been advancing across the continent.

The Guardian's account of Sánchez's record highlights his advocacy for immigration as Socialist party leader.

The Guardian characterizes Sánchez as having stood up to Donald Trump and as a notable voice for Palestinians in Gaza. The Guardian includes both positions in its portrait of the Socialist leader whose eight-year tenure could end Sunday.

If the election leads to a conservative coalition including the far right forming government, Sánchez would be replaced after eight years in office. The report's language remains conditional, presenting Sánchez's departure as a possibility rather than a settled result.

Jones and Kassam also said that a gamble led Sánchez to call the election. The decision now places his eight-year tenure before voters, with a conservative coalition including the far right presenting the alternative described in the episode.

The report comes from Jones and Kassam's segment for The Guardian's Today in Focus podcast, presented by Nosheen Iqbal with Sam Jones and Ashifa Kassam. The episode was produced by Tom Glasser, George McDonagh, Natalie Ktena and Ross Burns, with Sami Kent as executive producer.

The episode was published on Thursday, 26 November 2026, at 04.00 CET, as a preview of the weekend vote. Its central contrast is between Sánchez's eight years in power and the possibility of a conservative government including the far right, while its wider question is where that outcome would leave the left in Spain and Europe.
