## UK government backs new laws to protect in-person banking

**Subtype:** fact

- The UK government said on Monday it would adopt the recommendation of a Treasury-commissioned access to banking review to introduce new laws protecting essential face-to-face banking services across the UK.
- The access to banking review, released on Monday and commissioned by the Treasury earlier this year, said digital-only banks such as Monzo and Revolut could be required to contribute to high street banking facilities.
- Almost 7,000 bank branches have closed since 2015, and the review addresses nearly 3 million people who regularly rely on in-person banking across the UK.
- Richard Lloyd, the former Which? director who led the review, said "large numbers of customers of digital-only banks frequently rely on physical cash services".
- The review leaves unresolved which lenders must maintain and pay for in-person services and whether digital-only banks that never operated them would be forced to contribute.

The UK government said on Monday it would adopt the recommendation of a Treasury-commissioned access to banking review, released the same day, to introduce new laws protecting essential face-to-face banking services across the UK. The review outlined plans under which digital-only banks such as Monzo and Revolut could be required to pay towards high street banking facilities.

## Which lenders must fund in-person services remains unsettled

Many details still need to be settled, including which lenders will be expected to maintain and pay for those services going forward. That includes whether digital-only banks, which have never operated in-person services, would be forced to contribute.

The review, led by former Which? director Richard Lloyd, said many customers now held accounts at more than one bank, and that "large numbers of customers of digital-only banks frequently rely on physical cash services". That reliance has fallen on existing services offered by traditional high street lenders, such as NatWest, Barclays, and Lloyds.

The report said the banks responsible for safeguarding these services should be up for "regular review to ensure relevant market developments and changing consumer needs are taken into account". It added: "There is also recognition that vulnerable consumers of all banks may struggle to have their more complex needs met, and this can be expected to become more significant as the profile of customers changes, for example through ageing or vulnerabilities."

## Almost 7,000 branches have closed since 2015

The review addresses nearly 3 million people who regularly rely on in-person banking across the UK, including many older and disabled customers, and people with mental health challenges. The closure of almost 7,000 branches since 2015 has reduced the in-person banking options available to those customers.

Banks have closed branches over the past decade to cut costs after the 2008 banking crash, as well as during the digital push of Covid-era lockdowns and amid growing competition from digital-only banks such as Monzo, Revolut, Starling and JP Morgan's Chase bank.

Those closures have left customers needing in-person banking for everyday tasks such as handling cash, deposits and payments, as well as more complex issues such as fraud, debt, power of attorney, and handling the accounts left behind by family members after they have died.

Monzo, Revolut and Innovate Finance, which represents digital banks and fintechs, were approached for comment.
