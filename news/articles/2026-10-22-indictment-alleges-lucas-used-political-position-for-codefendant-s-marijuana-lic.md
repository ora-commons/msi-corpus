## Indictment alleges Lucas used political position for codefendant's marijuana license

## Blanche said the investigation began well before the second Trump administration

The federal indictment against Virginia state Sen. L. Louise Lucas alleges that she used her political position this year to obtain a recreational marijuana license for another defendant in the case, with the understanding that proceeds from sales under the license would be shared with her to support the alleged enterprise. The document also alleges that law enforcement made multiple controlled purchases of marijuana from Lucas' shop, The Cannabis Outlet, in 2024 and 2025, even though the business purported to sell hemp products. The indictment names six other defendants, including several of her relatives. Lucas faces charges of drug trafficking and money laundering, racketeering conspiracy, conspiracy to commit honest services fraud and maintaining a drug-involved premises; the other defendants face a mix of those charges and other charges. It alleges that Lucas used her political power to insulate the operation from scrutiny.

Lucas surrendered, the Justice Department said, and made a brief appearance in federal court in Norfolk on Thursday afternoon, where she was released on her own recognizance, according to court records. She left the court without addressing journalists who shouted questions her way and went straight into a waiting vehicle. An arraignment is scheduled for next month. AP described Lucas as one of the state's most powerful Democrats. A statement attributed to Lucas posted on her social media account did not directly address the substance of the charges but said Thursday's actions amounted to the federal government wielding its "full weight" against a great-grandmother. "I will not be intimidated. I will not be bullied. And I will not back down," Lucas said in the statement. Her attorney at the hearing declined comment outside.

U.S. Attorney General Todd Blanche, speaking at a news conference with FBI Director Kash Patel, said Lucas "put her own financial interests before the people she was elected to serve." Patel said in a statement that Lucas and a close-knit group of family members and associates orchestrated a multi-year scheme to enrich their own criminal marijuana enterprise, "the Lucas Enterprise." Patel said the group used Lucas' position as chairwoman of the Senate Finance and Appropriations Committee to insulate the enterprise from criminal scrutiny and influence legislation to benefit themselves financially. The indictment alleges that Lucas led a wide-ranging enterprise that acquired and distributed large quantities of marijuana and laundered the proceeds. It describes Lucas as the "leader of the enterprise" who "approved and directed criminal activities," including arranging for the acquisition and distribution of marijuana and supervising the laundering of proceeds.

The charges came after the FBI searched Lucas' hometown office and The Cannabis Outlet in May. At the time of the search, marijuana was recovered at the shop, according to the indictment. Lucas — who has been a vocal critic of Republican President Donald Trump's administration — characterized the search as an attempt to intimidate her.

The indictment comes amid intense scrutiny of Justice Department investigations and prosecutions of political opponents of President Trump. Critics have described those investigations and prosecutions as politically motivated. Blanche rejected any suggestion that politics played a role in Lucas' case, noting that the investigation began well before the second Trump administration started in January 2025. "We don't do political paybacks," Blanche told reporters. A person familiar with the May search, who spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss a continuing criminal investigation, told The Associated Press that the probe opened during Democratic President Joe Biden's administration.

Lucas, who advocated legalizing marijuana, partnered in opening The Cannabis Outlet in 2021. She has publicly spoken about her ownership stake as recently as June, though her most recent financial disclosure forms do not list the business by name. Since 2021, marijuana has been legal to possess in Virginia, and many shops across the state have sold cannabis-derived products. Full recreational sales are not yet underway; legislation enacted earlier this year will allow them beginning in July 2027.

Among the six other defendants who face a mix of the charges Lucas faces and other charges are two of Lucas' daughters, Lisa Lucas Burke and Theresa Lucas-Lamb, and her grandson, Ryan Eichelberger. All three made brief court appearances and were released on their recognizance, according to court records. Attorneys for Lucas-Lamb and Eichelberger declined comment; Andrew Sacks, who was listed in court records as an attorney for Burke, did not respond to requests for comment. Attorneys for two other defendants who are not family members declined comment, and a third defendant released Thursday had no attorney listed in court records.

At the time of the search, many Democrats spoke up to defend Lucas. On Thursday, Scott Surovell, the Democratic leader of the state Senate, said in a statement that the indictment contained "serious allegations" but emphasized that "Senator Lucas has the right to defend herself, and she has my full support as she does so." Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones, a Democrat who is close with Lucas, said the allegations against her must be tested in a "full and fair" legal process. House Speaker Don Scott, Lt. Gov. Ghazala Hashmi and Gov. Abigail Spanberger, all Democrats, echoed those sentiments, with Spanberger adding that the timing of the indictment so close to the midterm election would "further erode" Americans' trust in the Justice Department. Lucas is not on the ballot this year. Ryan McDougle, the Senate Republican leader, said he would not comment while the court proceedings play out.

The 82-year-old lawmaker has spent decades ascending the ranks of Virginia politics and has served since 1992 in the state Senate, where she is president pro tempore, overseeing the chamber in the lieutenant governor's absence. More significantly, she leads the powerful Senate finance committee, through which all legislation with any budget impact must flow. She has used the position to kill legislation she opposes.

AP reported that Lucas, who represents a district that includes her hometown of coastal Portsmouth, "operates with a sometimes-profane, unapologetic style that's drawn comparisons to Trump." The Associated Press also reported that she amassed a six-figure following on X and national attention, using the social media platform to advocate for her priorities and to "bash her critics," including former Republican Gov. Glenn Youngkin, and that she has occasionally "blasted members of her own party, including Spanberger, with whom she's had disagreements."

Earlier this year, Lucas helped lead Democrats' ultimately unsuccessful plan to redraw congressional districts in the blue-leaning state of Virginia into a map intended to elect 10 Democrats and only one Republican. Along the way, she posted quips about four GOP members the intended map sought to oust, joking that they soon would be working at McDonald's. Lucas and other Democrats defended the maps as a reasonable response to Trump-backed efforts to boost the GOP's midterm advantage in other states.

Lucas has notched several historic firsts in her career. According to a biography from the state library, in 1971 she became the Norfolk Naval Shipyard's first female shipfitter, a physically demanding job that involves working on metal assemblies for vessels. She launched her political career in 1984, when, the biography said, she became the first Black woman elected to the Portsmouth City Council. After Democrats flipped control of the Senate in 2019, her peers elected her to serve as president pro tempore, and in 2024 she was tapped to lead the chamber's budget committee.

AP reporter Sarah Rankin reported from Richmond. AP journalists Eric Tucker in Washington and Steve Helber in Norfolk contributed to the report.
