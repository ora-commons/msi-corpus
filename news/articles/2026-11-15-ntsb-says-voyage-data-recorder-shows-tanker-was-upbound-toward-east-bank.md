## Port of New Orleans files $1m lawsuit over collision

Preliminary findings into the collision showed that the tanker had a steering failure, officials said, while the National Transportation Safety Board said in a media briefing on Sunday that it remained unclear what caused the "steering casualty" on the Atlantic Asphalt cargo vessel.

The NTSB said there are many different things that could be classified as a steering casualty, and it is "looking at all of them."

The NTSB's account of the tanker's path comes from the vessel's voyage data recorder. The agency said the Atlantic Asphalt was upbound on the Mississippi River as it moved toward the east bank of the river just before the collision, based on findings from that recorder.

Minutes before the crash, investigators said audio consistent with the ship's warning system was broadcast, along with commands to deploy the vessel's anchors and emergency ship handling orders.

"We are aware that there was an issue with the steering on board and causing the rudder to remain at a starboard position, causing the heading of the vessel to veer towards the east bank," investigator-in-charge Bart Barnum said during the news conference. The exact issue with the steering has yet to be determined, he added.

A preliminary report from the NTSB is expected in 30 days.

The collision left 32 people from the American Heritage riverboat in hospital with non-critical injuries, and no one was unaccounted for.

Calls about the collision began around 13:30 EST (18:30 GMT) on Friday, when people began trying to help passengers escape the boat as it took on water, Port of New Orleans chief Melanie Montroll said after the incident.

There were about 129 passengers, in addition to crew members, on the American Heritage at the time of the collision, according to the port authority.

Passengers began leaving the paddlewheeler vessel, which American Cruise Lines operates, as it started listing before tipping onto its side.

Tugboat operators, police and other rescuers rushed to help while officials shut down traffic on the Mississippi River during the rescue effort.

The American Heritage had been finishing an eight-day cruise calling at destinations in Louisiana and Mississippi along the waterway.

The Port of New Orleans filed a $1m lawsuit over the collision.
