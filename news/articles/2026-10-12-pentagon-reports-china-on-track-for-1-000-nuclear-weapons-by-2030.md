## Washington pushes Beijing for nuclear transparency

A Pentagon report finds China's nuclear weapons stockpile has grown to more than 600 warheads and is on track to surpass 1,000 by 2030, according to The Wall Street Journal. The Trump administration has quietly held talks with Beijing about the arsenal's rapid expansion while pressing China to address allegations it secretly conducted very low-level nuclear tests, which Beijing has denied.

Further talks between U.S. and Chinese officials are expected this month, with Washington continuing to push Beijing for more transparency over its nuclear forces, the Journal reported.

China's atomic program dates to the detonation of its first atomic bomb more than six decades ago. In the days that followed, scientists told the Journal that China probably had enough nuclear material for only a few more atomic bomb devices but could build a significant stockpile within three to five years and eventually build hydrogen bombs. "There are no known obstacles of which we are aware that would prevent it," one physicist said.

More than six decades later, China possesses more than 600 nuclear weapons.
