## Police union criticizes overhaul as administration pledges no additional cost

New York City will trade its Strategic Response Group for a civilian-led dialogue division that carries no weapons and no arrest powers, a 30-officer team assigned to demonstrations, and a new patrol division whose officers receive crowd-management training, under the plan Mayor Zohran Mamdani and NYPD commissioner Jessica Tisch announced Monday. Their joint statement said the overhaul would "transform how the city prepares for and responds to protests, placing communication, de-escalation and civilian coordination at the center of our approach, while protecting New Yorkers' constitutional right to protest".

The Community Dialogue and De-escalation Division, which the statement described as a "first-of-its-kind, civilian-led" body, will sit within the mayor's office of community safety. Unlike the Strategic Response Group, the new division will not have weapons or arrest powers, according to the announcement.

Inside the NYPD's community affairs bureau, the city will create what Mamdani and Tisch called a "specialized dialogue team" known as the First Amendment Support Team, or Fast, which is meant to "improve the NYPD's protest response". Mamdani and Tisch said Fast will consist of 30 uniformed officers trained in communication and de-escalation. A third unit, a patrol support division within the NYPD's patrol services bureau, will be staffed with vetted officers who primarily perform patrol duties and receive specialized crowd-management training to support responses to demonstrations, the statement said.

The statement also introduced "significant new restrictions on the tactics and weapons" officers may use during protests and demonstrations. Irritant gas, kettling and long guns will be banned; responding officers will no longer be allowed to deploy pepper spray "as a crowd-management tool"; and "long-range acoustic devices" meant to affect protesters' hearing "will be limited to 90 decibels", Mamdani and Tisch said.

The Strategic Response Group's counter-terrorism functions — "including heavy weapons deployments at critical infrastructure" — will be consolidated within the NYPD's intelligence and counter-terrorism bureau, Mamdani said. The statement said that helped ensure a complete separation between the city's counter-terrorism operations and protests protected by the rights to free speech and assembly guaranteed by the US constitution's first amendment.

Tisch said the new structure was "designed to consolidate our counterterrorism capabilities, provide greater support to our patrol officers and ensure we continue to respond to protests consistent with public safety and the first amendment". "These changes will make us a stronger, more effective department" she said. The administration said the Strategic Response Group-related reforms come at "no additional cost and without increasing or reducing NYPD headcount".

Civil rights groups praised Monday's announcement. Patrick Hendry, president of the New York Police Benevolent Association, a labor union representing more than 21,000 active NYPD officers, criticized it in a statement on social media. "Today is not a good day for New Yorkers who just want a safe and orderly city," Hendry said in the statement. "Mayor Mamdani is showing his true colors: he's willing to put your safety and police officers' safety at risk to appease his radical base." "So the next time your life is disrupted by demonstrators who are blocking bridges and roadways or causing chaos on our streets, remember: Mayor Mamdani is on their side, not yours," Hendry said.

The disbandment itself Mamdani announced in a video released Monday morning, saying the move stemmed from a campaign pledge he made to disband the New York City police department's strategic response group. "Today, we are keeping that promise," Mamdani said in the video. The unit, founded in 2015 ostensibly to focus on counter-terrorism, has faced criticism over the years from civil rights groups who accused it of using excessive force and unduly aggressive tactics against protesters.

Mamdani in January had reiterated promises to disband the Strategic Response Group after it arrested dozens of demonstrators occupying a hotel lobby in protests opposing Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE), which The Guardian described as the federal agency at the forefront of the mass deportation campaign Donald Trump has pursued throughout his second presidency.

After protests in 2020 over the murder of George Floyd by a Minneapolis police officer, New York City's department of investigation recommended that the NYPD "reevaluate the central role" of the Strategic Response Group "in response to large protests given their orientation to handle counterterrorism, riots, and other serious threats, and better calibrate their use to circumstances that require such specialized force".
