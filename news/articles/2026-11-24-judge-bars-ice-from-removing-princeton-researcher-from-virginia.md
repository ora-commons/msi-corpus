## Xie remains detained as judge seeks response by Wednesday

Eva Yi Xie, a Chinese neuroscience researcher at Princeton University, remains in immigration detention in Virginia after ICE officers took her into custody at an airport last week. Carlos Brody, her PhD adviser, said Xie was resigned to spending Thanksgiving in the facility and called into a weekly lab meeting on Tuesday to thank her co-workers for their support.

“She is in remarkable spirits,” Brody told The Associated Press in an interview. “Eva described it as a very interesting experience where all societal rank is stripped away and that you get to know people and support each other just as people. So she’s making the most of it.”

The Department of Homeland Security, which oversees ICE, says Xie overstayed her visa and did not have authorization to study in the United States. Kerry Doyle, Xie's lawyer, said Xie came to the United States at age 14 with her family from China and has had an asylum application pending for years. Doyle said the pending case permits Xie to live, work and study in the United States.

Xie attended a conference in Washington and was trying to catch a flight last Thursday when ICE officers took her into custody at Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport. The detention occurred at a time when federal authorities had stepped up immigration enforcement at airports.

Xie managed to contact her co-workers while being detained. “I’m detained by ice,” she wrote on the messaging platform Slack, according to a screenshot that Brody shared with the AP. “Plz help I’m in DCA.” She began trying to tell them where she was headed, but the message ended abruptly with the words, “they bring me to.”

The message set off a scramble among Xie's co-workers. Brody was called out of a class and immediately contacted Princeton administrators, who arranged for Xie to have an attorney.

A U.S. judge has ordered ICE not to remove Xie from Virginia, where she is being held at the Caroline Detention Center. The judge has asked the federal government to respond by Wednesday to a petition challenging Xie's detention.

Xie's Princeton colleagues have joined the effort to secure her release. New Jersey Democratic Sens. Cory Booker and Andy Kim have demanded answers and questioned the arrest.

Brody spoke at a rally Monday for Xie at the university, describing it as “focused on what an amazing person Eva is, how brilliant she is and also how deeply rooted in our community she is.”

Xie's research focuses on understanding how brains make decisions, Brody said, work that is crucial because “decision-making is strongly impacted in mental illness.” In the wake of her detention, Brody stressed that “she is one of us, she’s one of the best of us and this shouldn’t be happening to any of us.”
