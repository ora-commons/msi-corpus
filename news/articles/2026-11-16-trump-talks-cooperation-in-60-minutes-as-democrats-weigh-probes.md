## Peltola leads Sullivan as Alaska Senate race remains uncalled

In a “60 Minutes” interview the Sunday after the midterm elections, President Trump talked about working with Democrats on things such as immigration, according to Damian Paletta’s report in The Wall Street Journal. But Paletta pointed to various other data points: Stephen Miller has shown signs of digging in, and Democrats haven’t exactly been lockstep on how to deal with the White House. Democrats are talking about focusing on the “costs,” but many in their ranks want investigations into the White House, Trump’s allies and corporate America.

Paletta named the key Democrats to watch in this space: Sen. Elizabeth Warren; Sen. Richard Blumenthal of Connecticut; Rep. Robert Garcia of California; and Rep. Jamie Raskin of Maryland. Sen. Chuck Schumer and Rep. Hakeem Jeffries, both of New York, will be big players in this, too, Paletta wrote, and he advised keeping an eye on Sen. Adam Schiff of California as well.

The newsletter also revisited a warning already directed at Warren. On Sunday, a Texas-based U.S. attorney, Aaron Reitz, sent what he called an “arrow shot” to Warren, who will likely chair the powerful Senate Banking Committee in January. Reitz told Warren to preserve documents related to her potential future investigations into Wall Street, tech CEOs and others. He alleged her promises to investigate might be crossing a line, potentially a legal line. Reitz ran for Texas attorney general but lost in the GOP primary earlier this year despite being backed by the incumbent, Ken Paxton. Paletta wrote that it’s uncommon for someone like Reitz to send such directives to people far away, let alone members of Congress, but that Reitz appears to have the support of White House chief of staff Stephen Miller.

A separate dispute over aviation funding runs through the same report. Sean Duffy says the ground stop was caused by a lack of investment. In the hours after Friday’s ground stop started, the transportation secretary raced to the White House to brief Trump and later argue Congress needs to deliver $50 billion by the end of this year or else there could be more disruptions to America’s aviation industry. His pleas have been met with some skepticism on Capitol Hill. “It happened on my watch,” Duffy said on NBC’s “Meet the Press.” “But I can’t apologize for what was passed off to us. And I can’t blame Republicans or Democrats because it’s all of them. It goes back 25, 30 years of a lack of investment.”

Former foes Stephen Miller and Marco Rubio are now working together, having formed an unlikely alliance. Paletta wrote that the pairing combines Miller’s hard-edge bureaucratic muscle with Rubio’s diplomatic experience and skill for selling contentious policies at home and abroad. With Miller poised to wield more power as chief of staff, the two men will play leading roles in shaping the remainder of Trump’s presidency.

The Alaska Senate race remains uncalled, with Democrat Mary Peltola holding a lead over Republican incumbent Sen. Dan Sullivan. For his part, Trump’s Monday schedule has the president flying to Alaska; from there, Trump will head to the Philippines, where he will join a summit of Southeast Asian leaders in Manila on Tuesday.

Trump heads to the Philippines at a crucial time. The president’s visit comes at an anxious moment for both the Philippines’ relationship with China and the U.S.’s. The Philippines, a key U.S. ally, has taken a leading role in challenging China’s claims to much of the South China Sea, and would be a central staging ground for any U.S. defense of Taiwan, the self-ruling democracy claimed by Beijing.
