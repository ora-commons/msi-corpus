## Agenda remains unclear as analysts say the leaders bring very different priorities

China’s foreign ministry confirmed that Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin will meet in Shenzhen on Thursday, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit. The Guardian described the gathering as unprecedented, and it brings together the leaders of the world’s three largest nuclear powers as China hosts the APEC summit in the southern technology hub. The Guardian said the confirmation cements Xi’s status as a global powerbroker as he presides over events in Shenzhen.

Foreign ministry spokeswoman Mao Ning said the leaders “will exchange views in-depth on safeguarding world peace and promoting strategic stability in major-power relations, which is of groundbreaking significance.” She added that “Stronger communication among China, Russia, and the United States, along with managing differences and stabilising relations, serves the interests of all three countries.”

China was the last of the three powers to confirm the meeting and stopped short of confirming specific agenda items. In a call this week, China’s top diplomat Wang Yi and U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio “agreed on the importance of meaningful leader-level conversations to advance goals such as peace and denuclearisation,” the U.S. State Department said.

UN Secretary-General António Guterres said on Monday that if the leaders “really commit themselves to a meaningful denuclearisation, it will be a great achievement for the world.” Guterres was among those hoping denuclearisation would be discussed, though China had not identified it as an agenda item.

China has rebuffed previous attempts to become involved in nuclear-arms talks with Russia and the United States, which have the larger nuclear stockpiles. Trump has said he wants to talk about denuclearisation, as well as the US-Israeli war in Iran. China, a top economic partner of Tehran, has sought to mediate the conflict and says avoiding a return to hostilities is a “top priority”.

Russia’s invasion of Ukraine might not come up at all, as feared by Kyiv, despite Putin reportedly wanting it to. U.S. officials said Trump is not expected to raise the issue, and German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier said Xi showed “irritation” in a recent meeting when the idea of the trilateral talks covering Ukraine came up.

Many analysts warned against expecting a major breakthrough. Ryan Hass of Brookings said Xi will “bask in the glow of China serving as the convener and agenda-setter,” but he wrote that “symbolism may prove more enduring than the substance of this meeting.” Xiaoyu Pu from the University of Nevada told The Guardian that the three leaders will approach the talks with very different priorities.

Peter Alexander, the Shanghai-based managing director at Z-Ben Advisors, told CNBC that much of the political benefit for Xi would come from “the ability to bring Putin and Trump to the table.”

The Wall Street Journal reported last week that the Trump administration had pushed back against Beijing’s demands that the summit be tightly scripted, with prepared statements and little room for discussion.

On X, Sari Arho Havrén of the Royal United Services Institute wrote that “China dislikes surprises and wants to ensure neither Trump nor Putin steals the limelight from the host.” Havrén added that Xi “doesn’t want to be dragged into a discussion about Ukraine or anything else he is not prepared for.”
