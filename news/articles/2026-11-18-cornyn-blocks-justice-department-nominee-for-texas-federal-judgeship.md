## White House expects Trump's judicial nominees confirmed before year's end

Outgoing Republican Sen. John Cornyn said Wednesday that he will block Justice Department official Elliot Gaiser from advancing to a federal judgeship in Texas. Cornyn said he has not returned and does not plan to return the blue slip for Gaiser, whom President Donald Trump nominated earlier this month for a Dallas-area vacancy. The nomination is one of Trump's final judicial picks as the Republican-led Senate tries to fill remaining federal court vacancies before Democrats assume power in January.

A blue slip is effectively permission from home-state senators for a nominee to advance, giving them substantial veto power over a president's court picks. Gaiser leads the Justice Department's Office of Legal Counsel. Cornyn said the nominee was not from Texas, had not been vetted and would hold a lifetime job.

"He's not from Texas. He hasn't been vetted. And this is a lifetime job, and there's better qualified people to hold that job," Cornyn said Wednesday.

Cornyn also said the White House had provided no consultation before making the nomination. "This is part, of course, of the constitutional responsibility of senators in the Senate to provide advice and consent. But it's hard to provide advice and consent when there's zero consultation, which happened in that case," he said. Cornyn has repeatedly raised concerns about the nomination process, noting that Gaiser was not vetted by the bipartisan federal judicial evaluation committee traditionally used in Texas.

Cornyn identified Fort Worth lawyer Steve Fahey as his preferred candidate and said the White House had requested information about Fahey. Fahey served in the U.S. Attorney's Office for the Northern District of Texas from 2003 until 2021 and was a partner at Kirkland & Ellis until starting his own firm in 2024. Cornyn said he did not know whether Gaiser would be formally withdrawn, but stressed that he did not want to leave the court seat vacant.

According to Gaiser's nominee questionnaire sent to the Senate Judiciary Committee, Texas Sen. Ted Cruz's staff first contacted him about becoming a federal judge on Nov. 4, the day after the election.

In a statement Wednesday, Cruz said Gaiser was the "only nominee who could actually be confirmed by the end of the year." Cruz said the White House had been "explicit" that "there is no time for a background check to be confirmed for another potential nominee." Gaiser had already been through that process because he was confirmed for his current Justice Department position.

Cruz warned that failure to advance Gaiser would leave the judicial seat vacant under Democratic control. "If the Senate Judiciary Committee does not move Gaiser forward, the people of Texas will pay the price because it will effectively give the judicial seat to the Democrats, who will keep it vacant in perpetuity," Cruz said. "That would be a tragedy."

A White House official, granted anonymity to discuss the ongoing nomination process, said the administration expects all of Trump's judicial nominees to be confirmed by the end of the year.

The Justice Department responded that Cornyn's "political antics only hurt the American public and the rule of law." The department called Gaiser "a brilliant legal mind, a principled defender of the Constitution, and an outstanding judicial nominee" and said it was "deeply disappointing that Senator Cornyn would choose political obstruction over advancing a highly qualified nominee."

Despite his opposition to the nomination, Cornyn said, "He's a very bright young man, and I think has a very bright future." He added, "I kind of feel bad that he's been used basically as a pawn in this process." Cornyn's refusal, the White House's expectation of year-end confirmation and Cruz's warning of a potentially lasting vacancy left the nomination unresolved Wednesday.
