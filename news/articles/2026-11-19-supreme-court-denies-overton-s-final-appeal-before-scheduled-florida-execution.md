## Kirkland says Overton woke at 5 a.m. and remained compliant

The U.S. Supreme Court denied a final appeal in the Florida case of Thomas Mitchell Overton on Thursday, while Overton remained scheduled to receive a three-drug injection at 6 p.m. at a Florida state prison in Raiford. Florida Department of Corrections spokesperson Jordan Kirkland said during a news conference that Overton woke at 5 a.m. and remained compliant. Kirkland also said Overton did not meet with visitors or a spiritual adviser.

Texas had also scheduled Reinaldo Dennes, 70, for lethal injection about an hour later at a prison in Texas, with an appeal still pending in his case. If carried out, the execution would make Dennes the ninth person executed by Texas that year. The Guardian reported that Texas set an execution record in 2000, when 40 people were put to death.

Florida had carried out more than half of the nation’s 37 executions in 2026 under outgoing Republican Gov. Ron DeSantis, according to The Guardian. The Guardian characterized DeSantis as having “made aggressive use of the death penalty like few other governors.” The report said the scheduled cases came amid renewed scrutiny of capital punishment in the U.S. after Tennessee’s failed execution of Christa Pike in October. The planned executions of two men in their 70s reflected the aging of the U.S. death-row population.

Overton had been convicted in 1999 of two counts of first-degree murder and received a death sentence for each. His convictions related to an August 1991 break-in at the Florida Keys home of Michael and Susan MacIvor in Tavernier, where the couple and their unborn child were killed. Florida’s scheduled execution of Overton would have been the state’s 20th that year. Defense attorneys argued that investigators had planted the DNA evidence.

According to court records, the MacIvors were found dead in their Tavernier home in August 1991. Autopsies determined that both had been strangled and that Susan MacIvor, who was eight months pregnant, had been raped. The medical examiner said her unborn male child likely survived for about half an hour after her death.

Investigators collected DNA evidence from the scene but initially found no match with samples in the state database. Overton, who had worked at a gas station near the MacIvors’ home, was arrested on an unrelated burglary charge in 1996. Authorities said a sample taken from him then matched the sample collected from the home, and they charged him with the killings.

Dennes had been convicted of capital murder in the January 1996 death of Janos Szucs, a Houston diamond dealer found shot multiple times in his office during a $3.6 million diamond heist. Dennes was a jewelry designer whose office was in the same building as Szucs, according to court documents. A cleaning woman testified at trial that Dennes had arranged for her to open the building’s back door after hours. According to court records, the cleaning woman opened the door for Dennes and his brother on the night of the shooting and also distracted the security guard at Dennes’s request.

The guard was shot twice but recovered and identified Dennes as his attacker. According to court filings, police found Szucs’s body upon entering his office. The documents said officers found the jeweler’s safe empty, with no sign of the diamonds or the $200,000 in cash he was supposed to have on hand. The diamonds have never been recovered. Dennes’s attorneys later argued that there was insufficient evidence to prove that he killed Szucs.

Prosecutors noted in a recent court filing that Dennes admitted guilt during a Spanish-language interview with Telemundo in 2022. “I did those things, so I am guilty,” Dennes told Telemundo, adding that he regretted taking a man’s life, the prosecutor’s filing said. A year after Dennes’s conviction, his brother, Jose Albert Dennes, pleaded guilty to murder in the case and was sentenced to 45 years in prison. He was released on parole in 2019.
