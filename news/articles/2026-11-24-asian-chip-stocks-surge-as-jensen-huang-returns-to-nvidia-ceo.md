## The S&P 500 has recovered just over half of last week's losses

Asian stocks advanced in Tuesday trading after a Wall Street rally led by Nvidia, whose shares rose 31.5% following co-founder Jensen Huang's return as chief executive. U.S. futures edged higher and oil prices fell, with chip-related shares leading gains across the region.

Taiwan's Taiex led the region, climbing more than 9%. Chip-related stocks Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Corp. and Mediatek each gained nearly 10%.

In Seoul, the Kospi advanced 6.5% to 5,014.41. Samsung Electronics rose 7.5% and SK Hynix gained 8.6%.

Japan's benchmark Nikkei 225 rose 3.9% to 58,654.40. Computer chip equipment maker Tokyo Electron climbed 10.5%, and testing equipment maker Advantest surged 14.1%.

“We are seeing a reaction to the rally in US tech stocks overnight on the announcement that Jensen Huang is returning to NVIDIA as the CEO,” said Neil Newman, managing director and head of strategy at Astris Advisory Japan. “It is obviously a big surprise and a knock-on effect will be through Asia,” Newman said.

Other major indexes posted smaller gains. In Hong Kong, the Hang Seng edged 0.2% higher to 22,432.45, the Shanghai Composite index was up 0.7% to 3,563.58, and Australia's S&P/ASX 200 rose 1.7% to 8,718.80.

On Wall Street, the S&P 500 surged 4.7% on Monday, recovering just over half of its loss from last week, one of the worst since the 2008 financial crisis. The Nasdaq composite jumped 6.5%, and the Dow Jones Industrial Average was up nearly 3%. Trading in the futures market before Wall Street opened had suggested U.S. stocks were set to halt a seven-day losing streak that had dragged the S&P 500 more than 10% below its all-time high, set last month.

Nvidia's 31.5% surge was just shy of its best day since 2000, a time close to the peak of the dot-com bubble. The gain followed a plunge of nearly 23% the previous week.

Nvidia's board is still investigating whether Huang was the target of a foreign spy and whether his return created national-security concerns. Less than a week earlier, Nvidia had rejected an offer by Huang to return as chief executive.

In a CNBC interview Monday, Donald Trump said, “I believe my intervention was very important” in convincing Nvidia's board to reinstate its co-founder. Trump said his administration had assured Nvidia there were no national-security concerns about Huang's return. Trump may have had a direct influence on the most recent surge, and Monday's S&P 500 rebound was the index's best day since April 2025, when the president paused most of the tariffs he had announced on “Liberation Day” that had sent stock markets reeling.

The move carries direct implications for workers' 401(k) accounts, because Nvidia and other chip stocks have grown into some of Wall Street's largest and most influential holdings.

Analysts at Barclays suggested that risks facing the AI industry could weaken its growth, while also saying those risks should only “make the buildout slower and more selective rather than derail it.”

In the bond market, the yield on the 10-year Treasury inched up to 4.93% from 4.92% late Friday as investors felt less need to herd into investments seen as safer. Brent oil fell 3.6% to $86.68 a barrel, and the international benchmark has traded in a range between $72 and $114 since the United States and Israel attacked Iran in February. In early Tuesday trading, Brent fell another 0.5% to $86.27 a barrel and U.S. benchmark crude oil dropped 0.3% to $79.90.

The U.S. dollar fell to 158.92 Japanese yen from 158.98 yen, and the euro rose to $1.0913 from $1.0911.
