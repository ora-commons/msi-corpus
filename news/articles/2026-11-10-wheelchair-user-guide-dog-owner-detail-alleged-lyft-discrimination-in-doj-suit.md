## Justice Department seeks $53 million for people who filed complaints

A Nevada woman who uses a wheelchair because of injuries from a near-fatal car accident alleged that a Lyft driver drove away while she waved to catch his attention, according to a Justice Department lawsuit accusing the ride-share company of discrimination for denying service to passengers with disabilities.

A Texas man with vision and hearing impairments began leaving his guide dog at home despite the challenges of navigating without it, seeking to avoid further service denials, the lawsuit says.

The complaint, filed Monday in the U.S. district court for the Northern District of California, says Lyft drivers have routinely refused to transport individuals who depend on service animals or use mobility devices, such as wheelchairs, an alleged violation of the Americans With Disabilities Act.

The Justice Department accused Lyft of being privy to “widespread disability-based discrimination” on its platform but failing to take action to prevent the alleged mistreatment.

“Individuals with disabilities should be able to rely on rideshare services free of discrimination,” said Jesus A Osete, acting assistant attorney general, in a statement. “We intend to uphold the ADA’s promise that people with disabilities are entitled to equal access in every part of public life, including essential transportation services.”

Lyft firmly rejected the accusations in a statement. “We’ve cooperated with the DOJ throughout its review and remain committed to serving every rider, including riders with disabilities,” the company said.

Lyft said its policies require drivers to accommodate riders who travel with service animals or mobility devices, and that “drivers who violate these policies face serious consequences, including permanent deactivation”.

The lawsuit is the latest federal action targeting ride-hailing companies over the treatment of would-be riders with disabilities. The administration filed a similar lawsuit against Uber last year, which remains ongoing. Uber settled a separate suit in 2022, and Lyft settled a different one in 2020. The companies stress that their policies and in-app tools require that drivers provide ADA accommodations and remind them of the consequences of refusing to do so.

The Justice Department said it is seeking $53 million for individuals who have been discriminated against and have submitted complaints to Lyft or the department. The filing asks a federal court to address the ADA allegations against Lyft, which the company has denied.
