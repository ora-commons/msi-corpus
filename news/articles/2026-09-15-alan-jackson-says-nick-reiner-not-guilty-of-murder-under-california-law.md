## Jake Reiner says parents 'never stopped helping' Nick amid addiction history

Alan Jackson, Nick Reiner's former attorney, previously said that "pursuant to the laws of California, Nick Reiner is not guilty of murder." Jackson did not elaborate in available public reporting on the legal grounds for the assertion.

Jake Reiner, the eldest son of Rob and Michele Singer Reiner, said in a recent interview that his parents "never stopped helping" Nick. Family members have publicly described Nick Reiner's history of addiction and mental health challenges. He collaborated with his father on the film "Being Charlie," which was reportedly inspired by his experiences.

Separately on Tuesday, Los Angeles County District Attorney Nathan Hochman said outside a Los Angeles courthouse that his office will not pursue the death penalty against Nick Reiner if he is convicted. Hochman spoke to reporters ahead of a pre-trial hearing and said prosecutors had weighed the wishes of Reiner family members and mitigating factors in making their decision.

Reiner, 33, has pleaded not guilty to two counts of first-degree murder. The charges include a special circumstance of multiple murders and a special allegation for the use of a dangerous weapon. A grand jury indicted Reiner last month on two counts of first-degree murder with an additional special circumstance of lying in wait.

The maximum penalty Reiner faces, prosecutors said, is life in prison without parole.

While the death penalty remains legal under California law, Governor Gavin Newsom imposed a moratorium on the practice in 2019, and the last execution in the state took place in 2006.

Rob Reiner, 78, was a celebrated director and actor. Michele Singer Reiner, 70, was his wife. They were fatally stabbed at the family's Los Angeles home in December 2025, an event that the Guardian reported "sent shockwaves through Hollywood." Reiner appeared at his arraignment on the murder charges on 23 February 2026 in Los Angeles.

Reuters and the Associated Press contributed to the Guardian's reporting on Tuesday's announcement.
