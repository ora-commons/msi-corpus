## Hegseth blocked an investigation George sought into an unauthorized helicopter flyover

Donald Trump nominated Gen Chris LaNeve to become the US Army's chief of staff on November 17, 2026, with the nomination intended to make LaNeve's interim service permanent. LaNeve has served in the Army's top job on an interim basis since Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth fired Gen Randy George in April.

LaNeve had been serving as Hegseth's senior military commander. He was nominated for a fourth star and served as vice chief of staff of the Army in February. Two months later, Hegseth fired George, and LaNeve moved into the chief of staff position on an interim basis.

The reason for George's firing has never been explained.

George had insisted on an official investigation into an unauthorized March 28 Army helicopter flyover near the Tennessee home of musician Kid Rock.

Pete Hegseth blocked the investigation.

The Guardian cited Wall Street Journal reporting that LaNeve's relatively short tenure as a four-star general had raised questions among Democrats and Republicans.

LaNeve is 59. He graduated from the University of Arizona in 1990 and later earned master's degrees from Central Michigan University and the US Army Command and General Staff College.

His command experience includes a rotation as commander of the 82nd Airborne Division and the 8th Army in Korea.

A photograph published with The Guardian's report shows LaNeve at a ceremony in Normandy in June 2022. The caption credits Staff Sgt. Vincent Levelev of the 82nd Airborne Division.
