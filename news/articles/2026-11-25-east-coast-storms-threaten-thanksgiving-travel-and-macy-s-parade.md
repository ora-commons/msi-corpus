## Forecasters warn of flooding, outages and flight delays

Two storms that converged in the U.S. southeast were expected to move up the East Coast, potentially disrupting Thanksgiving plans for millions of Americans traveling to celebrate the holiday. Heavy rain, strong winds and possible flooding were forecast from Virginia to New York. Strong winds were expected to arrive in the northeast on Thursday, with gusts potentially hitting 60 mph. The gusts could prevent the balloons from flying at the Macy’s Thanksgiving Day Parade in New York City.

New York City emergency management commissioner Christina Farrell told WABC that the city was considering starting the parade earlier than its scheduled 8:30 a.m. She recalled that lightning and storms had reached New York City on the Fourth of July and that the fireworks were moved up slightly. “That is one option. If the weather is coming in a little later, can we start things a little earlier?” Farrell said.

The parade is celebrating its 100th anniversary and has been grounded only once before, when weather kept its balloons deflated in 1971. New York City guidelines say the inflatables cannot fly if sustained winds exceed 23 mph or gusts surpass 34 mph. The guidelines were introduced after gusts of up to 40 mph blew a “Cat in the Hat” balloon into a lamp-post in 1997, and falling debris injured a spectator.

Forecasters expected the storm to move over Cleveland, Buffalo and New England later Thursday, with up to five inches of rain. Wintry weather could also appear in the Mississippi valley and upstate New York. The National Weather Service said “powerful wind gusts of 50 plus mph are forecast along the coast, with 45 plus mph gusts inland to the I-95 corridor and central Appalachians. Power outages and tree damage are possible.”

The service urged coastal residents and people traveling on the East Coast over Thanksgiving to prepare for coastal flooding, strong winds and possible power outages. AccuWeather vice-president of forecast operations Dan DePodwin said the storm would come at a time when millions of people were traveling and businesses were preparing for the holiday season. He said a significant deterioration in the weather was expected to spread northward along and east of the Appalachians from Wednesday through Friday.

Flights to or from Miami, Orlando, Raleigh-Durham, Charlotte and Atlanta were expected to face delays Wednesday. FlightAware showed 738 delays within, into or out of the United States and 30 cancellations that day.

In South Florida, the forecast called for up to 8 inches of rain, damaging wind gusts and brief tornadoes on Wednesday. National Weather Service Miami said flooding was a concern across east-central Florida, especially the Treasure Coast, Brevard and Okeechobee counties.

Airports had also faced recent communications-related outages. The New York Times reported Wednesday that passengers on domestic flights were being trapped between the gate and the runway for more than three hours at rates not seen in more than 15 years.

On the Pacific Coast, high tides associated with the “Beaver” supermoon could break 40-year-old records and flood low-lying coastal communities, with tides peaking on Wednesday at 2.8 feet above normal in the San Francisco Bay Area, according to Dial Hoang, a National Weather Service forecaster.
