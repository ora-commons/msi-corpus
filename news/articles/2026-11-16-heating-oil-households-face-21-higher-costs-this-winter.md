## Heating oil prices rose 74.2% over the past year, topping $6 a gallon

Millions of homeowners in the US will pay hundreds of dollars more this winter to heat their homes as rising energy prices push up the cost of heating oil. The US Energy Information Administration projects that households using heating oil can expect to pay 21% more this year compared with last winter, with warmer temperatures expected to partly offset price increases.

About 3% of US households rely on heating oil as their primary heat source. In the winter of 2023-2024, 4.79 million US households used heating oil as their primary heat source, 82% of them in the north-east. A report from the National Energy Assistance Directors Association found that overall heating costs are expected to climb more than 7% nationwide, with average heating bills rising 16.9% in the north-east.

Recent data showed heating oil recorded a 74.2% increase from September 2025 to September 2026, costing more than $6 a gallon on average. Gas prices at the pump have risen more than 30% compared with last year, and other oil products, including diesel, have gone up even higher.

Much of the surge has been driven by the war in Iran, where the closure of a key oil trading route has led to a prolonged spike in energy prices. Russia's export ban on diesel, which is similar to heating oil, amid the country's war with Ukraine has also affected energy prices, said Patrick De Haan, head of petroleum analysis at GasBuddy. In 2025, Russia exported 10% of internationally traded diesel. The Trump administration has attempted to strike a deal with Russia to export some diesel to the US, but the effort has failed so far.

In Maine, where temperatures can reach below 0F in many parts of the state, residents are bracing for a pricey winter. Around 50% of households in Maine relied on heating oil in 2024, a share that has declined in recent years, though Maine still has the highest proportion of homes using heating oil in the US.

A Reuters photograph from Veazie, Maine, dated October 31, 2026, showed an oil tank standing outside a residence at a mobile home cooperative as energy costs, from home heating oil to gasoline, were a key concern for Maine voters ahead of the US Senate election.

"My home heating oil costs have doubled this year," said Heidi Unruh, 61, of Kittery, Maine, where winter lasts from December through the end of March. "They were already impossibly high in Maine before Trump took office, and I will feel serious pain as the Maine weather dives to it's typical winter chill." It costs more than $1,500 to fill her 250-gallon tank, which has to be refilled every 47 days when temperatures are low. The increase has forced her to cut back on other expenses. "Between oil prices, tariffs, and rising food and energy prices, it will be an especially difficult winter," Unruh said. "Mainers everywhere will struggle along with me. To compensate, I grew a lot of my own food this year. I have cut back on unnecessary expenses, and am trying overall to need less."

Nikki Papadopoulos, who lives in Piscataquis, a county in central Maine, said her family has started using a wood stove because the cost of propane has also been high, and has cut back on hot water, washing dishes in cold water because the water heater also uses oil. "We keep our thermostat turned down to the absolute lowest setting available, and honestly, we're often cold in our own home. We're wearing layers, sweaters and jackets indoors just to stay warm," Papadopoulos said. "We've cut out pretty much all unnecessary spending. We're down to the bare essentials." Her husband recently received a pay increase, but it has been nearly eaten up by the higher cost of heating. "We're essentially making compromises in our everyday routines just to stretch what's in the tank," she added. "We're taking the hit from every direction."

Justin Barnes, a fuel delivery driver in the Bangor, Maine region, said he has had to give residents bills much higher than in recent years. "It's a necessity. For people in colder regions of America, they need it, it's what they keep themselves warm with," Barnes said. "It seems like these politicians ain't doing a lick to lower the prices or make our lives easier, which is their responsibility."

In the recent midterm elections, Democrat Troy Jackson, a logger and former state senator, won his election against longtime Republican senator Susan Collins, in a cycle in which Republicans across the country lost their races.

In Rhode Island, Bob Carpenter, an electrician in Greenville, relies on heating oil to heat his home and has had to save up, bracing for the price hikes to refill his home's oil tank. When his heating oil was delivered last month, it cost $1,425 for roughly 240 gallons — about $600 more than he paid in previous years. The higher heating cost comes on top of other bills that have gotten more expensive, including electricity. "Two years ago, I thought $250 was expensive. Lately, my bills have averaged around $350 a month, which is starting to feel like the new normal," Carpenter said.

Carpenter said he is concerned about the effect higher prices will have on the ability of younger generations, including his children, to save for emergencies. "When necessities take up more of your budget, there's less left for an unexpected car repair or a roof that needs replacing," he added. "What concerns me goes beyond energy. It's the combined cost of groceries, housing, insurance, and other necessities. Inflation means our money doesn't stretch as far."
