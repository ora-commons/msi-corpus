## Some analysts say Oracle relies on Nvidia to backstop the deal

Kathleen Brooks, XTB's research director, described the Oracle-OpenAI arrangement as a $300bn deal under which Oracle would finance five years of cloud computing for the maker of ChatGPT.

Oracle said late Friday that it had secured the capital needed to fulfill its cloud infrastructure contract with OpenAI, making the announcement just after Wall Street closed for the week. Oracle described itself as the first hyperscaler fully funded for the current artificial intelligence investment cycle. The announcement followed investor scrutiny over the amount of capital Oracle would need to fund its expansion of AI data centres.

The financing claim did not reassure all of the market. Some AI critics seized on the statement as a sign that “the lady doth protest too much” and that Oracle “must be in trouble,” while some analysts said they were convinced Oracle relied on Nvidia to backstop the arrangement. The analysts said the deal could fall through without Jensen Huang at the helm.

If the OpenAI contract failed, the analysts said, Oracle could be left responsible for financing $300bn in a rising-interest-rate environment or facing a costly break clause in its contract with OpenAI.

Kathleen Brooks said: “Over the weekend Oracle posted on social media that it had secured full financing for its contracts with OpenAI. This is a $300bn arrangement whereby Oracle would finance a 5-year cloud computing arrangement for the maker of ChatGPT. This would provide large scale AI infrastructure and compute capacity to power OpenAI. Oracle then confirmed that it would post positive free cash flow in fiscal 2028.”
