## Two passing hikers help Sebastián put on spikes and reach safety

Guillermo Batres, an avid 44-year-old hiker, died after slipping on an icy section of a southern California mountain trail while trying to help his teenage son, according to local authorities. The fall occurred Sunday morning during an attempt to summit Mount Baden-Powell in the San Gabriel Mountains.

Steve De Jong, a lieutenant with the Los Angeles county sheriff’s department, said Batres and his son set out Sunday morning with Mount Baden-Powell as their destination. Batres’s wife had turned back earlier in the hike.

Batres recorded a video on Sunday, which was obtained by the Los Angeles Times. The footage panned across the wintry mountain trail, surrounded by green pines.

In the clip, Batres’s son Sebastián held trekking poles and smiled faintly beneath a jacket bundled up toward his nose. The video cut off abruptly as Batres warned his son to be careful in Spanish.

De Jong said Sebastián had slid down the side of a hill and was clinging to a tree. Batres attempted to aid him before losing his footing on the ice and falling down the mountain into a gulch, authorities said. The fall was roughly 500 feet.

The Los Angeles county medical examiner’s office determined that Batres died from blunt-force injuries sustained in the accident.

Two hikers who passed Sebastián after the fall helped him put spikes on his shoes and get to safety, according to De Jong.

Jennifer Morse, Batres’s niece, wrote in a GoFundMe that the family had been left “shocked, grieving and facing an unimaginable loss.”
