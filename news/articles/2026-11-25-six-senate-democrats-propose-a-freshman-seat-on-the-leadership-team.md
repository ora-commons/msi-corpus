## Schumer and Van Hollen back the proposal before next week's vote

A group of Senate Democrats elected in 2024 sent a letter to colleagues on Tuesday asking their party to add an incoming freshman to the leadership ranks, days before Senate Democrats choose their leader next week. Under the proposal, "The Freshman Representative to Leadership would be elected by members of the incoming freshman class and confirmed by the Conference," the letter said. The letter added: "We intend to request a vote on this proposal."

The letter, first reported by Politico, is signed by California Sen. Adam Schiff, Maryland Sen. Angela Alsobrooks, Michigan Sen. Elissa Slotkin, Arizona Sen. Ruben Gallego, Delaware Sen. Lisa Blunt Rochester and New Jersey Sen. Andy Kim. It nods to the diverse class of incoming freshmen who helped catapult the party to controlling both chambers of Congress. "These freshmen have their fingers on the pulse of long-time Democratic voters, those who voted for Democrats for the first time this past Election Day, and everyone in-between," the letter reads, making the case that they deserve a seat at the table.

Senate Democrats have been signaling a desire to uplift new voices in the party. "On election night, people voted very clearly for a new generation of leadership. That was very, very clear," Slotkin said during remarks at a conference of Democrats after the midterms.

New Jersey Sen. Cory Booker told MS NOW that leadership extends beyond the top post. "This is a time for new leadership," Booker said, mentioning wanting to see more of incoming Sens. Annie Andrews of South Carolina and James Talarico of Texas. "I want to see them out front, I want to see them taking issues, I want to see them running on the field."

Senate leadership is comprised not only of the top position of majority leader but of additional conference officers, and both candidates in the leadership contest support adding a position for a freshman to that leadership group. "There should be a freshman senator on the Senate leadership team. As I've said, we need a new way to organize so we can deliver for the American people," Van Hollen posted to social media on Tuesday.

A spokesperson for the incumbent leader also backs the idea. "Leader Schumer was already planning to do this and is incredibly supportive of the idea," a spokesperson told NPR. The spokesperson was not identified.

The rule change comes ahead of the leadership election next week, when Van Hollen faces Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer, D-N.Y., for the majority leader position. Under the proposal, the freshman representative would be chosen by members of the incoming freshman class and confirmed by the Conference.

The proposal does not settle the race for the top job. It adds a seat to the leadership group, and the six senators who sent the letter have said they intend to request a vote on the proposal.
