## Both parties voice concerns over Nvidia and Trump's role

A review of President Donald Trump's financial disclosures found that his investment portfolios bought or sold Nvidia stock at least 44 times this year. The most recent public declarations of his investment trading show purchases of between $5.2 million and $17 million and sales of between $4.4 million and $12 million from January through September, and place his Nvidia holdings between $12.5 million and $58 million at the end of 2025.

The review, by BBC Verify, also marks the limits of the record. Financial disclosures to the Office of Government Ethics report a low and a high range for transaction values, so a more precise analysis of how much Nvidia stock was traded is not possible. The BBC's analysis also does not include other investment products Trump holds that may include Nvidia shares.

The White House says Trump's stock and bond portfolio is independently managed by third parties and that neither he nor his family "has any ability to direct, influence or provide input regarding how the portfolio is invested or when investments are bought or sold." The disclosures document a range of values for each trade rather than a single figure for how much stock moved.

The trading record ends before the recent turmoil at Nvidia. Co-founder and chief executive Jensen Huang resigned on 12 November after reports of extramarital affairs, including with one woman who has alleged ties to Chinese intelligence, and Nvidia's share price dived after news of the resignation. On Monday, Nvidia's board announced that the 63-year-old was being reinstated as chief executive.

Trump told US media that he had "made the deal" to "convince" the tech giant's board to bring Huang back. The board said it had "received input from senior levels of the United States government" that he posed "no national security risk."

Nvidia's share price surged 31% on Monday following Huang's return, adding about $1tn (£763bn) to the company's market value. Even after the gain, the shares remained below the level they held before reports of Huang's alleged affairs began to circulate. The financial disclosures do not cover that period of turmoil or the volatility in Nvidia's share price.

Concerns remain among both Republican and Democratic politicians about Huang's actions, Nvidia's compliance with US controls over the export of advanced computer chips to China, and Trump's involvement in getting him reappointed as CEO.

Nvidia's chip technology and investments have been critical to the growth of the artificial intelligence industry, making it one of the world's most valuable companies. The firm now represents more than 6% of the S&P 500 share index, according to State Street financial services, which makes it the second most valuable company on the index after Apple, whose shares Trump's accounts also frequently trade. Nvidia ranks highly in many global indices, and investment accounts held around the world, including pensions, are likely to include its shares.

Trump made at least $2.2bn (£1.7bn) from a variety of investments and commercial activities, according to the financial disclosure report for the first year of his second term.

Barbara Perry, a presidential historian at the University of Virginia's Miller Center, told the BBC there is "no precedent for this," adding: "It's beyond anything we've ever seen in the presidency."

Taken together, the disclosures document at least 44 Nvidia transactions across Trump's portfolios this year, each valued within a disclosed range, while the filings do not establish how many shares changed hands or how much Nvidia stock moved. The White House maintains that neither Trump nor his family can direct the timing of the trades, and Nvidia's board and the president have both described the administration's involvement in Huang's reinstatement.
