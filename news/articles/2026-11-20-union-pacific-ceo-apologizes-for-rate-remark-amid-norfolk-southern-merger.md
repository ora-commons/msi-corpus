## Opponents cite Vena’s comments to the Surface Transportation Board

The Surface Transportation Board was reviewing the $72 billion Union Pacific-Norfolk Southern merger on Thursday when Union Pacific CEO Jim Vena apologized for saying at a conference in March that he would raise rates for customers or shippers who did not support the deal. Vena spoke during a panel at the RailTrends conference in New York.

“If I had to do it again, I wouldn’t have said it,” Vena said. He added, “It was my attempt at humor. I made a mistake.”

Esther Fung of The Wall Street Journal reported that the transaction would create the first transcontinental U.S. railroad with a single operator and that Vena is trying to gather support for the $72 billion deal.

Merger opponents have cited Vena’s March statement before the Surface Transportation Board. According to TrainsPRO, several comments the opponents filed with the board mentioned or alluded to his remarks.

At the March conference, Vena said he would raise rates for shippers who did not support the merger, “to teach you a lesson.”

Proponents say the combined railroad would cut shipping times and take less-efficient trucks off highways. Opponents argue that the transaction would decrease competition and leave shippers worse off with fewer rail options.

During Thursday’s panel, Vena repeated that Union Pacific does not retaliate against customers and said the railroad was open to discussions with concerned shippers.
