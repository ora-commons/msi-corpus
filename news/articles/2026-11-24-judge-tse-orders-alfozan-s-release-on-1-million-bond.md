## Alfozan must wear an ankle monitor and stay within Northern California

U.S. Magistrate Judge Alex G. Tse ordered Saudi national and former OpenAI engineer Abdulrahman Alfozan released from jail on a $1 million bond pending trial on Tuesday. The judge determined that Alfozan did not present an immediate flight risk, and Alfozan was set to be released Wednesday morning from Santa Clara jail.

Alfozan will have to wear an ankle monitor to track his location and is restricted from leaving Northern California, according to the ruling. He is also to stay at home in Millbrae, California, while the case is pending.

Alfozan pleaded not guilty this month to recording oral communications, and his trial is set to begin in January. The bond order resolves the immediate custody question but leaves the criminal case pending.

Federal prosecutors allege that the 35-year-old Alfozan spent weeks in September collecting secret recordings at OpenAI. According to prosecutors, he used the company's AI tools to help put together inexpensive recording devices.

According to prosecutors, the devices captured conversations in conference rooms, including meetings with OpenAI customers and government officials.

Alfozan was arrested in early October after one of the devices was discovered wrapped in toilet paper behind a wall-mounted conference-room television set in late September.

Federal prosecutors argued that Alfozan should be held without bail because they were concerned he would flee to Saudi Arabia. Tse rejected that request after determining that Alfozan did not present an immediate flight risk.

Prosecutors also allege that Alfozan had been in contact with Saudi officials, including a member of the royal family, about jobs in the kingdom.

Tse's order permits Alfozan to await trial at home under an ankle monitor and the Northern California travel restriction. The underlying allegations and Alfozan's not-guilty plea remain for trial in January.
