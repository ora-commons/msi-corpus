## Hoyer says the nomination bypassed required bipartisan consultation

Senate Republicans Thom Tillis, Susan Collins and John Curtis have raised concerns about President Trump’s nomination of Eric Ueland to lead the Government Accountability Office, according to The Wall Street Journal. A Senate committee hearing is scheduled for Thursday as the 53-47 Republican majority seeks to advance Ueland’s nomination for a 15-year term before Republicans lose control of the majority in January.

Ueland is a longtime Republican aide who worked in the Senate and now serves as deputy director of the White House Office of Management and Budget. Republicans have been trying to move his nomination through the Senate before they lose control of the majority in January. The GAO is Congress’s investigative arm and audits and oversees the federal government, including the executive branch.

Tillis, a Republican from North Carolina, said Tuesday that he was “not a fan” of Ueland’s nomination. He pointed to what he described as OMB’s inability to tally any savings generated by the Department of Government Efficiency, the sweeping effort last year to trim government jobs.

Tillis said, “To me, there’s a lack of discipline and business experience in there to answer that simple question.” He added, “Or the reason they don’t answer it is they don’t have an answer.” Tillis also said Ueland should receive a separate Senate vote rather than be included in a batch of executive-branch nominees slated for an up-or-down vote in December.

Collins, a Republican from Maine, and Curtis, a Republican from Utah, also raised concerns about moving someone from Trump’s budget office into the nonpartisan oversight role. A spokesman for Curtis said the senator had “concerns that the nomination is too partisan” given the nature of the job and questioned what he saw as the rushed manner in which the nomination was brought up.

Republicans now have a 53-47 majority in the Senate. If all Democrats and four Republicans vote against Ueland, his nomination would fail; the number of Republican votes needed to defeat him would fall to three on Dec. 15 when Sen.-elect Sherrod Brown of Ohio is expected to be sworn in. Thursday’s hearing is the first step before a committee vote and a floor vote.

Ueland’s nomination has also drawn objections from Democrats and other critics who say he is too close to OMB Director Russ Vought. The Wall Street Journal reported that Vought moved to stall or cut funding already appropriated by Congress over the past two years, setting up regular clashes with Democrats and some Republicans.

During Trump’s second term, the GAO has sharply criticized OMB, alleging that the office violated federal laws governing how the executive branch spends or withholds money appropriated by Congress.

The law governing selection of the comptroller general calls for a bipartisan consultation process to suggest names to the president. Democrats argued that Republicans short-circuited those steps to accelerate the nomination while the GOP still controlled the Senate. Rep. Steny Hoyer said, “It is disturbing and unacceptable for President Trump to circumvent the statutory bipartisan selection process and nominate a deputy of OMB Director Russ Vought—a proponent of unchecked executive power—without input from both parties in Congress.”

Not all Republicans objected. Sen. James Lankford of Oklahoma said, “I think he’s very, very competent, actually.” He added, “He has very good experience in a lot of different agencies, so he brings a pretty diverse set of experience there.”

Republicans are anticipating investigations and oversight actions led by Democrats after they take power in January. The Wall Street Journal said a Trump appointee and ally leading the GAO could benefit Republicans. Congress can assign the agency certain audits and investigative actions, which in theory could be hindered by a Republican-controlled agency. The Journal also said a GAO run by a Trump ally could be more deferential to OMB’s attempts to rescind or alter funding approved by Congress.

Gene Dodaro, the previous GAO director, retired when his term ended in December 2025, and GAO has been run by an acting comptroller general since then.
