## O'Dowd says Treasury figures put NI funding below need level for next two years

Northern Ireland's public funding has worsened relative to the rest of the UK as a result of the Westminster budget, Stormont's finance minister John O'Dowd has claimed. O'Dowd said the Treasury's figures show that Northern Ireland will now be funded below its objective "level of need" next year and the year after.

Needs-based funding recognises that it costs more to deliver public services in some parts of the UK compared to others, and attempts to give each part of the UK the same quality of public services. It recognises that public services can be more expensive to deliver in Northern Ireland because, for example, it has a more rural and dispersed population than in England.

Northern Ireland is therefore assessed as needing £124 per head for every £100 per head spent on public services in England. This is known as the 124% needs-based funding level.

O'Dowd said the Treasury's figures put the region below that mark. "Treasury's own assessment shows that we are underfunded, relative to our current estimate of need, in 2027/28 and 2028/29," he said. "My officials' analysis also indicates that we are underfunded this year, and therefore in each year of the Spending Review."

It is understood O'Dowd believes that in cash terms this means public services will be underfunded by around £450m over the next three years. It is understood the Treasury figures and Department of Finance calculations show Northern Ireland will be funded at around 123% this year and in the two following years.

O'Dowd said the size of the UK government's offer underlined the gap. "Around one third of the currently proposed financial package from the British government is required on its own to merely bring us to the 124% level of need," he said.

The Secretary of State, Sir Chris Bryant, has offered £1.5bn over the next three years and said that if Stormont ministers accept his offer, that will take funding comfortably above the level of need. "The NI executive has been offered an additional £1.5bn over the next three years, which would mean NI remains funded above 126% in all years of the Spending Review," he said.

Bryant also pressed the executive to conclude its own budget process. "If the executive is to avoid a major public services crisis in the new year it needs to set a budget as a matter of urgency. That is its legal responsibility."

Executive ministers held further talks about the budget deadlock on Monday afternoon at Stormont Castle. Following the meeting, First Minister Michelle O'Neill said the Secretary of State is "not listening" to the Stormont executive over its continuing budget pressures.

O'Neill said the executive's concerns may now need to be "elevated" to the prime minister and warned Andy Burnham that Stormont's political and financial stability were "two sides of the one coin". She reiterated the executive's argument that Northern Ireland has been significantly "underfunded" in comparison to the devolved governments in Scotland and Wales, and said it was "very clear that we have taken this to where we can with the Secretary of State".

Deputy First Minister Emma Little-Pengelly said what the executive was asking of the government was "absolutely reasonable". Speaking after the executive meeting, she said it was "positive that we're continuing to discuss these issues".

"What we are asking the UK government to do is absolutely reasonable, and that is to work with us to try to get a balanced budget to actually fulfil the legal duties that we have," she said. "We will be doing everything we can in the DUP to support getting that budget settlement to enable us to do public sector pay."

Stormont ministers are currently trying to get more money from the UK government which they say they need to reach a balanced budget. Stormont ministers say Bryant's £1.5bn is not enough, and O'Dowd says the needs-based underfunding makes the situation worse.
