## We Are CASA says ICE removed Ábrego García's ankle monitor before departure

Kilmar Ábrego García arrived in Madrid on Wednesday, 25 November 2026, after leaving the United States of his own volition. He did not speak to reporters as he made his way through the airport, raising his fist in the air as he climbed into a waiting van.

Immigration and Customs Enforcement agents met Ábrego García at the airport and removed his ankle monitor before his departure, according to We Are CASA, a Maryland-based advocacy group. The group described the interaction as “a powerful symbol that ends a chapter marked by illegal deportation, imprisonment, abuse at Cecot, retaliatory prosecution, and government surveillance.”

Ábrego García described the departure in a statement provided by We Are CASA as proof that justice was still capable of prevailing over the most powerful government in the world. “In this case, despite everything they tried to say, every obstacle they put in my way, and every attempt to shame me, justice prevailed under this administration in a way that many people never thought was possible,” he said. “I am leaving happy. I am leaving with my freedom.”

The Department of Homeland Security said on Tuesday that Ábrego García had “self-deported” to Spain. The Washington Post reported earlier this week that Ábrego García had negotiated a deal with federal authorities to depart the United States voluntarily in order to avoid being forcibly removed.

His lawyer, Simon Sandoval-Moshenberg, said on Wednesday that the Trump administration had wanted to deport Ábrego García “handcuffed, on an ICE flight bound for Liberia” on Tuesday. “And what happened? He walked out a free man, without any restrictions, without shackles on his feet. He left for the country of his choosing, and he did so on a commercial flight, alongside everyone else,” Sandoval-Moshenberg added, describing the outcome as “a victory.”

Chris Newman, another of Ábrego García’s lawyers, said his client had landed in Spain as a tourist. “I can confirm that he’s coming here with the intention of staying for just a few days and that he already has tickets, one could say, for his next appointment,” Newman told Spanish media.

Sandoval-Moshenberg described the departure as temporary. “Mr Ábrego García has many options to return to the United States as the husband of a US citizen spouse and the father of a US citizen child,” he said. “We don’t expect that to happen under this administration, which we know will never give him a fair shake, but we expect that the next administration will prioritise reunifying this family and bringing him back to Maryland.”

Ábrego García came to national attention in 2025 after he was deported to El Salvador in what an official later called an “administrative error.” His mistaken deportation turned him into a symbol of Donald Trump’s mass deportation efforts. The 31-year-old had been living in the United States since 2019, after a judge ruled that he could not be deported to El Salvador because of the dangers posed by a gang that had threatened his family. Married to an American woman and living in Maryland, he was sent to Cecot, an anti-terrorism mega-prison, in violation of the US immigration judge’s 2019 order.

He was returned to the United States after a decision by the supreme court, amid public outcry. Since then, Ábrego García has been fighting government efforts to deport him for a second time to a series of African countries proposed by Department of Homeland Security officials.

His departure was celebrated by many people who had sought to push back against the Trump administration’s pursuit of Ábrego García. Chris Van Hollen, the Maryland senator who visited Ábrego García at Cecot, said in a statement: “A year and a half ago, the Trump Admin wanted to leave Kilmar to rot in a Salvadoran gulag.” He added: “Today, he is traveling to a country of his choice, a free man – unharmed and with dignity.”

Both the Department of Homeland Security and Ábrego García’s lawyers described the departure as voluntary, though they differ on what preceded it: the department said he “self-deported,” while Sandoval-Moshenberg said the administration had sought to remove him by force on Tuesday and that he instead left on a commercial flight “without any restrictions, without shackles on his feet.” Newman’s account of a tourist stay of a few days leaves open when, or whether, Ábrego García returns to the United States.
