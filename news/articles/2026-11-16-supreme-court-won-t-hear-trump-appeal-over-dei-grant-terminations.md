## Supreme Court won't hear Trump appeal over DEI grant terminations

**Subtype:** fact

## Three conservative justices signal interest in California bias-course case

The Supreme Court on Monday turned away the Trump administration's appeal over DEI funding cuts to academic research, an order the justices issued even as some conservative justices publicly raised questions about implicit bias training in a separate California case.

Declining the appeal — the justices' first decision — left in place the lower-court rulings holding that the administration likely ran afoul of the First Amendment when it pulled millions of dollars in grants from University of California researchers. No justice publicly dissented from the brief order declining to hear the case.

The cuts came after an executive order issued in January 2025 telling the government to end programs linked to DEI, an acronym for diversity, equity and inclusion. Contesting the lawsuit, the Trump administration has contended that the federal government has no obligation to keep funding research that no longer matches its priorities.

The challenge reaching the justices involves only a portion of the administration's research funding cuts, and the lawsuit will continue to play out.

The court separately declined on Monday to take up a challenge to a California requirement for continuing medical education courses to include discussion of how unconscious bias can affect healthcare outcomes.

Three conservative justices voiced an interest in those claims. Thomas and Alito dissented from the refusal to hear the case, writing that the California requirement raises questions about state regulation of private speech. Gorsuch also noted briefly that he would have heard the case.
