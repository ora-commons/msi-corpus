## The more coin a guest bought, the closer the access to Trump

President Donald Trump held another dinner last night to reward the top holders of the Trump meme coin, a cryptocurrency he launched right before his inauguration. The event drew over a hundred guests to his golf club in Northern Virginia on a damp evening and was promoted as the most exclusive dinner in the world. Two attendees who spoke with NPR's Rafael Nam described what the night bought and cost them, and how one of them was removed from the gala before the president arrived.

Christensen and his sister Gabrielle Felen, visiting from the Netherlands, rode to the club together in an Uber, with Felen in a black dress. Christensen was upbeat about the company rather than the politics. He said: "Yeah. I'm excited. It's always nice to dress up for an event. My sister looks amazing." Felen said: "Yeah. I'm also excited, a little bit nervous, but excited about how it's going to be."

Both siblings said Trump's politics were not what drew them to the event. Christensen said he thinks meme coins are stupid, but the draw for him was a chance to see "the leader of the free world" up close and mingle with guests like Kevin O'Leary from "Shark Tank."

Christensen has attended these events before, and this time it was a lot less expensive. The coin has cratered in price. Nam said a sophisticated trading strategy helped them attend for free, and Christensen also said they were basically going for free.

Christensen and Felen agreed to share voice memos through the night. Christensen, Felen and Nam buckled in for the event. Trump's crypto events are controversial, and access is at the center of the dinners. Nam said the top holders of the coin get to spend time with the president, and the more a guest buys, the closer the access. In the past, many attendees at Trump's crypto events have not been from the U.S., raising concerns that Trump could be influenced, which the White House denies.

Christensen told Nam who he had met in the gala room: "One was from Saudi. One was from London. Met a couple of people from Ukraine, two people from India, four from China. I think one was from South Korea."

What Christensen expected when he arrived was not what he got. He said he believed he was on the VIP list. "I got into the entrance, and they said, I'm not on the VIP list, even though I have the email of VIP and everything, and they just said, I'm not on the list, so..."

Nam reported that a couple of people approached Christensen just as Christensen and Felen were getting ready for Trump to walk in, and that Christensen was being kicked out for posting pictures online and talking to people in the media, including Nam. Christensen ended up outside the golf club in the middle of soaking rain. "So I got removed from the event, from the Trump gala. I'm not sure why," he said. NPR did not get a response from organizers as to why.

At the golf course, Christensen and Felen decided together that Felen would stay. She had come all the way to see the president, and she finally got the moment she had been waiting for. An unidentified musical artist sang, "God bless the USA." Cheering followed. An unidentified person said, "Long live Donald Trump." Trump asked whether the music should continue: "Do you want to hear another one (ph)? Should we keep the song going?" An unidentified person answered, "Yeah."

Trump then delivered a speech that, Nam said, "veered everywhere, from crypto to digs on Biden" — a speech Christensen never got to hear.

Nam said Felen enjoyed hearing Trump, but she also felt for her brother. Felen said she was disappointed for him, especially after seeing that some VIP guests received one-on-one selfies with the president. "I was definitely disappointed for him, especially when, later, I saw that some people who were in VIP actually got, like, a one-on-one selfie with Trump, so probably also got to shake his hand, say some words. So that would have been a great opportunity," she said.

Christensen took it differently: "It happens, you know. It's a different kind of experience this time around - could have been better for me. But I will just laugh about it." As they departed the U.S., Christensen and Felen were no longer as excited, Nam said. Nam said that, like many who had purchased the Trump meme coin, they had "a different feeling - disappointment."

In the same segment, host Juana Summers said that with Democrats now holding majorities in Congress, Trump would likely face investigations into the billions of dollars he and his family had made in crypto. For the guests who stayed, what the access was worth was uneven: Felen heard the speech and saw some VIP guests receive one-on-one selfies with Trump, while Christensen heard nothing and ended up outside in the rain.
