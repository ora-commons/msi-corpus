## Republican and Democratic lawmakers criticize Huang's reinstatement

Nvidia's shares fell almost 8% on Tuesday, reversing part of the gain that followed Jensen Huang's return as chief executive, as U.S. lawmakers questioned President Donald Trump's role in the reinstatement. The stock remained well above Friday's close, before the comeback was revealed. AMD dropped more than 10%, while Broadcom and Marvell Technology also traded lower.

Huang, 63, was handed back his job at the AI microchip maker on Monday, less than two weeks after he was forced to resign following revelations of extramarital affairs, one involving a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy. The announcement initially sent Nvidia's market value surging, but volatile Tuesday trading erased some of those gains.

Nvidia's share price was already down almost 30% over the previous six months, and the decline began before the scandal involving its co-founder emerged earlier this month. The broader chip-stock declines suggested investors were weighing potential regulation by newly elected Democrats and whether the large sums being invested in AI will pay off.

Nvidia's board reinstated Huang on Monday in a U-turn from its original position. Harvey Jones, chairman of Nvidia's board, said the return was in the "best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers." The board said it had received input from senior levels of the U.S. government and was confident the reinstatement posed no national-security risk.

President Donald Trump said he had spoken with Nvidia's directors and convinced them to bring Huang back. "They did ask for assurance on the national security threat," Trump told CNBC. "And we gave them assurances at least with everything we know. And we know a lot."

Republican and Democratic lawmakers criticized the reinstatement. Republican representative Don Bacon said Nvidia should have "ignored the Administration's inexplicable desire to save Huang." Republican senator Pete Ricketts said Huang's behavior "doesn't absolve Nvidia of answering for national security concerns and unaccounted-for chips."

Democratic representative Robert Garcia, the incoming chairman of the House oversight committee, said Trump's "direct involvement" with Nvidia's decision "raises serious national security and corruption questions." Democratic senator Ruben Gallego said Congress needed to "investigate what stock positions took place before the announcement."

Huang remains set to appear before Congress next week. The Nvidia chief executive will be questioned about his relationship with Alice Huang, who is alleged to be a spy for China, because of national-security concerns.

The U.S. Commerce Department announced that it was preparing to blacklist Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer where Alice Huang, who is not related to Jensen Huang, is chief executive. China denied the spying allegations.

Tuesday's partial reversal of the initial stock gains occurred as Republican and Democratic lawmakers scrutinized the reinstatement and Trump's role in it, with the board's national-security assurance and Huang's coming testimony still under scrutiny.
