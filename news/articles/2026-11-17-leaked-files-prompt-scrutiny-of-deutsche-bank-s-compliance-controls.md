## Bank cites €7bn in control spending from 2019 to 2025

An international investigation based on a trove of 15 million leaked Deutsche Bank files alleges that the German lender dismissed compliance warnings about high-profile Russian oligarchs and other risky clients to protect profits. The investigations were published by outlets including Der Spiegel, the Financial Times, the New York Times and Le Monde, according to The Guardian, which said the documents show repeated inattention to internal risk warnings.

The reports suggested that Deutsche Bank continued expanding its Russian business until 2021 despite warnings from compliance officers and growing geopolitical tensions between Nato allies and Moscow. The reports also alleged that the bank resisted withdrawing from Russia after the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, before reversing course and exiting the country in March that year.

The investigations suggested that competitive pressure, including competition from American banks, shaped a growth strategy pursued by chief executive Christian Sewing. The Financial Times reported that Deutsche Bank’s “most sensitive clients” covered roughly 50 corporate clients, ranked by closeness to the Kremlin and by other potential risks, including financial crime. Names allegedly on the list included Russia’s state-owned gas company Gazprom, petrochemicals group Sibur and aluminium magnate Oleg Deripaska.

Deutsche Bank said the issues raised in the investigations “date back many years, in some cases as much as two decades” and had been addressed by leadership. In its fuller response, the bank said: “Where relevant, we reviewed these matters at the time and worked closely with regulators.”

The bank said it had “gone through a fundamental transformation” under Sewing and described €7bn in direct investments in its control environment between 2019 and 2025. Deutsche Bank tied those investments to sustainable profitability, citing record profits in 2025 and recent quarters.

Deutsche Bank also said: “We remain committed to ensuring continuous enhancements of our control environment and a culture of integrity and completing any outstanding remediation.” The statement came in the bank’s public response to the investigations.

Beyond Russia, The Guardian said Deutsche Bank faced fresh scrutiny over allegations that it processed almost $600m (£454m) of suspicious payments linked to Jeffrey Epstein, a figure the report said was much higher than previously known. Deutsche Bank said it “regrets [its] historical relationship” with Epstein and had made “considerable investments in strengthening controls.”

The bank was also accused of accidentally leaking US President Donald Trump’s tax documents to another client during his first term in office and of keeping the incident secret from the White House. Deutsche Bank said it did not comment on former or existing client relationships. The bank cut ties with Trump in 2021.

The new reporting also revisited Deutsche Bank’s earlier history. The Guardian said the bank had already been caught being used to launder billions of dollars for Russian criminals, spies and oligarchs between 2010 and 2015. The Guardian described the latest findings as a fresh blow to a lender trying to repair its reputation after a number of scandals and dealings with controversial clients including Trump and Epstein.

The reports prompted a 4% drop in Deutsche Bank’s German-listed shares on Tuesday.

Deutsche Bank also addressed the confidentiality of the leaked material. “Regardless of how the information was obtained, it includes confidential information about clients, employees and other third parties, which remains legally protected. We have acted, and will continue to act, decisively to protect this,” the bank said.
