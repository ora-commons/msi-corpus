## Bishara Bahbah rebukes Trump as El-Sayed rejects extremist ties

Donald Trump called Abdul El-Sayed a “jihadist” who was “on the side of the terrorists” at a Michigan rally Sunday and questioned the U.S. Senate candidate’s loyalty to the United States. Trump said El-Sayed, a former Detroit health official, would “have to go through a metal detector before he’s allowed to go into the Senate.”

Trump repeatedly invoked 9/11 during the rally for Republican Michigan Senate candidate Mike Rogers. According to AP, he compared preventing El-Sayed from entering the Senate chamber with the passengers who fought back against the hijackers aboard United Flight 93.

El-Sayed, who would become the nation’s first Muslim senator if elected, called attacks on his loyalty “shameful” and repeatedly denied any ties to extremist groups.

The remarks drew a sharp rebuke from Bishara Bahbah, a founder of Arab Americans for Trump whom The Guardian described as a former top Arab American ally of the president. The group helped lead Trump’s outreach to Arab American voters in 2024. Bahbah said in a phone interview, “The president’s statements are out of line,” and added, “They are crossing a red line. They are racist.”

El-Sayed, who would become the nation’s first Muslim senator if elected, denied any ties to extremist groups after Trump called him a “jihadist” and said he was “on the side of the terrorists.” Bahbah said the statements crossed a red line and were racist.

## Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program enrollment falls 13%

Enrollment in the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program also declined significantly over the past year after restrictions imposed by the Trump administration, stakeholders said. The restrictions forced more people to seek help from food banks or go hungry, the stakeholders said.
