## Johnson seeks two-thirds support for a vote before Thanksgiving

New Mexico Democratic Rep. Melanie Stansbury on Monday urged fellow House members to vote against the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act as written or delay the roughly 500-page permitting measure for at least six weeks. She was joined on the call by Joey Sanchez, chairman of the All Pueblo Council of Governors, as the bill moved from the Senate to the House. Stansbury said the newly elected Democratic majority in both chambers should have time to improve the legislation.

The measure cleared the U.S. Senate last week by a 62-33 vote without hearings in Senate committees. Sen. Martin Heinrich of New Mexico, a sponsor, said the legislation would streamline and hasten the federal process for approving or denying projects such as solar arrays, gas pipelines and electrical transmission lines. Heinrich and other sponsors urged Congress to pass the bill this year, saying it would help expedite projects and bring down costs.

President Donald Trump supports the bill. Since its introduction in October, the legislation has drawn widespread criticism, with opponents saying it would weaken longstanding environmental protections and speed construction of artificial intelligence data centers and fossil fuel projects.

Stansbury has emerged as one of the bill's leading House opponents. Reps. Teresa Leger Fernández and Gabe Vasquez, the other two New Mexico members of the House, are also Democrats and have publicly opposed the legislation in its current form. Sen. Ben Ray Luján, also a Democrat, voted against it last week.

Stansbury said, “This is a bad bill at a bad time that does bad things,” and urged representatives to “wait until next Congress. Wait until we can work on putting together a truly bipartisan bill that is put together through tribal consultation, through a public process, through public hearings, and with consideration for the impacts for the environment, climate change, affordability and our communities.”

The All Pueblo Council of Governors represents 19 New Mexico pueblos and has said the bill would deprive tribes of meaningful input on federal projects that could threaten historic or culturally important sites. Sanchez joined Monday's call to warn against legislation he said would destroy “places that have been sacred to us for thousands of years.” “To just break that up, that’s something that can never be replaced,” he said. “So we are strictly and adamantly opposed to this bill.”

House Speaker Mike Johnson announced last week that he would push to fast-track the bill to a vote before Thanksgiving through a procedure requiring support from two-thirds of the members in attendance, Politico reported. Stansbury's request for at least six weeks and the All Pueblo Council of Governors' opposition come as the legislation reaches the House.


MSI previously reported [that the Senate passed the permitting measure 62-33 and sent it to the House](/articles/2026-11-20-senate-passes-permitting-overhaul-bill-moves-to-house/). Stansbury and tribal leaders are now pressing their objections in the House as Johnson seeks a fast-tracked vote before Thanksgiving.
