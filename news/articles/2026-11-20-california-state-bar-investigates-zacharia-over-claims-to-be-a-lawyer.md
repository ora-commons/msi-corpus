## Zacharia earned a law degree but never passed any U.S. bar exam

The California state bar is investigating Katie Zacharia over claims that she repeatedly represented herself as a lawyer despite never passing a bar exam. The former Trump administration White House press secretary was hired and fired in the same week in October 2026, and her credentials now face a state bar inquiry.

The Guardian reported that earlier this month, the California state bar told Campaign for Accountability it had assigned an investigator and legal adviser to examine the complaint. The nonprofit watchdog submitted its complaint in mid-October.

Zacharia received a law degree from Pepperdine University in 2011 but never passed the bar exam in California or any other U.S. state. The complaint focuses on the distinction between holding a law degree and being licensed to practice law.

Before her White House tenure, Zacharia repeatedly described herself as a lawyer on cable news programs. Her LinkedIn profile said she had served as general counsel for a nonprofit organization in California, and Donald Trump called her an “attorney” when announcing her appointment.

Michelle Kuppersmith, the director of the Campaign for Accountability, said in a statement: “Zacharia may have thought she was just playing a lawyer on TV, but by repeatedly holding herself out as an attorney on news programs, she was pretending to have authority she lacks,”

“We’re pleased the California Bar is taking this matter seriously and investigating,” Kuppersmith added.

The complaint says calling oneself a lawyer without a license in California “isn’t puffery; it’s a crime.” The Campaign for Accountability asked the state bar to investigate whether Zacharia misled the public about her credentials and to hold her accountable if she did.

A spokesperson for Zacharia said in a statement to The Guardian: “These are baseless allegations. This complaint is politically motivated and a clear waste of the State Bar’s time and resources. We are confident it will be dismissed with no finding of wrongdoing.”

Zacharia separately defended describing herself as a “lawyer,” arguing that there was a difference between a “lawyer” and an “attorney,” a title reserved for those licensed to practice law. In an email to The Guardian, she linked to an American Bar Association Journal article distinguishing the two terms.

Zacharia became the shortest-serving White House press secretary after replacing Karoline Leavitt in mid-October and serving five days. She delivered her first press conference on 13 October 2026.

During that first briefing, Zacharia stumbled over words and took questions only from predominantly conservative media outlets.

The central issue remains whether Zacharia misled the public about her credentials, as the complaint alleges. Her spokesperson denies wrongdoing, and The Guardian reported that it had asked the California state bar for comment.
