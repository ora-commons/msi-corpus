## No injuries reported; bridge operator promised update by evening rush hour

A cargo ship struck the Tacony-Palmyra Bridge across the Delaware River on Thursday, leaving the 3,659-foot (1,115-meter) span unable to close, according to Pennsylvania state Rep. Ed Neilson.

Neilson said the collision caused a major backup on the Philadelphia side. No injuries have been reported, he said in a news release.

The steel bridge carries vehicles along Route 73 between Palmyra and the Tacony neighborhood in northeast Philadelphia. It has three lanes of vehicular traffic, two into Philadelphia and one into New Jersey. Estimates from 2025 show the bridge can carry as many as 76,200 vehicles each day.

No one answered the phone Thursday afternoon at the Burlington County Bridge Commission, which owns, operates and maintains the bridge.

A recorded message said the bridge is closed to vehicular and pedestrian traffic until further notice, and that an update would be provided in time for evening rush hour.

Opened in 1929, the Tacony-Palmyra toll bridge was found to be in "fair" condition according to the Federal Highway Administration's 2026 National Bridge Inventory data released this fall.

Inspectors rated the deck of the Tacony-Palmyra Bridge as in good condition, the substructure as in fair condition and the superstructure — the component that absorbs the live traffic load — as fair.
