## Talarico leads Paxton 51% to 45% in new Texas Senate poll

At least half a dozen demonstrators interrupted Donald Trump's campaign rally at the Freeman Coliseum in San Antonio on Wednesday, Oct. 7, with police leading some from the arena and Trump briefly stepping away from the microphone after one protester appeared to approach the stage. One demonstrator was physically assaulted by an older Trump supporter as he tried to leave the arena.

Trump was making his third trip to Texas in less than a month to urge voters to back Paxton, whom he endorsed during the Republican primary after turning against incumbent senator John Cornyn.

"He's going back home to mom," Trump said after the man was removed. "But what he doesn't know is his mom voted for Trump."

Supporters waving cowboy hats and chanting "USA" drowned out some of the protesters, while Trump told demonstrators to "get the hell out of here." He mocked their appearance and claimed without evidence that "they're paid by enemies of our nation."

Trump also used the rally to address recent comments in which he appeared to suggest that Iran could destroy Los Angeles or San Diego, remarks that drew widespread condemnation from Democrats. "Let them hit San Diego. Let them hit Los Angeles and you want to see some bad things happen," he said. "We protect our cities, we protect our country, we're not letting that happen."

Paxton was called on stage and thanked the president. "Mr President, I want to thank you for coming for me, for coming for us, and for coming for Texas," Paxton said. "We are going to win this race."

Trump described Paxton as "perhaps the greatest attorney general in the United States of America," and said: "He was tough and he was with us all the way. He's a fantastic man."

The Republican campaign to maintain control of the Senate has become increasingly focused on Texas, where Talarico has emerged as a serious threat. A New York Times/Siena College poll released last weekend showed Talarico leading Paxton 51% to 45% among likely voters. Texas has not elected a Democrat to the Senate since 1988.

Trump won Texas by almost 14 percentage points in 2024, but his approval rating has fallen sharply. A Reuters/Ipsos poll completed on Monday put his approval rating at 32%, while registered voters preferred Democrats to Republicans 44%-37%.

Trump's relationship with Paxton has sometimes appeared uneasy. Trump called Paxton "a pain in the ass sometimes" last week, but on Wednesday he did not discuss a leaked recording in which the Senate candidate reportedly complained about the war in Iran, rising fuel prices and the way his campaign was being run.

Instead, Trump spent much of the rally attacking Talarico, repeatedly referring to him as "Tala-freako." He claimed without evidence that Talarico is vegan and recently disappeared: "He's sick because he's been forced to eat meat for political reasons and he's throwing up all over the place."

Trump insisted: "The people of Texas are not going to let weak, gutless, self-hating, transgender-promoting freak shows destroy everything that we've built."

The president's political difficulties in Texas were underlined by the absence of Republican governor Greg Abbott, a close Trump ally who is himself facing a competitive re-election race against Democratic state representative Gina Hinojosa. Abbott was instead due to attend the dedication of a hospital about 330 miles away. Democrats seized on his decision not to appear with Trump.

Republicans have now devoted more than $300 million to elections in Texas, according to the Associated Press. Trump's broader 32-day campaign blitz has taken him through several Republican-leaning states, including Oklahoma, Alabama, Ohio and Nebraska.
