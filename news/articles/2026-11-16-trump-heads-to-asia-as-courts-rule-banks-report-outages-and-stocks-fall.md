## The summit agenda covers the Iran war, denuclearization and Ukraine

President Donald Trump departed the White House on Nov. 16 for an Asia trip that includes regional meetings in the Philippines before talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin later in the week. The three leaders are due to meet in Shenzhen on Thursday for what PBS News described as their first trilateral summit.

The agenda announced by Trump included the Iran war and denuclearization. Speaking to reporters as he left the White House, Trump said he would raise Russia’s war in Ukraine and that he believed Putin was softening his stance. Trump said: “I think he's backing down a little bit. I think he could be tougher. I think he could be a lot tougher, frankly. I think he's backing down. I think he'd like to -- frankly, I think he'd like to see it finish.”

Trump also said he might meet North Korean leader Kim Jong-un while in China, though no further details were provided.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy warned that North Korea was providing Russia with ballistic missiles and called on Europe to boost support for Ukraine in response. Zelenskyy said through an interpreter: “Ukraine also needs clear parity so that we can respond to the Russian threat on equal terms.” In his evening address, Zelenskyy said Ukrainian officials also spoke again that day with U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner about prospects for ending the war. Ukraine has scaled up its aerial attacks in recent months, while Russia said that day it intercepted more than 1,300 drones, which PBS News described as Ukraine’s biggest aerial assault of the war.

The U.S. Supreme Court declined to hear a challenge to Maryland’s ban on guns in so-called sensitive places. The decision leaves in place a block on firearms in places such as health care facilities, schools and public transportation, and the justices are preparing to hear a separate case on state laws banning AR-15s and other semiautomatic firearms.

In another decision, the court rejected the Trump administration’s effort to cancel research funding at the University of California. The justices left intact a lower-court ruling that the administration violated researchers’ free speech rights by terminating grants over objections to diversity, equity and inclusion initiatives.

Lindsay Clancy will face a second trial in the killings of her three young children, with a Massachusetts judge setting a trial date for late March. Her first murder trial ended in a mistrial in September after a lone holdout prevented the jury from reaching a unanimous decision. Clancy has been charged with three counts of first-degree murder. Her lawyers have argued that she was suffering from severe postpartum psychosis when she carried out the killings in 2023 and cannot be held criminally responsible.

Customers at some of the country’s largest banks reported difficulty accessing accounts online. Users listed Bank of America, Chase, U.S. Bank and Wells Fargo among affected institutions, according to Downdetector. PBS News, citing the Associated Press, reported that a technical issue at J.P. Morgan Chase was initially to blame and then affected other banks. Chase said the problem was not the result of a cyberattack. The disruption followed a major outage at Microsoft last week that affected services including Teams and Outlook.

Robert Pattinson was named as the next 007 in a social media post, ending years of speculation about who would succeed Daniel Craig. The 40-year-old British actor becomes the seventh actor to play Bond. Pattinson first rose to fame in the “Twilight” films, and his previous roles include “Batman,” “Harry Potter” and “Dune.” Filming on the next James Bond movie is expected to start next year.

British singer-songwriter Raye led the field of 2027 Grammy nominations with 11, including for record, song and album of the year. The total ties the record for female artists set by Beyonce, who has 100 career Grammy nominations. Classically trained Spanish singer Rosalia earned nine nominations, while Miranda Lambert, Olivia Rodrigo, Phoebe Bridgers and newcomer Ella Langley each received seven. The 2027 Grammys take place in February.

Stocks fell as the week began, with Nvidia leading another selloff in technology shares. The Dow Jones industrial average dropped more than 700 points, the Nasdaq fell close to 2.5 percent and the S&P 500 declined for a third straight session. FRED’s raw daily readings for Nov. 16, 2026, listed the [Dow Jones Industrial Average](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DJIA) at 51,608.17, the [NASDAQ Composite Index](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NASDAQCOM) at 25,136.27 and the [S&P 500 Index](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/SP500) at 7,561.67.

Freeman Johnson, the oldest known survivor of the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor, died at 106. Johnson was 21 and serving aboard the USS St. Louis when Japanese forces attacked on Dec. 7, 1941. In an Associated Press interview earlier this year, Johnson recalled being below deck and unaware as the attack unfolded. He was among roughly 87,000 American military personnel stationed on Oahu that day; fewer than a dozen known survivors remain.
