## Miami-Dade crews called the response a “mass casualty incident” after 15 hospitalizations

Local media reported that 18 people fell ill with possible food poisoning after attending Leon Medical Center's annual Thanksgiving lunch at the medical center's headquarters in Doral, Florida, on Wednesday. Miami-Dade fire rescue crews who received reports of the possible food poisoning upgraded the call to a “mass casualty incident” after 15 of those who fell ill required hospitalization, the Miami Herald reported.

The center's account of what happened and the question of what caused the illnesses are not yet settled. The Guardian reported that no patients at the center were affected. Leon Medical Center said it continues to work with the caterers to determine, if possible, the cause while providing support to staff and their families. The Guardian reported that investigations into the exact cause and nature of the food poisoning were continuing.

The Thanksgiving lunch is an annual event at Leon Medical Center, and this year's was held on Wednesday at the medical center's headquarters in Doral. The Guardian described the resulting illnesses as severe enough that local emergency officials said it was a “mass casualty” event, with numerous stricken diners sent to hospital.

Leon Medical Center said in a statement to NBC Miami that “Some of the staff began to experience gastrointestinal symptoms shortly after the luncheon and required treatment.” The statement did not specify how many of the affected were staff.

Miami-Dade fire rescue crews received reports of the possible food poisoning and upgraded the call, the Miami Herald reported. Fifteen of the 18 people who fell ill required hospitalization, according to the same report.

The center's statement to NBC Miami described a continuing inquiry into the meal itself. “We continue to work with the caterers to determine, if possible, the cause and continue to provide support to our staff and their families.” Leon Medical Center said.

The Guardian reported that no patients at the center were affected. The Guardian's account did not identify a specific food or dish as the source of the illnesses. The center's statement described the symptoms as affecting staff and pointed to the luncheon rather than to a named item.

Investigations are continuing as to the exact cause and nature of the food poisoning, The Guardian reported.

The Guardian's account draws on reporting from the Miami Herald and a statement Leon Medical Center provided to NBC Miami. The count of those who fell ill and the mass-casualty upgrade were attributed to local media, while the center's description of symptoms and of its support for staff came from its own statement.
