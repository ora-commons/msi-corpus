## Judge has temporarily blocked construction as veterans and Kennedys sue

President Donald Trump marked Veterans Day by suggesting that the towering 250-foot arch he wants to build in his own honor near Arlington National Cemetery and the Lincoln Memorial would also be dedicated to the U.S. military and those who served in its ranks.

Trump made the comments Wednesday during an annual ceremony at Arlington Cemetery. They came despite three veterans having sued to stop the project, which a federal court has since temporarily blocked, and a divide among military organizations: some groups have cheered the arch, but many others have opposed it or joined efforts to stop construction. A retired general has called Trump's proposed arch near Arlington a "sacrilege."

"And soon we're going to dedicate a magnificent triumphal arc to our veterans," Trump said. "And that's in honor of our great veterans and our great militaries." He added: "They've been waiting for it for 200 years."

Trump has described the arch for months as a 250-foot (76-meter) structure set for construction near Arlington National Cemetery and the Lincoln Memorial, inspired by the Arc de Triomphe in Paris and possibly to be called the "Arc de Trump." He has also insisted that Washington has wanted a similar structure for centuries — even though that is not true, according to AP's reporting. At Wednesday's ceremony, he suggested it was veterans who had longed for an arch in the capital.

The arch received approval from oversight boards whose members were chosen by Trump. The three veterans are among several entities suing to block its construction, and nine family members of the Kennedy political dynasty are also seeking to stop the project, arguing the arch would cast a shadow over their relatives' gravesites at Arlington National Cemetery.

Last week, U.S. District Judge Tanya Chutkan issued a temporary restraining order prohibiting work on the arch for 14 days.

With Democrats soon taking control of Congress after wins across the country in last week's midterm elections, Trump's renovation projects are set to encounter far more scrutiny and roadblocks, according to AP's reporting — from his effort to put his own name on the Kennedy Center to remaking a public golf course, building a massive White House ballroom and building the arch.

Despite the midterm defeats by his party and the potential legal and legislative hurdles, the White House has continued to promote the arch project. That includes a website unveiled last week proclaiming that the structure will be known as the United States Military Triumphal Arch and is "long overdue, and has been sought for many years, especially by the Military."

The website prominently displays a picture of and quote from Trump. It features the word "Triumph" in gold cursive script recalling the typeface used at the president's Mar-a-Lago estate in Florida, along with similar flourishes that have been affixed to the White House as part of his overhaul of the Oval Office and West Wing.

The plans call for the arch's columns to store munitions, with areas on the pedestal and observation deck set aside for a sniper's nest. The base would be designed to launch drones, and the site says the overall monument "will be Highly Secretive but, at the same time, a Top Level and Lethal structure, the likes of which has not been seen in this Country before."

The website does not say how much the project will cost or how it will be paid for, although some estimates have reached as high as $100 million.
