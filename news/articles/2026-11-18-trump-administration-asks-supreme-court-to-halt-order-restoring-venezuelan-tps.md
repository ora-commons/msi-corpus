## About 600,000 Venezuelans have received Temporary Protected Status

The Trump administration asked the Supreme Court on Wednesday to halt an order that would restore deportation protections for hundreds of thousands of Venezuelan migrants. Federal attorneys said that if the justices do not intervene again before Friday, the administration could be required to release about 1,800 Venezuelans from detention. The emergency appeal followed a California-based judge's finding that ending the migrants' temporary protected status was unlawful.

The case has moved through a ruling from a California-based judge and a temporary appeals-court pause. The judge found that ending Venezuelans' temporary protected status was unlawful, and the appeals court subsequently paused the decision that kept the program in place.

The appeals-court pause is set to expire Friday. The emergency appeal asks the Supreme Court to halt the order before that deadline, preventing the judge's decision from taking effect while the administration continues its challenge.

The Supreme Court had allowed the administration to strip protections from Venezuelans when the case was at an earlier stage last year. Federal attorneys wrote that if the justices do not intervene again before Friday, the administration could be required to release about 1,800 Venezuelans from detention.

The Supreme Court also allowed the Trump administration to end temporary legal protections for people from Haiti and Syria in June.

President Donald Trump has moved to withdraw protections that have allowed immigrants from multiple countries to remain in the United States and work legally, some for decades. The Venezuelan case is part of that broader effort.

Temporary Protected Status, known as TPS, was created by Congress in 1990 to prevent deportations to countries suffering from natural disasters or civil strife. The status is granted in increments of up to 18 months.

About 600,000 Venezuelans have received TPS protections. The Supreme Court request concerns whether the order restoring those protections takes effect before the appeals-court pause expires Friday.
