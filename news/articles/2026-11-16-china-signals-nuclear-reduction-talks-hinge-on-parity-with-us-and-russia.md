## Ukraine fears Putin may use the trilateral summit to press for Moscow’s terms

The Guardian reported that China has signalled it will not enter nuclear-arms reduction agreements until it is closer to parity with the US and Russia. Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin are due to hold their first trilateral summit on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum in Shenzhen. Chinese foreign ministry spokesperson Mao Ning told reporters on Monday that the leaders “will exchange views in depth on safeguarding world peace and promoting strategic stability in major-power relations, which is of groundbreaking significance”.

Some analysts characterised the planned meeting as a “big three” gathering echoing the 1945 Yalta meeting of Winston Churchill, Franklin D Roosevelt and Joseph Stalin. The Guardian described Yalta as having in effect carved up postwar Europe, between a communist-ruled east and a democratic, capitalist west. Ukraine has been alarmed by the prospect and fears that Putin, who initially suggested the trilateral meeting, will use it to persuade Trump and Xi to agree to a settlement of the war with Russia on Moscow’s terms.

Trump has said Ukraine will not be up for debate at the meeting and that its main purpose will be to discuss ending the US’s war with Iran and “denuclearisation.” The Guardian described “denuclearisation” as shorthand for a deal among the three powers to limit nuclear arms. The proposal was believed to be unpalatable to China, which has been increasing its nuclear arsenal and signalled an unwillingness to enter reduction agreements until it is closer to parity with the US and Russia.

Expectations for a substantial agreement are low. Lyle Goldstein, an international affairs fellow and China specialist at Brown University in the US, said the Trump-Xi-Putin meeting would not have a substantive result. “There will be no ‘Yalta’, as many have feared,” he said.

Jonathan Czin, the Michael Armacost chair in foreign policy studies at the Brookings Institution, said Trump’s “lame duck status” would deter Xi and Putin from trying to reach long-term arrangements with the US. “Both men know Trump’s time is expiring even more quickly than his leverage – and that he will be irrelevant in a mere 26 months [when his term expires],” Czin told an online Brookings forum. “Why would either man make a deal with a leader whose time and leverage is waning? They will only do it if they think they can pilfer more from Trump diplomatically than they will be able to gain from the next American president.”

Analysts said the meeting’s main beneficiary was likely to be Putin, whose standing would be boosted by being treated on an equal footing with two other leaders whose countries were, in reality, much more powerful.

Goldstein, who took soundings on Chinese expectations for the summit during a visit to Shenzhen last week, predicted Trump would follow the pattern of previous presidents by turning increasingly to global affairs as he faces the prospect that his domestic influence will be curtailed after Democrats won the House of Representatives and the Senate. “The president has been badly damaged by the election and will have limited prospects for the next two years,” he said. “That may cause him to be more active in foreign policy since he can act in that domain with fewer constraints. It would not surprise me if Trump is ready to go all-out to make some further splash in foreign policy.”

With the risks of reigniting the war with Iran high and the Russia-Ukraine war still resistant to diplomatic resolution despite White House efforts, Goldstein suggested Trump could attempt “a dramatic foreign policy flourish in Asia,” such as an agreement with Kim Jong-un, the North Korean leader with whom Trump has frequently boasted of having a close relationship. Kim was not expected at the APEC gathering, although The Guardian reported rumours that he might appear.

Trump’s itinerary begins in Manila in the Philippines on Tuesday for the ASEAN summit before he travels to Shenzhen for APEC. It is his first overseas trip since his Republican Party’s defeat in the midterms, and The Guardian said the journey may signal a renewed focus on foreign policy for the remainder of his presidency. At ASEAN, Trump is expected to sign a trade agreement with Thailand, push deals with other countries and lobby fellow leaders to favour the US over China on artificial intelligence.

He is also due to take part in the first leaders’ meeting of the Quad partnership — the US, Japan, India and Australia — since he returned to the White House in January 2025. White House officials said Elon Musk, the Tesla and SpaceX entrepreneur, would accompany Trump to APEC. Musk spent almost $500m (£380m) unsuccessfully supporting Republican candidates in that month’s midterms. The reason for Musk’s presence was not explained.

The China leg comes amid continuing fallout from the Nvidia spying scandal, which prompted company chief executive Jensen Huang to resign amid revelations that he had an affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy. Trump is under pressure to raise the matter with Xi during their bilateral meeting on the sidelines of APEC. Trump has said he does not plan to bring up the subject; administration officials insist he will not do so and say the meeting will be dedicated to implementing a trade agreement between the two superpowers. Huang had persuaded the Trump administration to lift restrictions on sales of Nvidia’s AI chips to China that had been imposed during Joe Biden’s presidency.
