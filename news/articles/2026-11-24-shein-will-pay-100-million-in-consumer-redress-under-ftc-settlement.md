## The order covers shipping claims, reviews, customer service and traceability

The Federal Trade Commission announced Tuesday that fast-fashion retailer Shein will pay $100 million to settle allegations that the company misled consumers about shipping times, product reviews and the origin of cotton used in some of its goods. The Singapore-based company, founded in China in 2012, will make the payment under an order that also sets new restrictions on Shein's shipping claims, customer reviews, customer service and product traceability.

The agency's allegations reached beyond delivery promises. The FTC alleged that cotton the company represented as U.S.-grown was found to originate from China, and that cotton used in at least some of Shein's products could not be traced to any country of origin at all.

In a release Tuesday, Christopher Mufarrige, director of the FTC's Bureau of Consumer Protection, said: “Shein attracted consumers with promises of affordable clothing, fast delivery and use of suppliers that earned above-average ratings on standards related to child and forced labor, among other representations.” Mufarrige said: “In reality, the company misled millions of consumers about its products, its suppliers and the authenticity of its customer reviews.”

Mufarrige said the company further complicated its problems by making it difficult for shoppers to seek customer service.

Under the order, Shein's $100 million payment will be used to provide redress to consumers harmed by the company's actions, according to the FTC. The restrictions cover shipping and delivery times, the suppression of negative product reviews, the adequacy of customer service, and claims about supplier and manufacturer audits and product traceability.

In an email to The Associated Press, a Shein spokesperson said the agreement “does not constitute an admission of liability, and we continue to disagree with aspects of the FTC's characterization of our business and compliance practices.” Shein said it agreed to add additional measures to strengthen its consumer practices and compliance efforts as part of the settlement.

The settlement follows Shein's move to go public in September on the Hong Kong exchange. The company's appeal to customers has been built on ultra-fast, affordable fashion, delivered from China to the West in just days.

That model now operates in a changed trade environment. The end of “de minimis” tariff exemptions in the United States and the European Union has raised duties for low-value parcels from China, including Shein's products.

Higher logistics costs, fueled in part by the war in Iran, have also squeezed the company's low-price business model and profitability.
