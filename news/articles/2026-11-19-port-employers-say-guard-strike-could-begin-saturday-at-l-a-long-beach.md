## Employer and union negotiators return to the bargaining table Friday

West Coast port employers said a strike by 250 security guards at the ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach could begin as early as Saturday. The Pacific Maritime Association said Wednesday it was struggling to reach a labor agreement with the union local that represents the guards.

The guards voted last month to authorize a strike. A spokesman for the International Longshore and Warehouse Union, which represents the guards, declined to comment.

The unarmed guards work at six container-handling terminals at the two ports. Those terminals are responsible for moving more than half of the containers through the Los Angeles-Long Beach port complex, the busiest U.S. container-port complex.

Although the guards make up 2% of the ILWU workforce at the ports, West Coast port employers said other unionized dockworkers would refuse to cross picket lines, extending the potential effect of a strike beyond the security guards themselves.

The Pacific Maritime Association said the union's demands “have not materially changed” since bargaining began more than a year ago.

The employer group also said the union, which had already extended the contract twice, was unwilling to extend it further.

The two sides will meet again at the bargaining table Friday, the Pacific Maritime Association said, with the current contract expiring at 12:01 a.m. Saturday. The strike threat remained unresolved.
