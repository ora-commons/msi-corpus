## Ferrell-Zabala: gun-rights and gun-industry groups spent just under $3 million in 2026

More than 200 candidates endorsed by Everytown for Gun Safety, the umbrella organization for a network of violence prevention groups, won state and federal races in the 2026 U.S. midterms, according to data the nonprofit shared with The Guardian.

Nearly 550 former volunteers for Moms Demand Action, a group that is part of the Everytown network, ran for office as incumbents and first-time candidates. South Carolina senator-elect Annie Andrews, Arizona representative-elect JoAnna Mendoza and Pennsylvania representative-elect Paige Cognetti were among them. Sandra Jauregui was among the Everytown-endorsed winners and was elected Nevada’s lieutenant governor.

The Guardian characterized the midterms as both a major victory for Democrats and a victory for the gun violence prevention movement, and as a sign of the declining power of the once-feared gun lobby.

The results formed part of a nearly decade-long shift in which gun violence prevention and gun control moved from third-rail issues seldom mentioned on campaign trails to subjects that candidates, most of them Democrats, are centering in their campaigns. The Guardian said the change tracks with public opinion: more than half of adults in the United States now favor stricter gun laws.

“People are lining up with us because they want this problem to be fixed,” said Angela Ferrell-Zabala, the executive director of Moms Demand Action. “They’ve seen us do our work and now it’s expanding and people are getting into office.”

The new winners follow earlier officeholders who entered politics through gun-safety organizing. Maxwell Frost, the representative from Florida, started his career as organizing director for March for Our Lives, the gun-safety group founded by survivors of the 2018 mass shooting at Marjory Stoneman Douglas high school in Parkland, Florida. Lucy McBath, a Georgia representative, volunteered with Moms Demand Action after her son, Jordan Davis, was shot and killed in 2012. Abigail Spanberger, the Virginia governor, was also a former Moms Demand Action volunteer.

According to analysis by Trace, the spending power of gun-control groups has eclipsed that of gun-rights groups. The shift reverses the pattern of previous decades, when the National Rifle Association spent more money in races, mostly to support Republicans. According to Ferrell-Zabala, Everytown spent $53 million on state and federal races in 2026, while pro-Second Amendment and gun-industry organizations spent just under $3 million.

For Jauregui, gun violence had been important for nearly a decade. In October 2017, she survived the nation’s deadliest mass shooting at the Route 91 music festival in Las Vegas, where 58 people were killed and nearly 870 were injured. A Nevada state assembly member at the time, she did not publicly discuss the experience until nearly two years later.

“I didn’t want to have to share my story. And then Parkland happened,” she said, referring to the shooting at Marjory Stoneman Douglas high school where 17 students and school administrators were fatally shot. “And these high school students were resilient and strong. They had to deal with the same post-traumatic stress I did, but they weren’t staying silent. So I drew strength from these students.”

Once she shared her story in 2019, Jauregui worked with groups such as Everytown to pass new laws, including a ban on bumpstocks, the gun accessory used in the Route 91 shooting, and a red flag law. “We can’t fight gun violence if we don’t get elected,” Jauregui added. “And I did that by having tough conversations with people who don’t agree with me.”

Ferrell-Zabala said the presence of officials such as Jauregui was especially important. Although homicides have been declining in recent years, guns remain a deeply polarizing topic.

Since Donald Trump took office again in 2024, the Bureau of Alcohol, Firearms and Tobacco has begun rolling back restrictions on transporting guns and accessories. The agency also lost 2.5% in funding. The administration eliminated funding for community violence prevention and cut off grants for research.

The Guardian wrote that making up for lost ground will require newly elected officials to tailor their work to the specific needs of their constituents, especially gun owners. “Every community needs something different,” Ferrell-Zabala said. “Organizers and people advocating for gun safety already understand how to work across different experiences. They’ll know how to build those relationships. This isn’t a zero-sum game.”
