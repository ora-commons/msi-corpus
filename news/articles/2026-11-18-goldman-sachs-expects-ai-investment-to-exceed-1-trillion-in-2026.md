## Coatsworth sees spending risk while Sobhani expects limited near-term impact

Goldman Sachs expects AI investment to exceed $1 trillion in 2026 and said AI will remain the “dominant driver of business investment growth” next year, according to BBC News. The report presented the forecast alongside a separate uncertainty: whether Nvidia could change its strategy and financing in ways that slow a wider AI investment cycle.

BBC News noted that Nvidia was not as much of a household name as Apple, Amazon and Microsoft, but said the company “has been just as integral to the booming US stock market of recent years.” Originally focused on chips that process graphics for computer games, Nvidia pivoted to AI chips and now dominates that market. The report valued the company at $3.8 trillion (£2.9 trillion), making it one of the most valuable firms. On Tuesday, Nvidia said its rapid growth was poised to continue, and its quarterly sales had doubled from a year earlier.

The report said co-founder Jensen Huang led Nvidia’s shift toward AI and maintained a firm grip on the company, with up to 200 people reporting directly to him. Huang resigned after revelations of extramarital affairs, one of which was alleged to have been with a Chinese spy. BBC News said hundreds of billions of dollars were wiped from Nvidia’s stock-market value following the revelations.

Dan Coatsworth, head of markets at investment firm AJ Bell, said Huang was also a key figure in “flying the flag” to drum up enthusiasm and investment for the wider AI industry. Coatsworth said Huang’s departure posed a “real risk” that Nvidia could now decide to change its current strategy and rein in spending. Such a move could spell a wider slowdown in AI investment, and Coatsworth said the size of any economic impact was “unknown.”

“If they decide to go with the much more cautious route... there could be a knock-on effect in that you have got various companies in the AI infrastructure space suddenly finding they don’t have a key source of finance,” Coatsworth said.

Kunjan Sobhani, senior technology industry analyst at Bloomberg Intelligence, expects the impact of Huang’s departure to be “limited” for now because Nvidia has strong commitments and a deep order book. He said that assessment could change over the long run and linked that possibility to Nvidia’s extensive network of financing deals with AI businesses.

BBC News described those arrangements as “circular financing.” Nvidia sells microchips to customers including Meta, SpaceX and OpenAI, and has provided funds to those companies and other firms in the supply chain through loans or direct investments. Some investors worry what might happen if Nvidia turns off the spending taps. They have asked whether other supply-chain firms would cut spending or jobs if investment dried up and whether those effects would spread to the wider US economy.

Coatsworth said he had never seen circular financing used at this scale before, but he noted that competitive rivals could insulate the wider AI industry from a potential slowdown at Nvidia. “I don’t think all AI activity grinds to a halt if Nvidia slows down what it’s doing and if it pulls some financing, but it does change how people would look at the whole situation,” Coatsworth said.

BBC News said the risk of a slowdown was perhaps why technology executives including Elon Musk and Tim Sweeney, the CEO of Fortnite maker Epic Games, called for Huang’s reinstatement. Musk claimed that “critical deals” involving Nvidia’s technology would be lost following the resignation. The resignation also prompted a “Bring Jensen Back” campaign by shareholders and supporters. Huang, speaking just after a week had passed since resigning, said he was open to returning.

Nvidia’s interim boss Colette Kress said it was “business as usual” without Huang at the helm as the company’s rapid growth remained on track. Huang said he would be “honoured” to return, but Nvidia’s board said it was not considering bringing him back. Coatsworth, who thinks “no CEO is irreplaceable,” said the pressure was on whoever succeeds Huang.

When Nvidia’s share price plunged after the affair revelations, shares of rival semiconductor makers including AMD, Broadcom and Marvell Technology jumped.

BBC News said the risk of a slowdown had already rattled stock markets. Investors took fright at Democratic gains in the midterm elections, with many having campaigned for tougher regulation of AI firms. Questions also remained about whether the huge sums being invested in AI would actually pay off.

BBC News said the value of Big Tech shares had cushioned the US economy from global economic shocks, including tariffs, while buoying investments and pensions for people worldwide. It also said billions flowing into AI and data-centre construction had bolstered the American economy, with Nvidia among the biggest spenders.

Goldman Sachs’ investment forecast did not resolve the questions raised by Nvidia’s financing role. Coatsworth’s downside risk was conditional on Nvidia changing strategy and reining in spending, Sobhani expected a limited near-term impact but said the long-run picture could change, and investors’ question about the payoff from very large AI investments remained open.
