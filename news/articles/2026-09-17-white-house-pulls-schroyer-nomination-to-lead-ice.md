## White House pulls Schroyer nomination to lead ICE

**Subtype:** fact

- The White House on Thursday withdrew its nomination of Lance Schroyer, a former Oklahoma state trooper, to lead Immigration and Customs Enforcement.
- The withdrawal came less than three months after the administration announced Schroyer's selection for the role.
- Schroyer has no formal experience working in ICE and previously served on the security detail of Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin.
- The nomination stalled in the Senate because Rand Paul, the Republican chair of the homeland security and governmental affairs committee, had not scheduled a confirmation hearing.
- ICE has not had an official director since 2017 and has been led by acting directors, most recently David Venturella.

## ICE has operated without an official director since 2017

The White House announcement on Thursday offered no reason for pulling Schroyer's nomination. The Guardian has asked the White House for more information.

Schroyer previously worked as a member of Mullin's security detail when Mullin was in the Senate, and he has been serving as an adviser to Mullin since. Critics had objected from the start that Schroyer had no formal experience working inside the agency he was selected to lead.

The nomination's progress through the Senate was complicated by Paul, the Republican who chairs the homeland security and governmental affairs committee. Paul had not scheduled a hearing, leaving Schroyer's confirmation in limbo.

ICE has not had an official director since 2017. The agency has been overseen by several acting directors during that period, most recently Venturella.
