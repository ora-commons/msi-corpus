## July forced-labor levies were the first post-ruling step

The Trump administration's planned subsidy tariffs would mark the second step in an effort to replace the broader duties it set last year. Those duties were announced in April 2025, and the Supreme Court ruled them unconstitutional in February.

The first step came in July, when the White House imposed levies of 10% to 12.5% on 60 trading partners. The administration charged that those partners had inadequately enforced bans on goods produced by forced labor. Those duties are separate from the subsidy investigation now underway.

Bloomberg and The Wall Street Journal reported the plans earlier Tuesday, according to the Associated Press.

Two people familiar with the matter, who were not authorized to comment publicly and spoke on condition of anonymity, said the timing for the tariff rollout could still change. The White House and USTR did not respond to requests for comment.

According to the two people, the administration is expected soon to announce plans to raise tariffs on China, the European Union and 14 other economies. The two people also said the U.S. Trade Representative's Office was expected to release the results of a monthslong tariff investigation into industrial subsidies by the 16 economies in the coming days.

The White House has said the affected economies' excessive manufacturing output, boosted by subsidies, led to a flood of goods at lower prices that undercut American companies. The planned tariffs are intended to respond to that assertion.

The Associated Press characterized the anticipated trade action as demonstrating that the Trump administration remained willing to impose import taxes despite a sweeping Republican Party loss in this month's midterm elections. The AP attributed the electoral result largely to affordability concerns as inflation remained elevated.

The new duties could also test how Democrats respond to tariffs imposed unilaterally by Trump. Even with majorities in both the House and Senate, Trump could veto legislation that overturns or waters down the tariffs.
