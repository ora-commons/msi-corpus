## Land & Buildings plans shareholder letter urging Invitation Homes to sell houses one at a time

**Subtype:** fact

- Jonathan Litt's Land & Buildings Investment Management plans to send Invitation Homes shareholders a letter saying private talks with the company's management and board have gone nowhere.
- Land & Buildings owned less than 1% of Invitation Homes as of June 30 and estimates the company's net assets are worth around $45 a share.
- Invitation Homes shares closed Friday at $26.85, and the company's stock is down more than 30% over the past five years.
- Land & Buildings is asking Invitation Homes to return a minimum of $2 billion to shareholders annually, either through share repurchases or dividends.

## Land & Buildings estimates Invitation's net assets around $45 a share; stock closed Friday at $26.85

Invitation Homes is one of the largest U.S. owners of single-family rental homes. The Dallas-based company held nearly 86,000 houses as of September and carries a market value of around $15.5 billion.

Land & Buildings wrote that Invitation Homes should keep selling homes "one at a time" for as long as its shares trade below the underlying value of its net assets, a level the firm estimates at around $45 a share. Invitation Homes shares closed Friday at $26.85 each.

"The Board should say plainly that it will keep selling homes and returning capital for as long as the discount persists, even if that ultimately means liquidating the Company," Litt writes in the letter.

Land & Buildings, an activist hedge fund, wants Invitation Homes to sell more of the houses it owns and take other actions to increase its share price. The firm, which owned less than 1% of Invitation Homes as of June 30, is also calling on Invitation Homes to return a minimum of $2 billion to shareholders each year, either through share repurchases or dividends. The firm's other demands include a 6% down-payment rebate for tenants who buy the homes they rent and a pullback in acquisitions and development.

Invitation Homes has already been selling houses and repurchasing stock. In the third quarter, the company sold more than 600 homes, put $245 million toward buybacks and bought more than 200 homes. Invitation Homes Chief Executive Dallas Tanner has said the company can largely operate within the proposed rules for implementing a new law that restricts large single-family buyers.

Invitation Homes stock has lost more than 30% of its value over the past five years. The Wall Street Journal attributed part of the decline to rising interest rates and to a law backed by President Trump that is set to take effect soon. Under that law, institutional investors that control at least 350 single-family homes will be barred from buying more, with exceptions such as certain homes built for rent. Land & Buildings writes in the letter that the new law limits Invitation's growth potential and that the best use of the company's cash is to buy back its own shares.

Land & Buildings, based in Stamford, Conn., focuses on publicly traded real-estate companies and has agitated at companies including Equity Commonwealth and First Industrial Realty Trust.
