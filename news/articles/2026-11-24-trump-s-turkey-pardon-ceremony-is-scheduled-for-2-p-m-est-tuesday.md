## Freedom and Feathers will live out their days at Texas A&M University

President Donald Trump is expected to pardon Freedom and Feathers at 2 p.m. EST on Tuesday, according to Associated Press reporting. The two West Virginia-native birds will both receive a reprieve from ending up on a Thanksgiving dinner table, and each will live out its days at Texas A&M University.

The online public vote selects which of Freedom and Feathers will receive the ceremonial pardon from Trump at the White House. The other bird will not receive the in-person honor, though both turkeys will be spared from Thanksgiving dinner.

The White House calls Tuesday's event the 79th turkey pardoning.

The tradition did not become annual until George H.W. Bush's administration in 1989. Associated Press reporting described the ritual this way: "Typically a light-hearted moment before the quintessential American holiday, the turkey pardoning can offer a brief departure from politics and policy."

An early story of presidential turkey pardoning dates to Abraham Lincoln's administration, according to the White House Historical Association. Lincoln granted a reprieve to a bird that was to be served for Christmas dinner after his son Tad intervened on the gobbler's behalf.

In 1954, a photographer captured the moment a smiling Dwight Eisenhower fed a cranberry to a speckled turkey. In 1963, John F. Kennedy, just days before he was fatally shot in Texas, pardoned a turkey wearing around its neck a sign that said, "Good Eating, Mr. President."

In 2024, Joe Biden relished wordplay while pardoning Peach and Blossom, who came from a farm in Minnesota. Peach, Biden said, "lives by the motto, 'Keep calm and gobble on.'" Blossom has a different motto: "No fowl play. Just Minnesota nice."

The ceremony has not always remained outside politics. In 2025, the first year of Trump's second term, he spared the turkeys but spent part of the ceremony attacking his political opponents. Trump said the birds should be named Chuck and Nancy, after Democratic leaders Chuck Schumer and Nancy Pelosi. "I would never pardon those people," Trump said at the time.
