## Schumer accuses Huang of shipping advanced chips to China and violating export laws

President Donald Trump said a pardon for Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang would remain available if needed, but said he did not think it would be necessary. The remarks came as Huang is scheduled to testify before a US Senate banking committee hearing on export controls, and as Trump continued to publicize his role in convincing Nvidia's board to reinstate Huang.

Trump made the comment in an interview with the New York Post. “It’s always an option, I guess, but I don’t think it’ll be necessary,” he said. Trump also said he could intervene again to help Huang if needed, extending the assistance he said he provided when the board reinstated the CEO.

Asked about Huang's upcoming testimony before a US Senate banking committee hearing on export controls, Trump said he saw no reason for the chief executive to invoke his Fifth Amendment right to avoid answering questions that might incriminate him. “I don’t think he needs to take the Fifth. I think it’s an interesting story for people,” Trump said. “I think I want him to just tell the truth, and I mean, his lawyers are going to have to advise him,” he added. Trump also said he had always found Huang to be 100% aboveboard and very smart.

Nvidia's board reinstated Huang on Monday. Trump said he had intervened on the executive's behalf and had assured company leadership that Huang did not pose a national security risk. Huang had admitted an affair with a Chinese woman suspected of links to Chinese intelligence.

The board's action came despite questions surrounding Huang's relationship with Alice Huang, a tech executive of no relation to him. Alice Huang has been accused of acting as a Chinese asset and routing advanced chips to Beijing via her datacenter company, Megaspeed.

Schumer made the accusation yesterday. The current Senate minority leader, who is vying to lead the Democratic majority in the next Congress, accused Huang of shipping “extremely advanced chips to China” and violating US export control laws. Schumer said the move “amounts to the equivalent of selling rocket engines to the Soviets during the space race.”

US Attorney General Todd Blanche did not answer questions about how deeply the Justice Department had “looked into” Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, amid concerns related to Huang's alleged affair with a suspected Chinese spy.
