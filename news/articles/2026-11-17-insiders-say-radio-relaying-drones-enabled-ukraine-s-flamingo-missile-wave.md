## Armed forces source links bridge strike to Putin’s planned summit bluff

The Guardian described Ukraine’s operation as a surprise, multi-pronged attack and characterized the SpaceX satellite system as faltering. Hundreds of attack drones followed the same linear path for four hours, first over land and then over the Black Sea, before reaching the Kerch Bridge in the early hours. Leon, a participant identified by a pseudonym, said the goal was to overwhelm Russia’s organized air defenses rather than the bridge itself.

At about 10 p.m. on Monday, a Ukrainian soldier in a muddy field in central Ukraine scrawled a message on the wings of a kamikaze drone before a mission. The message read: “The bridge is fucked.” The soldier added: “We will stand and Moscow will fall.” It was a freezing night with a bright, starry sky and no clouds. Lines of ice had formed on the drone’s bulky wings as it headed toward a target about 500 km to the south. The soldiers got ready to launch, then watched the biplane-like drone, laden with explosives, disappear across the southern Ukrainian steppe.

Typically, the soldiers who gathered in the muddy field in central Ukraine were attacking oil refineries deep inside Russia. In recent months, the work had become almost mundane: they had hit so many refineries. Leon’s 7th Deep Strike Centre is part of Magyar’s Birds, a brigade named after the call sign of founder Robert Brovdi, who heads Ukraine’s unmanned systems forces. Leon said: “We knew what the target was. Everyone was enthusiastic and excited.” Leon said: “Nobody gets excited any more about oil refineries. This was different.” The Guardian said the target was unique because it was a structure personally associated with Vladimir Putin and his grandiose imperial ambitions.

Vladimir Putin opened the monumental road-and-rail crossing in 2018, driving across it in a Kamaz truck, four years after his undercover troops, known as “little green men,” seized the peninsula. The Guardian reported that Russian propagandists described the bridge as “impregnable” and said it was as strong and unyielding as their country’s “iron leader.” The Guardian reported that, for Ukraine, the bridge was a hated symbol of Russian arrogance and colonial thinking. Ukrainian officials called the structure illegal. Kyiv had tried to destroy it twice, with a truck bomb in 2022 and a sea drone the following year.

The Guardian reported that Ukraine’s military’s careful, multi-stage plan worked perfectly. Leon said: “It was a well-coordinated operation. Waves of drones started from different locations. The goal was to overwhelm Russia’s well-organised air defence system, rather than the bridge itself.” The initial salvo tied up Russia’s local defences. According to insiders, Ukraine then launched a second drone wave whose function was to act as a relay system for radio signals.

Ukrainian units had previously used Starshield, the military equivalent of Starlink, for mid-range strikes on occupied territory. The Guardian reported that Elon Musk’s SpaceX owns the satellite internet network, which is overseen by the Pentagon and the Trump administration. Ten days before the attack, limits were reportedly imposed on Ukrainian drones, keeping them from flying above 161 km/h (100 mph). SpaceX insisted there had been no slowdown, a statement Volodymyr Zelenskyy said was untrue. On Monday night, it appeared Kyiv had bypassed the US restrictions by setting up its own real-time video and radio connection.

Once the relay was in place, a third wave of attacks began with Flamingos, a potent Ukrainian-made cruise missile capable of carrying 1,000 kg of explosives, enough to seriously damage the bridge. The number of missiles was unknown, although some reports said at least four had been launched from southern Ukraine and sent toward Crimea.

One Flamingo’s onboard camera recorded the crossing looming through a ghostly night scene against a grisaille background before a gigantic steel arch filled the frame and the feed cut out. Other footage showed an orange fireball at impact. A drone view released by the Security Service of Ukraine on 17 November appeared to show smoke rising and explosions at the Kerch Bridge. Photographs taken Tuesday morning showed the road arch lying in the water after tumbling there overnight, with a large concrete chunk of the road crossing nearby and damage to the adjacent railway arch span. The Guardian wrote: “Nobody will be driving across the bridge anytime soon.” Leon said: “We were very happy, swapping messages all night.”

One source in Ukraine’s armed forces said the timing of the operation was deliberate. Volodymyr Zelenskyy called it “unprecedented.” The operation preceded a Thursday meeting in the Chinese city of Shenzhen between Xi Jinping, Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin on the sidelines of an Apec summit. The source said: “The bridge is a symbol of Putin’s rule. Destroying it sends a message to the other two great leaders that Putin is not as strong as he pretends.”

The source added: “We understand that what we did shows the world that the king is nude. Putin is weak and vulnerable and we just proved it. The operation was purposefully designed for this specific historic moment. Putin’s plan was to bluff at the summit about how powerful he is. In reality, he can’t even protect the most important and symbolic pillars of his regime.”

The Guardian said disabling the bridge had practical consequences beyond Putin’s political embarrassment before his peers. Since annexing Crimea, Russia had militarized the peninsula, effectively turning it into a giant and fortified army base. Since 2022, the peninsula had been used as a launch platform for drone and missile attacks on the port of Odesa and other Ukrainian cities in the region.

Russia’s occupying southern army had relied on the bridge for regular deliveries of ammunition, equipment and fuel. With the crossing partially closed and rail travel reportedly reopened Tuesday, those forces would have to use an under-fire land corridor connecting Rostov oblast with the Moscow-controlled parts of Ukraine’s Zaporizhzhia and Kherson provinces. Leon predicted: “We have disrupted their logistics to Crimea. There will be a lot of problems for Russia’s army in the south.”

Debris from the bridge strike obstructed the narrow shipping corridor through the Kerch Strait, linking the Sea of Azov and the Black Sea. The Guardian described the Sea of Azov as resembling a lake, with passenger ferries unable to move and the occupied ports of Mariupol and Berdyansk cut off.

The soldiers returned from their mission jubilant and elated. Back at their headquarters, they gathered as usual for a minute’s silence in memory of those killed in Russia’s war before receiving congratulations on the operation. “We had our daily ritual as usual. Then there were congratulations on a very successful and important operation,” Leon said. He added: “We are paying them back.”
