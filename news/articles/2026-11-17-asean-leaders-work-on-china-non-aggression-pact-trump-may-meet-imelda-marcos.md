## Scheduled Thailand deal follows tariff reversal and aircraft pledge above $18 billion

ASEAN leaders are working on a non-aggression pact with China in the contested waters of the South China Sea, a key global trade route, during President Donald Trump’s scheduled Manila visit. Confrontations have particularly escalated in recent years between Chinese and Philippine coast guard and naval forces in disputed shoals.

Philippine officials are arranging a possible brief reunion at Malacanang between Trump and Imelda Marcos if she is well enough and receives her doctors’ approval. Now 97 and wheelchair-bound, the former Philippine first lady and mother of President Ferdinand Marcos Jr. had partied in New York decades ago in her heyday. AP described her as the controversial wife of a Philippine strongman who was to be ousted in a pro-democracy 1986 “People Power” public uprising.

The friendship with Trump endured, with Trump often asking Marcos Jr. and other Philippine officials: “How’s Imelda?” Philippine Ambassador to Washington Jose Manuel Romualdez told The Associated Press. “Personal connections are very important to him,” Romualdez said. “He has asked President Marcos in phone calls, ‘I know your mother, how is she?’”

When Marcos Jr. met Trump at the White House last year, he carried a note from his mother thanking the U.S. president “for his strong backing of the Philippines.” “President Trump was very happy and told Marcos, ‘please say hello to your mother,’” Romualdez said.

Trump is then scheduled to sign a trade agreement with Thailand. The U.S. Supreme Court overturned in February the 19% tariff Trump placed on goods from Thailand. The administration sought replacement tariffs in July by levying a 12.5% tax on imports from Thailand, saying the country had not taken steps to oppose the use of forced labor.

The United States had announced a trade framework with Thailand last October, according to the Office of the U.S. Trade Representative. The framework called on Thailand to remove tariffs on most American goods and barriers to importing U.S. autos, food products and pharmaceutical drugs. At the time, the Trump administration said Thailand would also spend more than $18 billion on U.S. aircraft and buy U.S. farm goods and liquefied natural gas. AP described the scheduled agreement as the type Trump had relished striking since launching a trade war the previous year, buttressed by steep tariff threats.

On Wednesday, Trump is set to receive a red-carpet welcome and full military honors from Philippine President Ferdinand Marcos Jr., followed by a state banquet during his visit to the Malacanang presidential palace. Trump and Marcos are expected to affirm their commitment to their countries’ longstanding treaty alliance. AP described the Philippines as Washington’s oldest treaty ally in Asia.

Trump is scheduled to arrive in Manila on Tuesday, the first stop on a three-day trip, where he is expected to meet regional partners and discuss trade. He is the guest of honor at the annual ASEAN summit, which includes a cocktail reception and dinner with other world leaders. AP described the Manila stop as an intermission from some of the thorniest issues at home. Shortly after he arrives, Trump is due to meet the leaders of the Quad, a group of nations including the United States, Australia, India and Japan. The group has aimed to counter China’s influence in the Asia Pacific. It would be the first meeting of Quad leaders since Trump returned to the White House last year.

After the Philippines, Trump is expected to go to China for what AP described as historic three-way nuclear talks with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin. Trump has said a meeting with North Korean leader Kim Jong Un may also be possible. Before heading to Shenzhen, China, for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, Trump is expected to announce commercial deals and investments in aviation, defense, energy and technology.

The Manila visit is Trump’s first travel abroad since his party suffered deep losses in midterm elections. AP reported that the White House and stock market had been rattled by Jensen Huang’s departure from Nvidia last week after Huang admitted to an affair with an alleged Chinese government agent; Beijing said it was not involved. AP reported that the visit also comes amid a major disruption to the U.S. aviation industry and a scandal that has shaken the global AI industry.
