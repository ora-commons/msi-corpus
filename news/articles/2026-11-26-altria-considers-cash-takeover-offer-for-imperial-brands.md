## Japan Tobacco reportedly considered a partnership bid for Imperial Brands

Altria said Thursday that it is considering a cash takeover offer for Imperial Brands, its smaller London-listed peer. If a deal went ahead, the U.S. tobacco and nicotine company said, the two businesses would be valued at around $157 billion combined. Altria also said any offer would be in cash and that it was in talks with an unnamed third party to buy part of Imperial Brands, though no agreement had been reached.

Altria's statement described two routes at once: a potential cash offer for the whole of Imperial Brands and separate discussions about buying part of the company alongside an unnamed third party if a transaction proceeded. The company provided no financial details of a potential offer and did not describe the third party. Imperial Brands did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Altria had a market capitalization of $126.89 billion. Imperial Brands was valued at 22.77 billion pounds ($29.87 billion) at the close of business on Wednesday, the session before the announcement.

Imperial Brands shares were up 13% at 34.39 pounds in early-afternoon European trading on Thursday, a closing price the company had not seen since July 2017. The advance made Imperial Brands the biggest riser on the FTSE 100 index. Altria's own shares closed at $76.28 on Wednesday, and U.S. markets were closed for the Thanksgiving holiday.

Japan Tobacco was also reported to be weighing an approach to Imperial Brands. Bloomberg reported that the company was interested in making a move for the U.K. group and was considering a partnership to buy Imperial Brands before splitting the company's assets in different parts of the world. Bloomberg said the talks were at an early stage.

Japan Tobacco and Imperial Brands declined to comment on the Bloomberg report. Altria's announcement, by contrast, confirmed only that the U.S. company was considering an offer.

Any transaction would extend a wave of takeovers and overseas listing switches already seen at the London Stock Exchange. The most recent example was low-cost airline easyJet's agreement to a 5.7 billion-pound takeover by Global Management.

Altria's announcement left the price of any offer, the role of the unnamed third party and Imperial Brands' response unresolved. The company said no agreement had been reached with the third party and disclosed no financial details.

Two approaches to Imperial Brands were under discussion on Thursday: Altria's consideration of a cash takeover, and the partnership structure Bloomberg reported Japan Tobacco was weighing. Altria said no agreement had been reached with the unnamed third party, and Bloomberg described the Japan Tobacco talks as being at an early stage.
