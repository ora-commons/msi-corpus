## Jones says board does not intend to reinstate Huang

NPR reported on Nov. 18 that chip giant Nvidia executives had held their first earnings call since Jensen Huang stepped down; the call took place the previous day. Interim CEO Colette Kress, who had taken over, was on the call. The same Nov. 18 report said an online petition seeking Huang’s return as Nvidia CEO had nearly 200,000 names.

Kress projected business as usual during the call. She said: “Our ambition will not change. Our passion to innovate will not change. And our commitment to customers, partners and shareholders will not change.” NPR tech correspondent John Ruwitch said Kress told the call that the company was doing great and that its customers and employees were sticking with it. Ruwitch said Kress ended the call by saying, “we've got this.”

At the outset of the call, board chairman Harvey Jones said the board did not intend to reinstate Huang. Jones said: “The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.” He also backed Kress, who had served as chief financial officer for years and, in Jones' words, had been at the center of Nvidia’s extraordinary growth.

On the previous day, Huang issued a long post on X. He said there had been an outpouring of calls from Nvidia employees, investors and business partners for him to return as CEO and that he would be willing to make a comeback. The post was his first public comment since stepping down and his first since apologizing late the previous week for being unfaithful to his wife. Vocal supporters had mounted a Stand with Jensen campaign.

Nvidia posted a strong quarter, with revenue over $115 billion. Revenue was up over 100% from the same quarter a year earlier, revenue from data centers alone was over $100 billion and the company raised its revenue projection for next year to 75%. Ruwitch said the result was not surprising and the numbers would have been baked in before Huang stepped down the previous week. He described Nvidia as a giant crucial to the AI boom.

Gaurav Gupta, a technology analyst at the consultancy Gartner, said: “So that shows their confidence in the business and their commitments, the demand they have from all the customers.” He also said any shift in Nvidia’s business because of the new leadership would not be visible in the numbers for months.

NPR said Huang had stepped down over allegations about extramarital affairs and possible spying attempts by China. The New York Times and other outlets reported that Huang had multiple affairs, including one involving a Chinese woman who ran a company in Singapore that was buying Nvidia chips. The Times said the woman was suspected of being recruited by Beijing’s top spy agency. NPR said it had not independently confirmed that reporting.

Ruwitch said there would be a lot of investigations. There were also allegations that companies like the one run by the Chinese woman were helping China obtain banned chips. Ruwitch said that could potentially point to violations of U.S. export controls. Nvidia’s board is running an internal investigation, and Huang is set to appear before several congressional committees.

The board still needs to choose a permanent CEO. Jones said it would try to make the selection as quickly as possible without rushing the decision. Kress remains interim CEO as the board conducts its search.
