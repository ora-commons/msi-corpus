## California drops appeal over sale to co-founder's nonprofit

California Attorney General Rob Bonta on Monday announced the state will receive a $6 million settlement payment in 23andMe's bankruptcy, resolving state legal claims related to a 2023 data breach that exposed 6.9 million customers' genetic and other personal information. More than 855,000 Californians had their genetic information exposed in the breach, according to the attorney general's office.

The agreement ends California's effort to block the $305 million sale of 23andMe's assets to TTAM Research Institute, a nonprofit controlled by 23andMe co-founder Anne Wojcicki. The state had argued that the bankruptcy sale violated California's Genetic Information Privacy Act and that consumers had not given express consent to the transfer of their data. California had unsuccessfully tried to stop the sale.

Once a trailblazer in DNA testing kits, 23andMe filed for bankruptcy in March 2025, seeking to sell its business at auction after a decline in demand and the legal fallout from the data breach.

A US bankruptcy judge ruled against California's objections to the sale, and later ruled that the state could not sue to recover money from the bankrupt company's successor, according to court documents filed on Monday. California lost a subsequent appeal in federal district court.

As part of the $6 million settlement, California agreed to drop any further appeals of the asset sale, the court documents show.

Customers of 23andMe retained the option to delete their data during the bankruptcy sale process, but California had argued the court should have disallowed the transfer of any data unless 23andMe customers first gave express consent to the sale.

"I am proud to fight for the privacy rights of Californians and to deliver this settlement for consumers who put their trust in 23andMe," Bonta, a Democrat, said in a statement on Monday.

"I want to remind Californians that customers of 23andMe can delete their private, genetic data — no matter who owns it," Bonta added.

Separately, 23andMe agreed to pay $46.75 million to victims of the 2023 data breach as part of its bankruptcy.
