## Measure targets diesel deal and Zelenskyy's claims on U.S. support

Democratic Sen. Tim Kaine said he will force a Senate floor vote in the coming weeks on a resolution requiring the State Department to report on the human rights consequences of President Donald Trump's policies toward Russia. Kaine is introducing the measure Thursday as Trump returns from a meeting in China with Russian President Vladimir Putin and China's Xi Jinping.

Kaine is invoking a provision of a foreign policy law that allows any senator to trigger a vote requiring a report on another country's human rights practices. Unlike most bills, the privileged resolutions Kaine has introduced under the Foreign Assistance Act, War Powers Act and other foreign policy laws can prompt a vote within weeks and bypass committees. The measure is part of Senate Democrats' strategy since the beginning of Trump's second term to force votes on reversing parts of his agenda.

The resolution seeks more information about the effects of an October diesel deal Trump struck with Russia, which waived sanctions against Moscow and allowed the sale of millions of tons of fuel. Trump said on Oct. 9 that Russia would immediately supply more than 300,000 tons of diesel, followed by an additional 500,000 tons in November and 1 million tons "immediately thereafter." He said Russia would deliver an additional 3 million tons "within a short period of time" after that. Trump did not address the sanctions or Russia's war. He wrote on social media: "Lower prices for Americans, especially our Great Farmers, Ranchers, and Truckers, is my Greatest Priority."

The measure also reaches other U.S. policies toward Ukraine based on claims from Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy. Zelenskyy has said that the Pentagon delayed the delivery of Patriot air defense interceptors and that U.S. intelligence warning of incoming Russian attacks against Ukraine had been interrupted. If adopted, Kaine's resolution would require the State Department to report on the consequences of those policies.

If passed, the resolution would ask the State Department to calculate how Trump's policies affected Russia's ability to wage war in Ukraine and whether the policies caused or worsened human rights abuses. It would also ask for an update on the current status of negotiations to end the war, which Russia launched in February 2022.

Kaine called the resolution a "test of good faith" and said he hopes it can pass with bipartisan votes. "If they were to give us a nonstarter human rights report that whitewashes Russia's record, that will tell us a lot and that will tell the American public a lot," the Virginia senator said in an interview with The Associated Press on Wednesday.

Kaine has led multiple war powers votes, including several on the war in Iran. Last year, he triggered a vote on a resolution requiring more transparency from the Trump administration about the human rights effects of deportations to El Salvador. The Republican-controlled Senate voted along party lines to block that resolution.

The strategy has proven effective for Democrats by taking floor time from the Republican majority and forcing senators to go on record on the Iran war and other issues ahead of this year's midterm elections, in which Democrats ultimately won control of both chambers of Congress. Kaine said he will continue finding ways to trigger votes even when Democrats take the Senate majority next year.

Getting past a presidential veto will be hard, Kaine conceded, but he said the vote would make senators accountable for their positions. "I want to make everybody here own what's going on," Kaine said. "We have to put bills on the president's desk."
