## The founders launched their first restaurant with a $600 loan

Wichita State University announced that Dan Carney, who co-founded Pizza Hut with his younger brother Frank in Wichita, Kansas, died on Nov. 13 at age 95. The university, Carney’s alma mater, made the announcement earlier this month.

Carney and Frank launched the first Pizza Hut in Wichita in 1958 while both were students, according to the university. They used a $600 loan from their mother to open one small restaurant.

The business quickly found success, and franchised locations expanded its reach. Pizza Hut’s website noted, “The sign out front only had room for eight letters and, just like that, Pizza Hut was born.”

Pizza Hut’s familiar red roof debuted in 1969. By 1971, the company was the top pizza chain in the world by sales, and its website counted nearly 20,000 locations worldwide at the end of last year.

The International Pizza Hut Franchise Holder’s Association credited Carney with much of the company’s mission. The association said, “Dan believed in doing things the right way, taking care of guests, looking out for people and giving back to the community. Those values helped build Pizza Hut into the brand it is today, and they are just as important to us now. We’re grateful for what he and Frank started, and we’re proud to carry that legacy forward.”

Carney spent much of his life doing philanthropy and advocacy. He served as board chairman of the Cerebral Palsy Research Foundation for 50 years. The foundation said it was “deeply saddened” by his passing and called Carney a “devoted champion” of its mission.

Wichita State University described Carney as a decades-long supporter. He provided scholarship support for students and helped open the university’s on-campus Pizza Hut museum. “Pizza Hut is known worldwide, but Dan’s legacy at this university is just as lasting,” Wichita State President Rick Muma said in a statement.

Carney’s younger brother and fellow Pizza Hut co-founder, Frank, died in 2020.

Pizza Hut is now headquartered in Texas. Amid growing competition, the chain has had several different owners over the years. PepsiCo acquired Pizza Hut in 1977 but spun off its restaurant division, which became Yum Brands, in 1997.

Earlier this year, private equity firm LongRange Capital bought Pizza Hut for about $1.5 billion, excluding the mainland China business.
