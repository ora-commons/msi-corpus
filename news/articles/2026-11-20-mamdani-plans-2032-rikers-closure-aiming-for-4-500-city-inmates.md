## Brooklyn jail expected to open first in 2029

New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani announced Friday a proposal that would close Rikers Island by 2032 and replace it with four borough jails. The schedule extends beyond a legal requirement to close Rikers and replace it with four smaller, more modern jails by 2027; New York City is all but certain to miss that deadline.

The closure effort has a long history. The Associated Press reported that criminal justice activists have pushed for decades to close Rikers, a complex that has housed jail inmates since the 1930s. In 2019, the New York City Council voted to close the jail complex and replace it with newer jails in the city's boroughs.

Local lawmakers invoked the names of former prisoners who died, including Kalief Browder, who took his own life at 22 after being held for three years at Rikers. At the time of that vote, city officials also called for shrinking the jail population to accommodate the closure. Critics opposed that approach, arguing that reducing the population would mean more violent criminals on city streets.

The Associated Press reported that for years Rikers Island had stood as “a symbol of dysfunction,” with the network of jails plagued by violence and overcrowding. A federal judge cited “abnormal and unacceptable” conditions and appointed an outside manager to restore order and implement reforms.

Mamdani, a Democrat, has supported closing Rikers, though the longstanding plan to build new jails has met opposition. His predecessor, former Mayor Eric Adams, opposed federal oversight and borough jails.

“It is time to finally close Rikers Island,” Mamdani said in a statement. “For decades, New Yorkers have demanded a correctional system that is safe and humane.”

The replacement plan would place people in custody in jails in the Bronx, Queens, Brooklyn and Manhattan. People needing health care or psychiatric treatment would go to a handful of medical facilities. Rikers Island is in the East River near LaGuardia Airport. The plan also calls for reducing the number of beds for women and diverting them into community-based programs.

City Hall expects the Brooklyn jail to open in 2029, followed by the Bronx and Queens in 2031 and Manhattan in 2032.

The population target applies to the city overall. More than 6,500 people are housed at Rikers, while the administration aims to reduce the city's overall inmate population to around 4,500. The Associated Press reported that Mamdani's strategy for closing Rikers hinges on substantially reducing the number of people kept behind bars.

The administration proposed a new slate of diversion and supervised-release programs, an expansion of housing and social services to prevent recidivism, restoration of the online bail payment system and various measures to reduce case delays.

Mamdani also said: “We have a legal and moral obligation to close Rikers. Working alongside the City Council, Courts and nonprofit partners, we will meet it — for the people in our custody, the staff who serve them, victims of crime, and every New Yorker.”
