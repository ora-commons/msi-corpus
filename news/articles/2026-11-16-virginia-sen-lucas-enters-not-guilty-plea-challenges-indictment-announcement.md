## Virginia Sen. Lucas enters not-guilty plea, challenges indictment announcement

**Subtype:** fact

- Virginia state Sen. L. Louise Lucas pleaded not guilty Monday to federal racketeering, drug, money laundering and other charges.
- Federal prosecutors accuse Lucas of leading a criminal enterprise that acquired and sold large quantities of marijuana and laundered the proceeds.
- Lucas also faces charges of conspiracy to commit honest services fraud and maintaining a drug-involved premises, with a jury trial set for mid-September.
- Lucas' legal team filed a motion arguing that Justice Department and federal law enforcement officials violated a court rule on the press and fair trials in announcing the indictment.

## Jury trial set for mid-September in Lucas' federal case

Lucas pleaded not guilty in federal court Monday to federal racketeering, drug, money laundering and other charges. She also faces a charge of conspiracy to commit honest services fraud and a charge of maintaining a drug-involved premises. Court records that reflected the plea set a jury trial for mid-September.

Federal prosecutors accused Lucas of leading a criminal enterprise that they said acquired and sold large quantities of marijuana and laundered the proceeds. At a news conference and in charging documents, authorities said Lucas used her political power to insulate the marijuana operation from scrutiny and sought to influence Virginia marijuana legislation to financially benefit the alleged enterprise.

One of her attorneys, Anthony Capozzolo, said by email that Lucas "looks forward to litigating this matter in the future" and declined further comment. Later Monday, supporters of Lucas planned to hold a tele-town hall to discuss the case.

The charges against Lucas were announced after the FBI searched her hometown office and a cannabis shop she owns in May.

Lucas, 82, has served in the state Senate since 1992 and is currently its president pro tempore. She also chairs the powerful Senate finance committee and has told reporters she will not resign or step down from that leadership role while the case plays out. A vocal critic of President Donald Trump, Lucas has carried on with her official duties since her indictment, participating in committee hearings while the Legislature is out of session.

Lucas told The Virginian-Pilot for a story published Sunday that she will seek reelection next year. Before Monday's plea, she made clear she intended to contest the charges and characterized the Justice Department's work in the case as an effort to intimidate her.

On Sunday, Lucas' legal team filed a motion arguing that Attorney General Todd Blanche, FBI Director Kash Patel and First Assistant U.S. Attorney Theophani Stamos of the Eastern District of Virginia violated a court rule dealing with the press and fair trials in statements they made at the news conference announcing the indictment. The court should impose "appropriate sanctions for those violations," Lucas' team wrote.

Scottie Howell, a spokesperson for the Eastern District of Virginia U.S. Attorney's Office, declined to comment on the motion. Audrey Cook, a Justice Department spokesperson, said by email that the department "remains confident in its case and will not comment further on pending litigation."

Since 2021, marijuana has been legal to possess in Virginia, and many shops across the state have sold cannabis-derived products, but full recreational marijuana sales will not be allowed until July 2027.
