## Imperial's portfolio and job cuts amid a UK takeover wave

Altria faces a fixed timetable under UK takeover rules: by 5 p.m. on December 24, the company must announce a firm intention to make an offer for Imperial Brands or walk away. Altria said Thursday that it was considering a bid, but Thursday's disclosure was not itself a firm offer.

Altria said any offer, if made, was likely to be made solely in cash. The U.S. company also said it was discussing with a third party a possible acquisition of part of Imperial Brands' business, a disclosure that suggested Imperial could be broken up. The part-business discussion leaves open the possibility of a transaction that would not cover the whole company.

Imperial makes JPS, Davidoff, Gauloises and Lambert & Butler cigarettes, Golden Virginia rolling tobacco, Rizla rolling paper and the Blu vaping brand.

The company is also one of the founder members of the FTSE 100, which launched in January 1984.

Last week, Imperial said it was cutting up to 4,600 jobs in a cost-saving drive as it used more AI and other technologies. The takeover interest came while the tobacco group was reducing its workforce.

The Guardian said Imperial could become the next UK company to fall to a foreign takeover. Several other large UK companies have already fallen to takeover offers this year, including UK warehouse landlord Segro and asset manager Schroders. The low-cost airline easyJet is in the process of being acquired.

Altria's announcement came after Bloomberg reported that Japan Tobacco was evaluating a potential acquisition of Imperial. Imperial shares jumped 13% to the top of the FTSE 100 leaderboard, hitting their highest level since 2017.

Altria's Philip Morris division makes Marlboro and Benson & Hedges cigarettes. The takeover discussions placed Imperial at the center of a possible bidding contest, but no agreement to acquire Imperial or any part of it had been announced.
