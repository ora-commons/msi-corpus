## Some Trump administration contracts remain live on Kalshi

Kalshi will no longer list some betting markets associated with President Trump after the Commodity Futures Trading Commission ordered the company to pause contracts that predict what the president might say in public, according to people familiar with the matter. The company is halting all contracts that allow users to place bets on what President Trump might say on specific dates, the people said.

The CFTC on Friday ordered Kalshi to temporarily halt those contracts over concerns that they could be susceptible to manipulation. Kalshi has previously said it has security protocols in place to guard against efforts to manipulate its markets.

The platform and the Trump administration came under scrutiny after the president's former teleprompter operator allegedly used inside information to make successful bets on the president's speeches. Gabriel Perez, the teleprompter operator who made the wagers, reached a settlement with federal regulators earlier this year.

Prediction-market bets on what public figures will say, known as mention markets, have attracted a relatively small amount of trading activity but outsize public interest. The Wall Street Journal has previously reported that online prediction-market influencers turned mention-market bets, including those tied to the State of the Union, into viral streaming moments as concerns about possible manipulation mounted.

Kalshi had insisted until recently that mention markets were a valuable way for traders to hedge business risk. Last month, The Wall Street Journal asked Kalshi's chief regulatory officer, Sudhir Jain, why the company continued listing mention markets after regulators flagged them for insider risk. He specifically mentioned Trump markets as having a legitimate purpose.

“There are mention markets with true hedging utility, with true business cases, and we list those,” Jain said at the New York event. “If you look at what we used to list, and what we list right now, we have significantly trimmed. We want to focus on the ones which we list right now, which are related to Trump, related to earnings, related to some keynote addresses, but very, very limited. I think we have cut that down by more than 50%.”

Under Trump-appointee Michael Selig, the CFTC has attempted to walk a fine line between boosting prediction markets and signaling that it is a regulator poised to rein in platform excesses, The Wall Street Journal reported. The independent CFTC ordered Kalshi to restrict Trump mention markets and launched a wide-ranging investigation into Kalshi's primary competitor, Polymarket.

The CFTC has also struck a combative tone in state litigation over whether prediction markets amount to unlicensed gambling, intervening on behalf of the platforms in a dozen states. Most recently in Louisiana, a judge last week ordered prediction markets to cease operations.

Kalshi plans to continue allowing some mention markets related to the Trump administration, according to the same people familiar with the matter. A market related to what Vice President JD Vance will say at an event on Wednesday remains live on Kalshi. The site also still lists markets on whether senior officials will leave the administration this year and whether Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick will remain in his job.

The Wall Street Journal reported that Kalshi and other prediction markets are likely to face inquiries from Democrats in Congress as they prepare to take control of key investigative committees following sweeping victories in the November midterm elections. Elizabeth Warren, who is poised to lead the Senate Banking Committee next year, wrote to the White House last week. Her letter said the teleprompter incident “should have been a wake-up call” to enforce stronger ethics policies and requested information on how the White House is responding to the incident.

Kalshi had ties to President Trump's son, Donald Trump Jr., who until recently served as an adviser for the company. Kalshi announced that Trump Jr. and the company mutually parted ways after 1789 Capital, the investment firm where Trump Jr. serves as a partner, opted to increase its investment into rival Polymarket.

The CFTC's Friday order was temporary, while Kalshi's decision removes the affected Trump remark contracts from the platform. The agency's Polymarket investigation, its state litigation and the likely congressional inquiries remain separate matters.
