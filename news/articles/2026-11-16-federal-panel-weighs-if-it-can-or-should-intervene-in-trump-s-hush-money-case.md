## State appeal remains pending as Trump pursues dismissal on immunity grounds

On Monday, a three-judge panel of the 2nd U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals spent more than an hour questioning Trump's lawyer and a state prosecutor over whether the federal appeals court can or should intervene in President Donald Trump's hush money case, after a New York State appellate court heard arguments in his bid to erase the conviction. Trump's lawyer, Robert Giuffra Jr., asked the panel to take control of the case and halt state proceedings. The appearance was the second court appearance by the sides in 11 days, and the panel issued no decision.

Trump's two-pronged legal strategy targets the May 2024 verdict. He was convicted on 34 felony counts of falsifying business records to obscure a $130,000 payment to adult film performer Stormy Daniels. In the first path, he is pursuing a traditional appeal through New York's state court system; the Appellate Division heard arguments Nov. 5 and has not ruled. In the second, he asked the 2nd Circuit to shift the case to federal court, where he could seek dismissal on presidential-immunity grounds.

The case went to trial as Trump campaigned for his return to the White House. The Republican president denies wrongdoing and was given no punishment. The conviction made him the first U.S. president convicted of a crime, an association that would remain regardless of the outcome. With little over two years left in his presidency, Trump continues to pursue ways to overturn the verdict.

Giuffra urged the panel to issue an order as soon as possible, even before writing its full decision, to take control of the case and stop the Appellate Division's deliberations. Without federal intervention, Giuffra wrote, Trump "will likely have to wait years" for state appellate courts to rule before he can take the matter to the U.S. Supreme Court.

Chief Judge Raymond J. Lohier Jr., appointed by President Barack Obama, and Judge John M. Walker Jr. questioned the lawyers. Walker, who was appointed to the appeals court by his cousin, President George H.W. Bush, asked, "How does that work?" He then asked, "I mean, assuming we were to agree with you, there's been an argument in the Appellate Division. Briefs have been filed in the Appellate Division. Will those briefs be refiled by everybody here?" Federal law does not specify precisely how such a transfer would proceed.

Giuffra proposed that the case first go to a federal district court judge, who would adopt the state-court verdict, and then return to the 2nd Circuit for arguments and a decision on dismissal on presidential-immunity grounds. He said the briefs would remain the same, "albeit with different covers."

Lohier said, "You're asking us to embrace an entire procedural mechanism that I don't see anywhere authorized by Congress."

Giuffra replied, "We think it is authorized," and called a provision allowing post-verdict transfer petitions "a safety valve that's intended to protect against exactly the gamesmanship that's gone on here."

Trump asked the 2nd Circuit to step in after a federal judge in August ruled against him for a third time. Judge Alvin K. Hellerstein, who died last month at 92, wrote in his decision that "paying hush money to an adult film star or engaging in a cover-up of an embarrassment are not subsumed in presidential immunity."

Trump first sought to move the case to federal court weeks after his indictment in March 2023. He tried again after his conviction and the U.S. Supreme Court's July 2024 ruling that presidents and ex-presidents cannot be prosecuted for official acts and prosecutors cannot use official acts as evidence that unofficial actions were illegal. Giuffra argued to both the state and federal appellate panels that prosecutors used that type of official-act evidence, including testimony about Trump's Oval Office conversations, workflow and social media posts.

Giuffra also challenged the prosecution's description of the hush money payment as an illegal campaign contribution under the Federal Election Campaign Act. He argued that allegations of that kind belong in federal court, not state court.

After Trump appealed Hellerstein's second denial, the 2nd Circuit ordered the judge to reconsider, finding that he had failed to weigh "important issues relevant" to the president's request to move the case. The panel, which also included Lohier, said it expressed no view on how Hellerstein should rule but instructed him to closely review evidence Trump claimed was related to official acts and whether Trump could argue those actions were taken as part of his White House duties.

Steven Wu, chief of the appeals division at the Manhattan District Attorney's Office, urged the 2nd Circuit panel to uphold Hellerstein's ruling and leave the case in state court. Wu argued that even if Trump prevailed on the federal request, the state appeals court would remain free to rule.

The case remains unresolved in both courts. The 2nd Circuit has not decided whether it can or should intervene, and the Appellate Division has not ruled. Under Giuffra's proposal, a federal district judge would first adopt the state verdict before the 2nd Circuit considered dismissal on presidential-immunity grounds.
