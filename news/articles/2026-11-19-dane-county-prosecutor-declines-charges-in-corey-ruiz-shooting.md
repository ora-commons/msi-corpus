## Dane County prosecutor declines charges in Corey Ruiz shooting

## Police union supports the decision as the family's attorney disputes the shooting

Dane County District Attorney Ismael Ozanne announced Thursday that Madison police officer Kiel Baitinger-Peterson will not face criminal charges for shooting and killing Corey Ruiz on July 22. Baitinger-Peterson is a white officer with more than a decade of experience in law enforcement, and Ruiz was a 38-year-old Black man.

Police said Baitinger-Peterson was one of four officers who responded that day to calls about someone checking out parked cars and an alleged burglary attempt. The officers surrounded Ruiz, who was on a bicycle, police said.

Ozanne said he concluded that the shooting was legally justified. "I conclude it was reasonable for the officer to use deadly force when this person resisted officers, produced a knife, and was not affected by multiple attempts to use Tasers while officers attempted to take him into custody," the prosecutor said in a statement announcing his decision.

The Wisconsin Department of Justice said immediately after the shooting that two officers attempted to use a Taser on Ruiz but were not successful. Ruiz "brandished a knife and injured" Baitinger-Peterson, the department said.

Ozanne said he reached the decision after reviewing police reports, diagrams, surveillance videos, police squad car videos and citizen videos. Madison police officers do not wear body cameras, so the confrontation was captured by bystanders and other video sources rather than by officer-worn cameras.

Civil rights attorney Ben Crump, who represents Ruiz's family and had called for Baitinger-Peterson to be charged, said the videos Ozanne showed the family on Thursday did not justify the shooting. "The medical examiner told the family that if the officer had not fired that shot to his head, Corey would have survived," Crump said. "It was clearly an excessive use of force. This police killing in Madison, Wisconsin, was unjustifiable, unreasonable, unnecessary, and unconstitutional."

At a news conference, Crump described the video as showing officers tackling Ruiz, tasing him and kicking him before Baitinger-Peterson fired. "The video is the objective unbiased witness here," Crump said. He said the video shows three shots, one to the chest and a final shot to the head, and called the killing an execution.

Ozanne said his decision was a correct interpretation of the law. "A person does not have to like and/or agree with the law, however I am sworn to apply the law to the facts at hand," he said. "The law applies equally to members of law enforcement and to those who are not."

Jim Palmer, executive director of the Wisconsin Professional Police Association, said the decision that the officer's use of deadly force was legally justified "is supported by the evidence and consistent with the legal standards governing the use of force by law enforcement officers." Baitinger-Peterson did not reply to an email seeking comment.

The killing was the third time since 2012 that Madison police had shot and killed someone in the same neighborhood. It sparked days of protests and led the city to reckon with calls for police reform addressing racial justice issues.

City officials announced in September that a $350,000 donation from a local nonprofit could equip all Madison police officers with body cameras by the end of 2027. The donation will cover operational expenses, while other costs are being funded through grants and other means.

Baitinger-Peterson and the other three officers who interacted with Ruiz on July 22 remain on paid administrative leave. Ozanne's decision resolves whether the officer will be charged, but the assessments set out by the prosecutor and the family's attorney over the video and the medical examiner's account remain divided.
