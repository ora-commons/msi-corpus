## Body-camera footage and recorded phone calls were presented at Thursday’s hearing

At a Thursday hearing in Camden, South Carolina, former Horry County Deputy Police Chief Brandon Strickland and former Sgt. Damon Vescovi were charged before Circuit Court Judge Heath Taylor with misconduct in office and obstruction of justice over their response to the Sept. 9, 2023 road-rage shooting that killed Scott Spivey. Strickland also was charged with conspiracy. Both former officers were involved in the immediate aftermath of the shooting.

Special Prosecutor Barry Barnette presented slowed-down body-camera footage of a note for Judge Taylor. Vescovi, then a patrol sergeant, had arrived at the scene soon after the shooting, wrote the note and held it up for Boyd to read: “ACT LIKE A VICTIM. Camera.” Barnette also played snippets of calls recorded on Boyd’s phone, including Boyd telling Williams, “I had a f—ing blast.”

Minutes after the shooting, Boyd called Strickland, then the deputy police chief, and begged for his help. The two men were longtime friends who had hunted and fished together. In one of 90 recorded conversations reviewed by The Wall Street Journal, Strickland later told Boyd, “You’re taken care of.”

The killing had drawn little attention until a Wall Street Journal investigation last year aired conversations recorded on Boyd’s phone. The recordings had been included in police files but were not brought to light, and some calls indicated Boyd was intent on pursuing Spivey and had anticipated a shootout while still en route.

The officers’ charges came a day after murder charges were brought against Weldon Boyd and Bradley Williams for Spivey’s death. At Thursday’s afternoon hearing, both men were formally charged with Spivey’s murder and a related firearms charge, and Boyd also was formally charged with conspiracy. Both were taken into custody and transported for the two-hour drive back to Horry County for processing at the J. Reuben Long Detention Center.

Boyd and Williams initially avoided arrest by saying they feared for their lives when they chased and fatally shot Spivey, 33. The case has tested South Carolina’s “stand your ground” law, which allows people to defend their lives when faced with a lethal threat. Earlier this year, a South Carolina judge denied the men’s claim of self-defense in a wrongful-death case filed by Spivey’s sister, Jennifer Foley. That decision raised the possibility of criminal charges in the case.

Circuit Court Judge Heath Taylor set $10,000 bonds for Strickland and Vescovi, a $110,000 bond for Boyd and a $100,000 bond for Williams. Lawyers for all four defendants said their clients were innocent and argued that the men had deep ties in their communities and posed no flight risk. The lawyers said they expected their clients to post bond, though it was unclear how soon they might be released.

Jennifer Foley asked Taylor to deny bond for the shooters and to make an example of the former officers for what she called their betrayal of public trust. “If we can’t trust the police to protect us, who can we trust?” Foley said. “Who can we call?”

Boyd’s lawyer, Shaun Kent, said his client’s words were crude and inexcusable. “He can be an a—hole,” Kent said. “I cannot stand some of the things he said.” Kent said those conversations occurred well after the shooting and that the most legally relevant call was Boyd’s contemporaneous description of Spivey’s erratic driving to a 911 operator.

Williams’s lawyer, O’Bryan Martin, said the defense team would push for a trial as soon as next year. Martin said Boyd and Williams were eager to tell their side of what he described as “a highly publicized case that has so far focused on the Spivey family’s perspective.” “There is somewhat of a relief,” Martin said. “We just want the full story out then let a jury make a decision.”
