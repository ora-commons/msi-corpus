## Four-hour ground stop disrupted more than 15,000 flights nationwide

President Donald Trump on Friday backed the administration's $50 billion request to rebuild US air traffic control systems, describing the existing systems as "ancient" and "obsolete." He said the systems were at least "55 years old" and that some towers were not in good shape structurally. His comments came a week after a four-hour ground stop halted flights across the country, the longest such event since the 11 September 2001 attacks.

Trump said: "It's very safe. But it's safe for a different reason because we do it very slowly." He added: "In other words, we travel slower. That's why you have lots of waits at the airport."

The 13 November stop delayed or canceled more than 15,000 US flights, according to flight-tracking data, and affected an estimated 1.5 million passengers. Thousands of additional flights were canceled or delayed over the weekend as the disruption continued. In some cases, passengers were left stuck inside planes on the tarmac, unable to take off or return to gates.

The outage also disrupted schedules around the world, with some flights departing from the UK and Europe canceled. Federal Aviation Administration officials said the agency planned Friday to share more information about what caused the four-hour stop. Top aviation officials within the Trump administration said the cause was related to old equipment overloading and updates to the systems.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said a government contractor was upgrading an aviation telecommunications network by swapping copper lines for fibre and installing a router. He said the switch caused the router to start flooding other parts of the air traffic control system, sending 1,000 connection attempts every four to five seconds. It was unclear whether the wire replacement or a fault in the router was the root cause.

The resulting problems affected voice and ground communications at 17 of 22 air route traffic control centers in the US. The aviation authority took 45 minutes to identify where the problem was because maintenance was logged manually on paper.

Since the outage, transportation leaders have repeatedly pressed Congress for billions of dollars to upgrade US systems. They have warned that outages like the one on 13 November would continue without an overhaul.

Trump endorsed the administration's $50 billion request to rebuild the air traffic control system. He said the transportation department had been allotted $12 billion, an amount he said was insufficient for the undertaking.

Trump said he told Duffy to limit spending to keeping the existing system running because it would be torn out when a replacement arrived. The cause of the four-hour disruption remained unresolved.
