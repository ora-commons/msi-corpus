## Josh Turek defeats Ashley Hinson in Iowa Senate election

**Subtype:** fact

- Democrat Josh Turek defeated Republican Ashley Hinson in Iowa’s U.S. Senate race, giving Democrats their first Iowa Senate win since 2008, according to The Guardian.
- Josh Turek is set to replace Republican Sen. Joni Ernst, who declined to seek re-election.
- Turek’s victory moved Democrats closer to a Senate majority, and The Guardian said a majority could “block Trump’s legislation and hold up his nominees, including to the supreme court.”

## First Democratic Iowa Senate victory since 2008

Turek’s victory was Democrats’ first Iowa Senate election win since 2008 and moved the party closer to a majority in the chamber. The Guardian said a Democratic majority could be used to “block Trump’s legislation and hold up his nominees, including to the supreme court.”

Iowa’s campaign took place against several pressures on voters. The Guardian reported that the state “has been rocked by the president’s tariff regime, which has harmed the markets for soya beans, one of its major crops.” It also said the Iran war had sent prices for fuel and fertilizer spiking. The state was also grappling with how to lower unusually high rates of cancer. Democrats seized on the issue, The Guardian reported, to “blame the Republicans for ignoring their voters’ health needs.”

The Guardian described Turek as a two-time Paralympic gold medalist in wheelchair basketball who represents a right-leaning district in Iowa’s legislature. It said he sought “to harness voter frustration with these issues, while avoiding the divisive culture war traps that had snared other Democrats.” In an advertisement aired late in the campaign, Turek objected to girls playing in “boy’s sports” and said “taxpayers paying for sex changes.” LGBTQ+ groups condemned the advertisement.

The Guardian described Hinson as a former television broadcaster who defeated a Democrat in 2020 to represent a Cedar Rapids-based district in the House of Representatives. She had condemned Turek as “too liberal for Iowa” and promised to “continue the progress made over Trump two years back in office.”

The Guardian reported that Turek had pitched himself as a successor to Tom Harkin, Iowa’s longtime Democratic senator, who retired in 2015. It said Harkin’s departure and his replacement by Republican Joni Ernst “marked a shift in the fortunes of Democrats in Iowa.” Iowa had voted for Barack Obama in back-to-back elections before voting for Trump in all three elections in which he was on the ballot. Turek is set to replace Ernst, who declined to seek re-election this year.

Turek was photographed at the University of Northern Iowa homecoming parade in Cedar Falls, Iowa, in October. The photograph was credited to Scott Olson/Getty Images.

In its post-election analysis, The Guardian described Turek’s win for Democrats as “a significant victory in a Republican-led state where they have seen their power ebb in the age of Donald Trump” and called it “a milestone in Democrats’ quest to regain control of the Senate.” Its accompanying summary described the race as a “heated contest in former swing state” and said the result was “a sign Republicans may be losing rural voters.”

The Guardian also described the outcome as “a sign that the party may be able to rebuild its standing with rural voters who once supported them,” referring to Democrats, and tied that assessment to Trump’s policies, which it said “pushed up prices across the country and upended relations with major trading partners.” The Guardian wrote that Hinson “appears to have been dragged down by Trump’s dismal approval ratings and voter frustration with prices that continued to rise after he took office, despite the president’s pledge of lower costs.”
