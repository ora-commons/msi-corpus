## Dario Amodei and six co-founders each made about $280 million

Anthropic, the company behind Claude, completed its first day of public trading on Thursday, with NPR putting the company's valuation at about $2 trillion based on its stock price. The IPO gave Anthropic substantial funding. Amodei and his six co-founders each made about $280 million by selling shares in the week's IPO, and each held an Anthropic stake worth about $23 billion at Thursday's closing price.

Anthropic became the first organization focused solely on artificial intelligence to go public, and NPR described the offering as a success for shareholders, including the founders. The listing came as protests questioned whether the company could responsibly steward AI technology.

Outside in Times Square, a small group of protesters rejected the listing. Unidentified protesters chanted, "Why you doing IPO? This AI bubble about to blow." Their demonstration placed the financial success of Anthropic's IPO alongside a warning about an AI investment bubble.

Activists and AI safety researchers gathered outside Anthropic's headquarters in downtown San Francisco. They called on the company to slow the development of advanced AI, with unidentified activists chanting, "Please do not accelerate" and "Just ask the state to regulate." A handful of protesters were arrested in both cities.

Before ringing the Nasdaq bell on Thursday, CEO Dario Amodei said Anthropic remained committed to keeping the technology under human control. He also said the company would not bend to business or political pressure.

Amodei said Anthropic's example could influence other companies in the industry. "If we can show it's possible to make the right choices on safety and still build an incredibly successful company, others in our industry will be inspired to do the same. We call this a race to the top." His answer was that a successful company could inspire stronger safety choices rather than abandon them.

The IPO arrived while debates about AI included the effects of data centers, fears of job losses and worries that AI could go rogue. NPR reported that Anthropic itself had fueled some of those fears. The company was founded five years ago by a breakaway group of OpenAI employees who were concerned that OpenAI was not taking AI safety seriously enough.

Executives and co-founders marked the listing by ringing the ceremonial bell inside the Nasdaq stock exchange to open trading Thursday morning.

Shannon Bond reported the story for NPR News. NPR also noted that Anthropic is a financial supporter of NPR.
