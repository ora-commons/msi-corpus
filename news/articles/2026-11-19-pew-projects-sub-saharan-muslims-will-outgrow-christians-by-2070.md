## Buddhist populations in China, Japan and South Korea are projected to halve

A Pew Research Center report released Nov. 19, 2026, projects that sub-Saharan Africa will contain more than a quarter of the global population by 2070, with its Muslim population growing faster than its Christian population. The report also projects Buddhist populations in China, Japan and South Korea will shrink by half and that 62% of the world's Jews will live in Israel by 2070, up from 46% in 2020. Pew further projects the global religiously unaffiliated population will decline even as unaffiliated shares rise in many countries. It also projects that U.S. Muslims and Hindus will each outnumber U.S. Jews by 2070 if current demographic trends continue.

Pew projects that Hindus and Jews will grow in absolute numbers while declining as a share of the world population. Buddhists, by contrast, are expected to shrink in both number and their portion of the global population.

The research organization links some of the trends to population growth. Most population growth in the 21st century is expected to occur in Africa, and by 2070 more than a quarter of the global population will live in sub-Saharan Africa. The vast majority of people in the region will be Christian or Muslim. The region's high fertility rate and lengthening lifespans will expand both populations, with the Muslim population growing faster.

Pew projects that the Democratic Republic of the Congo will surpass the United States as the country with the largest total number of Christians by 2070. Alan Cooperman, Pew's religion research director, called this "global change in what one might think of as the center of gravity of Christianity."

Differences in fertility rates have major effects on religious population projections, according to Pew. Around the world, Muslims tend to have more children than people of other faiths. In recent years, the fertility rate for Muslim women has been 3.1 children per woman, compared with about 2 children per non-Muslim woman.

Pew also projects that Muslims will become the world's largest religious group by 2035, with Christians second, if recent trends continue. By 2070, Pew expects the global Muslim population to reach 3.6 billion, compared with just under 3 billion Christians. The research organization expects both populations to grow, with the Muslim population growing faster.

In the United States, Pew expects the projected change to be driven mostly by religious switching rather than birth rates. Christians currently make up 62% of the U.S. population, but their share is expected to decline to just under 48% by 2070. The religiously unaffiliated share is expected to reach 41% during the same period.

Christians tend to shed more adherents than they gain through religious switching, and most people leaving Christianity move to religious non-affiliation rather than another tradition, Pew says. The United States is expected to become increasingly religiously diverse, with Muslims and Hindus projected to outnumber Jews by 2070 if current trends continue.

Other projections for 2070 include declining overall populations in China, Japan and South Korea, accompanied by Buddhist populations in those countries shrinking by half. The Jewish population is also expected to become more concentrated in Israel. In 2020, 46% of global Jewry lived there; Pew projects that 62% will live in Israel by 2070.

The religiously unaffiliated share is expected to increase in many countries even as the global population of unaffiliated people declines. Pew attributes the global decline to the large concentration of unaffiliated people in China, which is expected to experience dramatic population losses because of falling birth rates.

Pew built its projections using data from national censuses worldwide, fertility rates and religious-switching patterns. The research also draws on United Nations reports, global migration estimates and earlier Pew studies on religious migration.
