## Warren says Anthropic share swings could roil broader markets on IPO day

Protesters chanted slogans outside Anthropic's San Francisco headquarters after the IPO on Thursday. Across the US, opposition to AI companies has included protests against local data-center plans, and a number of municipalities and states voted for bans or restrictions at the recent midterm elections. The Guardian wrote, “Newly minted Anthropic investors will also have to contend with rising public sentiment against AI.”

Chief Executive Dario Amodei stood with Anthropic executives celebrating the IPO launch on Thursday. He has warned that products he and his rivals are developing may pose dangers to humanity and has called for a slowdown in developing the most advanced AI models after employees expressed concerns that the models were being built irresponsibly.

Anthropic's leaders are likely looking ahead to changes in government next year, when Democrats take over both chambers of Congress after securing large majorities in the midterm elections. Senior Democrats have suggested that technology companies could face a “regulatory crackdown” over the dangers advanced AI models pose and the volatility AI IPOs could introduce into the economy. Last month, Democrat Elizabeth Warren, a senior member of the Senate banking committee, told Amodei in a letter that Anthropic's IPO “could inject extreme volatility and risk into the financial system, which threatens to undermine Americans’ economic security.” She wrote, “The fact that Anthropic is burning through cash and requires an insatiable stream of capital to feed its growth is not a reason to expose everyday Americans to the company’s risks in our public stock markets … Swings in Anthropic’s share price have the potential to roil the broader markets on day one.”

The Guardian wrote that “US economic growth is inextricably tied to the booming AI sector,” highlighting the data-center investments that power programmes such as Claude and ChatGPT. One estimate from Harvard professor Jason Furman suggested “investment in information processing equipment and software” accounted for 92% of US GDP growth in the first half of 2025. The report said a slowdown in data-center expenditure could have far-reaching consequences for Anthropic and the global economy.

Anthropic's investor prospectus put revenue at $16.2 billion in the first six months of 2026, up from $4.6 billion for all of 2025. The company reported losses of $80 billion in the first half of 2026, largely because of a one-off charge, and $104 billion in the first nine months of 2026. The firm has said its costs will rise as it invests further in data centers. The IPO gave Anthropic a pool of new cash for those investments, but the company has promised to spend about $900 billion on data-center infrastructure over the next two decades, and experts said that commitment was likely to drive its debt higher still.

That cash came from a record offering. Anthropic, the maker of the Claude chatbot, sold 980 million shares when it began trading at Nasdaq Market in New York on Thursday, raising $81 billion in the largest initial public offering on record and surpassing the $75 billion raised by Elon Musk's SpaceX in June. According to reports, demand for Anthropic shares was about six times what the company was able to offer. Anthropic shares closed at just over $97, about 17% above the offering price, after opening 34% higher. The opening briefly took the company's valuation above $2 trillion. The Guardian's main text put the closing valuation at roughly $1.7 trillion, while a summary line in the same report listed $1.3 trillion.

Founded in 2021 by a number of former OpenAI employees, Anthropic initially lagged behind OpenAI, its rival frontier lab. Analysts said the company's focus on software for business and programming clients helped position it as one of the market leaders. The Guardian said Anthropic's “breakneck growth has left investors stunned” and described its six-year path from a fledgling start-up to a company worth more than Walmart, JP Morgan, Visa and ExxonMobil after one day of trading. The report also called Anthropic one of the world's most valuable firms and said many of the company's founders, investors and employees were worth billions of dollars, with demand for the stock remaining resilient.

Major US stock indexes fell to seven-month lows on Thursday, which the report linked to growing concerns about the longer-term outlook for the AI industry. In early Friday trading, Asian stocks fell by as much as 4%. The Guardian said the early-Friday decline suggested Anthropic's debut had “done little to settle nerves on trading floors.” The Kospi index in Seoul was down 4.08%. [FRED's NASDAQ Composite Index](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NASDAQCOM) stood at 23,879.2 as of Nov. 20, 2026, on a raw basis, providing a supplementary snapshot of the tech-heavy equity market.

Bob O'Donnell, chief analyst at TECHnalysis Research, said, “The blockbuster Anthropic launch is a critical step in reassuring the market about the potential for AI, but it’s really just the beginning.” He said, “We still need to see what happens with OpenAI, how the Nvidia situation evolves, how the political climate for data centres shapes up, and what the historical performance for Anthropic proves to be before we’ll get any real sense of stability.” Experts said the coming months could prove bumpy, with the AI industry facing a variety of challenges and global headwinds that could threaten it.

Jensen Huang, the former CEO of Nvidia, is due to appear before Congress next month to answer questions about his affair with a suspected Chinese spy, according to the report. Nvidia supplies advanced chips used in AI systems. The Guardian linked the affair with a suspected Chinese spy and Huang's departure to bipartisan national-security concerns.

Earlier this year, Anthropic's chief rival OpenAI postponed its initial offering until 2027. Experts cited by The Guardian said Anthropic's market reception and share-price movements will act as a signal to others in the industry over what appetite there is for another large AI IPO. Earlier this week, OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman said his company would go ahead with the IPO next year despite increased volatility. Speaking to US publication Semafor about demand for Anthropic's shares ahead of Thursday's stock-market debut, Altman said, “There’s been way too much investor demand and not enough supply.”
