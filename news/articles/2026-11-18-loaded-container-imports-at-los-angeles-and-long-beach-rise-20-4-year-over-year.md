## U.S. import prices rose 1.1% in October; Genesis AI raised $500 million

Total loaded container imports into the ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach were down 5.2% from September in October but up 20.4% from a year earlier, according to The Wall Street Journal. The figures were measured in 20-foot-equivalent units.

Overall U.S. import prices rose 1.1% in October from the month before, the third consecutive monthly increase and the same rate seen in September, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. The agency could not provide a year-over-year change because it did not collect survey data for October 2025 because of last fall’s government shutdown. Import prices exclude duties, such as tariffs imposed on imports by the Trump administration, as well as transportation costs.

Import fuel prices rose 3.6% in October, led by a 3.7% increase in petroleum import prices and a 5.1% climb in natural-gas prices. Prices for all nonfuel imports rose 0.8% last month, driven by capital goods, consumer goods, automotive vehicles and nonfuel industrial supplies, which offset declines for foods, feeds and beverages.

Robotics startup Genesis AI said it raised $500 million in Series A funding that values the barely two-year-old company at $3.5 billion, Steven Rosenbush of CIO Journal wrote. The report said capital was pouring into robotics, which many companies and investors viewed as the next major wave in the ongoing development of AI. Genesis AI was focused on building a general-purpose robot that pushes the limits of dexterity in settings from warehouses to laboratories to the home.

Genesis AI co-founder and CEO Zhou Xian said the fundraising would support expanded data acquisition and large-scale training, which he described as crucial to extending the range and complexity of robotic tasks. The report said newer techniques in the field scale that effort by digitizing the work of large groups of people instead of pairing individual workers and robots in a training process. Zhou said: “I think next year, sometime next year, we’ll have a robotics model that just works out of the box and generalizes across all different kinds of environments and tasks and skills.”
