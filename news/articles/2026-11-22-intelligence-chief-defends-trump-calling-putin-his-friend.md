## Clayton says he trusts Trump's language on Putin 'completely'

Clayton, the director of national intelligence, said Sunday that it was "entirely appropriate" for President Donald Trump to describe Vladimir Putin as his friend, defending remarks that Democrats have characterized as a betrayal of American values.

The defense came after Trump met with the Russian leader and Chinese President Xi Jinping for rare three-way talks in China last week. The summit appeared to be more symbolism than substance, but Trump and his top aides have described it as a historic gathering of the three countries with the largest nuclear arsenals.

Trump drew attention with a warm greeting of Putin and repeated references to him as his "friend." The language continued into the weekend: on Saturday the Republican president posted an image on Truth Social of himself shaking hands with a smiling Putin under the caption, "My Friend."

"In that circle — in that circle of that conversation, using the term 'friend' is entirely appropriate," Clayton said on ABC's "This Week."

On CNN's "State of the Union," Clayton described it as "historic" and "remarkable" for Trump to meet with the presidents of the other leading nuclear powers. "In that context, whatever language the president wants to use, I trust him completely," he said.

Like Trump, Clayton declined when pressed to characterize Putin as a friend of the United States, noting Russia's invasion of Ukraine, an American ally. "He is not America's friend in Ukraine. Not at all. Not at all," Clayton said on ABC.

Trump's fondness for Putin has been a defining characteristic of his two terms as president. He beamed when meeting the Russian leader last week. After returning to Washington, Trump made no effort in the White House briefing room on Friday to walk back the comments, and he noticeably sidestepped a specific question about whether he views Putin as a friend to the U.S.

"I can't speak to America," Trump said, then immediately shifted to his "very good" personal relationship with Putin. "I consider him to be somebody that I have a very good relationship (with)," Trump said. "It's actually very good."

Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer of New York criticized the summit as a "historic embarrassment" for the United States. "America would have been far better off if Trump just stayed home," Schumer said from the Senate floor.

Clayton's comments place the country's top intelligence official behind the president's choice of words about the Kremlin leader, even as he drew a line at describing Putin as a friend of the country. His remarks on the two Sunday programs marked the administration's most direct defense of the language Trump has used since the meeting.
