## Watson links credit-data integration to voter-roll increase

Mississippi Democratic lawmakers are calling on legislative oversight committees to investigate how credit-reporting data was used to examine voter rolls ahead of the November midterms. The demand followed an increase of nearly 60,000 in the number of people listed as inactive since the 2024 presidential election. More than 230,000 Mississippi voters had been marked inactive before this year's election, according to Secretary of State Michael Watson.

Watson said the increase was caused by integrating Experian data into Mississippi's overall voter-verification process. In a social media video, he said the state has started using data from Interactive Data, a new vendor that draws on information from all three major credit bureaus.

For years, Mississippi had relied primarily on Postal Service change-of-address data to maintain its voter rolls. In 2025, Republican Secretary of State Michael Watson announced a partnership with Experian, one of the country's three major credit reporting agencies, intended to “strengthen the integrity” of the state's elections. Under the agreement, county election officials received access to credit and Social Security data in addition to Postal Service data.

Scott Anderson, a spokesperson for Experian, said the company had not provided services to Mississippi since early 2025. He said, “Decisions regarding voter registration policies, procedures and record updates are made solely by election officials in accordance with applicable local, state and federal laws.”

Under Mississippi law, election officials must move voters whose addresses are questioned to inactive status at the same time they mail a confirmation card. Voters can use the card to reestablish active status. The MIT Election Data and Science Lab said policies in Delaware, Maryland, Oklahoma and Rhode Island generally allow voters time to respond before their status changes. Mississippi's process could leave voters unaware that they had been inactivated until they tried to vote, forcing them to cast provisional ballots.

Associated Press data showed 185,000 Mississippi voters listed as inactive during the 2022 midterm elections and 171,000 during the 2024 presidential election. Before this year's election, more than 230,000 people had been marked inactive after Experian data was added to the verification process. Elizabeth Jonson, a spokesperson for Watson's office, said the 230,000 figure was accumulated over more than five years of maintenance by local officials and that Mississippi does not classify voters as inactive solely because of Experian data. Watson attributed this year's increase to the integration of Experian data into the overall process.

The political stakes widened after Democratic candidate Scott Colom defeated Republican incumbent Cindy Hyde-Smith in the U.S. Senate race. Just over 8,000 votes separated the candidates with 99% of ballots counted, a margin smaller than the increase in inactive voter listings. Mississippi will hold elections next year for governor, other statewide offices, the Legislature and the state Supreme Court. Gov. Tate Reeves, who will be termed out of office, won his 2023 reelection bid by three percentage points.

“I don’t apologize for cleaning voter rolls,” Watson said in an interview. “That is my job.” In another social media video, Watson told voters that any “mistakes” made during voter-roll maintenance could be corrected by casting a provisional ballot, called an affidavit ballot in Mississippi. County election commissioners reviewed those ballots and approved or rejected them.

Jonson said 21,484 provisional ballots were cast in this year's midterms, nearly matching the 21,321 cast in 2024 despite higher turnout in that presidential election year. Watson's office said 75% of provisional ballots were approved statewide. Associated Press data showed the approval rate in majority-Black Hinds County, which includes Jackson, fell to 34% from 60% in 2024. It remains unclear whether the use of Experian data played a role in that decline.

State Rep. Cheikh Taylor, the chair of the Mississippi Democratic Party, said he intends to introduce legislation in the new year that would increase transparency in how credit data is used to verify voter eligibility. The legislation would also give voters whose records are flagged an opportunity to confirm their identities before being deactivated. Taylor said he had not seen evidence that Interactive Data would be substantially different from Experian's and said he hoped to win support from some Republican colleagues.

“This is not a partisan issue,” Taylor said. “The right to vote and the freedom to vote belongs to us all.” He said lawmakers should move quickly because Mississippi's elections are next year.

The Mississippi Legislative Black Caucus has scheduled a news conference Thursday to discuss its demand that legislative oversight committees investigate the use of credit-reporting data. Democratic state Rep. Kabir Karriem, the caucus chair, said, “We need answers to give to our constituents, because the question is still out there: Did my vote count?” He added, “It was disenfranchisement called by another name.”

Republican state Sen. Jeremy England suggested legislation increasing transparency in the use of credit data could draw Republican support. “I don’t think there was anything nefarious involved with it, but look, Mississippi also does have a dark past when it comes to elections,” England said. He said the state needed to ensure registered voters were not marked inactive for an unjustified reason.

Watson has defended the state's voter-roll maintenance work, while Democratic lawmakers are seeking a formal inquiry and new safeguards before next year's elections. The cause of the higher inactive count and the role of credit data in Hinds County's provisional-ballot approval rate remain disputed or unclear.
