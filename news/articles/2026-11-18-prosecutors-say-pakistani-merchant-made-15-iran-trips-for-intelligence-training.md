## US media report jury deliberated less than two hours in Merchant trial

US media reported that the jury deliberated for less than two hours before returning its March guilty verdict. Merchant was found guilty of attempting to commit an act of terrorism transcending national boundaries and murder for hire after trying to hire hitmen to kill Trump, former President Joe Biden and former US ambassador to the United Nations Nikki Haley. The people Merchant approached as hitmen were undercover FBI agents.

Prosecutors said Asif Merchant traveled to Iran 15 times, received training from Iranian intelligence officers and flew to the United States in April 2024 to carry out the plot to assassinate President Donald Trump and other officials. Merchant is a Pakistani man with ties to Iran's government. Prosecutors said Merchant initially traveled to Iran for religious pilgrimages and was then introduced to an Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps handler.

New York federal judge Eric Ross Komitee sentenced Merchant on Tuesday to 330 months, or 27 years and six months, in prison, months after the jury handed down its guilty verdict.

Prosecutors said Merchant paid $5,000 to the undercover hitmen as part of an initial payment. He was arrested in July 2024 in Texas as he was about to leave the United States.

In court, Merchant argued that he "had no other option" than to go along with the plot because his handler indicated that he knew who Merchant's Iranian relatives were and where they lived.

CBS, the BBC's US partner, reported that Merchant said he had anticipated being arrested before anyone was killed and intended to cooperate with the US government. Merchant said he had hoped that cooperation would help him obtain a green card.

In court, Merchant said he was acting under the direction of Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) to avenge the 2020 US assassination of Qasem Soleimani, Iran's topmost military commander.

Although other US officials were potential targets, the assassination plot was largely focused on Trump because he was president at the time Soleimani was killed. Trump has spoken publicly about ordering the US airstrike that killed the Iranian general while he was travelling in Iraq. In response to the killing, Iran's late Supreme Leader Ayatollah Ali Khamenei said, "severe revenge awaits the criminals" behind the attack.

"An Iranian agent who came to the United States to coordinate an assassination attempt against President Trump has been justly sentenced for his criminal conduct," Attorney General Todd Blanche said in a statement. "The Department of Justice will not tolerate foreign terrorist plots against our leaders and will not stop until those responsible are held accountable."
