## White House says no credible threat as Iran rejects allegation

On November 17, President Donald Trump, speaking to reporters in Manila, Philippines, while attending the Asean Summit, said the United States was taking the potential Iranian plot very seriously. When a reporter asked about the CNN report, Trump nodded and said: "We're looking into it."

A CNN report citing unnamed U.S. intelligence officials said Iran could target senior figures in Trump's administration with an attack involving a so-called dirty bomb in Washington DC. BBC News said it had not independently verified CNN's reporting.

A dirty bomb would contain radioactive material, such as uranium, which would be scattered through the air when a conventional explosive detonated. BBC News said concerns about the devices grew after the 9/11 attacks in 2001, although no such attack had ever occurred.

Asked whether there was reason to believe an attack was imminent, Trump said, "I hope it's not," but that the United States took the threat seriously "because they [the Iranians] happen to be crazy."

In a statement before Trump's comments, White House spokesperson Lauren Bis said: "We remain vigilant monitoring any and all potential threats, but at this time there are no credible threats to the homeland."

Iranian Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei rebutted the CNN article and other U.S. reports. In a post on X, Baghaei wrote: "Those who initiated the aggression against Iran are now seeking to lay the media groundwork for their sinister designs by manufacturing and disseminating baseless allegations."

Mick Mulroy, a former CIA paramilitary officer and deputy assistant defence secretary, told the BBC: "A dirty bomb is not difficult to make or deploy." He added: "Although it is not a weapon of mass destruction, it can be a weapon of mass disruption."

Imran Bayoumi, a geostrategy expert with the Atlantic Council in Washington DC and a former policy adviser to the defence department, said it remained "unclear how serious" a supposed Iranian dirty bomb would be. He said: "But Iran has planned and threatened to attack the US in the past," and added: "It is also unclear why this report is becoming public now."

The report came as intelligence officials and members of the Trump administration sought congressional renewal of a key national security law. Section 702 of the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act, which allows the US to spy on foreign nationals, expires next March.
