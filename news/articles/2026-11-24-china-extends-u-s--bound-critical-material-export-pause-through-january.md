## Shenzhen talks precede the renewed pause as chip controls strain ties

China’s Commerce Ministry said Tuesday that it would continue to halt the export ban on dual-use items related to gallium, germanium, antimony and superhard materials to the United States. The announcement sets that suspension from Nov. 28, 2026, through Jan. 27, 2027, extending a pause that had been due to expire Friday.

The ministry said it would also continue easing stricter reviews on graphite exports to the United States during the same period. The graphite provision is separate from the listed dual-use materials. Together, the measures keep the material restrictions paused and the eased graphite reviews in force through the new expiration date.

China first suspended these restrictions last November as part of a trade truce reached between China and the United States a year ago. The extension changes the duration of that suspension, not the decision to keep the restrictions from taking effect during the announced window.

In recent weeks, Beijing and Washington have acted to extend previous agreements, with both sides announcing continued pauses on tariffs and trade curbs. The critical-materials decision extends that pattern while keeping the existing suspension and eased review procedures in place.

The announcement followed last week’s Shenzhen summit between President Trump and Chinese leader Xi Jinping. The meeting took place in the technology hub as the two countries continued to extend pieces of their trade truce.

It also came less than a day after President Trump helped secure the reinstatement of Nvidia Chief Executive Jensen Huang, according to The Wall Street Journal. Huang has been a vocal critic of strict chip export controls.

Washington’s controls on advanced AI chips to China continue to strain the bilateral relationship, which The Wall Street Journal said remains dogged by a scandal centered on Huang. The broader trade truce remains widely viewed as fragile despite the renewed material-export suspension.

China’s latest decision changes the period during which the government will pause its restrictions on gallium, germanium, antimony and superhard materials and ease graphite reviews. It does not change the separate U.S. controls on advanced AI chips to China, which remain in place.

China’s move therefore pairs an extended break in selected export restrictions with continued restrictions on advanced AI-chip shipments to China. The two governments have continued to pause tariffs and trade curbs, but The Wall Street Journal said the latest material decision did not remove the broader strain.

For the announced Nov. 28-to-Jan. 27 period, the Chinese material-export rules will remain suspended and graphite reviews will remain eased. The separate U.S. chip controls remain active, and the broader trade truce remains widely viewed as fragile.
