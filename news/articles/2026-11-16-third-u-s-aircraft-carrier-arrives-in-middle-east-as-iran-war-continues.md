## Third U.S. aircraft carrier arrives in Middle East as Iran war continues

- A third U.S. aircraft carrier, the USS Theodore Roosevelt, has arrived in the Middle East, a U.S. official said Monday, joining the USS George H.W. Bush and USS George Washington in enforcing a naval blockade of Iranian shipping.

## Navy flotilla in Arabian Sea now numbers three carriers and 13 destroyers

## Third carrier joins blockade force in Arabian Sea

The U.S. official who confirmed the Roosevelt's arrival spoke on condition of anonymity to discuss sensitive military operations. The carrier and its accompanying strike force left San Diego in September, bringing thousands of additional troops to the Middle East.

The Roosevelt joins the Bush and the Washington, along with smaller Navy ships, in a force assembled to maintain a naval blockade of Iranian shipping. The Bush arrived in the region in April and the Washington in August. The Navy previously had three carrier strike groups in the Middle East in April, for the first time since 2003.

According to the Navy, the flotilla in the Arabian Sea now includes 13 destroyers, two cruisers and a littoral combat ship. Two more destroyers are in the Red Sea.

The Roosevelt deployed alongside the USS Makin Island amphibious readiness group, which includes the USS John P. Murtha and USS Anchorage amphibious landing ships and around 2,000 Marines. Those ships have yet to make it to the region.

## Midterm losses sharpen the politics of the war

The third carrier arrived nearly two weeks after midterm elections that saw voters deliver major losses to Trump's Republican Party, driven in part by the unpopularity of the Iran war and its economic effects. The results led Vice President JD Vance to publicly break with Trump over the conflict, posting on social media that "the reason we lost is the war."

Trump has insisted the war was the right thing to do and has not ruled out restarting the conflict, which has been locked in a stalemate for months. Last week, Trump warned Iran that the U.S. military is "locked and loaded" and that the U.S. was keeping an eye on Pickaxe Mountain, a facility linked to Iran's nuclear program, for signs of activity.

"If something does not work out, I would not hesitate" to act, Trump told conservative broadcaster Hugh Hewitt on Thursday.

Yet Trump also told reporters last week that he believed it was highly unlikely he would need to order new major strikes on Iran, declaring that the war "militarily is effectively over" after meeting with Qatar's prime minister. Trump also declared that the U.S. no longer needs a deal with Tehran and told Vance — who was a chief negotiator in earlier ceasefire talks — instead to focus on the administration's domestic anti-fraud campaign targeting Democratic-controlled cities and states.

## Talks stalled over nuclear program and blockade

Negotiations have stalled due to differences over Iran's nuclear program, an issue that Trump insists be part of any deal meant to end the war. Iran wants the U.S. first to agree to several core conditions, including lifting the naval blockade that is stopping its oil from getting to world markets.

## Shipping attacks continue in the Strait of Hormuz

A focal point of the simmering conflict remains the Strait of Hormuz, a critical waterway where a fifth of the world's oil passed before the war. Commercial ships are still coming under attack or facing other risks, while the U.S. has carried out limited strikes on Iranian targets near the strait and is helping guide vessels through the contested waters.

Iranian state TV, quoting the naval arm of Iran's Revolutionary Guard and the army, warned shipping companies Monday that a U.S.-supported route through the waterway is dangerous because of mines. Mines have damaged six oil tankers since Sunday, polluting the strait's coastlines, according to the Revolutionary Guard's navy.

Separately, a projectile struck a tanker trying to transit through the strait, starting an engine room fire, but no casualties were reported, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Organization said.

U.S. Central Command, which oversees military operations in the Middle East, said it has redirected 150 merchant vessels and disabled five since June under the blockade. Independent assessments from Lloyd's List, a maritime tracking and analysis firm, show that traffic through the strait remains at less than a third of prewar levels.
