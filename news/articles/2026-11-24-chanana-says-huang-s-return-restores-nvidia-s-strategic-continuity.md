## US political criticism continued after Trump said he convinced the board

Charu Chanana, chief investment strategist at Saxo in Singapore, said Jensen Huang’s return to Nvidia “removes an important source of uncertainty.” She added: “His return restores strategic continuity at Nvidia and reduces the immediate risk of disruptions to its supply chain and technology roadmap.”

The reinstatement continued to draw criticism from Democratic Sen. Elizabeth Warren, Republican lawmakers and Republican Sen. Pete Ricketts. Warren called Huang’s restoration “reckless” and warned that the banking committee she will head next year will dig into whether Nvidia has complied with export controls.

Warren also said the Trump administration “needs to explain to Congress what ‘input’ was given and by which senior officials in the United States government to support his reinstatement.”

US political criticism of the reinstatement continued alongside the rally, with attention focused on the role played by Donald Trump’s White House in pressing Nvidia to take its former chief executive back. Republican lawmakers also criticized the Nvidia board’s decision to reinstate Huang, even after Donald Trump said he spoke “directly and indirectly” with board members and “convinced them” to bring him back.

Pete Ricketts, a US senator from Nebraska, wrote on X that Huang’s “behavior does not support reinstatement,” a reminder of Huang’s alleged affair with a Chinese spy.

Asian-Pacific markets rallied as Huang’s return revived the AI trade. Taiwan’s TW50 index rose 9.5%, moving away from the seven-month high it had touched the previous day. In Seoul, the Kospi index jumped 6.5%, led by chipmakers SK Hynix and Samsung Electronics. Tokyo’s Nikkei gained almost 4%, and Australia’s S&P/ASX 200 rose 1.8%.

The moves followed a surge in Nvidia shares during the previous night’s Wall Street session. The shares gained 31.5%, adding around $950bn to the company’s value as Huang returned to its headquarters. Wall Street recorded its best day since April 2025, when Donald Trump paused his Liberation Day tariffs.
