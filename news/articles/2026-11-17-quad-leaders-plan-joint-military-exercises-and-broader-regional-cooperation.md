## A 2027 Quad business forum will seek economic growth across the Indo-Pacific

U.S. President Donald Trump and Australian Prime Minister Anthony Albanese met Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Japanese Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi in Manila late Tuesday and heralded plans for new joint Quad military exercises, The Guardian reported. The leaders discussed enhanced cooperation on AI, critical minerals, infrastructure and maritime security. The Guardian said the planned exercises would involve maritime patrol aircraft and were designed to push back on China’s expansionist ambitions. Trump and Albanese promised the grouping with India and Japan would guarantee “peace through strength.”

The meeting was the first gathering of Quad leaders since 2024. The Guardian reported that the Quad was established as China seeks to build its military might and exert influence across the region, and the leaders said they would cooperate with Beijing to maintain peace and stability. The Guardian also reported that the Quad leaders wanted the group to present itself as a multilateral coalition resisting Beijing and seeking to undermine its dominance, including in the South and East China seas. The Guardian said the plan for exercises involving maritime patrol aircraft was likely to prompt strong criticism from China.

Trump said, “These nations represent some of the most important partnerships in the entire world.” He added, “They’re among the most successful nations in the world, and in certain ways among the most powerful nations of the world.” Trump said, “Our countries are the four corners of a free, open, stable, and prosperous Indo-Pacific.”

Albanese said, “When we work together for our common view in the Indo-Pacific of peace, security, stability, and prosperity, then the world will be a safer and more secure place, and the Quad has a critical role in achieving that.” He praised Trump and said a new Quad business forum planned for 2027 would build economic growth across the region.

Takaichi said the Quad would help shape the future of the region. She said, “We would like to realise a free and open Indo Pacific by strengthening resilience of countries in the region and promote their freedom to decide for themselves across a wide range of areas including maritime security, economic security, energy and critical and emerging technologies.”

As the four leaders posed for a group photograph before the meeting, Trump praised Modi and Takaichi as “tough,” then gestured to Albanese and said, “We’re OK too.” Albanese laughed at the remark. It was Albanese’s first meeting with Takaichi since a weeks-long saga over comments he made on a podcast about melons presented to him during a visit to Australia in July.

The planned exercises would build on the recently completed Malabar joint exercise near Japan, where sailors completed anti-submarine and anti-surface warfare drills.

The Manila meeting came as Albanese and Trump prepared for separate engagements with Chinese President Xi Jinping later in the week. Albanese was due to visit Xi in Shenzhen on Friday, a day after three-way talks between Trump, Xi and Russia’s Vladimir Putin. Trump said he wanted talks with North Korean leader Kim Jong-un but conceded that he was not the host of the China summit and that an invitation would have to come from Xi. Trump also said he could meet Putin one-on-one “if it’s available.”

Trump said the United States was poised to catch up to China on rare earth production within 10 months, citing progress in Australia, Japan and India. He also discussed the Aukus nuclear deal with Australia, saying, “We make the greatest submarines anywhere in the world, and we’re working with Australia and others on that.”

Trump was accompanied by Cabinet members Marco Rubio, Scott Bessent, Howard Lutnick and Jamieson Greer, along with his new chief of staff, Stephen Miller. The Guardian reported that officials privately believed the meeting alone showed the group was progressing. Australian officials welcomed Trump’s engagement in regional affairs as he entered the final two years of his presidency. Trump took credit for reinvigorating the grouping and said he placed a premium on relations with Albanese, Modi and Takaichi.

Trump said: “When they have difficulty, when they want to tell me something, my phone is always open, and the Oval Office is always open to you, and you know that.”

Before the meeting, Albanese said he would push Trump to end the war in Iran. The Guardian reported that the conflict had produced an unprecedented global energy shock and badly distorted conditions in the Australian economy.

Earlier, at the East Asia Summit, Albanese called for regional partners to work together to boost AI protections. Trump, who calls the technology “super intelligence,” said it represented “the greatest opportunity for economic growth since the Industrial Revolution.”
