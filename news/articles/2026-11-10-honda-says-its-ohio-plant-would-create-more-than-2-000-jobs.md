## Honda estimates new plant would boost capacity by around 240,000 vehicles a year

Honda says its $3 billion Ohio plant would create more than 2,000 jobs and boost the automaker's production capacity by around 240,000 vehicles a year, according to The Wall Street Journal. The hybrid-vehicle facility is to be located near Honda's existing and extensive Ohio manufacturing operations and is expected to be operational in 2029.

The Wall Street Journal's Owen Tucker-Smith described the announcement as part of Honda's attempt to pivot away from EVs. The Japanese automaker had invested heavily in EVs and suffered major losses when it pulled the plug this year. Honda canceled several EV-related projects and wound up with its first annual loss as a listed company.

At the same time, Honda is racing to develop hybrid models in an attempt to meet surging demand for the vehicles. It is also retooling other plants to support ramped-up hybrid production.

Mark R. Long, editor of The Wall Street Journal's Logistics Report, placed the Honda manufacturing announcement alongside separate briefs on freight conditions, modular data centers and oil storage. The items shared a focus on physical capacity—hybrid-vehicle output for Honda, modular data-center units for Mavria and storage space for crude oil—alongside a separate snapshot of freight conditions.

**Freight conditions.** U.S. containerized imports, measured in 20-foot-equivalent units, rose 3.4% in September from August and were 12.6% higher than a year earlier, according to the Global Port Tracker from the National Retail Federation and Hackett Associates.

Avery Vise, FTR's vice president of trucking, said, “Multiple signs point to a truck freight market that probably will be considerably stronger over the next few quarters than we had previously forecast.”

**Oil reserves and storage.** A separate brief examined efforts to replenish depleted oil reserves and build storage facilities to withstand the next supply shock. Georgi Kantchev and Summer Said reported that the Iran war drained global crude stockpiles to their lowest levels in years.

In August, government-held oil stocks in the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development were at their lowest since 1989. The U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve is now around its lowest since 1982, according to the Energy Information Administration.

Persian Gulf producers now seek storage closer to buyers, and governments are eager to expand oil reserves to withstand future disruption. Last month, Saudi Aramco reached a preliminary agreement to store crude in Singapore's Jurong Rock Caverns, which can hold 9 million barrels of oil. The United Arab Emirates' Adnoc is exploring storage arrangements in India totaling as much as 30 million barrels.

In Australia, the government has pledged over $2.1 billion to stockpile a billion liters of diesel and jet fuel. Japan has set up a $10 billion regional financing framework supporting oil stockpiles and storage across Asia.

**Modular data centers.** The report's data-center item focused on Mavria, a Texas startup founded over the summer by three veterans of President Trump's Energy Department. Jennifer Hiller reported that Adam Blake, Nick Poradek and Seth Cohen met while focusing on nuclear power at the Energy Department. The startup has raised $130 million at a $1 billion valuation.

Mavria is aiming to address some physical bottlenecks of the AI build-out with a standard design for modular data centers that can eventually be shipped by truck. Its product, called Terrain, is a modular 5-megawatt data-center system that includes power distribution, direct liquid cooling, controls and server racks. Units could be added like Lego blocks to a larger location.

The first Terrain unit is under construction at a Houston factory. Mavria, which has 35 employees, plans to begin testing in early 2027 and scale up production by midyear.
