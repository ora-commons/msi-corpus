## Labor unions and pro-voting groups saw related Justice Department suit dismissed

The 9th Circuit Court of Appeals on Monday approved the Justice Department's request to dismiss its own lawsuit, ending the federal case over roughly 20,000 contested California ballots. The case had sought to stop California from counting the ballots until officials could verify the immigration statuses of recipients whom federal prosecutors alleged were noncitizens.

The decision marked the official end to a flurry of litigation that unfolded in California immediately before the competitive and high-stakes November midterms. Federal prosecutors previously said the effort would become moot after Election Day because local officials would no longer be able to prevent the ballots from being counted.

The disputed ballots were at the heart of a protracted legal battle between the Trump administration and California over voter eligibility in the heavily Democratic state.

The case also came amid broader federal scrutiny of local elections nationally. Justice Department prosecutors alleged that the recipients of the contested ballots were noncitizens.

The Associated Press reported that the Trump administration issued a raft of subpoenas in Alaska in what the Associated Press described as a spoiler candidate controversy. The administration moved to drop those efforts two days after former Rep. Mary Peltola won an outright majority in the race to become Democrats' 56th senator, as part of what the Associated Press described as a Democratic midterm blowout.

The requested remedy was to stop California from counting roughly 20,000 ballots until state officials verified the recipients' immigration statuses.

Before the litigation ended, the federal government threatened to charge California state election workers who counted the contested ballots. The federal government also issued criminal grand jury subpoenas to clerks in Los Angeles and Orange counties.

Labor unions and pro-voting groups challenged the federal efforts in a related lawsuit against the Justice Department. The unions and voting groups said ballot processing was already underway when the subpoenas were issued. Their lawsuit argued that the Justice Department's efforts risked interfering with the lawful administration of state and federal election law.

That related suit was dismissed this month. The 9th Circuit's action also brought the federal case over the contested ballots to a formal close, ending the flurry of California election litigation surrounding the November midterms.
