## Newsom called clemency among the hardest decisions of his career

The Associated Press reported that California's Supreme Court must approve a majority of Gov. Gavin Newsom's commutations of death sentences for more than 500 state inmates to life without parole. President Donald Trump is scheduled to deliver a Monday Justice Department speech condemning Newsom's clemency decision for every state inmate facing death. It would be Trump's second address to the department in two years.

The White House said the speech will turn on protecting American families and Newsom's decision to commute the death sentences of more than 500 inmates to life without parole. The Associated Press described the Justice Department as having taken aggressive steps to fulfill the White House agenda and promote criminal cases against Trump's adversaries.

Newsom, a Democrat who regularly exchanges criticism with Trump and is considering a 2028 presidential run, said the decision was among the hardest of his career because it would bring up painful memories among the families of the victims of those inmates. The California Supreme Court must approve a majority of the commutations.

It remains unclear how far the speech will go beyond the clemency dispute. Trump could again promote legal proceedings against his political adversaries or renew his longstanding complaints about a Justice Department that repeatedly investigated him during the past decade.

The timing follows pronounced Republican defeats in this month's legislative elections, which gave Democrats control of Congress and opened the possibility of House and Senate investigations. Potential subjects range from the mass firing of agents and prosecutors who investigated Trump to a series of Justice Department investigations that the Associated Press described as politically charged and that grand juries and magistrates rejected.

A large banner bearing Trump's face hangs from the facade of the Justice Department's headquarters. The Associated Press described the display as embodying his influence over the department, while critics said it demonstrates the erosion of the institution's historical independence.

Trump will be accompanied by Attorney General Todd Blanche, his former personal lawyer, who has said the president has the right to impose his will on the Justice Department. Blanche, a former deputy attorney general, was promoted in April to replace Pam Bondi. The Associated Press reported that Trump fired Bondi in April after she struggled to meet his demands and withstood bipartisan criticism over her handling of files in the Jeffrey Epstein case.

Presidential speeches at the Justice Department are rare, though not unprecedented. Trump last visited the building in March 2025, when he delivered an address intended to garner support for administration policies on violent crime, drugs and illegal immigration.

He used that appearance to enumerate grievances about the criminal cases he faced during the Joe Biden administration, when he was accused of storing classified documents at his Mar-a-Lago estate in Florida and of conspiring to overturn his 2020 electoral defeat.

Trump promised reprisals for what he described as lies and abuses that had occurred inside those walls, saying, “mentiras y abusos que han ocurrido dentro de estos muros.” He also promised to expose what he called the “delitos atroces y el grave comportamiento indebido” of his adversaries.

Since then, the Associated Press reported, the Justice Department accelerated efforts to investigate Trump's adversaries, but encountered cases dismissed by judges or rejected by grand juries that declined to issue formal charges.

What remains unsettled is whether the dispute over California's commutations will widen into a demand for federal prosecutions or whether the speech will renew Trump's complaints about the department's handling of cases against him. The White House has framed the address around the more than 500 commutations and protecting American families.
