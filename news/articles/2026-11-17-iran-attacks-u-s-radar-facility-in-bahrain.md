## Economic pressure and stalled negotiations raise escalation risks, analysts say

Iran fired drones on Tuesday at a U.S. radar facility in Bahrain, its third attack in recent days on American military targets in the Persian Gulf kingdom after a roughly two-month lull in fighting. The strike came as the U.S.-enforced blockade of Iran was constricting the country's oil exports and as negotiations to end the eight-month war remained stalled.

Bahrain issued an alert before the incoming fire and said its air-defense systems had successfully intercepted the "treacherous Iranian aerial attacks." The U.S. military said no U.S. equipment was damaged and no American personnel were injured.

Iran's previous attacks came Friday against a U.S. early-warning radar facility and Sunday against a radar system at an American helicopter base in Bahrain. Despite claims of success, there appeared to be little or no damage from those earlier attacks, the Associated Press reported.

Iran's attacks followed U.S. strikes on Iranian communications towers and ongoing drone surveillance operations, and Iran said it was retaliating. Bahrain hosts the U.S. Navy's 5th Fleet, which has sustained damage in previous Iranian attacks since the United States and Israel attacked Iran on Feb. 28.

The wider conflict has extended to the Strait of Hormuz, a crucial waterway for world energy supplies where Iran and its proxies have disrupted shipping since the war began Feb. 28. Iran has operated under a naval blockade enforced by the United States for months in and around the strait, curbing the flow of Iranian crude to the global market and damaging Iran's economy. Oil tankers moving through the strait under U.S. military protection have helped return Gulf oil shipments to roughly prewar levels.

President Donald Trump has been circumspect about the next steps in the war, now in its eighth month despite his initial promise that it would last only a matter of weeks. He has said new strikes are highly unlikely, but he also sent a third aircraft carrier and thousands more troops to the Middle East and told Vice President JD Vance that talks are not needed at this time.

The arrival of the USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier strike group has brought combined U.S. naval assets in the Persian Gulf, Arabian Sea, Red Sea and Mediterranean above the force deployed when the United States and Israel launched the war. The Roosevelt joins the USS George H.W. Bush and the USS George Washington aircraft carriers, along with smaller U.S. Navy ships maintaining the blockade of Iranian shipping. A new analysis by Israel's Institute for National Security Studies said the presence provides greater offensive firepower and missile-interception capacity. U.S. Central Command would not comment on whether it planned to keep all three carrier strike groups in the region.

Mostafa Najafi, a Tehran-based security analyst, told the Associated Press that Iran's attacks could signal a shift in strategy after months during which Iran waited for an attack and then retaliated to target the operational capability of bases. Hamidreza Azizi, a senior Iran analyst at the International Crisis Group, wrote in an analysis posted to X that Iranian leaders would be weighing whether "breaking the blockade through escalation is less costly than accepting its continuation."

"In other words, the risk of renewed war no longer depends solely on whether Trump decides to attack Iran again," Azizi said. "It also depends on whether Tehran concludes that the costs of continued restraint have become greater than the risks of escalation."

Sascha Bruchmann, a Bahrain-based defense analyst with the International Institute for Strategic Studies, said the attacks were a sign that Tehran was growing impatient as its economic woes mount. "Shooting at ships causes fear, but is not effective enough to change the strategic equation," Bruchmann told the Associated Press. "Tehran knows it needs to do something, but I wouldn't overestimate what we saw against Bahrain this week. This is not it — yet."

In a statement, Iran's Revolutionary Guard Corps said, "Continued aggression against the country's airspace, as well as the use of regional military bases and facilities for hostile acts against Iran, will not go unanswered."

Egypt, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the United Arab Emirates each condemned the attack, calling it a violation of Bahrain's sovereignty and a threat to its security and stability. Negotiations to end the war have stagnated despite intensified mediation efforts from Qatar.

Qatar said Iran's "continued attacks" on Bahrain "undermine diplomatic efforts." Qatar Foreign Ministry spokesman Majed Al Ansari, speaking at a news briefing in Doha, warned that any escalation in fighting posed an "extremely grave danger" to the prospects of reaching an agreement.

Iranian officials have termed the current situation "no war, no peace" and vowed to end it. Tehran has said it is open to a negotiated deal but has signaled a readiness to escalate the conflict in the face of tightening sanctions and a blockade that are throttling Iran's economy.

Mahdi Mohammadi, an adviser to Iran's influential parliamentary chief, posted on social media on Monday that leaders viewed U.S. economic pressure as an effort to create a domestic "security crisis."

Iran has also continued to attack ships seeking to transit the Strait of Hormuz. On Tuesday, the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations said a tanker was reportedly attacked twice north of Oman and that the second strike caused a fire on board. The organization also received a report that a cargo vessel off the coast of Yemen repelled an attempt by armed men in a skiff to board it.

Iran's Fars news agency reported that two ships were struck by mines in the strait, causing them to catch fire. It was not immediately clear whether one was the incident reported by the United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations.

In Yemen, Iran-backed Houthi rebels have solidified their control of the Bab el-Mandeb Strait, another critical shipping route connecting the Gulf of Aden to the Red Sea. A group allied with the Saudi-backed Yemeni government retreated over the weekend, representing a setback for the anti-Houthi coalition.
