## Up to 100 federally activated soldiers support public safety and outreach

On Tuesday, Iowa Gov. Kim Reynolds issued a proclamation of disaster emergency for Des Moines, Lee and Van Buren counties. With Reynolds's approval, the Iowa National Guard is providing up to 100 federally activated soldiers to support public safety and community outreach efforts around the detonation at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown. The Army plans to dispose of the nation's entire 9,700-pound (4,400-kilogram) stockpile of bomb-making lead azide in the southeastern Iowa town of about 400 residents. The explosion risks releasing plumes of toxic chemicals across a 100-mile (160-kilometer) radius.

Reynolds said, “Iowa agencies are actively engaged alongside our federal and local partners, and we remain focused on ensuring communities have the information and resources they need before, during, and after the operation.”

Senator-elect Josh Turek said he had been briefed on the detonation. He said the chemicals “should never have been allowed to degrade to this point” and said, “I will continue asking questions about how we got here. There is no excuse for such a failure of transparency and communication, especially when the health and safety of the public is involved.”

In a social media post, Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said, “FEMA will ensure every resident who needs to temporarily evacuate will receive the support they need, including transportation, lodging, meals and other essential assistance.” FEMA Administrator Cameron Hamilton said in a statement, “We’ve made it very clear to those who are within a southwesterly location: if they choose to relocate, we will offer services.”

The Army's operation remained scheduled for 10 a.m. ET Wednesday. New maps released Tuesday forecast a path that would move southwest of Middletown before extending into northern Missouri. The Army said the exact landing area for toxins could change as the weather forecast changes.

The Army warned: “All people, but especially infants, children, pregnant women, adults and children with chronic diseases such as heart disease, kidney disease, or dementia, should avoid outdoor activities within affected areas until further guidance is provided.”

In a Tuesday public-safety advisory, the Army said: “A more precise time will be communicated after evaluating changing weather patterns and waiting for the safest possible conditions.”

According to the Army, the Federal Emergency Management Agency was supporting “relocation for all residents of Middletown, Iowa, and immediate surrounding impacted areas” out of an “abundance of caution.” An emergency notification sent to cellphones on Tuesday said some people living within five miles southwest of the plant might be evacuated. It advised people up to 20 miles away to remain indoors with windows closed to “avoid airborne lead dust, noise and vibration.” The Army encouraged residents outside the recommended evacuation zone to shelter in place.

At Tuesday's press conference, officials said they could not find a “viable alternative” after the material was discovered to be dangerously unstable earlier this year. Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle said, “There is consensus across experts across the country that a controlled, mitigated detonation of the lead azide here at Iowa Army Ammunition Plant is far preferable to an uncontrolled, spontaneous detonation of that substance.”

The operation was initially planned for last Friday but was rescheduled three times as public concern about potential health and environmental effects grew and environmentalists sued to block the detonation. Donald Trump ordered the military to hold off pending a court ruling and sign-off from Democratic governor-elect Rob Sand, who does not take office until January.

On Monday, a three-judge panel of the Eighth Circuit Court of Appeals said the Army could proceed, finding that the Environmental Protection Agency had adequately incorporated safeguards in an emergency permit to minimize the dispersal of dust. The Army continued with the Wednesday schedule after the ruling.
