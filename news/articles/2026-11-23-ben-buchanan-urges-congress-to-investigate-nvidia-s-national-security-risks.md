## Bipartisan bills seek tighter China chip export controls

In an interview on NPR's All Things Considered published Nov. 23, Ben Buchanan, a former special adviser for artificial intelligence in the Biden administration and a Johns Hopkins University professor, called for a full congressional investigation into the national security stakes of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang's return after his recent resignation. Buchanan said Congress should determine whether chips had been smuggled and whether China was accessing them remotely through data centers in Southeast Asia. He called those questions the crux of the national security matter. NPR host Scott Detrow noted that Buchanan's new book on the subject is called "The Bitter Struggle."

Nvidia's board said it "received input from senior levels of the United States government" and was "confident that Mr. Huang's reinstatement poses no national security risk." President Trump said the reference amounted to an acknowledgment that he had provided input to the board. The statement and Trump's response placed direct government involvement at the center of Huang's reinstatement.

Buchanan said he was surprised by the White House's direct involvement. He recalled serving in the White House during Sam Altman's firing and reinstatement, when the administration's posture was that the White House did not want roles in selecting executives of American companies. He described the current involvement as a departure from past norms.

Buchanan said Nvidia and its chips were "absolutely incredibly important." He argued that the technology's significance should reinforce the need to keep chips from going to China, rather than reinforce the importance of any one particular person.

NPR host Scott Detrow asked Buchanan about an alleged relationship involving a Chinese espionage asset. Buchanan said it was difficult to know from his vantage point what was true and who knew what. He said a lot of reporting appeared credible that the woman at the center of the allegations had been in the middle of chip smuggling and perhaps had a relationship or ongoing conversations with China's Ministry of State Security. Buchanan said the reported chip-smuggling activity was enough reason for serious concern and described possible intelligence links as secondary.

As he asked how policy had changed from the Biden administration to the Trump administration on access to the chips in other countries, particularly China, NPR host Scott Detrow noted that "there's been a lot of reporting that Huang and Nvidia have had a lot of influence on President Trump." Buchanan said the Biden administration entered office in 2021 convinced that AI would become very powerful and matter for national security, with Nvidia chips driving the increase in power. In 2022 and each year afterward, the administration implemented and tightened export controls that denied China access, cut smuggling routes and restricted access through remote data centers in places such as Singapore.

Buchanan said President Trump, who came into office in 2025, initially talked and acted tough on China, but the administration walked back or declined to enforce a number of export-control policies during the late spring and summer of 2025 and into the fall. Buchanan said that "as this scandal broke" last week, Trump put some rules back in place. He said those rules, so far as he could tell, remained on the books and brought policy closer to the Biden administration's approach.

Buchanan said the effect of Huang's return with Trump's full support would depend on what Congress does. He said that because Trump had walked back the policy during his first two years in office, Congress had mounted a bipartisan move to tighten export controls once more through legislation and deprive the administration of the opportunity to change the policies back and forth. Buchanan said the move included what he called "some very MAGA senators," naming Pete Ricketts, Jim Banks and Tom Cotton. He called the MATCH Act and OVERWATCH Act "the two big ones." Buchanan said he thought their bipartisan support gave them a good chance of passing this Congress. He said some of the leaders of those bills had come out "today after this news" and still had a lot of questions for Jensen Huang; he added that those leaders seemed committed to implementing the policy fully.
