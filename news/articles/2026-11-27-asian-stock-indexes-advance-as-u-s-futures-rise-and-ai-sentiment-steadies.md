## Oil prices diverge after attacks in Kuwait and Saudi Arabia

Most Asian stock markets rose Friday, tracking advances in U.S. stock futures as sentiment around artificial intelligence stabilized. The regional move was broad but not universal: Taiwan's Taiex fell while indexes in South Korea, Japan, China and Hong Kong advanced.

Westpac's Neha Sharma said in an email, “U.S. markets were closed for Thanksgiving, leaving global markets to trade largely off the late recovery on Wall Street in Wednesday's session.” Asian markets also took cues from stronger U.S. stock futures.

E-mini Nasdaq futures rose 1.3%. Contracts tied to the S&P 500 and Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 1.0% and 0.7%, respectively.

South Korea's Kospi rose 1.65%, and Japan's Nikkei Stock Average gained 0.7%. China's Shanghai Composite Index added 0.2%, while Hong Kong's Hang Seng Index was also 0.2% higher. Taiwan's Taiex bucked the trend and dropped 0.3%.

Friday's gains came despite Nvidia ordering manufacturing partners to pause shipments to four Southeast Asian countries. The Wall Street Journal reported that the order followed the United States' escalation of its push to keep Nvidia's AI chips out of China's hands.

Oil prices were mixed after an overnight rally. Iran had stepped up strikes targeting some Gulf neighbors and ships trying to move oil and natural gas out through the Strait of Hormuz. Most recently, an Iranian drone attack on Kuwait killed two Italians performing maintenance on antidrone equipment, authorities said.

Yemen's Houthi militant group also attacked Saudi Arabia's Jazan oil refinery on Thursday and killed one worker, the kingdom's Ministry of Energy said in a statement.

ANZ Research analysts said in a note that the attacks offset hopes of a diplomatic resolution to the conflict. Separately, U.S. commercial crude-oil inventories rose for a third consecutive week in the seven days through Nov. 20. Gasoline and distillate stockpiles also increased, according to the latest data from the U.S. Energy Information Administration.

ICE data showed front-month West Texas Intermediate crude-oil futures rising 2.1% to $85.41 a barrel, while front-month Brent fell 0.4% to $96.20 a barrel.
