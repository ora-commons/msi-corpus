## Judge detains Paporakis ahead of Thursday morning arraignment

Police said a grand jury indicted Paporakis, 19, of Palatine, Illinois, on Nov. 5 on six counts of terrorism, two counts of armed violence and two counts of attempted terrorism, all felonies. The jury also added nine more counts of making a terrorist threat. The indictment also charged him with possession of a firearm without a serial number and with not having a police-issued identification card.

The case stems from allegations that Paporakis planned a mass shooting at a Hindu temple in Aurora, Illinois, on Oct. 11, the first day of the Hindu holiday of Navaratri. Paporakis' co-workers at a Taco Bell reported to authorities that he told them he planned to “kill Indian people”. Police arrested him that afternoon. He was initially charged with making a terrorist threat and illegally possessing a rifle.

Prosecutors said Paporakis calls himself a white supremacist and neo-Nazi. Prosecutors also said he wrote a document detailing his fantasies about murdering non-white people.

Police said they recovered a rifle, ammunition, a tactical vest and a patch displaying imagery associated with white supremacist extremism from Paporakis' house.

It was not clear why police delayed releasing information about the grand jury indictment.

A judge ordered Paporakis detained and declared him a threat to the community. Paporakis is due to be arraigned Thursday morning. Fedor Kozlov, an attorney for Paporakis, said in a text message Wednesday that his client still planned to plead not guilty.

The thwarted plan alarmed members of Chicago's Hindu community and South Asian leaders, who said an uptick in racist rhetoric against their community had fueled the threat.

U.S. Rep. Raja Krishnamoorthi, who was at the temple that was targeted on the day of the planned attack, urged authorities to charge Paporakis with a hate crime. Authorities have not announced any such charge.

The FBI’s Chicago field office said Wednesday there was no additional information about possible federal charges. The U.S. Attorney’s Office for the Northern District of Illinois has not responded to a request for comment.
