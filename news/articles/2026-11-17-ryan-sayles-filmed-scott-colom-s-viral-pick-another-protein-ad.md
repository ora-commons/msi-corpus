## Colom called Sayles the election’s ‘breakout-star’

Ryan Sayles was not looking forward to the assignment when he picked up his camera to film what became the “pick another protein” ad. The 27-year-old freelance videographer and photographer from Jackson was tired on the Friday of homecoming weekend at Alcorn State University, his alma mater, and drove more than three hours to Columbus to make the commercial. The 30-second spot helped define Democrat Scott Colom’s U.S. Senate campaign against Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith. “But thank God I did,” Sayles told Mississippi Today in an interview.

A Jackson native who graduated from Murrah High School, Sayles had never worked on a political campaign before. He initially came to Colom’s campaign casually. Early in Colom’s run, the campaign asked him to take some photos and shoot a video. The campaign liked the results, and his work grew from there.

His first ad showed Colom at a Mississippi State University football game lamenting that rising grocery costs had made tailgates more expensive. The operation was so small at the time that campaign manager Gerard Henderson held a microphone in the background.

At the time of the interview, most Mississippians were familiar with the ad. Henderson and Andrew Colom, the candidate’s twin brother, wrote the script for the later spot. The commercial has a WLOX journalist ask Hyde-Smith about the high cost of beef. Hyde-Smith, a cattle farmer, responds that there are “so many proteins to choose from.” The 30-second spot then shows Mississippians reacting. The line that stuck was that they would rather pick another senator. One says, “Pick another protein? Shiiii-, I’d rather pick another senator instead.” Another says, “Pick another protein? She done lost her damn mind. I’d rather pick another senator.”

The video drew attention beyond Mississippi. E.J. Dionne Jr., a longtime columnist and commentator, praised the ad in a guest essay in The New York Times, describing it as a creative way to make “economic issues front and center.” Political pundits called it one of the best ads of the midterm election cycle.

Colom won the Mississippi seat, a victory that shocked the nation, and was set to become the state’s first Democratic U.S. senator in over four decades. He called Sayles the election’s “breakout-star.” “He’s going to be a giant in the media consultant game,” Colom wrote in a post on X. “He did GAME CHANGING work with SO little.”

As Colom’s advertising strategy grew more sophisticated, it kept the “scrappy, gritty” core it had held since the campaign’s inception. Sayles said he works best alone, with creative control and a “run and gun” approach.

Sayles thinks the “pick another protein” ad resonated because it focused on a pressure people were already feeling and let ordinary Mississippians say what they thought, elongated curse words included. “I think a lot of everyday people felt that way and just weren’t saying it,” he said.

Sayles also came up with the title “Sinators” for another viral campaign ad. The spot parodied the film “Sinners” and starred the Colom twin brothers, who made Hyde-Smith a vampire in the Mississippi Delta.

Sayles said the election result should not have surprised people. “What I think a lot of people missed is that Mississippi isn’t a lost cause. It’s been a betrayed cause,” he said. “People have been so close to winning.”

“Now that we’ve broken through, a lot of people are scratching their heads,” Sayles said. “But all it took was a little bit more investment. I think that came in part because of the ad.” He said the campaign saw donations rise after the ad came out.

Sayles said he dislikes the negative image Mississippi sometimes receives and hoped the moment would show what is good about the state. He said people who disliked Colom mostly disagreed with him on party, not personally.

Since the election, Sayles has received many messages from people who want to meet him. He said he does not know whether those contacts will lead to work or what will come next, and he is not making commitments. He said his heart will always be Mississippi. “I love Mississippi, and that’s where my heart is,” Sayles said. “I would love to continue to be helpful to bring about change in Mississippi and in the South for sure. That’s what I would desire to do. Mississippi matters.”
