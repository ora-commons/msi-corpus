## EU clears UniCredit's bid to take control of Commerzbank

**Subtype:** fact

- The European Commission approved UniCredit's bid to take control of Germany's Commerzbank on Monday, saying the transaction would not raise competition concerns in the bloc.
- The European Commission said the two groups' combined market shares in Germany were moderate and that customers could still switch providers in trade finance.
- The European Central Bank approved the deal in October, and UniCredit said Monday it had also won approval from the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System.
- Teresa Ribera, the EU's competition chief, said Europe needs stronger banks that can compete, grow and support businesses and families.

## European Central Bank and Federal Reserve have also approved the deal

The European Commission, the executive arm of the EU, said Monday that the transaction wouldn't raise competition concerns in the bloc. The approval clears the merger regulator's review of UniCredit's bid to take control of Germany's Commerzbank.

The commission said the groups' combined market shares in Germany were moderate. It also examined trade finance — letters of credit and guarantees — where it said both banks hold a strong position, and concluded that customers would still be able to switch to competing providers.

"Europe needs stronger banks that can compete, grow and support our businesses and families," Teresa Ribera, the EU's competition chief, said. Cross-border banking deals can help lenders better withstand economic shocks, she said. The approval indicates an appetite among EU officials for cross-border consolidation, arriving as officials are in the process of revamping the bloc's merger regulation guidelines with a view to boosting the region's competitiveness globally by allowing companies to scale.

Two other regulators have also approved the deal. The European Central Bank approved the deal in October. UniCredit said Monday that it had received approval from the Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System for the deal.
