## Army changed the planned date three times amid public concern

President Donald Trump said Monday that the Army will defer to the 8th Circuit on whether to detonate several tons of degraded bomb-making material at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown. The operation involves 68 barrels of lead azide, and Trump said it will not proceed unless the court agrees and incoming Gov.-elect Rob Sand also agrees.

“At the same time, have ordered the military not to do so until such time as the 8th Circuit agrees that we can go forward, and am further making this decision fully subject to the incoming Gov.-elect Rob Sand agreeing to the operation,” Trump said in a statement.

Trump said he preferred to take care of the volatile material as soon as possible and that a controlled detonation would be much safer. If Sand and the court did not agree, Trump said, the administration would have no choice but not proceed, and he said he would hold the incoming governor responsible for “any and all death or destruction caused by this very unfortunate, and longterm, situation.”

The Army had no immediate comment when asked about Trump's social media post. Environmentalists sued last week to stop the operation and won a stay from the appeals court pending a decision on the validity of an emergency permit approved by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency that cleared the way for the detonation.

The detonation was initially planned for Friday, but the Army changed the date three times following an outpouring of public concern about potential health and environmental effects. The Army had planned the operation for Wednesday, saying the weather was most favorable that day.

In a Sunday social media post, Rob Sand said, “No good options exist and the situation is more dangerous and unpredictable than it ever should have been.” He questioned why a contract to remove the explosive risk was canceled, why nothing was done and “why we are rushing to demolition now in late November.”

Sand said, “The safety of Iowans should be the top priority of everyone involved.” He also said, “All relevant federal, state and local government resources should be focused on protecting everyone that could be impacted.”

Some residents near Middletown have sealed windows and doors as a precaution against lead dust that might result from a detonation. Others who live farther away have raised concerns on social media about possible paths the plume could take as the wind shifts.

Officials acknowledge that lead is toxic and have recommended that people stay indoors whenever the detonation happens and clean outdoor surfaces with water afterward to limit exposure to lead particles.

In court filings over the weekend, Army Col. Aaron Teller, a munitions emergency response specialist, explained that less liquid inside the barrels makes the solution more susceptible to freezing. Freezing increases the sensitivity of crystals that have formed around the inside of the rim and underside of the lids, Teller said.

Teller warned that, “Even a small amount of friction can break the crystal formation, causing an explosive chain reaction,” in his declaration to the court. Officials contend that the risk of an uncontrolled explosion is growing as temperatures drop, and they said the chemicals may explode as temperatures are expected to fall below 30 degrees Fahrenheit (-1 Celsius) in the coming days.

Staff at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant found several dozen barrels of degraded lead azide in March. The material is used in munitions production, and its crystallization created an unstable and dangerous situation. The Army assembled experts in munitions chemistry, engineering and environmental safety to develop a plan.

According to EPA documents, officials considered alternatives such as neutralizing and decomposing the drums' contents with other chemicals. Officials said handling the drums would increase the probability of an accidental, fatal detonation. Environmental groups disagreed, arguing that other alternatives were not fully considered and criticizing officials for not sharing more information with the public.

Trump's order leaves the planned Wednesday operation contingent on agreement from both the 8th Circuit and Sand. The Army continues to argue that delay raises the danger posed by the material, while nearby residents have been told to prepare for a possible detonation.


Trump's order places the Army on hold after it sought emergency provisions to detonate the degraded lead azide despite the appeals court's stay, as [MSI previously reported](/articles/2026-11-22-army-seeks-emergency-provisions-to-detonate-iowa-chemicals-despite-stay/).
