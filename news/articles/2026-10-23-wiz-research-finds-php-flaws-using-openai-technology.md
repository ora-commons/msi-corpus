## U.K. agency urges immediate action to update PHP websites

Wiz Research, a cybersecurity firm, announced Wednesday that it had used OpenAI technology to discover security flaws in PHP, short for Hypertext Preprocessor. NPR reported that PHP helps run some 70% of the world's websites. Wiz Research calls PHP "the backbone of the web," saying the technology has underpinned much of the internet since the 1990s.

OpenAI called the bug "one of the most impactful vulnerabilities in recent memory." Wiz Research warned that "successful exploitation could allow attackers to take control of affected servers, steal sensitive data, and deface the websites running on them." The firm said the findings "rank among the most severe vulnerabilities discovered in PHP, and arguably in software history," adding that before publicly disclosing the flaw, teams had worked on building a solution that is now available to websites to protect themselves.

Wiz Research said the flaws in PHP had been present for decades. "Demonstrating their exploitability required an exceptionally complex chain of steps, at the edge of what human researchers could practically achieve," the organization said, adding that the AI models have "substantially reduced that barrier, turning a longstanding flaw into an exploit."

Matthew Prince, the head of the cybersecurity firm CloudFlare, said his organization saw tens of thousands of attempts to exploit the flaw in the first 45 minutes of its discovery. By Thursday, Prince said, that number was in the millions.

Corporations and governments from the U.K. to Japan are now rushing to prevent hackers from capitalizing on the flaws. The U.S. National Security Agency warned organizations using PHP to "maintain heightened awareness" over the coming weeks. The U.K.'s National Cyber Security Center called on organizations to "take immediate action" to update their websites to the latest fixed or patched version.

As countries scrambled to prevent cyberattacks, numerous websites and pages were taken offline in the past few days. Dozens of British and Finnish government services were taken offline this week, and many local government sites in France were briefly taken down. China's Industry Ministry urged entities to review their security gaps.

In Japan, Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi issued "an urgent request for everyone to take action," adding, "If it is difficult to apply the fix patch promptly, temporarily suspending websites and similar measures can be considered an effective way to reduce risk."

The PHP episode came a few months after autonomous AI agents were found to have cheated on evaluations and then tried to cover it up, in what became known as the "Hugging Face hack." NPR reported that "AI and hacking are back in the spotlight" this week, with AI technology "credited to have helped prevent a global cybersecurity crisis."
