## Hiring remains sluggish despite stronger October job growth

The U.S. Department of Labor said Thursday that initial applications for unemployment benefits rose to 203,000 last week from a revised 202,000. The four-week average increased 2,000 to 197,750. Claims remained below 220,000 during 2026, while layoffs stayed at historically low levels.

At its Nov. 19, 2026 vintage, FRED’s raw [Initial Jobless Claims series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/ICSA) lists 200,000. That differs from the Labor Department’s 203,000 weekly report, and the supplied data do not explain the discrepancy.

Economists watch initial claims because applications for unemployment aid serve as a proxy for layoffs and can signal where the job market is headed. The Labor Department report suggested that most companies have been confident enough in the economy and their sales to retain workers, leaving most American workers with solid job security.

Hiring remained sluggish even as job creation improved in recent months, the combination associated with a “low-hire, low-fire” job market. U.S. employers added 165,000 jobs in October, far more than economists had forecast, and the unemployment rate fell to 4.1% from 4.2%.

FRED’s raw [Unemployment Rate (U-3) series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/UNRATE), at its Nov. 19, 2026 vintage, separately lists the official unemployment rate at 4.1%.

The broader economy was expanding at a solid pace. Consumer spending remained resilient despite elevated inflation, which eroded Americans’ inflation-adjusted earnings. Investment in data centers was also powering the economy.

Yet that growth had spurred only limited hiring. Many economists were watching job data to evaluate whether artificial intelligence was displacing workers, but the technology had not led to a pickup in layoffs so far. Some companies may have been delaying hiring as they implemented the technology.

The economy grew at a 3.4% annual rate in the July-September quarter, up from 2.2% in the previous three months. The Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta forecast similarly healthy 3.2% growth in the final three months of the year.
