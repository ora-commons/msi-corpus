## Xi greets the U.S. president with a military flyover on the tarmac

President Trump met Russian President Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen earlier in the day, before the trilateral meeting with Chinese leader Xi Jinping was due on Thursday. The Kremlin said the Trump-Putin meeting focused primarily on Ukraine. Trump said after greeting Putin, “He’s my friend,” adding, “We knew each other for a long time, and I think a lot of wonderful things can come out of this.”

Trump headed first to a meeting of Southeast Asian leaders in Manila and then to Shenzhen for APEC, the annual gathering of Asia-Pacific leaders hosted that year by China. When Trump landed Wednesday, Xi welcomed him on the tarmac. His personal greeting was a first by a Chinese president, and Xi arranged a military flyover. The Wall Street Journal described the welcome as featuring the pomp Trump relishes and said it mirrored Trump’s welcome for Xi when Xi arrived in the United States in September.

The Wall Street Journal said the trilateral meeting would place Xi on equal footing with Trump for the third time this year while he stood alongside a close partner, Putin, over whom Xi held an overwhelming advantage. The newspaper described Xi, even while playing host, as in some ways the reluctant party among the three and said Xi nevertheless benefits just by being there.

Julian Gewirtz, a China-focused official in the Biden-era National Security Council, said: “What Xi gains from this trilateral meeting is the chance to look like the pivot player in international politics, convening this would-be ‘G3’ on Chinese soil and sitting alongside his closest partner on the world stage, Putin, to negotiate with a remarkably friendly American president.”

Ahead of the trilateral, Trump met with Xi and framed his “very special relationship” with the Chinese leader as the cornerstone of U.S.-China stability. Trump also emphasized his confidence in the relationship, saying as he met with Xi: “As long as you and I are in charge of things, we’ll never have a problem.” He added, “We’re never gonna have a problem.”

The Wall Street Journal described the planned first-ever gathering of the American, Chinese and Russian leaders as an unprecedented show of unity among the stewards of the world’s three largest nuclear arsenals. It characterized the gathering as a further sign that the U.S.-dominated unipolar order was ending, while cautioning that its replacement might be more uncertain and unsettled. The Journal said the three leaders remain divided by competing ambitions and sharply conflicting visions of what a new world order should look like.

The newspaper said Russia and the United States are bogged down in wars, while Beijing faces mounting economic problems and is deeply reluctant to take on the kind of global leadership role historically played by the United States. The Journal said the three leaders share a desire to operate without constraints in their respective spheres, with no one else at the table.

The Journal said Putin wants Washington to stay out of his way in Ukraine. It said Xi wants the United States to accept his claim that self-ruled Taiwan belongs to China. It said Trump has made pushing China and Russian influence out of the Western Hemisphere a central element of his so-called “Donroe Doctrine” to reassert U.S. dominance in the region.

There was little advance indication that progress could be made on the wars in Ukraine and Iran or the proliferation of nuclear weapons. The Wall Street Journal said that, for the three leaders in the spotlight, solving problems was almost beside the point.

Going into the trilateral, Trump embraced the historic gathering to promote an image of himself as a global peacemaker alongside two autocrats whom he had long admired, The Wall Street Journal said. The newspaper said Trump left Washington with his party reeling from its midterm defeats and was eager to use his final two years in office to cement his legacy on the world stage.

Before the meeting, Trump said in a Fox News interview: “All my life I read you’d never want to have Russia and China team up. That was like 101 in politics or in warfare.” He continued, “They both like Trump. Now, what happens after me? That I can’t tell you.” As he departed for Asia, he said, “We have a chance to accomplish a lot.”

Trump offered little indication of what he hoped to accomplish with Xi and Putin beyond opening talks on nuclear arms control, and U.S. officials played down prospects of a breakthrough. Trump acknowledged in the Fox News interview that reaching an agreement on reducing nuclear arsenals would be challenging. “When they look at me and they say, ‘OK we’ll do it, but will you reduce your nuclear weapons by 2,000 weapons?…It’s a hard thing to do,” Trump said. “For me, it’s a hard thing. And it’s probably hard for them to do also.”

The New Strategic Arms Reduction Treaty, or New START, was the last treaty limiting Washington and Moscow’s strategic nuclear arsenals, and it expired earlier this year. Trump has pushed for a broader agreement that would include China. The Wall Street Journal said China’s rapid rise as a nuclear power brings new complications to any effort at denuclearization. Beijing has long said that it does not belong in arms-control talks because its arsenal is dwarfed by those of Washington and Moscow.

Putin first proposed the trilateral summit alongside the APEC meetings as a venue for discussing an end to the Ukraine war. The Wall Street Journal said the planned gathering would mark a remarkable diplomatic comeback nearly five years after Putin’s invasion of Ukraine made him an international pariah. The newspaper said Putin’s place at the sit-down with the leaders of the world’s two largest economies, together with his separate meeting with Trump earlier in the day, underscored the limits of Western efforts to isolate him.
