## House China panel has investigated DeepSeek over spying and chip export controls

**Subtype:** fact

- The House Select Committee on China investigated how DeepSeek is a tool for China to spy, steal and subvert export controls on world-leading chips, according to Rep. John Moolenaar.
- Rep. Moolenaar called DeepSeek "a dangerous AI model" with no place in the federal government and said the Trump Administration was right to install a "commonsense AI guardrail."
- The Commerce Department and U.S. Navy barred DeepSeek in 2025, according to United Press International.
- South Korea, Italy and Germany have banned DeepSeek over security concerns, according to United Press International.
- DHS warned that use of DeepSeek products by U.S. government employees or vendors may result in sensitive data being transferred to China.
- DHS said DeepSeek models may have malware or other vulnerabilities, citing research that showed multiple critical vulnerabilities in the iOS app.

## Moolenaar calls DeepSeek dangerous and endorses the federal ban

Rep. John Moolenaar, R-Mich., chair of the House Select Committee on China, agreed with the Department of Homeland Security's ban. In a statement, he said: "DeepSeek is a dangerous AI model that has no place in the federal government, and the Trump Administration is right to put this commonsense AI guardrail in place."

He also said: "Last year, the Select Committee investigated how DeepSeek is a tool for [China] to spy, steal, and subvert export controls on the world-leading chips that keep America ahead of [China]."

Several departments within the federal government had already limited DeepSeek before DHS's broader order. United Press International reported that the Commerce Department and the U.S. Navy barred the application in 2025.

UPI also reported that several countries have banned DeepSeek over security concerns, including South Korea, Italy and Germany.

On Tuesday, DHS posted its order banning DeepSeek products on the Federal Register and told civilian agencies to remove the technology from IT systems, including third-party products. The order applied across the civilian federal government but made an exception for the Department of Defense.

In the filing, Homeland Security wrote that DeepSeek's models "present multiple threat vectors" and that use of the products "may result in the transfer of sensitive data to [China] if used by U.S. government employees or vendors."

The agency also said the models may have malware or other security vulnerabilities. DHS cited research showing multiple critical vulnerabilities in DeepSeek's iOS app.

Moolenaar's statement focused on the committee's investigation and linked DeepSeek to Chinese spying, theft and subverting export controls on world-leading chips. DHS's filing separately focused on possible sensitive-data transfer, malware and software vulnerabilities.
