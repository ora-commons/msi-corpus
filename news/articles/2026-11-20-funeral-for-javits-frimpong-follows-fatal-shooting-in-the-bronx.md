## Police charge 17-year-old and continue search for two others

A funeral was held Friday for Javits Frimpong, a 15-year-old Ghanaian boy killed in the Bronx while walking to school. His father, Thomas Frimpong, New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani and civil rights leaders delivered eulogies. At his funeral, Frimpong was remembered as a student who dreamed of becoming a doctor.

"I worked hard to bring you from Ghana to America because I wanted you to have opportunities, a better future, and a chance to achieve everything that you dreamed of," his father, Thomas Frimpong, said. Frimpong had moved to the US about a year before the shooting.

His mother was granted an emergency visa from Ghana to mourn her son at Friday's funeral service, following Mamdani's lobbying of President Donald Trump earlier in the month.

"Javits deserved so much better and so do every single one of you," Mayor Zohran Mamdani said. "Though he was only a New Yorker for a short time, New York City will forever be proud to call Javits one of our own."

"Gun violence is never the answer," Ben Crump, an attorney representing the family, said at the funeral as he vowed to seek justice for Frimpong.

Frimpong was chased down and shot on 29 October as he walked to school. He was taken to a local hospital, where he was pronounced dead. BBC News reported that "His death has sparked outrage in both Ghana and the US, and reignited debates about gun violence in America."

Police say three teenage boys were involved in the shooting. A 17-year-old has been arrested and charged with murder and will be tried as an adult. Police are still searching for the other two.

Ghana's government condemned the killing and said it would follow the case closely to ensure justice was achieved. It urged US authorities to conduct a thorough investigation and said it was ready to provide legal and consular support to ensure the perpetrators were brought to justice.
