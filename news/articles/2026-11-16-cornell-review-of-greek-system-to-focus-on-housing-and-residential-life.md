## Kotlikoff pledged a public accountability dashboard and academic center

Cornell University's coming review of its Greek system will focus in particular on the system's relationship to housing and residential life, President Michael Kotlikoff said in a letter to the community Monday. Kotlikoff said details of the review will be announced in the coming weeks.

The letter arrived just over a week after Cornell's Faculty Senate passed a vote of no confidence in the university administration's ability to address sexual assault on campus. Kotlikoff addressed that vote directly in the letter. "I know many in our community have lost confidence in the Cornell administration's ability to prevent and respond to sexual violence," he wrote. "You have raised necessary questions about what the administration is doing to keep our students safe and where we have fallen short."

Kotlikoff pledged more regular communication about the university's efforts, including a new public accountability dashboard tracking its progress.

Beyond the review itself, Kotlikoff outlined plans to expand sexual assault prevention education, create more late-night social opportunities outside residential spaces and establish an academic center dedicated to researching campus sexual violence. The university said it is exploring ways to revitalize businesses and develop public gathering places in Collegetown, a neighborhood near campus where many students live and socialize.

"To prevent sexual assault, we must reshape the social environments, interactions, and physical spaces that can contribute to increased risk," Kotlikoff wrote.

The plans follow a report of a gang rape at a fraternity house in 2024 that focused scrutiny on the Ivy League school. The female student's report alleged that seven members of the Chi Phi fraternity drugged and sexually assaulted her in October 2024. The woman, identified in court documents as Jane Doe, filed a lawsuit against Cornell and the men in September.

Cornell expelled or suspended several students after an internal investigation and barred the fraternity chapter from campus. The accused men have denied wrongdoing, and none has been criminally charged.

Kotlikoff has faced criticism for his handling of the case, including his acknowledgment that he never read the campus police report and did not follow up on the outcome of the investigations. He has defended the university's disciplinary process and said administrators were prohibited from interfering in criminal investigations.

Cornell previously suspended parties at fraternities and announced additional measures to prevent sexual violence on campus.

An independent review led by former acting U.S. Attorney General Sally Yates is underway. Kotlikoff said its findings would guide longer-term changes to how Cornell investigates and adjudicates allegations of sexual misconduct.
