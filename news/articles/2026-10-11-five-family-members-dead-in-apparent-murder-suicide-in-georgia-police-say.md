## Chief says one of the victims placed the initial emergency call

Five people were fatally shot in an apparent murder-suicide at a residence in Douglas, Georgia, on Friday, authorities said. The deceased were all family members who lived at the home in Douglas, a city in south Georgia.

Brannen Pruette, the Douglas police chief, said at a Saturday news conference that officers responded to reports of an active shooter later on Friday and that one of the victims placed the initial emergency call.

Responding officers found four deceased adults inside the home. A fifth person was transported to a hospital, where they were later pronounced dead.

Details of how the tragedy unfolded remain unclear, including who opened fire. Police did not offer additional information, citing the ongoing investigation.

Hours before the deadly shooting, a female victim called police to complain that her husband was "killing her" with his smoking, and EMS transported her to a local medical center.

"We recognize the profound impact this loss has on those closest to the victims in our community. We ask everyone to respect the privacy of the family and allow them the time and space they need to grieve," Pruette said.

Charlie Davis, the Douglas city manager, said it was the area's second murder-suicide incident in three months. "Domestic violence doesn't know race. It doesn't know religion. It doesn't know geographic location," Davis said. "We encourage everyone to refrain from speculation and allow the family the space, dignity and compassion they deserve during this time."

In a similar episode of violence, a man in Pennsylvania fatally shot several family members on Friday, before killing himself.
