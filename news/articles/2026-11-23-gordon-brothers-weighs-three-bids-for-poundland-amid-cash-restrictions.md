## Martin Property Group offered a going-concern purchase; Xyra made a rescue bid

Gordon Brothers, the US investment firm that owns and lends to Poundland, is weighing three bids for the retailer after restricting its access to cash needed to restock stores and meet day-to-day expenses. Martin Property Group, an Irish firm, has offered to buy the business as a going concern; London-based holding company Xyra Group has made a rescue bid, although doubts remain about the credibility of Xyra's offer; and a third bidder is unnamed. For now, Poundland has avoided immediate collapse after a deadline was extended by 10 days. A collapse into administration could put the jobs of the retailer's 11,000 employees and potentially thousands of jobs at supplier firms at risk. Poundland has a major presence on the High Street, with 600 stores in the UK and Ireland, and also trades under the Dealz brand.

Gordon Brothers put Poundland up for sale in September but rejected offers to keep the business afloat. The US investment firm bought the retailer from Pepco last year for £1 and specializes in buying and restructuring distressed companies. Since acquiring the business, Gordon Brothers said it had provided significant funding to support Poundland's ability to trade and had made about £18m from a combination of debt interest payments and other fees and charges.

Gordon Brothers is also one of Poundland's lenders and is owed loans, including a £22.5m shareholder loan it inherited when it bought the business for £1. As the main creditor, Gordon Brothers would be first in line for repayment if the company went under, while suppliers would not be first in line. Julie Palmer, managing partner at insolvency specialist Beggies Traynor, said: "When the owner is also the main lender, its first priority is recovering [loans]." Palmer added: "Sometimes the route that best protects the lender is different from the one that saves the most jobs, and that tension is why this has become such a political story."

Trading improved even as the sale remained unsettled. Poundland was struggling when Pepco sold it, but the business turned a corner over the year to September, moving from a £68m loss to an £18.1m profit before interest, tax and certain accounting charges. Going into the vital Christmas period, first-quarter like-for-like sales were up 7%, while operational profits reached £1.9m, £1.2m higher than a year earlier. Halloween sales were 11.4% above the previous year. Retail analyst Catherine Shuttleworth of Savvy Marketing said Halloween sales usually are a good indicator of how a retailer will do at Christmas. People have not stopped shopping at Poundland, Palmer said: "This isn't a story about shoppers abandoning Poundland. The tills are busier than they were a year ago." Palmer added: "On the face of it, a turnaround seems well under way."

Funding remained the immediate pressure. Gordon Brothers had frozen about £50m in funds, leading to concerns that Poundland's shops would run out of stock. It later released around £21m over two weeks, giving the chain a little more breathing space. Palmer said Poundland seemed to be facing "a funding and ownership problem rather than a trading collapse." Poundland boss Barry Williams said the business was making a "strong recovery," while Gordon Brothers said the chain remained "challenged and vulnerable to any potential downturn in future trading."

Palmer said Poundland had two possible paths: a solvent deal in which a buyer bought the company and it continued trading, or a collapse followed by a break-up. Gordon Brothers has also been weighing options to break the retailer up. The US company's view was that a break-up would ultimately provide employees with more long-term security because buyers of the store estate would also have to agree to keep employees on.

Insiders critical of how Gordon Brothers handled the sale said the investment firm was driving Poundland into the ground to make money through the insolvency process. Gordon Brothers denied the claim.

Palmer warned: "The immediate make-or-break issue is Christmas: a value retailer that can't get stock on its shelves in November and December is in serious trouble, whatever its full-year results say."

Prime Minister Andy Burnham and Business Secretary Jonathan Reynolds have applied political pressure to Gordon Brothers. They called for answers over why the Martin Property Group offer to buy the business, which would have protected jobs, was rejected. A committee of MPs also questioned Gordon Brothers last week, adding to pressure.

Shuttleworth said Poundland's future was "really unclear, with the owners and the management at loggerheads about its future." Because Gordon Brothers is a US firm, Shuttleworth said it appeared "unmoved" by the arguments the UK government had put forward.
