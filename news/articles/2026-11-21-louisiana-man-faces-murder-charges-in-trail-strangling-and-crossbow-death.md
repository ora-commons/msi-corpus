## Sheriff says suspect confessed to strangling stranger, then killed co-worker

A man accused of killing a woman he had never met on a wooded trail north of New Orleans was already wanted in a second, unrelated homicide when detectives formally charged him in the first, authorities said Saturday.

Jared Schurr, 36, faces counts of second-degree murder in the deaths of Lizeht Rodriguez, 24, and Woodrow Scott, 37, on Nov. 11 and Nov. 13, respectively, investigators said.

The St. Tammany Parish sheriff's office said its major crimes unit obtained the arrest warrant in Rodriguez's death on Saturday. Her body was found at the Northlake Nature Center in Mandeville, Louisiana.

In a statement, the agency said Schurr described the encounter to detectives after his arrest.

"Schurr confessed to encountering Rodriguez, whom he did not know, while walking along a trail at the Northlake nature center," the statement said.

"Schurr told detectives he passed Rodriguez on the trail before grabbing and strangling her, then dragging her body to the area where she was later discovered by another person walking along the trail," the statement continued.

The sheriff's office said Schurr was already wanted in connection with a separate homicide that occurred two days after Rodriguez's killing. In that case, investigators suspect him of shooting his co-worker Scott in the back with a crossbow.

He was arrested on Nov. 13 after a vehicle chase that began near Bogalusa, Louisiana — north of Mandeville — and ended in Pearl River County, Mississippi. As of Saturday, he was being held in the Pearl River jail pending extradition to Louisiana.

Authorities said they homed in on Schurr after an investigation that involved assistance from the FBI and license-plate reading cameras.

The sheriff's office said its detectives were awaiting the results of DNA analysis expected to provide additional evidence linking Schurr to Rodriguez's murder. The agency also said it would continue looking into both homicides to determine what the motive may have been.

Rodriguez's death, as officials described it, is a rare form of U.S. homicide: a killing carried out by a stranger. FBI research estimates that fewer than 10% of murder victims are killed by people not known to them, and that the vast majority are instead victimized by people they know.

Both cases remain under investigation, and the second-degree murder counts against Schurr have not been adjudicated.
