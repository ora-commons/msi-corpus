## Apple had sued OpenAI and two former employees over alleged poaching

OpenAI filed counterclaims against Apple in a California federal court on Friday, November 27, alleging that Apple stole trade secrets and discouraged hardware suppliers from working on future OpenAI devices. The filing answered Apple’s July lawsuit, which accused OpenAI of poaching employees to gain access to Apple’s technology.

OpenAI said in the filing that Apple designed its employment practices to “trap” employees and prevent them from working on competitors’ technology. The counterclaims also placed OpenAI’s planned AI-enabled personal electronic devices at the center of the dispute, with the company saying they threatened to “outcompete Apple on what Apple views as its own turf.”

Apple sued OpenAI and former Apple employees Tang Tan and Chang Liu in July, alleging misappropriation of trade secrets to benefit OpenAI’s foray into consumer hardware. Tan was a vice president of product design for the iPhone and Apple Watch and was part of the group that launched the AI device startup io Products before OpenAI acquired it in 2025.

OpenAI’s trade-secret allegation centers on a former employee who spent six months at OpenAI. OpenAI said Apple gained confidential information by rehiring the employee, who had access to specific information about a new device and OpenAI’s roadmap for future hardware products. OpenAI also linked the timing of the moves to an allegation that the employee took the documents.

Apple denied using the rehired employee to access OpenAI’s confidential information and denied trying to suppress competition in AI device manufacturing. In a Friday statement, Apple said, “OpenAI’s counterclaims are a transparent attempt to distract from the substance of Apple’s lawsuit.”

OpenAI, Tan and Liu denied the allegations in Apple’s original lawsuit. OpenAI and the employees said the case depended on Apple’s unauthorized monitoring of Liu’s personal messages after Liu stopped working for Apple. OpenAI and attorneys for Tan and Liu did not immediately respond to a request for comment on Friday’s counterclaims.

Friday’s filing also focused on Apple’s practices for employees’ personal devices and accounts. OpenAI said Apple encouraged workers to perform company work on personal Apple devices and accounts, then prevented orderly separation of work and personal accounts when employees left for new jobs.

OpenAI said Apple continued to access former employees’ personal devices and accounts after they departed, giving the company what it described as a means to “monitor and ensnare departing employees” who left for competitors.

OpenAI said 400 employees have left Apple for OpenAI so far. The company alleged that Apple wanted to intimidate other employees from leaving to find other technology companies.

The allegations come as the race to develop AI products intensifies competition between the companies for talent and proprietary technology. OpenAI’s counterclaims broaden the dispute beyond the two former employees named in Apple’s July suit to confidential hardware information, departing workers and suppliers for future OpenAI devices.
