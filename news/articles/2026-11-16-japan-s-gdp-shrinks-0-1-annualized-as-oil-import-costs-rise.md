## Japan's GDP shrinks 0.1% annualized as oil import costs rise

- Japan's private non-residential investment grew at an annualized 6.8% in the quarter, supported by solid digital demand.

## Consumer spending falls while business investment grows 6.8%

Japan's economy contracted at an annualized rate of 0.1% in the July-September quarter, weighed down by slowing consumer spending and the rising cost of oil imports, the Cabinet Office said Monday in preliminary data. The annualized rate is what the growth or contraction would have been if it had continued for a year.

On a quarter-to-quarter basis, Japan's gross domestic product — the sum of a nation's goods and services — was basically flat, although it technically shrank, by a smaller amount than 0.1%. Some analysts had expected a low level of growth for the latest quarter.

The contraction reverses a run of moderate growth. Japan eked out moderate growth over the previous three quarters, while contracting in the same quarter a year ago.

On the energy side, Japan imports virtually all its oil, and the country has been severely hurt by surging oil prices because of the war in Iran. The main route for oil shipments, the Strait of Hormuz, is effectively blocked.

The weak yen is adding to the problem of rising energy prices. The U.S. dollar has recently been trading at about 160 yen, up from below 115 yen five years ago.

Consumer spending was weak because of various factors, including poor weather and rising prices, with private consumption down at an annualized 1.1% for the quarter.

Business investment moved the other way. Japan's investments remained strong in the third quarter, thanks to solid digital demand, with private non-residential investment marking an annualized 6.8% growth for the quarter.

The government of Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi is trying to get bills passed to deliver a tax cut on food. Prospects remain uncertain because of worries about the ballooning public debt and how such tax cuts would be financed.
