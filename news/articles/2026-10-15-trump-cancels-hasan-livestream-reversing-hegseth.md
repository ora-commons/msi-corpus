## Trump told allies he wasn't involved in initial livestream decision

President Trump on Thursday canceled plans to livestream the execution by firing squad of Nidal Hasan, reversing Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth. Hegseth's surprise comments that the execution would be public had sent Army officials, who had been planning for Hasan's execution by lethal injection for months, scrambling to figure out how to carry out the event, according to Army and Pentagon officials.

In a Thursday social-media post, Trump said Hasan "will die, as scheduled, by firing squad, but this event won't be streamed." The president called Hasan "a very bad and evil man" and said he thought "streaming the execution for all to see might have been a strong deterrent for the World to witness how America deals with Terrorists and SCUM like Hasan." But, he said, "many very good and well meaning people would rather not watch him die, through streaming, or through any other visual device." The president noted that immediate family members of the 2009 victims could still choose to view the execution in person.

Trump made the decision after talking to White House advisers and other allies in recent days, expressing concern about a bloody spectacle on live television, according to people familiar with the matter. He told allies that he wasn't involved in the initial decision to livestream the execution, the people said.

The cancellation reversed a position Hegseth had previously announced in an interview with a conservative news outlet. Hegseth said he had wanted to ensure "people are able to watch it" because "people need to understand that there's serious consequences for these types of things."

Some U.S. officials had worried that broadcasting Hasan's death would be a propaganda victory for adversaries, and lawmakers from both parties had expressed concerns about the livestream plan in the days before Trump's reversal.

Hasan, a U.S.-born Muslim who worked as a psychiatrist, said during his court-martial that he went on a shooting rampage at Fort Hood because he believed the military was at war with Islam. A jury of fellow officers sentenced him to death for the shooting, one of the worst soldier-on-soldier crimes in U.S. military history. He is paralyzed from the waist down from injuries sustained in the attack, which killed 13 people at Fort Hood Army base in Texas in 2009.
