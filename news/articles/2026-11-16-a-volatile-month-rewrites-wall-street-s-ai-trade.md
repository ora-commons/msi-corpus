For three years, investors have minted money from the AI boom without having to make hard choices, The Wall Street Journal reported on Nov. 16, 2026. That "no-brainer" era is over, the Journal said, arguing that a punishing bout of volatility — stretching back to the Nasdaq's recent peak on Oct. 6, through the election's aftermath and Jensen Huang's stunning exit as CEO of Nvidia — has fundamentally rewired the AI trade.

The lesson the Journal draws for investors from the weeks of turbulence and anxiety is that they can no longer count on juicy returns from broad-based AI bets. Instead, investors must size up winners and losers, and, in the Journal's words, "look around corners to spot the risks that can trigger an avalanche of selling."

The Journal characterized the AI trade as vulnerable from a dizzying number of angles, with its reporters breaking down the forces shaking it and what could test it next.
