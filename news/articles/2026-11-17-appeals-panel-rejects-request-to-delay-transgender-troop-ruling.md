## Plaintiffs say 27 service members are covered; scope excludes new recruits

A three-judge panel of the U.S. Court of Appeals for the District of Columbia Circuit voted 2-1 on Tuesday to reject the Trump administration's request to stay its June 1 ruling. The decision implements the panel's order barring the Pentagon from removing current transgender service members named in the lawsuit. The June ruling does not allow new transgender recruits to join the military.

Plaintiffs' attorneys said Tuesday's order covers 27 service members, all of whom are currently serving. The order applies to the current service members named in the lawsuit, while the June decision stopped short of allowing new transgender recruits to join.

In June, the panel found that the Trump administration's ban had been unlawfully imposed. Circuit Judge Robert Wilkins, partially joined by Circuit Judge Judith Rogers, ruled that the Pentagon's policy was designed to exclude people from military service based on their gender identity. The majority decision barred the military from removing the current service members named in the lawsuit.

The administration had asked the D.C. Circuit to delay implementation while it petitions the Supreme Court to take the case. The Supreme Court had ruled in the administration's favor last year, allowing the Pentagon to start enforcing the ban while the litigation proceeded. Tuesday's ruling could set the stage for the issue to return to the Supreme Court, which previously allowed the ban to go forward for now in a different case.

On Tuesday, Wilkins said the administration had not shown how retaining the plaintiffs currently serving in the military would cause any irreparable harm to the government or endanger national security. He also noted that the scope of the June 1 majority opinion was narrower than the injunction the Supreme Court stayed last year.

“To the extent the government suggests that any injunction against the Executive Branch imposes irreparable harm, I am also unpersuaded,” Wilkins wrote.

Shannon Minter, legal director of the National Center for LGBTQ Rights, said the plaintiffs were all “decorated troops who have met every standard the military sets.”

“The Trump administration asked the court to let it keep pushing these service members toward the door while it tries to get a different answer from the Supreme Court. Today, the D.C. Circuit said no,” Minter said in a statement.

The June 1 majority opinion partially upheld a March 2025 ruling by U.S. District Judge Ana Reyes, who concluded that Trump's executive order excluding transgender troops from military service likely violated their constitutional rights.

Trump signed that executive order in January 2025. The order claims the sexual identity of transgender service members “conflicts with a soldier’s commitment to an honorable, truthful, and disciplined lifestyle, even in one’s personal life” and is harmful to military readiness.

Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth issued a policy in response that presumptively disqualifies people with gender dysphoria from military service. Gender dysphoria is the distress a person feels because their assigned gender and gender identity do not match. The medical condition has been linked to depression and suicidal thoughts.

The panel included Wilkins, nominated by Democratic President Barack Obama; Rogers, nominated by President Bill Clinton, also a Democrat; and Circuit Judge Justin Walker, nominated by President Donald Trump, a Republican. Walker wrote in his June dissent that judges lack the power to second-guess the decision to exclude transgender troops. He also voted Tuesday to grant the administration's request to suspend the decision.
