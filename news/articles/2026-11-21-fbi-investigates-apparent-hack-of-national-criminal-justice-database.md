## Trump says breach news is "not good" as scope remains unclear

The FBI is investigating an apparent hack of the National Crime Information Center, a bureau-maintained database of criminal justice information, a person familiar with the matter said Saturday.

The person confirmed the incident to The Associated Press on the condition of anonymity because they were not authorized to speak by name. CNN first reported the apparent hack.

The FBI declined to comment Saturday on the incident.

The NCIC is a computerized database available to local, state and federal law enforcement agencies. Its records cover fugitives, missing persons, criminal history data, stolen property and other topics — information that agencies across the country query in the ordinary course of investigations.

The scope of the problem was not immediately clear. It was not known Saturday what data, if any, was accessed, or how long the apparent intrusion had gone on before it was detected.

Asked about the subject on Saturday, President Donald Trump addressed it in a Fox News Channel interview. "We just heard about it and I will say that it's not good," Trump said.

News that the NCIC was targeted comes as the country has encountered a series of digital outages in the government and private sector. Trump administration officials have said those outages were caused by routine efforts to upgrade computer systems and fix vulnerabilities, rather than a cyberattack. There was no immediate indication that the FBI incident was related to those outages.

The bureau has historically been a frequent target for hackers.

In September, the FBI said it was investigating a hack of its job portal website after a criminal hacking group known as ShinyHunters claimed it had obtained a massive trove of data on bureau employees and applicants.

And in March, the FBI disclosed that it was investigating "suspicious activities" on an internal system that contains sensitive information related to surveillance operations and investigations.
