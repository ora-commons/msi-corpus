## Policy details remain unclear amid Google's Finnish expansion

President Donald Trump said at a press briefing after returning from a trip to Asia that US companies should keep AI data centers at home. "We're going to keep it all in this country," he said, while threatening a "tremendous tax" for technology firms that build AI data centers outside the United States. He provided little detail about the proposal.

Trump also said: "We're not going to allow our companies to build data centres outside of our country." He tied the proposal to his claim that China would otherwise take the facilities, saying, "Because China will take all of them, and we're not allowing that to happen." Trump said the United States was leading in data-center construction and would not let China beat the country in AI, which he calls super intelligence, or SI.

Trump said he had not yet raised the idea with his staff. "I don't know if that's a breaking story, but I haven't even said that to my people. But I'm telling them now, they're here," he said. BBC News reported that other members of his cabinet were hearing about the proposal for the first time and that it remained unclear whether the idea would become policy. It also remained unclear how such a policy would be enforced if it was brought in.

Data centers were already a central issue in the midterm elections earlier in November, and many Democratic candidates promised tougher regulation on construction. BBC News reported that concerns about noise pollution and energy consumption helped drive Democratic candidates who promised tougher rules on data-center construction to wins on November 3.

According to the Pew Research Centre, the United States had about 3,000 data centers as of April, with another 1,500 at various stages of approval and construction. Many are built in rural areas.

BBC News described data centers as crucial to the AI boom. The warehouse-style buildings house advanced computer chips and servers needed to run AI programs such as Claude and ChatGPT.

US technology companies are already building abroad. Tech giants including Meta and Google have entered agreements to build data centers overseas. Large tech firms — often called AI hyperscalers because of the size of their AI investments — have also been branching out.

In September, Google announced a €13bn ($15bn: £11bn) investment in AI infrastructure in Finland, which included funding for three data centers. Last month, building work for two of those centers was halted over concerns about clearing more than 300 hectares of forest. Trump called out the Finnish build specifically, saying his administration was "not allowing that to happen." BBC News said Google had been contacted for comment.

Canada has made moves to offer itself as an alternative location for data centers, with less local pushback against projects so far.

Trump also encouraged communities to host domestic projects. "Make them pay you, make them pay your community, but take advantage don't shut them off," he said. "We have a lot of places that want them badly, and they're great places. They have energy and they have everything. They're tremendous assets."

Trump's remarks paired a threatened overseas ban with encouragement for communities to accept US-based data centers. He supplied little detail about the overseas proposal, and BBC News reported that enforcement remained unclear and that it was unclear whether the idea would become policy.
