## Iran links Strait reopening to blockade and regional fighting

According to The Wall Street Journal, U.S. forces have carried out a series of strikes in recent weeks on Qeshm Island, Iran’s largest island, as well as other locations near the waterway. On Tuesday, Iranian state media said Tehran had struck Didamar, a small, uninhabited Omani island in the Strait of Hormuz. The strikes came as the two sides fought for control of a waterway carrying commercial traffic nearly nine months into the conflict, and President Donald Trump has signaled that he does not plan to restart major combat operations, but the attacks continued.

Qeshm is home to a major naval facility for the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. The corps has used the island to launch drones and missiles, and the island has tunnels the force uses to prepare weapons. Chris Long, a former British navy officer in the Gulf who works at the maritime security firm Neptune P2P, said, “Iran is firing missiles from there and has air defense radar and interceptor sites there.”

U.S. and Gulf officials said the strikes were designed to stop Iran from attacking ships in the waterway, not to pave the way for a major military operation on the island. U.S. officials, including Trump, described them as minor and defensive. After The Wall Street Journal first reported on the strikes, Trump said the U.S. had hit an antiaircraft facility Iran built over two months. Asked about the continuing attacks on Friday, Trump said, “I don’t want to talk about it. We do minor strikes.”

Iranian state media reported the sound of explosions on a near-nightly basis, often without explaining whether the blasts came from Iranian launches or U.S. strikes. The explosions were heard by roughly 150,000 residents of Qeshm, and more blasts rang out Sunday night. Ahmad Nafisi, a deputy governor for Iran’s southern Hormozgan province, told the state-run IRNA news agency that a U.S. military strike caused the blasts. U.S. Central Command declined to comment.

On Tuesday, Iranian state media reported that Iran’s military struck Didamar, a small, rocky and uninhabited Omani island typically seen as the entry and exit point to the Persian Gulf. The island contains navigational equipment and a lighthouse built by the U.K. in 1914. Iranian state television said the navigational equipment was helping the flow of oil smuggling by the enemy. An Omani coast guard official said an explosion struck the island but did not elaborate, and Oman’s Maritime Security Center declined to comment.

Despite the fighting, the U.S. military has been running near-daily operations from warships and land bases to help commercial vessels move into and out of the Persian Gulf. Those operations have helped regional oil exports reach levels near where they stood at the start of the war. The U.S. has repeatedly struck Qeshm Island as well as other locations near the waterway. The Strait’s role in global markets makes the attacks economically consequential even as U.S. officials portray them as limited operations.

The strikes are unfolding as Trump faces a difficult choice over how to proceed in the conflict. Vice President JD Vance has blamed the war for big losses Republicans suffered in the midterm elections. The Wall Street Journal reported that Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth and other administration officials are pushing for a return to major combat operations. After meeting the Qatari prime minister on Monday, Trump said Gulf states would prefer “the status quo” of continuing the blockade on Iran and a new American effort to stop Tehran’s overland trade. “Iran, militarily, is effectively over,” he added. Oil prices fell after his remarks.

Mohsen Rezaei, a former Revolutionary Guard commander who now serves as secretary of Iran’s Supreme National Security Council, wrote on X: “The war on Iran has already wrecked the US government.” He added: “But Washington flails like a desperate gambler. FOLD YOUR HAND. STOP manufacturing FAKE NEWS about imaginary talks.”

Tehran has also put forward diplomatic conditions for reopening the waterway. Esmail Baghaei, Iran’s Foreign Ministry spokesman, said Iran had offered a two-day plan to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. Washington would have to lift the blockade and end fighting on fronts across the Middle East, including the Israeli military operation in southern Lebanon, before any talks, Baghaei said. “The Americans must make a genuine decision, if they have properly understood the message of their own people,” he said, referring to the midterm elections in which Democrats took control of Congress.

The United States has kept its blockade in place. Over the weekend, an American military aircraft attacked the St. Kitts-flagged ship Huda in the first such U.S. attack in about a month, according to The Wall Street Journal. Troops from the United Arab Emirates rescued the ship’s 22 crew members. U.S. Central Command said the United States had disabled five ships during the blockade.

Ali Vaez, an Iran expert and deputy program director at the International Crisis Group, said the back-and-forth attacks “may look like a low-level, simmering conflict, but it’s not a stable formula.” “It can escalate at any point,” he said.
