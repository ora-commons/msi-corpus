## TICMS tracks munitions inventories across more than 200 locations worldwide

The Air Force said it does not comment on the security posture, vulnerabilities or operational status of specific networks and systems. It said it continuously takes prudent measures to protect its information, systems and mission capabilities.

Reuters, citing two sources, reported that hackers breached Cloud One and that the intrusion caused disruption across the platform. The sources said the breach was discovered within the past month or so during a systems upgrade.

Reuters said it could not establish the hackers' identities, the extent of the intrusion or whether any data had been stolen.

Cloud One was designed to serve as a hub for Air Force information-technology needs, including personnel data, pay, logistics and maintenance. The platform uses services from Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet.

Cloud One also supports command and control and the theater integrated combat munitions system, or TICMS. TICMS tracks and manages munitions inventories across more than 200 locations worldwide.

“A breach here is a big deal,” one of the sources told Reuters.

One source told Reuters that intruders exploited a software vulnerability to reach the control system. The source said intruders could in theory use that access to check the operational status of military aircraft or the movement of spare parts.

Reuters said the intrusion could jeopardize the security of sensitive tactical, personnel and logistics data. The Air Force statement did not address the reported incident or those potential consequences.

The reported breach comes after reports of electronic break-ins at the FBI, Department of Homeland Security personnel information, the Treasury Department and two spy agencies. Earlier in the week, reports said hackers had breached a system used by the CIA and the Office of the Director of National Intelligence to reach unclassified information.

In a separate Pentagon breach, hackers accessed the defense manpower data center, affecting 2.76 million living people and 294,000 deceased individuals, a U.S. defense official told Military.com. The exposed information included Social Security numbers and, depending on the individual, names, birthdates, contact information and military occupational specialties, Military.com said.

The New York Times said the system used by the CIA and ODNI was an intelligence-analysis tool that scans open-source and unclassified material for insights and information. The CIA declined to comment, while an ODNI representative said in an emailed statement to Bloomberg that “there have been no disruptions to critical capabilities or classified systems.”


The Cloud One intrusion adds an Air Force platform to the recent sequence of federal-system hacks that [MSI previously reported](/articles/2026-11-24-hack-disrupts-cia-and-national-intelligence-networks/) at the CIA and ODNI. The Air Force case remains unresolved on the hackers' identities, the intrusion's extent and whether data was stolen.
