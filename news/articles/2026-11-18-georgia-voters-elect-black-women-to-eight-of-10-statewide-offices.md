## Georgia voters elect Black women to eight of 10 statewide offices

**Subtype:** fact

- Georgia voters elected Black women to eight of 10 statewide offices in the 2026 midterms.
- Keisha Lance Bottoms will become the first Black woman elected governor in the U.S. after defeating Republican billionaire Rick Jackson.
- Democrats won every Georgia state government office on the ballot.
- Democrats won a majority in the Georgia state House.
- State representative Carolyn Hugley was nominated to become Georgia's first Black woman House speaker after serving over 30 years in the legislature.

## Keisha Lance Bottoms will govern Georgia after a historic victory

Georgia's 2026 midterms will place Black women in eight of the 10 statewide offices on the ballot when new agency leadership begins in January. Keisha Lance Bottoms will take office as governor, alongside Black women serving as attorney general and secretary of state. When the legislature convenes next year, Georgia will also have more Black women representatives.

NPR's DorMiya Vance, reporting with WABE from Atlanta, said Georgia voters had made clear they wanted a change in who represented them next year. Democrats won every state government office on the ballot and a majority in the state House, while Bottoms' victory made her the country's first-ever Black woman elected governor.

Democrat Keisha Lance Bottoms, a former mayor of Atlanta, defeated Republican billionaire Rick Jackson. In an archived victory message, she said, "Thank you, Georgia. I am honored to be your 84th governor." Bottoms did not emphasize her identity in the campaign, and political scientist Tammy Greer at Georgia State University said voters were ready to give new candidates a chance.

Maui Bigelow cast her ballot in a south Atlanta suburb on Election Day and said the moment was pivotal for getting more Black women into state government. "Every time there's a fire, y'all call Black women," she said.

Greer said the candidates' decision to come forward mattered in a political environment that allowed their message to reach different groups. "So the political environment happened. These women had the audacity to come forward. And they were able to get their message to translate across demographics," she said.

She described the prominence of Black women in Georgia government as new. "For so long, Black women have been in the background who have pushed others ahead of themselves while doing all the work," Greer said. The incoming lineup places Black women in eight statewide offices and brings more Black women into the legislature.

As governor, Bottoms can appoint and fill thousands of positions in state government and will have a say over the state budget. She will also have an ally leading the statehouse because Democrats won the majority there.

State representative Carolyn Hugley, who has served for over 30 years, has been nominated to become Georgia's first Black woman House speaker. Her nomination would add a Black woman to Georgia's top state leadership as the legislature prepares to convene with more Black women representatives.

Hugley said she expects the incoming class of Black women to be judged under a microscope. "Sometimes people have unrealistic expectations of African American women in particular," she said. She said she has dealt with prejudice and judgment throughout her career, including comments about her hairstyles and clothes, and she expects harsh criticism ahead.

She also addressed the prejudice and judgment she expects to face. "That has always been a part of being a Black woman in America. And this is not new territory for me. I've been a Black woman for a long time," she said. Based on her experience, Hugley said the new leaders should remain focused on their responsibilities as elected officials rather than be distracted by unreasonable backlash.
