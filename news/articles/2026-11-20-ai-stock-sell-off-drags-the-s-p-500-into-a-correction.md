## Nasdaq composite falls 5.3%, leaving the index negative for the year

A sell-off in artificial intelligence stocks on Friday, Nov. 20, 2026, pulled the S&P 500 down 3.7% and left the benchmark index in a correction, according to Associated Press reporting carried by NPR. The decline gave the S&P 500 its worst weekly loss in more than 18 months and pushed the Nasdaq composite into negative territory for the year. The Dow Jones Industrial Average, which has less exposure to technology stocks, fell 1.9%.

## Index closes and correction status

The S&P 500 fell 265.44 points to close at 6,910.39, the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 949.73 points to 48,147.33, and the Nasdaq composite lost 1,261.47 points to close at 22,617.73. The report said the S&P 500 had fallen more than 10% from its Oct. 6 all-time high, the decline that professional investors call a "correction."

Associated Press said the S&P 500's previous correction came in March 2025 during the Trump administration's trade war. It said Friday's weekly decline was the worst since April 2025, when the trade war escalated. The weekly loss capped a seven-day losing streak, the longest for the index since the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020.

The Nasdaq composite, which the report said is more influenced by technology stocks, fell 5.3% and moved into negative territory for the year. Its weekly loss was just short of its worst week since March 2020, during the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic.

For separate supplementary context, FRED's raw [Dow Jones Industrial Average series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DJIA) recorded 49,097.06 and its [Nasdaq Composite Index series](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/NASDAQCOM) recorded 23,879.2 for Nov. 20, 2026, at their raw published values. The Associated Press reported closes of 48,147.33 and 22,617.73, respectively.

## Stock-specific moves and Anthropic

The Associated Press report described Nvidia, Micron Technology, Broadcom and Advanced Micro Devices as major beneficiaries of AI data-center construction and said much of the sector's decline was concentrated among them. Advanced Micro Devices fell 13.3%, Micron Technology 13.9% and Broadcom 12.1%, while the PHLX Semiconductor Index fell 10.7%. Other companies connected to the AI buildout included optical products company Corning, which fell 10.9%, and energy provider Bloom Energy, which fell 15.3%.

Nvidia fell for the ninth day in the last 10, closing 13.8% lower. The report attributed pressure on the stock to investor anxiety about the strength of the AI buildout and, more recently, concerns about the chip maker's future following news about Jensen Huang, its former CEO and co-founder. Since that news earlier in the month, Nvidia had fallen more than 25%.

Anthropic, maker of the Claude AI tool, fell 9.1% to $88.30 in its second trading session after an IPO on Thursday, remaining above its $83 IPO price. The report described the offering as the largest public stock offering ever and said it surpassed $2 trillion in value at one point. Friday's decline amounted to roughly 23% from Anthropic's brief peak of $115.10 a share the previous day. Associated Press said that was a larger decline than Facebook, now Meta, experienced in the early days after its May 2012 IPO.

The report noted that technology-company market debuts are often followed by a volatile period. SpaceX, which previously held the title of biggest IPO, rose nearly 50% in its first three days but fell below its IPO price a month later. Outside technology stocks, most stocks gained ground: Cheerios maker General Mills rose 3.7% and homebuilder Lennar gained 1%.

## Investor explanations

Associated Press said AI stocks carried large market values and had a greater effect on market direction. It described them as responsible for both Wall Street's gains this year and the subsequent market decline.

The report said the AI trade had been losing momentum partly because of concerns that the sector's large valuations and heavy spending were unjustified. It also said fears of a bubble within the sector had been intensified by the midterm election results, including victories at both the federal and state level for candidates advocating slower data-center construction.

Michael Antonelli, a market strategist at Baird, said, "The whole notion that AI can continue to spend unlimited amounts of money and support the stock market, I think the notion of that is ending as we speak, that's what's being priced right now." He added, "But I don't think that means that the overall bull market that started in October 2022 is over."

## Global market declines

Technology-share declines were also reported in global trading. Japan's Nikkei and South Korea's Kospi each fell more than 5% overnight. The report said European indexes also posted sizable declines.

## Consumer, rates and oil signals

The University of Michigan's latest consumer-sentiment index rose sharply from the previous month and came in slightly ahead of economists' expectations. Consumers cited some relief from easing gas prices and improved expectations for business conditions following the midterm elections. The increase reversed the pattern seen earlier in the year, although the reading remained below its February level, before the start of the U.S. war with Iran.

The yield on the 10-year Treasury fell to 4.93% from 5.04% late Thursday.

Brent crude, the international standard, rose 0.6% to settle at $92.57 per barrel amid continued tension near the Strait of Hormuz as the U.S. war with Iran continued. Saudi Arabia said on Friday that Iran-backed Houthi rebels had fired more missiles and drones at the kingdom days after Saudi Arabia launched a major air campaign across Houthi-controlled parts of Yemen. Iraq apparently reached a compromise with a powerful Iran-backed militia that Iraq accused of smuggling oil.

The report's broader account placed AI stocks' outsized market values behind Friday's index losses, which pushed the S&P 500 into a correction and the Nasdaq composite negative for the year. It contrasted those losses with consumer sentiment rising, the 10-year Treasury yield falling to 4.93% and Brent crude settling at $92.57 a barrel as tension persisted near the Strait of Hormuz.
