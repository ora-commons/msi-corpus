## Virginia seeks relief and penalties of up to $2,500 per violation

Virginia filed a lawsuit against SpaceX, the parent company of X and its AI tool Grok, on Nov. 23, alleging that Grok generated realistic sexualized images of people, including children, without consent. The state alleges that the conduct was contrary to SpaceX's pledges to protect users' content and privacy. The case was filed in Richmond City Circuit Court.

In a statement, Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones said: “With a single existing photograph, Grok could, and did, fabricate a realistic image or video of a person nude, or partially nude, or engaged in sexual activity, all without the individual’s knowledge or consent. This violation is not acceptable.” He called the suit a “first step towards holding this company accountable.”

Jones referenced a study by the Center for Countering Digital Harm. The study reported that between Dec. 29, 2025, and Jan. 8, 2026, Grok created and posted about three million sexualized images on X. That total included “23,000 images that appeared to depict children,” the study said.

According to the study, nearly 30% of the sexualized images of children were still visible in X posts a week or more after they were identified. Images marked “removed” by the platform were also still accessible through separate URLs.

The Virginia lawsuit was filed in coordination with Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield, who lodged a separate but similar suit in Oregon. Rayfield said, “It’s no surprise that Grok and X became a haven for perpetrators using AI to create and share child sexual abuse material.”

Rayfield also said, “The lack of needed safety measures was considered a feature, not a bug, which they promoted as ‘spicy mode.’”

Virginia's complaint alleges that SpaceX “derived substantial revenues and other commercial benefits, including benefits in the form of data collected from consumers, via Grok’s use in the Commonwealth.” The lawsuit seeks relief for X users and victims in Virginia and civil penalties of up to $2,500 for each willful violation of the Virginia Consumer Protection Act. A hearing has not yet been set.

European Union regulators representing 27 nations began investigating X in January over Grok's sexualized images of people, including content that “may amount to child sexual abuse material,” PBS News reported. Malaysia and Indonesia briefly blocked access to Grok earlier this year over concerns about sexualized deepfake pictures.

Jones added: “Companies that deploy powerful tools don’t get to ignore consumer safety and call it innovation. The law applies to everyone, and this office will hold big tech accountable when they break the law.”
