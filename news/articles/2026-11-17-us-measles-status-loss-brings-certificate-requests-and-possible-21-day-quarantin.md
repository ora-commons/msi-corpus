## Brewer says restoration requires an integrated national plan

Americans are being asked to dig up decades-old vaccination certificates and could face 21-day quarantines after measles transmission became endemic in the United States, The Guardian reported. Cases once typically arrived with international travelers, but sustained domestic transmission now exposes residents to new risks of infection. The Guardian described the designation as carrying real consequences for Americans, including requests for old vaccination documentation and possible quarantine.

David Margolius of the Big Cities Health Coalition said earlier in November: “Now is the time to go and find that evidence, because now this is a real day-to-day life thing that could happen to you.”

The Guardian reported that expert epidemiologists recommended the US status change during a closed-door meeting in Washington DC. A range of evidence would have been presented, including interviews with people who had tested positive, collected by local US health authorities, and whole-genome sequencing that can provide a fingerprint of the virus's origin.

Noel Brewer, a behavioral scientist at the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill who studies vaccination behaviors and specifically measles, chairs a committee that considers evidence on US elimination status and contributed to the scientific work that underpinned the Pan American Health Organization's decision. Re-establishing elimination status will require “a national plan to eliminate measles again,” Brewer said, describing it as “an integrated plan that’s publicly communicated with specific tasks assigned to named parties.” He said the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention and the president should speak out “forcefully” in favor of vaccination, and Congress should allocate more funding.

The Pan American Health Organization, a regional branch of the World Health Organization, announced on November 17, 2026, that the United States had lost its measles elimination status. The recognition that transmission was endemic came after the country had held elimination status for 26 years. The Guardian reported more than 5,400 US cases and eight deaths in 2026 amid the worst outbreak since 1991.

Dr Jarbas Barbosa da Silva Jr, the PAHO director, said: “Measles is resurging globally amid the persistent gaps in vaccination coverage, and this global trend is also affecting the Americas.” He said: “The endemic measles transmission has now been re-esatablished in Bolivia, Mexico and United States.” Silva made the final decision on US elimination status.

The Guardian reported that Donald Trump and his health secretary, Robert F Kennedy Jr, had spent nearly two years sowing confusion about vaccine safety. In one recent example, Trump called for the measles, mumps and rubella combination vaccine, known as MMR, to be split into separate shots, arguing without evidence that “together, there could be a possibility they’re quite lethal”.

Experts cautioned against attributing measles outbreaks solely to the Trump administration. The Guardian reported that the MMR vaccine had been the target of misinformation for more than two decades, including work by Kennedy before his White House role. Some experts argued that leaders, not misinformation, change health behaviors.

Noel Brewer said: “There is a profound problem with measles in all three North American countries.” He added: “The US has had large outbreaks and exported those to Canada and Mexico, and those cases have come back again to the US.”

David Margolius said: “What we’re experiencing is not a United States specific thing,” describing a “global surge in cases, global decline in vaccination rate.” The Guardian noted that the UK and several European nations had also recently lost elimination status.

The Big Cities Health Coalition, which leads health departments that serve 47 million Americans, called the announcement a “profoundly sad moment for our country.” In a statement, BHC members said: “Recent declines in measles vaccination rates have opened the door to outbreaks across the country, resulting in preventable illness, hospitalizations, and tragic loss of life.” They added: “These outcomes are especially heartbreaking because measles is a disease that we know how to prevent. Yet too many voices spreading fear and false information are exacerbating the problem.”
