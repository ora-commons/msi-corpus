## Cat found alive and reunited with teen owner after Brooklyn collapse

**Subtype:** fact

- New York City officials said Sunday that a cat missing since last week's deadly Brooklyn building collapse was found alive and reunited with 13-year-old Zariah Coleman.
- Allie Taylor, director of the Mayor's Office of Animal Welfare, said her office worked with the veterinary nonprofit Flatbush Cats to locate the cat, named Isaac, and return him to Zariah.
- Workers found Isaac on Sunday in a cabinet under the kitchen sink of Zariah's apartment after hearing a soft meow, Zariah and her mother told The New York Times.
- The Wednesday collapse killed eight people, injured 18 others and forced the evacuation of several nearby buildings, including the one next door where Zariah lives.

## Isaac survived four days without food or water under a sink

The search for Isaac ended four days after the collapse tore off the side of Zariah's building and left a hole where her bedroom wall had been. Zariah and her family had not been allowed back inside since Wednesday, when the neighboring building collapsed, killing eight people, injuring 18 others and prompting evacuations of several nearby structures.

Waiting outside as workers carried the cat's cage out of her apartment building, Zariah hugged the cage. Isaac had gone four days without food or water. After his rescue, he was taken to Flatbush Cats' veterinary clinic for a checkup.

"I had it in my heart they would find Issac," Zariah told the Times. "I knew they would."

The reunion was the work of several city and nonprofit offices. Allie Taylor, director of the Mayor's Office of Animal Welfare, said her office worked with Flatbush Cats to locate Isaac and reunite him with Zariah. Mayor Zohran Mamdani heralded the reunion in a social media post, thanking Taylor's office, the city's Buildings Department and the nonprofit for the outcome.

"Isaac is now receiving the veterinary care he needs," Mamdani said. "We're grateful to everyone who helped bring them back together."

At a news conference on Friday, Zariah tearfully spoke about Isaac, telling reporters: "I just really hope they come and get our cat. He's hiding from them, and I think he's scared."

Zariah and her mother recounted Sunday's discovery to The New York Times. The collapse killed eight people, injured 18 others and forced the evacuation of several nearby buildings, including one next door where Zariah lives.
