## Video shows agents taking pregnant protester to the ground outside Modesto jail

ICE agents pepper-sprayed a 23-year-old pregnant US citizen and threw her to the ground during a protest outside the Stanislaus county jail in Modesto, California, on Sunday, according to video of the encounter and an account from her family's attorney.

Lizbeth Vargas Andrade, who is 12 weeks pregnant, was filming and protesting the presence of several ICE officers outside the jail before officers took her down and arrested her on her stomach during the encounter, the footage and her attorney's account show.

A commotion began when ICE agents moved to arrest a man who appeared to have just been released from the jail, video shows. The circumstances of that arrest were not immediately clear, and the man was not identified. Agents wore law enforcement vests and ICE badges, and at least two of them had masks covering their faces, according to the footage.

Vargas Andrade's husband, Adolfo Talavera, filmed the scene as several people protested the agents making the arrest. In the footage, Vargas Andrade holds a phone in one hand as she and an agent appear to bump into each other; the agent grabs her and begins to handcuff her, and another agent pulls out what appears to be a pepper-spray canister and points it toward Vargas Andrade and her husband. The footage partially captures the spray being deployed, though it was difficult to discern how long it lasted or where the chemicals landed.

Seconds later, two agents took Vargas Andrade to the ground and handcuffed her with her hands behind her back on her stomach, before one agent moved her onto her side, video shows.

The sound on the footage repeatedly cuts out, and what happened immediately before Vargas Andrade's arrest was unclear. One woman was heard screaming at the agents to get away from her, and another protester cursed at the agents, telling them, "You should be embarrassed … you guys are so fucked up."

## She told reporters she warned agents she was pregnant

Vargas Andrade said she is part of a group of local residents who monitor ICE sightings and that she had gone to the jail parking lot after agents were spotted in the area. She spoke at a press conference Sunday evening.

"They threw me to the ground, they maced me, then they handcuffed me," Vargas Andrade told reporters. "I told the ICE agent … 'I'm pregnant, so don't put your hands on me.' … They right away threw me on the floor … I was burning. I couldn't breathe. The mace went up my nose."

Talavera shouted "She's pregnant with child!" as the agents moved to detain his wife, video shows. Another ICE agent then gave Vargas Andrade water to wash her face, she recalled.

"I got scared," Talavera said. "I was like, what if she has a miscarriage?"

Vargas Andrade said she was particularly worried because she had suffered two previous pregnancy losses. Doctors performed an ultrasound after the incident and did not identify any concerns, she said.

Vargas Andrade was taken into custody and transported to an ICE field office in Stockton, according to her attorney, Patrick Kolasinski. Her sister called an ambulance, which arrived at the ICE facility and took her to a hospital, her family and a local advocate said at the press conference.

## Released the same day, with no charges filed

Vargas Andrade said officers told her she was accused of assaulting an ICE agent, an accusation she denied. She was released later that day, and she has not been charged with any crimes, according to Kolasinski.

Spokespeople for the Department of Homeland Security did not immediately respond to inquiries on Monday. A spokesperson for the Department of Justice declined to comment. A spokesperson for the Stanislaus sheriff's office, which runs the jail, said sheriff's deputies were not involved in Vargas Andrade's arrest.

The encounter happened amid reports of increased ICE activity in California's central valley, the region home to the state's agricultural industry. DHS and the justice department have repeatedly accused anti-ICE protesters of assaulting agents, though many of those prosecutions in California have fallen apart in federal court.

"We're not going to stop helping the community even though this happened," Vargas Andrade said. "We want our immigrants to feel safe here."
