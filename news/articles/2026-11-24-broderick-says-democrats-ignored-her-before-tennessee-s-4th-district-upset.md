## Broderick to become sole Democrat in Tennessee delegation

Victoria Broderick said the Fourth District had been neglected and that voters wanted a representative who understood what they were going through.

> “We’ve been neglected in this district,” she said earlier this month. “People want representatives who understand what they are going through, who understand the costs of going to the gas station or grocery store. I’m that representative.”

Broderick, 34, is a former technology and logistics professional who previously ran for Congress in 2024. In an interview before the race was called, she told The Guardian that she connected with constituents who had not voted in years and with Republicans concerned about affordability who appreciated the “integrity” she brought. The Guardian described her victory over eight-term Republican incumbent Scott DesJarlais as one of the biggest House upsets of the 2026 cycle.

The Guardian reported that Tennessee’s congressional map had been redrawn before this year’s midterm elections as part of the GOP’s mid-decade gerrymandering effort. Election forecasters rated the 4th District “solidly Republican,” and many national Democrats dismissed Broderick’s campaign as a long shot. According to Broderick, she managed to pull off the upset without support from the Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee, the party’s House campaign arm.

> “The Democratic party wasn’t paying attention to this race,” she said. “I knew I was the underdog.”

DesJarlais significantly out-raised Broderick in the run-up to the election. He was 62 and aligned himself closely with Trump during his time in office.

Broderick won on Election Day, 3 November. DesJarlais conceded last week after the razor-thin contest, and the Associated Press officially called the race on Tuesday, reporting that Broderick had prevailed by fewer than 400 votes.

Broderick’s victory expands Democrats’ House majority in the 120th Congress to 259 seats, their largest advantage in three decades. It also flips a seat Donald Trump carried by more than 20 points in 2024 and will make Broderick the sole Democrat in Tennessee’s congressional delegation on Capitol Hill. The Guardian said the result underscored the scale of an upset that few in either party saw coming.

> “We all leave here with our heads held high, and we hope the Fourth District continues to receive the attention and care it so richly deserves,” DesJarlais wrote in a statement. “All of us hope that the new Member of Congress feels the same level of awe and gratitude for this tremendous undertaking that I felt every day I had the privilege of serving.”

On 4 November, the day after the election, Broderick had already declared victory.

> “We did not have the political machine behind us. We didn’t have corporate Pac money, we didn’t have powerful insiders. We had you, the people,” she wrote on social media. “That turns out that is all we needed.”

The Guardian also detailed controversies during DesJarlais’s time in office. The outlet reported that he urged his ex-wife to have two abortions before their marriage despite his anti-abortion stance, and that divorce trial transcripts showed he admitted to sexual relationships with patients. DesJarlais is a physician.


MSI previously reported that [DesJarlais had conceded before the Associated Press formally called the race](/articles/2026-11-16-broderick-wins-tennessee-s-4th-district-after-desjarlais-concedes/). The Guardian’s new account places the official call on Tuesday and adds Broderick’s pre-call interview, her account of running without Democratic Congressional Campaign Committee support and DesJarlais’s fundraising advantage.
