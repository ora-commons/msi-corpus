## Incoming leaders face questions about their readiness

Hakeem Jeffries is seeking a recorded House Democratic vote on his speaker nomination so Democrats can make their views known. Jeffries will have room to spare with one of the biggest incoming House majorities in decades, though questions have surfaced over whether he, Katherine Clark and Pete Aguilar are ready for their first time leading a majority.

House Democrats will meet privately Wednesday to elect their leadership for the new Congress and nominate Jeffries for speaker. The party is restive and eager for change after voters ousted Republicans in a rebuke of President Donald Trump and the party in power, the Associated Press reported. Phone calls and social media posts have flooded the offices of Democrats and newly elected members with demands for a fresh start, and protesters heckled Jeffries last week during a Democratic conference in Denver.

Ahead of Wednesday's closed-door voting, Jeffries said he takes nothing for granted. He was meeting with the caucuses that make up House Democrats and talking with almost all members in what will be their large majority. “I embrace those conversations,” he said.

Jeffries, a Democrat from New York, is running unopposed. He would serve with Rep. Katherine Clark of Massachusetts and Rep. Pete Aguilar of California, who also ran unopposed. Clark would become the first woman majority leader, while Aguilar would become the first Latino to serve as majority whip. If the full House elects Jeffries Jan. 3, 2027, he would become the nation's first Black speaker of the House.

The trio have risen together as the first new generation of leaders of the post-Pelosi era and are preparing to lead a majority for the first time. Wednesday's vote will be the first of their careers for dozens of members-elect who have not yet been sworn in. The full House will hold a separate roll call on Jan. 3, 2027, making the final decision on the speakership.

Support was already taking shape among incoming members. Rep.-elect Brad Lander of New York announced he would support Jeffries when he launched his campaign for Congress. Rep.-elect Randy Villegas of California said he was supporting whoever would help him best deliver for his Central Valley district.

Returning Rep. Emily Randall, D-Wash., said questions about House leadership surfaced during town halls in her district and in direct messages streaming into her Instagram feed. She began calling some of those constituents back. “People have messaged me asking if Bernie Sanders could be the speaker of the House,” she said. “Technically yes, and it would be probably difficult.”

The House can elect anyone speaker, but the Associated Press report said it was not likely to be Sanders because he already has a day job in the U.S. Senate. On one call, Randall said she listened to the constituent's concerns and then told him, “Truthfully, not like blowing smoke, whoever is our speaker — and I think it is going to be Hakeem Jeffries, and I am supporting Hakeem Jeffries for speaker — I think we will accomplish a lot of the things that he was asking us to.”

Lawmakers pointed to a livestream in which Rep. Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez tried to explain the speaker's role. At the Capitol, she said, “There is one candidate in this race: Hakeem Jeffries,” adding, “I think that everyone is far more concerned what that leadership means, what is it promising.”

The contest completes a leadership transition that began when Rep. Nancy Pelosi decided to step aside from leadership in 2022 after the party's electoral defeat handed Republicans the majority. Pelosi, the No. 2 Democrat Rep. Steny Hoyer of Maryland and Rep. James Clyburn of South Carolina had often moved together as they rose into and then stepped back from long-held positions. Jeffries, Clark and Aguilar are the first new generation to replace that era of House Democratic leadership.

A parallel contest is unfolding in the Senate, where Senate Leader Chuck Schumer faces a challenge from Sen. Chris Van Hollen of Maryland. Returning Rep. Yassamin Ansari, D-Ariz., said the situations in the House and Senate “are quite different.” She described Jeffries as a collaborative leader who listens to ideas from progressive caucus members such as herself and others. “His job is to reflect all of us, not just a faction,” Ansari said. “It is more productive to focus on preparing on what we’re going to do in the majority, and what we need and want to get done in those first 100 days.”

Rep. Shomari Figures of Alabama said he would support Jeffries after winning a redrawn district that favored the Republican candidate. Figures said the choice was not difficult and praised Jeffries for leading Democrats through a turbulent period. “It’s not even like a complex question,” Figures said of Jeffries, whom he said “led us through what is likely the most tumultuous two-year tenure of any presidency in the history of this nation.” Figures added, “I’m not going to not stand by him right here on the precipice of history.”

Wednesday's closed-door election will nominate Jeffries and the rest of the Democratic leadership slate, but it will not make him speaker. A recorded party vote would make members' views known before the full House acts Jan. 3, 2027.
