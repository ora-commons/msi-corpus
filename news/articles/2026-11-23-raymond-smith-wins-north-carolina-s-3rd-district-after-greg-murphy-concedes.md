## North Carolina Republican lawmakers shifted Democratic voters between congressional districts

Democrat Raymond Smith became the representative-elect for North Carolina's 3rd Congressional District on Nov. 11, when Republican U.S. Rep. Greg Murphy conceded. Smith had trailed Murphy by 233 votes at the end of Election Day. Tabulation of provisional and absentee ballots on Nov. 10 put him ahead by 293, according to unofficial results from the State Board of Elections.

North Carolina Republican state lawmakers moved some Democratic voters out of Democratic U.S. Rep. Don Davis's 1st District and into Murphy's 3rd District in last year's redistricting, aiming to unseat Davis by making his 1st District more Republican-leaning. Adam Newmark, a political science professor at Appalachian State University, said, "Republicans were trying to grab one more seat, and in doing so, you have to do one of two things." He said, "You either pack more Democrats into those three or four districts that you've crafted out as Democratic districts, or you have to shave off in some Republican districts from the sizable Republican advantage to try to create another district that's for Republicans."

The move made Murphy, who had held office since 2019, vulnerable. With Davis winning reelection and Smith unseating Murphy, some are calling the mid-decade gerrymander a "dummymander."

Smith's arrival in Washington brought him into a delegation of seven new members, each a Democrat who had flipped a seat in the Tar Heel State. He met the other new members of North Carolina's congressional delegation at the U.S. Capitol and shared a group photo on social media.

Smith's path to Washington came with a nine-hour heads up. He received a call around 9 p.m. on Nov. 11 saying he needed to be in Washington the next morning. Orientation for newly elected members of Congress had begun at the start of the week, but Smith said he was not invited until his race was called.

"I caught the first flight out of Raleigh, which was 6 a.m. I threw some stuff in a bag and got to Washington," Smith said. "I lived that life while I was in the military, so I'm used to moving fast and packing quickly and packing light."

Smith spent the week after the election raising funds for a potential recount while the race remained unresolved. He said it took a few days in Washington for his new reality to sink in.

"I started looking at the Capitol. I started looking at the Library of Congress. I started looking around at all of this history, and then it hit me," Smith said. "I said, 'Whoa, wait a minute. You're a congressman now,' and it became pretty emotional at that point."

Smith, 65, is an Army veteran born and raised in Goldsboro and a former educator. He served on the Wayne County school board before becoming a state legislator, representing Wayne and Sampson counties in the North Carolina House for two terms in Raleigh. North Carolina Sen. Gale Adcock, a Democrat from Wake County, was his mentor.

"I just found him to be a delightful person," Adcock said. "He's very easy to talk to. He's very approachable, very knowledgeable, well-spoken, friendly, willing to ask tough questions."

Smith lost his legislative seat in the 2022 election after a round of redistricting. During his time in the legislature, he advocated for more funding for public schools, educators and North Carolina's HBCUs. Smith said education is one of his top priorities for Washington, and he also wants a voice in decisions related to transportation, agriculture, appropriations, healthcare and the military.

His district includes Duplin County, home to the nation's largest pork producer, and Greenville, home to Eastern North Carolina's only Level I trauma center. Marine Corps Base Camp Lejeune and Seymour Johnson Air Force Base also sit within the 3rd District.

"I always tell people that the 3rd is the most eclectic district in the entire state of North Carolina," Smith said. "As I look around the various committees and things of that nature, I'm struck by the need for more attention to armed services." If appointed to the U.S. House Committee on Armed Services, Smith would join Davis and Republican U.S. Rep. Pat Harrigan from North Carolina's 10th Congressional District.
