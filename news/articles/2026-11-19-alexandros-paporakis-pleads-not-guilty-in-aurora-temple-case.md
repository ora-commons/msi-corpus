## Police recovered a rifle, ammunition, tactical vest and patch from Paporakis’s home

Alexandros Paporakis, 19, pleaded not guilty Thursday to all charges in a nine-count indictment alleging that he planned a mass shooting at a Hindu temple in Aurora, Illinois. Paporakis remains detained, and his attorney, Fedor Kozlov, has demanded a jury trial.

Authorities say Paporakis planned to attack the temple on Oct. 11, the first day of the Hindu holiday of Navaratri. Court documents show that Paporakis drove to the temple’s parking lot and tried to fire at a person outside. Police said the gun jammed, preventing the planned attack.

A grand jury indicted Paporakis earlier this month on nine charges, including terrorism and armed violence, both felonies. The indictment also lists weapons-related charges, including possession of a firearm without a serial number. Kozlov told reporters that the charges could carry a maximum sentence of life in prison.

Police said Paporakis was arrested later that day and that he told a co-worker he would try again.

Prosecutors said Paporakis wrote in a document that he was a neo-Nazi and white supremacist who fantasized about killing non-white people. The indictment alleges that Paporakis intended the shooting to terrorize Indian immigrants.

Police said they recovered a rifle, ammunition, a tactical vest and a patch displaying imagery associated with white supremacist extremism from Paporakis’s house.

Paporakis has not been charged with a hate crime, despite calls from local and national South Asian leaders for state and federal authorities to file such charges.

Paporakis appeared briefly in court Thursday in a tan jumpsuit alongside Kozlov and is due back in court Jan. 7.

Paporakis remains in detention after a judge ruled that his release would pose a threat to the community. Kozlov said Paporakis is being held in a medical facility in the Cook County jail.

Kozlov said Paporakis was aware of the gravity of what he was accused of and was “taking it day by day.” “He sees the world as a 19-year-old, in my mind, child,” Kozlov added. “It’s hard to describe and understand how he feels and how he processes it.”

Prosecutors declined to comment after Thursday’s hearing.
