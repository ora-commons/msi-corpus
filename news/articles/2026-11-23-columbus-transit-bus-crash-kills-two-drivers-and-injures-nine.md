## Columbus police cite early indications of a double-yellow-line crossing

At around 8 a.m. Monday, during the morning rush in Columbus, Ohio’s state capital, two Central Ohio Transit Authority buses collided head-on on the city’s north side, according to Columbus police. Columbus Police Sgt. Joseph Albert said a third vehicle also collided with the rear or side of one of the buses. Police said the crash killed both drivers and injured nine other people.

One driver was pronounced dead at the scene. The other was transported in critical condition and died later at the hospital, Albert said.

The nine injured people were either bus riders or passengers in the third vehicle. Columbus police said all were transported to area hospitals in stable condition with non-life-threatening injuries. No pedestrians were injured.

Police had not established what caused the crash. “Early indications are that one of the buses, for reasons unknown, crossed a double yellow line and struck the other bus head-on,” Albert said. A message was left with COTA seeking comment.

Columbus City Council President Shannon Hardin and council member Lourdes Barroso de Padilla, who chairs the public service and transportation committee, called the accident “heartbreaking.”

“Our hearts go out to all those impacted by Monday’s accident, especially to the loved ones of those killed,” they said in a statement. “While we try to understand how this tragedy happened, we must not forget the lives lost and the families that will be mourning them just as we enter the holiday season.”
