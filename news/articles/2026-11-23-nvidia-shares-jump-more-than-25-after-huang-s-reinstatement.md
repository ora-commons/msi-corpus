## Polymarket’s implied probability rose from about 10% to 18% overnight

Nvidia's shares jumped more than 25% when New York markets opened Monday after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive. The announcement marked a sharp reversal from the board's decision days earlier to rule out his return.

Before the announcement, trading on the prediction website Polymarket on Sunday indicated a sharply rising expectation that Huang would be restored. The probability among the site's users rose from about 10% to 18% overnight as Huang overtook Ian Buck, another senior Nvidia executive, in the market for Nvidia's next chief executive. Tens of millions of dollars were wagered on that and similar predictions.

In a statement, Nvidia said its directors had talked to Huang and were “satisfied with respect to Mr Huang’s candor and ability to lead Nvidia.” The company added that it had received input from “senior levels” of the U.S. government and was confident his reinstatement “poses no national security risk.”

Harvey Jones, Nvidia's chair, was appointed this month after Huang's resignation on 12 November. Jones said: “The board believes Jensen is the right person to lead Nvidia, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers.” The Guardian reported that Huang built Nvidia from a video-game chip designer into the dominant supplier of AI processors, steering its roadmap, customer deals and Washington ties along the way.

President Donald Trump had called for Huang's reinstatement overnight, posting on Truth Social: “I hope the Board of Nvidia rehires Jensen Huang. It would be the best thing for the Company, and also, for the U.S.A. President DONALD J. TRUMP.”

Trump said the U.S. government had no reason to believe Huang's return posed a national security risk. He also said the government had no “evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct,” adding that Attorney General Todd Blanche had “confirmed that the US government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.” Trump praised Nvidia's board for making “the correct and proper decision” and added: “The Country needs Jensen’s leadership at NVIDIA in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy.”

Huang resigned on 12 November after revelations of extramarital affairs, including one with a businesswoman whom U.S. authorities suspect of spying for China. Beijing denies the allegation.

Hours before the announcement, The New York Times reported that Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman identified in reports, had been in contact with an official in China's spy agency while she was having an affair with Jensen Huang. People with knowledge of U.S. intelligence reports claimed she remained in contact with the Chinese official until this autumn, The New York Times reported.

Arm Holdings' chief executive Rene Haas, who had been approached to succeed Huang, signed a new agreement that will keep him in his current role until 2033. Elon Musk, who had also called for Huang to return, posted on X: “Welcome back the main man,” alongside a picture of Musk shaking hands with Huang.

Dan Ives, a senior technology analyst, said the Nvidia board had to perform an about-turn because the alternative — a core company in the AI boom entering a crucial period without its CEO and founder — was “the bigger risk to shareholders.” He wrote in a note to shareholders: “At the end of the day despite this Jensen saga the board knew it had no choice but to bring him back and for the US technology sector this was the move it had to make.” Sam North, a market analyst at the trading platform etoro, said: “Jensen Huang’s return underlines how much of the AI trade still rests on one man.”


Earlier, MSI reported that [Huang had offered to return to Nvidia and agreed to testify before Congress](/articles/2026-11-17-jensen-huang-offers-to-return-to-nvidia-and-agrees-to-testify-to-congress/). Nvidia's reinstatement answered the conditional leadership question in that report.
