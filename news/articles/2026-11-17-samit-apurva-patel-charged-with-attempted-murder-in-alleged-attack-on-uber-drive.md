## Prosecutors say they could not prove bias beyond a reasonable doubt

Samit Apurva Patel, 25, has been charged with attempted second-degree murder and carjacking over an alleged attack on Mohamed Yusuf, a Somali American Uber driver in a Minneapolis suburb. Yusuf remains in critical condition with broken ribs, a broken leg, spinal injuries, collapsed lungs and significant internal bleeding, according to court documents.

Court documents say Yusuf was strangled with a seatbelt and run over by his own car early Saturday after picking up Patel. Yusuf was on the phone with his brother, who is also an Uber driver, when he picked up the passenger just before 1 a.m. The filing says the brother heard Patel tell Yusuf he did not like the name Mohamed, then later heard Yusuf screaming in Somali that he was being choked with a belt and run over by the car.

Patel’s parents called for a wellness check after he arrived home appearing intoxicated and “exhibiting signs of emotional distress and belligerent behavior,” according to the court filing. Officers found Yusuf’s car in the driveway with the exterior and driver’s seatbelt damaged. The phone call with Yusuf’s brother was still active through the car’s system, court records show.

Officers said Patel told them he had been drinking, admitted he took an Uber home and claimed not to know where his Uber driver was. Officers found Yusuf in a patch of burs in a ditch off the side of the road using location data from his smartwatch and a drone with thermal imaging.

Hennepin County Attorney Mary Moriarty said prosecutors could not prove beyond a reasonable doubt that Patel’s behavior was motivated by bias. She said prosecutors’ inability to establish bias beyond a reasonable doubt “does not diminish the pain that the family and the Somali community are feeling right now.” Moriarty said the motive remains unknown.

“There were some serious issues going on with this gentleman at the time he went home that so alarmed his parents that they called 911,” Moriarty said.

Online court records did not list an attorney for Patel, who was due to make his first court appearance Tuesday afternoon. A person who answered a phone number associated with Patel’s address said, “No comment,” before hanging up.

Uber said in a statement that it was horrified by the attack, had been in touch with Yusuf’s family and had banned the rider from the platform.

Jaylani Hussein, executive director of the Minnesota chapter of the Council on American-Islamic Relations, said the incident in a Minneapolis suburb has increased fear after years of targeted rhetoric against Somali Americans, including from President Donald Trump. “We feel as a community we’re under threat, constantly,” Hussein said. He also said, “Our community has, unfortunately, been feeling the brunt end of a disinformation and demonization campaign that has been going on now, and it’s coming from every corner.”

Minnesota has the largest Somali population in the U.S., with a concentrated share in the Minneapolis-St. Paul area. The overwhelming majority are American citizens.

The Trump administration’s largest immigration enforcement operation, Operation Metro Surge, targeted the Twin Cities. Trump said immigrants from the war-torn East African country have “caused a lot of trouble” and said he wants them to leave. He has also referred to Somali immigrants in the U.S. as “garbage.”

Hussein said dozens of community members had been stopping by the hospital to show support for Yusuf. Fear has been particularly acute among Uber drivers, he said, adding that many felt extremely vulnerable because of the way the incident happened.
