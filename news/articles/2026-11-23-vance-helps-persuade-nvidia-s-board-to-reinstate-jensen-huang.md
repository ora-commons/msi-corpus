## Trump said the board sought assurance Huang posed no security threat

Nvidia reinstated co-founder and longtime CEO Jensen Huang on Monday after Vice President JD Vance worked Saturday and Sunday to persuade the chip maker's board to bring him back, according to a U.S. official and another person familiar with the conversations. President Donald Trump had asked Vance to examine whether Huang had done anything wrong or represented a security risk, the official said.

Nvidia's Monday morning announcement cited “input from senior levels of the United States government” that convinced the board Huang's reinstatement would not pose a national-security risk. Vance told the board that the U.S. government did not view Huang as a national-security risk, according to the official. The person familiar with the conversations said Vance consulted Attorney General Todd Blanche and others before approaching the board. Trump told Vance he had not seen evidence that Huang had done anything wrong or represented a security risk, the official said. Trump also said on CNBC that he had spoken to the board “directly and indirectly.”

Huang resigned earlier this month after revelations that he had two affairs, including one with a woman U.S. officials thought was working with Chinese intelligence. During a Monday interview with CNBC, Trump said the board had asked “for assurance” that the former CEO did not represent a national-security threat given his prior entanglement with Alice Huang, an executive at the Singapore-based company Megaspeed who was suspected of ties to Chinese intelligence.

The Wall Street Journal said the intervention gave Vance an opportunity to burnish his Silicon Valley credentials. He moved to San Francisco after graduating from Yale Law School and later worked in venture capital and the biotech industry. Vance became close to Peter Thiel, the co-founder of PayPal, and developed relationships with other prominent technology executives.

Vance also remains close to David Sacks, a venture capitalist who co-chairs the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology and has emerged as a prominent defender of Huang. The intervention put senior U.S. officials directly into Nvidia's leadership deliberations. The Wall Street Journal said it underscored how vital the administration viewed Huang and Nvidia to the artificial-intelligence sector and the broader economy.

The dispute over Huang's return also reaches beyond corporate management into a larger debate over the future of AI. Huang has long argued for the democratization of AI and for so-called open-source models, which allow individuals to access the technology directly.

The Wall Street Journal reported that Trump and Vance became convinced Huang represented an essential counterweight to industry figures advocating so-called closed-source models, where AI development is sequestered and controlled by relatively few firms. The White House thought Huang could play a role in keeping those closed-source companies from becoming too powerful.

Trump, whom The Wall Street Journal described as fixated on maintaining U.S. dominance in AI, told CNBC he worried Huang might take his talents directly to a Chinese firm. According to a U.S. official, Trump and Vance had also concluded that reinstating Huang at Nvidia gave the United States the most leverage to control the development and export of advanced semiconductors.

“President Trump was totally involved and this is a great decision by the Nvidia board. We have to protect our geniuses,” White House communications director Steven Cheung said.
