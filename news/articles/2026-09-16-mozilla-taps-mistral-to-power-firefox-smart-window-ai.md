## Deal reflects Mistral's shift beyond enterprise customers

Mozilla will embed Mistral's artificial-intelligence models into the Firefox browser's Smart Window AI assistant, Mistral co-founder and CEO Arthur Mensch said in an interview. The partnership launches in the United States, Canada and France, with the United Kingdom and Germany expected to follow later this year.

Mistral said Mozilla would leverage its AI models to power Firefox's AI assistant, betting on a partnership with a browser provider known for its privacy and data protections to win over more users and expand its consumer base.

Smart Window is Mozilla's AI browsing assistant that helps users process complex searches, source information based on their tabs and remember details they may have clicked away from. Firefox already allows users to choose between chatbots including OpenAI's ChatGPT, Anthropic's Claude, Google Gemini and Mistral's Vibe — formerly Le Chat. Under the partnership, Mistral will soon become the recommended AI model through Smart Window.

Mensch said embedding Mistral's models within Firefox would help drive awareness among users of "what is ultimately a consumer product" and boost Mistral's business because the tokens AI models use to process information and create responses generate revenue for the company. "We have very strong alignment in terms of promoting an open ecosystem, and the fact that these are going to be open-source models promoted by an open-source product is great and appealing to many users," Mensch said.

The French startup has developed a network of alliances giving it exposure to enterprise customers — big corporations and institutions that pay for AI tools at scale and boost revenue. Enterprise customers have been Mistral's primary focus since its early days, and joining forces with Mozilla marks a step in its effort to ramp up expansion beyond enterprises and put its models directly in the hands of millions of consumers who use Firefox.

For Mozilla, the partnership provides multilingual model capabilities to power Smart Window and bring the assistant to more countries. For Mistral, the startup gains easy distribution to millions of Firefox users where Smart Window is available.

Anthony Enzor-DeMeo, CEO of Mozilla Corp., said Firefox averages about 200 million users monthly, with more than 50 million daily desktop users and between 14 million and 16 million mobile users. The United States, Germany, France and the United Kingdom account for the bulk of that audience, he said.

Mozilla Corp., a subsidiary of the not-for-profit Mozilla Foundation, has built a reputation around browser privacy protections, automatic tracker blocking and giving users control over whether and how to use AI features. "I don't think the future of the internet should be one company owning the browser, the model, and the data on the user," Enzor-DeMeo said. "That seems like a stark future."

Mistral has publicly championed open-weight models — AI systems whose trained parameters are released for others to download and modify — arguing they give users flexibility to tailor the technology while protecting data. Mensch said the partnership reflects that shared orientation.

The Mozilla deal gives Mistral exposure to millions of Firefox users. Firefox continues to support chatbots from OpenAI, Anthropic and Google as user-selectable alternatives alongside the newly recommended Mistral.
