## Ferguson urges every SeaTac airline to demand ICE leave

Protesters marched inside and around Seattle-Tacoma International Airport on Sunday, carrying bullhorns and signs that read “ICE out of Washington” and “Abolish ICE.” Footage and photographs showed demonstrators walking through the terminal and chanting “ICE out now” and other rallying cries. Reuters photographs by David Ryder showed protesters at the airport after ICE officers made the arrests; one photograph showed a man arguing with a police officer during the protest.

Seattle Mayor Katie Wilson joined the criticism of ICE's tactics, calling them “unacceptable and un-American.”

At a Saturday news conference with labor leaders and local elected officials, Washington Democratic Gov. Bob Ferguson said, “The video is horrific and it is chilling.” He called ICE's tactics “deeply un-American” and called for the agency's abolition. Ferguson said in a briefing, “I made my view very clear to Alaska Airlines: They need to call on ICE to get out of our airports.” He said he would make the same request of every other airline operating at SeaTac.

The Guardian embedded a 53-second video filmed by fellow passengers that showed the Saturday incident, and the footage quickly went viral on social media. Mendoza and Mejia were boarding an Alaska Airlines flight on Saturday when masked officers detained them. Other passengers used their cellphones to film the officers, who wore unmarked jackets and khaki pants. The video showed the officers struggling with someone on the ground. The flight, bound for Los Angeles, was delayed.

The Guardian reported that ICE had increased operations at airports since the summer and shifted from citywide raids to operations more specifically directed across the US, with arrests reaching record levels.

In a statement to The Guardian, ICE said its officers detained Mendoza, an immigrant from Mexico, after he overstayed his visa “in violation of our nation’s laws.” The agency said Mejia, also from Mexico, was in the US illegally and had overstayed her visa. ICE said Mejia “hurled hot coffee on the officers” and could be charged with assaulting a federal officer.

On Sunday, attorney Olia Catala said Mendoza and Mejia were traveling to attend a family baptism in California when masked agents detained them. Catala told the Associated Press that they were seeking asylum in the US, where they had lived since 2015, and that they had no criminal record.

Mejia, who is in her mid-20s, had married a US citizen and filed paperwork for adjustment of her status. Mejia's husband accompanied them but was not detained. “They’ve done everything correctly, and these are educated people,” Catala said. “She went to college, to university, she worked for a nonprofit. No criminal history [for] any of them. So it’s just heartbreaking.”

Catala said Mejia was carrying a cup of coffee when the confrontation occurred. “When somebody’s charging at you, you’re going to drop a cup of coffee,” Catala said. Catala described the scene this way: “It was just a lot of commotion, and honestly, if somebody is running at you or charging at you and they’re not wearing a uniform, you don’t know who they are.” She added, “There was a lot of panic, chaos, not knowing what’s going on.”

Catala said Mendoza and Mejia were being held at the Northwest ICE Processing Center in Tacoma.

Alaska Airlines said ICE officers gave no advance notice of the enforcement action, which occurred during a busy travel weekend ahead of the Thanksgiving holiday. The airline said it was working with the Port of Seattle, which manages the airport, to see if protocols were followed, and had raised concerns “directly with the appropriate authorities.”

The Port of Seattle said in a statement that the arrest was distressing not only for the family but for other passengers. The Port of Seattle said local authorities would “engage federal officials to understand more about what occurred.”
