## Florida gambling helpline director sentenced for $1.4 million theft

## Sullivan received nine years and eight and a half months in prison

On 16 October, Florida state judge Timothy Register sentenced Philip Sullivan, 43, to nine years and eight and a half months in prison. Sullivan had pleaded no contest to a half-dozen felony charges of grand theft, and Register adjudicated him guilty. Bay County authorities had charged Sullivan in March 2024 with stealing $1.4 million from Royal American, the Panama City Beach real-estate company where he served as chief financial officer; local outlets WMBB and WJHG had previously reported the case.

An internal audit of Royal American revealed numerous unauthorized company credit-card transactions and brought Sullivan under scrutiny. The audit preceded the March 2024 charges, while Sullivan’s no-contest plea on 16 October left Register adjudicating him guilty.

Sullivan’s employment with Royal American ended in 2024. According to his LinkedIn profile, in March 2025 — a year after his employment ended — he became director of 888-Admit-It, the Florida Council on Compulsive Gambling’s hotline. Sullivan said he took on the role as part of an effort to rebuild his life after the criminal charges.

Character witnesses for Sullivan asserted in court that he developed a gambling addiction after being prescribed the pharmaceutical stimulant Adderall around the time of the thefts. The case against Sullivan concerned a theft that he said financed his gambling problem.

Little more than a week before his sentencing, Sullivan appeared on the Addicted Recovery Show podcast. He said he had progressed from $25 sports bets to wagers of $50,000 at a time and had lost more than $2m through gambling. Sullivan also said he was grateful that his wife had stayed with him through his legal issues.

The helpline role positioned Sullivan to speak with more than 460 people with struggles similar to his, he said on the podcast. “It’s hard not to feel a certain way about gambling when it’s not only your account – but you talk to other people and you see … the pain that it’s causing them, too,” Sullivan said. He added: “I’m incredibly thankful for that opportunity. It has given me greater purpose than I feel like I’ve ever had in my life.”

Sullivan discussed how he and his wife would prepare their sons, ages 12 and six, for the prospect of his imprisonment. “We just have to prepare them for me being gone,” Sullivan said, becoming teary-eyed. “I can’t have them go to school that day [of the sentencing] wondering if … I’m going to be home.”

Register ordered Sullivan to pay restitution and remanded him to state custody to begin serving his sentence. The judge credited Sullivan with 40 days spent incarcerated between the time he was charged and when he posted bail while the case was pending, court records showed. WMBB reported that Sullivan had 30 days to appeal his sentence. The Guardian requested comment from the attorney listed for Sullivan.

The National Council on Problem Gambling estimates that about 2.5 million adults annually meet the criteria for a severe gambling problem in the US. The council estimates another 5 million to 8 million US adults would be considered to have mild or moderate gambling problems. Commercial casinos, online betting and prediction markets are multibillion-dollar businesses in the US.
