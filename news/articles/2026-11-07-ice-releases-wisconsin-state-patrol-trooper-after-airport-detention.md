## South African trooper had federal work authorization and a pending asylum case

A Wisconsin state patrol trooper detained by federal immigration agents has been released from custody, his law firm said Friday. ICE agents approached Carel Aaron Van Der Merwe, a South African national, on Thursday as he waited to board a domestic flight, according to his lawyers at Christopher & De León.

Van Der Merwe will remain subject to electronic monitoring and regular check-ins with immigration authorities. “His release is welcome news. But our fight is not over. Carel will still have to defend his immigration case before an immigration judge,” the attorneys wrote.

Van Der Merwe, a South African national, immigrated to the US at 14 and has been waiting seven years for his asylum case to be heard, his attorneys said in social media posts. In the meantime, he received work authorization, valid through 2030, to serve in Wisconsin's law enforcement. Video of his graduation ceremony last year showed his badge being pinned on by his father in front of Wisconsin's governor.

Van Der Merwe's family of white South Africans arrived during Donald Trump's first term. They had good reason to expect that they would be granted asylum after Trump returned to office last year and signed an executive order saying the US would “promote the resettlement of Afrikaner refugees.” The Guardian reported that the order was based on the false claim that they would be “escaping government-sponsored race-based discrimination” at home.

The Department of Homeland Security defended the detention in a statement this week, saying Van Der Merwe had overstayed a 2019 visa. “Work authorization or a pending application do not confer legal status in the US,” an agency spokesperson told the Milwaukee Journal Sentinel.

Van Der Merwe's lawyers rejected that position, noting that US Citizenship and Immigration Services, another DHS agency, “authorized him to work.” “We don't think ICE even believes its own statement,” the law firm added. In announcing the release, the attorneys wrote: “USCIS, an agency within DHS, authorized Carel to work. He used that authorization to serve and protect Wisconsin communities. ICE, another agency within DHS, then detained him. DHS owes this family more than silence.”

The agency did not immediately respond to an inquiry about what prompted the state trooper's release.

Van Der Merwe's law firm advised noncitizens with unresolved immigration paperwork to consult immigration attorneys before traveling by air. “Even if you have flown without problems before, do not assume the same will happen now,” the firm said.

The Guardian placed the airport detention within a broader immigration-enforcement pattern that included highly public ICE raids across several US cities, some of which were deadly, and detentions at domestic and international airports. Travelers with visas, green-card holders and people with pending asylum cases were detained at those airports. Some experts said the airport detentions were an effort to meet arrest quotas imposed on ICE by the White House.
