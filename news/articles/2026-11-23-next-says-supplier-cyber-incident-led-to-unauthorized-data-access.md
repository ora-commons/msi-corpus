## Next's current assessment: affected information cannot facilitate fraud against customers or Next

Next, the clothing and homewares retailer, warned Monday that hackers had gained access to data relating to the business through a third-party technology supplier. The company said it had been informed that one supplier had “experienced a cyber incident,” resulting in unauthorized access to the data.

Next said it believed customers’ personal data had not been compromised. The company described the information affected as “limited in nature and, based on our current assessment, cannot be used to facilitate fraud against our customers or Next.”

The Guardian said it was understood that the accessed data did not include information such as contact details, addresses, passwords or bank details — details that could be used to commit fraud.

Next said its own systems had not been compromised, although the incident was “affecting the supplier’s environment” in its business.

The Guardian described the Next incident as coming amid a spate of cyber-attacks against high-profile retailers around the world. It reported that fashion retailers including the owner of Hobbs and Whistles, TFG London and the online fashion seller Asos have faced cyber-attacks in which customer data was accessed.

Looking further back, The Guardian said several other British retailers, including Marks & Spencer, the Co-op and Harrods, were hit last year by damaging cyber incidents that affected operations for months.

Separately, The Guardian reported that hackers caused many millions of euros in damage in continental Europe through attacks on fridge and freezer control systems in recent months.

The Guardian said it emerged last week that a secret UK government cybersecurity operation had warded off the risk of a cyber-attack on fridges and freezers in UK supermarkets, warehouses, restaurants, petrol stations and stadiums. The outlet said the operation came after hackers caused the European damage described above.

Next said investigations into the hack were in progress. In a statement to investors, the retailer said: “Despite the limited and benign nature of the affected data, we are treating the matter with the utmost seriousness and are working closely with the supplier, law enforcement and relevant regulatory authorities.”
