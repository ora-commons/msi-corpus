## Schumer calls Trump's Putin comments 'embarrassing, despicable, and quite frankly pathetic'

Jay Clayton, the US director of national intelligence, said Sunday that he trusts Donald Trump "completely" after the US president called Vladimir Putin "a friend" during their recent meeting in China, defending the language as fitting for a summit of the three main nuclear powers.

Clayton made the remarks during media appearances in which he was pressed on Trump's warm characterization of the Russian president — who is viewed as a chief US adversary — following his trilateral summit with Putin and China's Xi Jinping.

After a backlash at home and abroad, Trump doubled down on Saturday and posted a photo of himself and Putin shaking hands with the caption "My Friend."

On CNN's State of the Union, Dana Bash asked Clayton whether he also thinks Putin is a friend to the US.

"The president is the greatest conductor of foreign policy in a very challenging time that we have seen," Clayton said. "Getting the three nuclear powers together and getting them to agree that they are not going to escalate, that they are going to communicate, that is historic. That is remarkable and, in that context, whatever language the president wants to use, I trust him completely."

Bash pressed on whether Clayton personally agrees that Putin should be considered a friend.

"Dana, in different contexts, you can use different words. The president pulling off that summit, getting the three of them together, is just remarkable," Clayton replied.

Speaking with ABC's Jonathan Karl on This Week, Clayton made similar comments, saying that "using the term 'friend' is entirely appropriate" in the context of the summit.

"That conversation, that picture – historic, historic picture – was in the context of the three nuclear powers, the three main nuclear powers, getting together and saying, 'We cannot have a nuclear incident,'" Clayton said, referring to Trump's post. "In that circle of that conversation, using the term 'friend' is entirely appropriate."

Clayton also dodged when asked again whether he thinks Putin should be considered a friend to the United States. "Is Vladimir Putin America's friend? He is not America's friend in Ukraine. Not at all. Not at all," Clayton responded.

Last week, Trump sidestepped questions about his views on a friendship with Putin. "I can't speak to America," Trump said in a press briefing. "I have fantastic relationships with Putin, that's a very good thing."

Trump's characterization of Putin has drawn criticism from US lawmakers and officials abroad.

"Who is he? He's the man who has abducted tens of thousands of children, killed tens of thousands of civilians, targeted civilian infrastructure across Ukraine, detained Americans wrongfully, and continues to do so daily," Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer posted on social media.

"Embarrassing, despicable, and quite frankly pathetic," Schumer added. "Senate Democrats will always stand with Ukraine and against Putin and autocratic dictators around the world."

"It's beyond me," Senator Jeanne Shaheen said in an interview with MS NOW. "I don't understand the president's admiration and cozying up to Vladimir Putin, who is a war criminal."

Criticism also came from abroad, including from the deputy leader of the rightwing party Reform UK. "It's just wrong at every level … And it's a grave mistake," Richard Tice told Sky News. "He is not our friend, Putin. He is, as others have said, a murderous dictator. And no one is in any doubt about that."

Trump's description of Putin as a friend also drew criticism from European allies of Ukraine. "Mr Putin is not our friend and will never be," the Polish prime minister Donald Tusk said.

Separately, Clayton faced questions about the recent spate of technology outages across the US and said Americans should expect more disruptions as companies and government agencies replace aging systems.

"And as part of that ongoing upgrade, we are going to have glitches, gaps, interruptions in service," Clayton said on CNN's State of the Union. "That is going to happen."
