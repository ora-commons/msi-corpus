## Treasury secretary points to Commerce over chip export oversight

Megaspeed said on Sunday it had launched an internal investigation into its business. The company also said Alice Huang — a suspected China spy linked to Nvidia's former boss Jensen Huang — left her executive role in August last year.

The Singapore-based company said it was aware of reports "containing assertions about our business, supply chain and global operations that are not supported by verifiable evidence". Megaspeed added that it "conducts legitimate and lawful business" and has "retained external legal counsel to conduct a thorough internal investigation".

Megaspeed is the biggest buyer of Nvidia chips in south-east Asia. The company said it "designs and maintains its global operational and supply-chain control frameworks to comply with all applicable laws and regulatory regimes", including "US export control requirements and local jurisdiction rules in every market where we operate".

Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive of Nvidia, the AI chip-making giant, after it emerged he had allegedly had affairs with Alice Huang as well as with one of his employees. The scandal has raised questions about the possibility that high-end AI chips may have ended up in China despite US export restrictions.

Megaspeed is suspected of giving backdoor access to Nvidia chips to Chinese companies via its data centres in Malaysia, Indonesia and Thailand. Nvidia has been banned from selling its advanced chips to China.

The dispute has also opened a rift between two members of President Trump's cabinet. On Friday, US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent blamed fellow Trump cabinet colleague Howard Lutnick's commerce department for failing to properly oversee the export of high-end chips.

Bessent told CNBC that several agencies "were in favour of putting Megaspeed on the entity list for months... but Commerce has the pen... and you will have to ask them about their internal process". The so-called entity list restricts US trade with overseas firms and individuals.

Lutnick has been called to testify before the Senate Banking Committee next month over his department's role in allowing Nvidia to export its advanced chips. Other congressional committees have asked both Lutnick and his department to retain all records relating to Nvidia and Jensen Huang.

Singapore does not prohibit the transfer of advanced AI chips to companies abroad, though such moves can breach US export controls. Singapore has said that while it does not enforce "unilateral export controls imposed by other countries" it expects all companies in its jurisdiction to respect such regulations.

China has denied that Alice Huang is a spy and warned the US not to use the scandal as a "pretext for imposing a technological blockade" on AI technology.
