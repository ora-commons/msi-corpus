## Updated U.S. export rules closed a remote-access loophole for Chinese companies

Nvidia has told Dell, Super Micro Computer and other manufacturing partners to halt server shipments to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia for an unspecified period, according to a person familiar with the matter. The pause followed Washington's update of U.S. export rules closing a remote-access loophole that Chinese companies used to access Nvidia AI chips in Southeast Asian data centers.

The Wall Street Journal reported that Chinese companies had drawn part of their computing power by remotely accessing Nvidia chips hosted in data centers in the four countries. Washington recently updated its export rules to close that remote-access route, part of an escalated effort to prevent Chinese companies from obtaining access to the chips.

The Wall Street Journal described Southeast Asia as the latest front in the U.S. fight with China for artificial-intelligence supremacy. The Institute for Progress think tank estimated that remote data centers in Malaysia and elsewhere supplied some 24% of China's AI compute.

Nvidia is increasing scrutiny of regional business partners to comply with U.S. export requirements, according to a person familiar with the pause. The person familiar with the matter said a process was in place for shipments to be released eventually, though the pause has no specified end date.

The Wall Street Journal described Nvidia as the dominant supplier of chips powering the development of AI models, placing the company at the center of the technology's geopolitics.

The Wall Street Journal also reported that Nvidia recently ousted its chief executive and co-founder, Jensen Huang, after he acknowledged having an affair with a suspected Chinese intelligence asset and chip smuggler. The company brought Huang back days later after President Trump lobbied for his reinstatement.

The report said Jensen Huang's former paramour, Alice Huang, previously worked for Megaspeed, a Singapore-based company that U.S. authorities have investigated since at least 2024 for allegedly smuggling Nvidia chips to Chinese companies.

The instructions to Nvidia's manufacturing partners had previously been reported by the Financial Times, according to The Wall Street Journal.
