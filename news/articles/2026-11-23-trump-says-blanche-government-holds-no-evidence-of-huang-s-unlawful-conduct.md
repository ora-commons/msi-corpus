## Staff, investors and Elon Musk supported petition seeking Huang’s return

President Donald Trump said Attorney General Todd Blanche had confirmed that the government “does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct,” referring to Jensen Huang. Trump called the reinstatement “the correct and proper decision,” wrote that the country “needs Jensen’s leadership at NVIDIA in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy,” and asked Nvidia to continue its internal investigation.

Trump also said the federal government had “no reason to believe” Huang’s return posed a national security risk. The president has a close personal relationship with Huang and had called for his reinstatement as recently as Sunday night.

Nvidia’s board reinstated Huang as president and CEO on Monday, shortly after rejecting his offer to return. Board Chairman Harvey C. Jones said in a statement: “The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers.”

The move came in the fallout from Huang’s admission of extramarital affairs, including one involving a Chinese businesswoman suspected of spying for the country. Nvidia stock was at the center of market volatility after the admission, which also had downstream effects in Washington. Lawmakers and elected officials called for investigations into Huang’s actions and Nvidia’s compliance with export controls.

The announcement came after days of speculation about whether Huang would return. Supporters had set up an online petition calling for him to get his job back, drawing many signatures from current and former Nvidia staff, investors and other Silicon Valley supporters, including Elon Musk.

Huang had posted an online message the previous week saying he would be “honored” to return if Nvidia asked him. The board responded then that it did not intend to reinstate him; Monday’s decision reversed that position.

Huang said in a statement that he was “honored” to return to the company and that he takes the responsibility “with the greatest humility and renewed commitment.” He thanked Jones and interim CEO Colette Kress, who will return as chief financial officer, along with Nvidia’s executive staff for their leadership during the leadership transition.

Huang also said: “I am also deeply thankful to our employees, shareholders, leaders across the industry and partners around the world for their extraordinary support,” adding, “Above all, I am grateful for the love and support of my family. I can’t wait to get back to work.”

The Associated Press said the Nvidia episode was not the first case of a technology leader being fired over transparency concerns and then quickly rehired after an outpouring of support from staff and investors. Three years earlier, OpenAI co-founder Sam Altman was ousted as CEO after a power struggle and then reinstated days later after employees revolted.
