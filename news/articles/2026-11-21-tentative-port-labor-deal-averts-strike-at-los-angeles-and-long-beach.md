## Guard contract extension awaits workers' ratification

Port employers and unionized security guards at the ports of Los Angeles and Long Beach reached a tentative labor agreement on Saturday, averting an immediate strike at the nation's busiest container-handling complex.

The Pacific Maritime Association said the existing labor contract with 250 guards, represented by the local chapter of the International Longshore and Warehouse Union, would be extended pending ratification of the deal by the workers. The contract had been scheduled to expire at 12:01 a.m. Saturday, and the guards had already voted to authorize a strike.

Shipping industry officials had warned that a strike would affect six of the 13 container-handling terminals at the two ports, accounting for more than half of the gateway's cargo. The threat came as retailers rushed last-minute holiday items through the complex.

The guards account for only 2% of the ports' unionized workforce. Other International Longshore and Warehouse Union dockworkers were expected to refuse to cross picket lines if the guard strike proceeded, potentially extending the disruption beyond the security staff involved.

“This agreement provides certainty for the ports, for the cargo owners and workers throughout the supply chain who depend on them, and for the Southern California, regional and national economies,” the employers said in a statement. Union representatives did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Port employers had warned earlier in the week that the prospect of a strike was growing because of a lack of progress in labor talks. The Pacific Maritime Association said its earlier contract offer included a pay increase that would lift average full-time wages to $198,000 from more than $170,000.

The employers did not provide details of the wage offer in the tentative agreement.

The Southern California ports have been deluged with cargo this fall as retailers rushed last-minute holiday items through the complex. In October, the ports moved a combined more than 1 million loaded import boxes, measured in 20-foot-equivalent units, for the second month in a row as the traditional peak shipping season extended into the final quarter of the year.
