## California inquiry examines whether advanced chips or confidential information reached China and whether Nvidia misled investors and the public about the risk.

Nvidia’s shares were down by a fifth since Jensen Huang admitted the extramarital affair, hitting a one-year low at the close of trading on Thursday and wiping almost $1tn from the company’s market value.

Singapore-based Megaspeed bought nearly $5bn (£3.8bn) of Nvidia chips and hardware in recent years and is being investigated for possibly diverting advanced restricted chips to China. Alice Huang, who is not related to Jensen Huang, is associated with Megaspeed.

Megaspeed said it is a “legitimate and lawful” business and that Alice Huang left the company late last year.

California Attorney General Rob Bonta issued the investigative subpoena on Thursday, a week after Nvidia co-founder and longtime chief executive Jensen Huang resigned after it emerged that he had multiple affairs, including one with Alice Huang, whom The Guardian described as a suspected Chinese spy. The subpoena is part of a state inquiry into whether exports of the California-based chipmaker’s advanced semiconductor chips ended up in China, contravening U.S. restrictions.

Bonta said the investigation would also examine whether Jensen Huang’s actions resulted in confidential information being passed to China. “My office is investigating whether Nvidia or Jensen Huang’s actions resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information,” he said. “And if Nvidia misled investors and the public about this risk, in violation of the law.”

“The conduct of Nvidia and its former chief executive raises bright red flags, including about the ways in which the company may have misled investors,” Bonta said. “Nvidia is in a messy, transnational situation but the underlying principle of our corporate securities law is clear: Companies cannot mislead our investors.”

Earlier this week, Nvidia chair Harvey Jones said the company had no intention of reinstating Huang after the former chief executive said he would be “honoured” to return. Elon Musk and a host of tech bosses had campaigned for Nvidia to bring him back.

Huang is due to testify before several congressional committees over the potential breach of national security on 1 December.
