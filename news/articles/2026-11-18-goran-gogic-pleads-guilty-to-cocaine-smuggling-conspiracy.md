## Goran Gogic pleads guilty to cocaine smuggling conspiracy

**Subtype:** fact

- Goran Gogic pleaded guilty in Brooklyn federal court to conspiring to smuggle 20 tons of cocaine from Colombia to Europe through U.S. ports.
- Gogic’s Maritime Drug Law Enforcement Act charge carries a mandatory minimum of 10 years in prison and a maximum of life.
- Prosecutors plan to ask a judge at the Feb. 16 sentencing for 23 years in prison for Gogic.
- Gogic agreed to forfeit $1 million associated with his crimes under his plea agreement.
- A federal judge abruptly dismissed Gogic’s jury a year ago after court officials learned of an alleged $100,000 bribery attempt.

## Prosecutors seek 23 years at Feb. 16 sentencing

On Wednesday, Goran Gogic, 47, pleaded guilty in Brooklyn federal court while shackled at the ankles and speaking through an interpreter. The former heavyweight boxer admitted conspiring to smuggle 20 tons (18.1 metric tons) of cocaine from Colombia to Europe through U.S. ports using commercial cargo ships.

Gogic pleaded guilty to conspiracy to violate the Maritime Drug Law Enforcement Act. The charge carries a mandatory minimum penalty of 10 years in prison and a maximum of life. Prosecutors said they plan to ask, at a sentencing scheduled for Feb. 16, that Gogic be ordered to serve 23 years in prison.

Under the plea agreement Gogic signed with prosecutors, the boxer agreed to forfeit $1 million associated with his crimes.

The cocaine case had been upended a year earlier by an alleged attempt to bribe a juror. Law enforcement authorities had arrested three people in a jury tampering plot.

Authorities alleged that one of the three called a juror in Gogic’s trial multiple times on his cellphone before the juror agreed to meet him on Staten Island. According to a criminal complaint, the man told the juror during the meeting that associates in the Bronx were willing to pay him to return a not guilty verdict. The attempted bribe was $100,000.

When word of the bribery attempt was reported to court officials, a federal judge abruptly dismissed the jury just as it was about to hear opening statements.

Law enforcement officials described Gogic as a “major drug trafficker” who operated on a “mammoth scale.”

Alan Futerfas, Gogic’s lawyer, said the former athlete, who is from Montenegro, is likely to be deported when he is released from prison. Futerfas had no comment Wednesday.

Gogic fought professionally in Germany from 2001 to 2012, compiling a 21-4-2 record, according to boxing website Sport & Note. He was listed as 6-foot-5 (1.96 meters) and weighed in at anywhere from 227 pounds (103 kilograms) to 250 pounds (113 kilograms).

In a news release, U.S. Attorney Joseph Nocella Jr. said, “Gogic stepped outside the ring and into the drug trade, and today justice delivered a knockout blow.”

The case is set for sentencing on Feb. 16, when prosecutors plan to seek 23 years in prison.
