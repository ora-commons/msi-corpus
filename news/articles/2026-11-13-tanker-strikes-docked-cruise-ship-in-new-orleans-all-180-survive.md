## Coast Guard says tanker appeared to lose steering before collision

A tanker that apparently lost control of its steering struck a docked river cruise ship in New Orleans on Friday with 180 people aboard, and every person aboard survived after a large rescue operation pulled passengers and crew from the Mississippi River as water began entering the vessel and it started to capsize.

Video from the scene showed the tanker Atlantic Asphalt hitting the cruise ship while it was tied up at the dock. Tugboat operators, nearby workers, police officers and other rescuers ran to help. Dozens of people aboard the cruise ship and at least two police officers were pulled from the river and lifted onto nearby boats.

The cruise ship began to list within minutes and capsized while emergency workers were still trying to get everyone off it.

New Orleans Mayor Helena Moreno told a press conference that no one was unaccounted for. "No tenemos a nadie desaparecido, lo cual verdaderamente es un milagro en el Mississippi", she said, as rescuers and others applauded. Moreno said 32 people were taken to hospital, but none had critical injuries.

Uninjured passengers and crew were taken to a nearby auditorium, where cruise line officials took a roll call to confirm that everyone was accounted for, Moreno said. Many people lost their identification documents and even their car keys in the disaster, and most were from outside the city, the mayor said. Moreno added that people needed phone chargers, that some had no phones at all, and that "Esto ahora se ha convertido en noticia nacional". They needed to contact their families to let them know they were safe, she said.

Coast Guard Captain Melanie Burnham said the tanker appeared to have suffered "algún tipo de pérdida de dirección", but said the exact cause of the collision was unknown.

The National Transportation Safety Board planned to investigate the crash, but its members could not reach New Orleans immediately because all flights across the country were suspended because of communication problems at regional air traffic control centers.

The cruise ship is operated by American Cruise Lines. Built in 2015, it has five decks and can carry up to 150 passengers, and it was finishing an eight-day cruise to destinations along the Mississippi River in Louisiana and Mississippi.

The tanker, 160 meters (520 feet) long, was bound for New Orleans, according to MarineTraffic, and was sailing under the flag of the Marshall Islands. The vessel specializes in carrying large quantities of asphalt and bitumen, a semi-solid substance often used in road and roofing construction, MarineTraffic said.

The tanker is owned by a company called Nave Shipping SA, registered in Monrovia, Liberia, according to the International Maritime Organization, and it is a subsidiary of China State Shipbuilding Corporation, a large Chinese state conglomerate, according to the ship tracker MarineTraffic. The Chinese company overseeing the tanker in 2022 represented nearly a quarter of the world market for commercial shipbuilding, according to a report from the Stockholm International Peace Research Institute, and was also the world's largest military shipbuilder at the time.

China State Shipbuilding Corporation did not respond to an emailed request for comment on Friday afternoon.

Jeffrey Collins reported from Columbia, South Carolina. Ed White in Detroit, Safiyah Riddle in Los Angeles, M Reo in Washington, D.C., Anna Wilder in Austin, Texas, and Beatrice Dupuy in New York contributed to this dispatch.
