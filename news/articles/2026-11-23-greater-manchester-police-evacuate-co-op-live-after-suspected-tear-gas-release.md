## Clinicians assessed more than 50 people following the arena evacuation

Greater Manchester Police evacuated Manchester’s Co-op Live arena after a substance believed to be tear gas was let off into the crowd near the end of US rapper Don Toliver’s concert on Sunday evening. More than 12,000 people were in attendance, and officers escorted concertgoers from the building so they could make their way home. Police said several people were assessed on site for side effects as a precaution and no serious injuries had been reported.

In a statement, Assistant Chief Constable Steph Parker said, “Our priority was to ensure the safety of every person that was attending the concert.” She said, “Several people were treated on site, and one has been taken to hospital at this time.” Parker also acknowledged concern among people with loved ones at the venue and thanked everyone for their patience as authorities dealt with the incident.

The North West Ambulance Service said it deployed several emergency crews, including its Hazardous Area Response Team, to support safe assessment and treatment at the scene. The service said, “More than 50 people have been assessed by our clinicians, with some reporting eye and respiratory symptoms. Decontamination is being carried out as a precaution and one patient has been taken to Manchester Royal Infirmary for further assessment.”

Teams remained at the venue assessing and caring for patients, and the ambulance service said the incident remained ongoing.

## Advice for attendees who later feel unwell

Police advised anyone who had left the event and had since become unwell to attend their local hospital. The North West Ambulance Service gave separate advice, saying, “Anyone at tonight’s event who has since noticed side effects or become ill should seek medical advice.”

Footage shared on social media appeared to show concertgoers coughing and covering their mouths as they left the venue.

## Inquiry focused on substance and cause

Parker said, “Our declaration of a major incident has allowed us to ensure emergency services and partner agencies can work together quickly and effectively to coordinate a safe response.” She said, “We will now begin to fully understand what this substance was and how this incident came about.”

Greater Manchester Police said inquiries were under way to determine the substance and cause of the incident. The North West Ambulance Service said it continued to work closely with emergency-service partners while inquiries were carried out to establish the substance.

## Venue and tour context

Co-op Live has a capacity of 23,500 people, and American singer Phoebe Bridgers was scheduled to perform at the venue Thursday. Don Toliver was partway through the UK leg of his Octane World Tour when the incident occurred near the end of Sunday’s show.
