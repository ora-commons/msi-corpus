## Threats raise concern about routes and ports far from the Middle East

The United States is investigating whether Iran was involved in dozens of cyberattacks against energy tankers, The Wall Street Journal reported on Nov. 24, citing U.S. officials and others familiar with the matter. U.S. officials identify Iran as a primary suspect, while some also view Russia as a possible culprit.

As many as 50 ships are believed to have been attacked, according to U.S. officials and others familiar with the matter. The vessels carried oil, liquefied petroleum, chemical products and other explosive substances.

Some ships also received warnings and bomb threats. The expanding attacks and threatening messages have raised concerns that Iran is seeking to sow fear among shippers.

The attacks have raised additional concerns that Iran is seeking to disrupt critical maritime routes or cause an explosion at a major port far from the Middle East.

Ships run on integrated systems that, if compromised, could result in an explosion, a collision or an oil spill.

U.S. officials said Iran is a primary suspect in the attacks. Some of those officials also said Russia is seen by U.S. authorities as a possible culprit.

The U.S. Coast Guard deployed cyber protection teams in recent weeks to respond to at least four of the ships as they approached the United States. The teams worked to mitigate the threats before those vessels continued on to American ports.
