## The Guardian reports no evidence Iran would proceed with plans

U.S. officials continued to debate whether Iran could or would execute a reported plot, according to a CNN report citing seven unnamed U.S. officials. CNN reported that new U.S. intelligence suggested Iran was working on plans to use a dirty bomb or a chemical weapon against senior Trump administration officials.

CNN described a dirty bomb as conventional explosives combined with radioactive material. The report said the plans included specific locations and targets.

The unnamed officials said Tehran’s planning was an effort by Iran to “give itself options rather than an indicator of an imminent attack.” CNN reported that U.S. officials “continue to debate whether Iran can or will attempt to execute such a plot.” The Guardian said the plans would signify Iran was willing to escalate its tactics against the United States, but reported no evidence to suggest Iran would go ahead.

President Donald Trump said in the Philippines on Tuesday that the U.S. was “looking into” whether Iran was developing plans to detonate a so-called “dirty bomb” in Washington.

Asked whether there was reason to believe such an Iranian attack was imminent, Trump said: “If there is, they will pay a price the likes of which no country has ever paid before. I hope it’s not, but we take it very seriously because they happen to be crazy.”

In a White House statement, spokesperson Lauren Bis said: “We remain vigilant monitoring any and all potential threats, but at this time there are no credible threats to the homeland.”

The unresolved question was whether Iran could or would execute the reported plans. The White House said there were no credible threats, while the unnamed officials said the planning was meant to give Tehran options rather than signal an imminent attack.

The report came as the United States and Israel were at war with Iran, a conflict that began in February. Early airstrikes killed Iranian officials, including Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei. Trump repeatedly claimed the war would be over soon, but it continued.

Trump argued that U.S. involvement in the war was necessary so Iran did not build nuclear weapons. The Guardian also reported that Iran had blocked trade through the Strait of Hormuz, creating rising fuel costs and rattling the global economy.

The Guardian described the war as deeply unpopular among Americans and said the Republican Party was widely rejected by voters in the midterm elections. The GOP lost control of both the Senate and the House of Representatives.
