## The search for a successor turned up few able and willing candidates

After Jensen Huang's Nov. 12 resignation, Nvidia's board weighed two alternatives: keeping him out or letting him back in. The Wall Street Journal reported that the directors saw both options as suboptimal and that each carried potential consequences. The board had hired Heidrick & Struggles to lead its search for internal and external candidates, and the search turned up few executives able and willing to do the job.

Arm Holdings CEO Rene Haas was seen as a front-runner in that search, according to The Wall Street Journal. Huang had tried to acquire Arm years earlier before running into regulatory roadblocks. Haas had known Nvidia board chair Harvey Jones for more than two decades, having served as an executive at Tensilica, a firm Jones co-founded in 1997, and had worked at Nvidia for about a decade. Haas's discussions with Heidrick progressed, and board members called technology executives for their opinions of Haas and others, according to people familiar with the matter. Arm's management and board, which includes billionaire Japanese tech investor Masayoshi Son of SoftBank, began outreach to Haas as soon as his name was mentioned publicly, to counter Nvidia's interest and to make sure he was happy in his current position, some of the people said.

Huang resigned on Nov. 12 following reports of another affair, this one with an executive at Nvidia, and Colette Kress, the company's chief financial officer, was named interim CEO. The two affairs had been widely reported, including one with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive suspected of having worked with Chinese intelligence, and the disclosures set off an investor sell-off across the AI trade. The Journal reported that Huang was widely considered "a political, legal and business liability," and that Nvidia's board had unanimously accepted his resignation and told investors that he would not return.

Trump publicly suggested former Apple CEO Tim Cook, saying on social media, "A Legend replaces a Legend!" In a Journal interview, Trump said he wanted to call Nvidia's board a week and a half earlier. "I'm not sure I would have been as successful," he said. "The climate became more and more right as the company stock began to tumble." Trump also said he became more convinced Huang was the best candidate to lead the chip designer after seeing the market's response and hearing from other executives such as Elon Musk, who helped lead Silicon Valley's support of Huang.

Nvidia said Jones would remain board chair and interim CEO Kress would continue as chief financial officer. The previous week, both had said the company was not considering bringing Huang back. Kress also said the company was taking "a strong look" at the types of companies it would finance, and The Journal said the remark raised concerns about Nvidia's role in financing companies across the chip industry.

## A Sunday session on candor and honesty

On Sunday afternoon, Nvidia's directors held a marathon session and spent hours talking to Huang, according to people familiar with the matter. Huang disclosed to the board additional affairs beyond the two that had been widely reported but was contrite about his behavior, people with knowledge of the discussion said. After conversations with Huang about the importance of candor and honesty, the board decided that having him return was the best path, the people said. A person familiar with the matter said the board had agreed that Huang broke Nvidia's code of conduct both with his relationships and a lack of candor about them. By Sunday night, the board had decided to reinstate Huang.

## The administration's assurances and a weekend lobbying effort

Attorney General Todd Blanche told Trump and Vice President JD Vance on Saturday that Huang was not the target or subject of any Justice Department investigation, according to U.S. officials. The officials also said Blanche told Trump and Vance that the department had no evidence to suggest that Huang had broken laws or was a national-security risk. Blanche's assessment was underpinned by Nvidia's cooperation for months during Huang's leadership with Justice Department investigations into suspected smuggling of powerful artificial intelligence chips by two of the company's customers into China, according to people with direct knowledge of the matter.

In the 48 hours that followed, Trump, Vance and other top administration officials relayed Blanche's assurances to Nvidia's board members, according to The Wall Street Journal, which noted that Trump had not heard of Huang when he began his second term. The Journal reported that the officials' purpose was to clear Huang's name after intelligence reports about him had circulated at the highest levels of government, and that the lobbying also aimed to help the U.S. stock market and economy. The Journal characterized the behind-the-scenes lobbying as "an unprecedented campaign by a U.S. president to bend a company board to his will."

Trump said he was told the government hadn't found that Huang had done anything illegal, only that "he made some indiscretions having to do with other things." Once he heard there was no illegal activity on Huang's part, "I began a push," Trump said. Trump also said he spoke to the board "in numerous ways" and "through representatives." Trump told advisers that he wasn't concerned about the affair and that Huang needed to come back for the markets to turn around, according to a person who heard his comments, and he said publicly that Huang should be given a second chance. Trump and Vance held discussions with Blanche to learn more about the national-security concerns, officials said, and Trump said in the Journal interview that he also spoke with Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton.

Vance, a former venture capitalist, recognized Huang's role as a U.S. champion of open-weight AI models that are cheaper and easier to customize, some officials said. That critical role in the race against China's tech sector made Trump and Vance think reinstating Huang was a priority, the officials said. Vance also spoke with Huang as part of his efforts to resolve the situation, believing that the relationship he and Trump had with the executive was the best leverage they had to control the chip industry, the officials said. Huang had helped shape the administration's AI and chip-export strategy, while Nvidia makes its own models and supplies other top model developers with chips.

Over the weekend, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who had previously said Nvidia should look outside the company for its next leader, appeared to walk back that comment. He told CBS News that Huang himself was then outside the firm and indicated that the company should investigate others to see who knew about risky chip-export practices, a development some observers viewed as a sign that efforts to bring Huang back were picking up speed. Bessent had been among the officials most concerned by reports that billions of dollars of chips were making their way to China or were being accessed by Chinese companies while located in other countries such as Malaysia and Thailand, people familiar with the matter said. He also helped push forward a rule targeting China's AI industry early last week.

Huang allies close to the president, including Elon Musk and White House AI adviser David Sacks, said the ouster had been premature. Supporters repeatedly referenced that Huang had never been given a defensive briefing by U.S. intelligence, a process commonly used to tell citizens they could be targets of foreign intelligence operations. A letter calling for his reinstatement garnered more than 200,000 signatures, and employees warned of "irreplaceable talent loss" if Huang did not return.

## Earnings, the Monday vote and the market's response

Nvidia shares continued dropping before the company's Nov. 17 earnings report. The results landed minutes after a Journal report that the Securities and Exchange Commission was investigating the company. Even though Nvidia reported faster-than-expected sales growth, the stock kept sliding. After Trump watched Kress on television, the day after Nvidia's Nov. 17 earnings report, he said: "I thought she was very good, but she's not a chip person."

On Monday morning, before the market's opening bell, Nvidia announced that its board had unanimously voted to reinstate Huang as chief executive, reversing the board's Nov. 12 acceptance of his resignation and its statement to investors that he would not return. Nvidia shares quickly recovered much of their roughly 30% decline since the resignation, a slide that had dragged down the broader market and fueled fears of a far-reaching contagion. By the close, Nvidia had held its rebound and helped power a rise of 4.7% in the S&P 500. Nvidia stock notched the largest one-day market-cap gain on record for a U.S. company. Trump said, "The response from the market has been very positive." He added, "That really shows the decision was right."

When board chair Harvey Jones emailed employees about the news on Monday, hearts, thumbs-up, shock, laughing and celebration emojis poured in on Microsoft Outlook. Huang returned to Nvidia's office on Monday to cheers and shouts of "we love you, Jensen!" from employees who lined the stairs and balconies. Wearing a blue zip-up jacket, Huang joked that it was his first day of work and that he did not have a computer before becoming more serious and thanking his staff. In a video seen by the Journal, Huang said, "I'm terribly sorry for putting all of you through all of this." He added, "I've made some very terrible mistakes and I'm going to work on coming back." He thanked employees for their support, saying, "Thank you all for this second chance. I'm going to be a better CEO the second time around."

Trump said he called Huang to congratulate him on being reinstated, and Huang told him about all kinds of new chips he wanted to invent. "There's really nobody like him," Trump said. Trump also said he liked getting involved in companies, pointing to the government buying a 10% stake in Intel. "If I can help a company that's got some problems, I do it," he said. Trump praised the move on social media and then applauded it in a lengthy CNBC interview. Trump told CNBC that China's leader, Xi Jinping, had been complimentary of Jensen Huang during the U.S. president's recent trip to China. Trump said the effort to reinstate Huang atop Nvidia was imperative to keep the U.S. ahead of China in the global AI race. "If we didn't do this, I could see China coming in and saying to Jensen, 'Hey, how would you like to come to China?'"

## Investigations and congressional scrutiny continue

Many national-security questions remain unanswered. The Justice Department is leading two probes of Nvidia customers over suspected chip smuggling into China, and Nvidia has cooperated for months with those investigations into Megaspeed International and Super Micro, according to people with direct knowledge. Super Micro, a maker of data center servers, has been linked to chip-smuggling allegations in the past. A committee of independent Nvidia directors is conducting an internal investigation into whether Huang shared corporate or national-security information during his romantic relationship with Alice Huang, and the board is sharing information with prosecutors.

The Commerce Department on Monday added Megaspeed and several other firms to the trade blacklist, barring U.S. companies from selling them chips and other technology. Megaspeed said it is a compliant company and that it hired outside counsel to investigate the allegations. Super Micro said it is cooperating with authorities and that it put accused employees on leave. Efforts to reach Alice Huang have been unsuccessful. China's Ministry of State Security has been overhauled to identify, approach and leverage potential sources like Alice Huang, and Alice Huang was in touch with the ministry during the affair, according to people with knowledge of U.S. intelligence. The intelligence was inconclusive as to whether she had been actively tasked with collecting intelligence on Jensen Huang.

Lawmakers and administration officials raised concerns that Nvidia chips were being smuggled directly to China in violation of export-control rules. Industry analysts said the shipments could help Chinese tech giants like Alibaba and Tencent develop better AI tools that aid the Chinese military. Many criticized the Commerce Department, which oversees export controls and had stalled actions that would hurt China over the past two years during trade talks. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has a close relationship with Trump. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was publicly critical of Commerce, saying other agencies wanted to add Megaspeed to a trade blacklist but Commerce blocked the move.

Trump had been briefed at least twice about Alice Huang's connections to Chinese intelligence. After Nvidia's board opened an investigation into Huang's conduct and potential national-security issues on Nov. 8, Trump stood by him, saying the Nvidia CEO, who had lobbied him for licenses to ship older chips directly to China, did not know that Alice Huang was suspected of being a spy. Trump also said Huang was "an amazing guy" who had helped keep the U.S. ahead in the tech race. In May, Trump let Huang join his trip to China after Rubio told him about the intelligence community's concerns, and Trump did not think the findings warranted action, people familiar with the matter said.

Lawmakers also called for their own investigations. House committees scheduled Huang to appear next week, and Nvidia was slated to host a conference in Washington showcasing its technology for developers, customers and others. Sen. Kevin Cramer (R., N.D.) said, "The legal and reputational risk of hiring a CEO who may very well be convicted in the United States or other nations of violating espionage and export control laws is now [borne] by the board of Nvidia, but the national security risk is passed along to the United States military and its citizens." Trump said he wasn't worried about concerns from Republican lawmakers that reinstating Huang jeopardizes national security. "I think they'll see the result is so positive because the public has spoken today," Trump said, referring to the stock market. Gregory Allen, chief executive of Decision Tree Research, a research firm focused on chips and geopolitics, said, "It is absurd that Trump intervened before any of the litany of investigations got fully under way."

The Wall Street Journal said the two-week sequence, from the board's Nov. 8 investigation into Huang's conduct through his resignation and reinstatement, "marked an extraordinary comeback for one of the world's most powerful tech leaders" and "signaled that Trump is uncowed in his aggressive and unconventional approach to governance." The Journal reported that the reversal came just weeks after a midterm election that delivered widespread Republican losses driven in part by Trump's unpopularity and voter concerns about AI. As the market decline accelerated at the end of the prior week, the administration began pressing the board for Huang's return.

Earlier Monday, Trump posted an AI-generated image referencing the 2024 assassination attempt against him at a rally in Butler, Pa. Instead of Trump being surrounded by Secret Service agents with blood spattered on his face, the photo showed Huang with an Nvidia flag behind him and clutching a red bra.
