## Greens also secured pensioner carve-out from private health rebate cuts

The Albanese government has agreed to end fuel-tax subsidies for private-jet owners after the Greens committed to supporting a minimum 30% tax on income received from discretionary trusts. The agreement also protects pensioners from planned reductions in private health insurance rebates and brings Greens support to a digital duty-of-care bill, leaving the government’s gas-reservation scheme as the last of four measures it wanted passed before parliament finishes for the year.

The trust tax will apply from 2029-30 and is estimated to raise $4.5 billion in its first year. Labor was seeking to pass four key pieces of legislation before the end of the parliamentary year, including a law to create a gas-reservation scheme.

Greens leader David Shoebridge said it was a “disgrace” that private-aircraft users paid only one-fifteenth of the fuel excise paid by ordinary motorists. He said the change would require private-jet owners to pay the same effective rate as other Australians, singling out Gina Rinehart, Clive Palmer and Pauline Hanson as people who would now pay that rate during a cost-of-living crisis.

The trust-tax agreement followed Labor’s earlier decision to carve pensioners out of reductions in private health insurance rebates for people over 65. The government made the concession to secure Greens support in the upper house. The original legislation had drawn public condemnation and was thrown out in the Senate last month by the Greens, the Coalition and crossbench members.

Shoebridge said the revised changes would stop 1.2 million pensioners from being forced to pay more. He said around 2 million insured older Australians lived on less than $37,000 a year and that the reductions would have left people going hungry or missing other essentials, with no plan to reinvest in healthcare.

Labor had promised that the $3 billion in savings from the budget measure would be spent on more aged-care beds. Health Minister Mark Butler said the government would still fund the same amount after the pensioner carve-out. Butler said, “The government also recognises the realities of the Senate, and reaching agreement was necessary to secure passage of the bill and deliver these important investments into aged care.”

The digital duty-of-care bill passed Wednesday afternoon with Greens support after the minor party negotiated for stronger penalties. Those changes included “multibillion-dollar” fines and allowing individuals to sue social media companies in court.

Opposition leader Angus Taylor accused the government of cutting a “dirty deal” with the Greens in a “war against small businesses.”

Labor has now secured Greens backing for three pieces of legislation, leaving passage of the gas-reservation scheme as the remaining hurdle. Industry Minister Madeleine King told News24 that the federal government still intended to pass the laws this week. “Each senator will have to decide if they’re backing Aussie gas for Aussies,” King said.
