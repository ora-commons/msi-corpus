## Murphy recovering in rehabilitation facility, doctors encouraged by progress

Sen. Chris Murphy said Friday that he has been discharged from the hospital and will miss “several weeks” of votes in the Senate while he recovers at a rehabilitation facility, after doctors diagnosed him with a small hemorrhagic stroke.

Murphy, 53, announced the discharge in a statement Friday. He said doctors are “encouraged by the progress” he's made so far.

“This has been a scary experience for me and my family, and I appreciate the continued respect for our privacy,” he said.

The Connecticut Democrat said he called 911 last Saturday after feeling that “something was not right” with his body. Doctors diagnosed a small hemorrhagic stroke, which is a bleed in the brain.

In the Senate, Murphy serves on the Appropriations Committee and is a leading voice on immigration and gun policy. He is considered a contender for more senior leadership positions in the Senate and is among the many Democrats seen as potential 2028 candidates for the White House.

His recovery will keep him away from the Senate's votes for several weeks.

Three other Senate Democrats have had strokes in recent years. Sens. Ben Ray Lujan of New Mexico and Chris Van Hollen of Maryland had strokes in 2022 and have since recovered.

Pennsylvania Sen. John Fetterman had a stroke while campaigning for his seat that same year and now uses adaptive technology in the Senate, including live captioning on a phone or tablet that transcribes words as people speak to him and helps him process the conversation.

Fetterman said on social media on Sunday that his family was “rooting for Chris and his family for a full and speedy recovery.”

Murphy had been traveling the country in support of House and Senate candidates as his party raced for majority control, which it secured in Tuesday's midterm elections.

A day before the stroke, he posted that he had been campaigning in his home state for Gov. Ned Lamont. The week before, he posted from Minnesota campaigning with Lt. Gov. Peggy Flanagan, the Democratic nominee for the Senate.

The final sprint before Election Day capped Murphy's whirlwind trip to the Middle East at the start of October for meetings with several leaders, including Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman.

In early September, Murphy completed his annual “walk across Connecticut,” covering about 70 miles (113 kilometers) over four days, talking with residents about their concerns along the way.
