## Sanders outlines labor, health, education and retirement proposals

In an opinion article published Nov. 27 by The Guardian, U.S. Sen. Bernie Sanders said the new Democratic majority in Congress must reject what he called failed status quo policies. Sanders argued that no previous period in human history had placed so much wealth and power in so few hands and described the resulting inequality as a worldwide phenomenon.

Sanders cited a Summit Against International Oligarchy in Paris where elected officials and economists from around the world, including four Nobel prize winners, gathered. He quoted the summit’s proclamation: “The explosion of extreme wealth is the major social fact of our time … We are sounding the alarm: billionaires are a threat to our democracies.” Sanders said he and other progressives had organized the Fight Oligarchy tour the previous year, drawing more than 340,000 Americans in 27 states.

Sanders cited summit members who said that in 1987 the richest 0.0001% held wealth equivalent to 3% of global gross domestic product, a group of 3,000 households. According to those members, the fortunes of those households had grown to 17% of global GDP. Sanders also wrote that the top 1% owns more wealth than the bottom 95% of humanity.

In the United States, Sanders said the top 1% owns more wealth than the bottom 93%, while Elon Musk owns more wealth than the bottom 53% of American households. He noted that the United States had a record 989 billionaires, including 15 of the top 20.

Sanders’s examples extended beyond the United States. In Russia, he said the latest figures showed the wealthiest 500 people controlled more wealth than the bottom 99.8% of the population. In France, he said more than one in five children lived in poverty and 53 billionaires owned more wealth than the bottom half of the population.

In Africa, Sanders said nearly 850 million people had struggled with food insecurity in recent years while four men owned more wealth than the bottom 50% of the continent’s population. Sanders also cited Saudi Arabia, the UAE and Qatar, where he said a handful of families held large wealth and power while most work was performed by what he called “impoverished and powerless migrant laborers.”

Sanders argued that the issue extends beyond the distribution of income and wealth. He said oligarchs control much of the global economy and media, avoid taxes by stashing trillions of dollars in offshore tax havens and accumulate wealth while children in their native countries go hungry.

The political stakes arise, Sanders wrote, when people stop believing their government responds to their needs. Under those conditions, he said, many people will give up on democracy and move toward authoritarianism. He cited the growth of extreme right-wing parties in Germany, France and the UK as part of the political concern he described.

Among working people’s concerns, Sanders listed jobs shipped overseas, factories automated, credit card interest at 25%, an inability to retire with dignity and children likely having a lower standard of living than their parents. He contrasted those pressures with what he described as the exceptional position of the top 1%.

Sanders proposed a wealth tax on billionaires, saying it could substantially reduce child poverty, build affordable housing, protect seniors and invest in the needs of working families. Health care was another proposal: Sanders called for guaranteeing it as a human right by passing Medicare for All.

For workers and education, Sanders called for raising the minimum wage to a living wage of at least $20 an hour and passing the Pro Act to make it easier for workers to join unions. He also called for strengthening public education and making higher education available to everyone regardless of income.

Sanders also called for expanding social security, scrapping the cap on taxable income and restoring traditional defined-benefit pensions so people could retire with dignity. On technology, he urged lawmakers to ensure that AI and robotics benefit working families rather than only a handful of big-tech oligarchs.

Sanders is a U.S. senator from Vermont, the ranking member of the health, education, labor and pensions committee and the longest-serving independent in the history of Congress. He concluded: “The new Democratic majority in Congress must act boldly and with an unprecedented sense of urgency.” Sanders called for moving forward together to take on and defeat what he described as a powerful, greedy and corrupt ruling class.
