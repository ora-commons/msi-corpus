## Plan echoes 2022 'Rescue America' messaging that drew GOP Senate criticism

Sen. Rick Scott of Florida on Tuesday rolled out a midterm-election messaging project called "Rescuing America," tying the Republican senator's affordability focus to what he characterized as the rising appeal of socialism among younger voters.

The project revives an earlier Scott effort under a slightly different name. In 2022, while chair of the Senate Republican campaign arm, Scott unveiled a "Rescue America" plan that prompted objections from fellow Republican senators who worried its proposals could be used against their own candidates in that year's midterms.

In a statement posted on his website, Scott argued that generational economic pressures are pulling young Americans toward socialist politicians. "The generation coming of age today has inherited Washington's failures and are paying for them every day," Scott said. "They're walking into a world where the starter home their parents bought doesn't exist for them anymore. They're abandoning the idea of having children because they simply cannot afford it."

Scott pointed to New York Mayor Zohran Mamdani as an example of the trend he described, saying that "more young Americans [are] following socialists like [New York Mayor] Zohran Mamdani because the status quo has failed and they're desperate for change." He added: "We must be clear that socialism isn't the answer. It will kill the American Dream."

The senator framed the project as an exercise in candor about economic conditions. "I put together a project with one goal: to be honest with the American public about how our economy has drastically changed, how the American Dream is out of reach for so many, and most importantly — how we can fix it," he said. "Socialism will destroy America if we let it take root. If we don't act now, the American Dream will not exist for young people today, our kids, or our grandkids."

Scott, who is seen as a potential presidential candidate, acknowledged voter frustration in remarks accompanying the launch. "The question is not whether people are angry — they are," he said. "The question is, who provides the answers?"

The project so far includes a series of videos about the challenges Americans face and a speaking tour at economic events, according to Politico. Those events are expected to focus on cutting spending, the national debt, cutting regulations, changing social safety net programs, and establishing term limits in Washington, D.C.

Scott recently traveled to Iowa and plans to visit New Hampshire this week — stops in states that hold the first presidential nominating contests and that often serve as early proving grounds for White House hopefuls.
