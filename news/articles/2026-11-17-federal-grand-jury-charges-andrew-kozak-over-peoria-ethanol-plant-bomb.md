## Authorities evacuated about 2,500 homes within one mile of the facility

A federal grand jury on Tuesday charged Andrew Kozak, 41, of Chillicothe, Illinois, with two felonies related to a bomb found at BioUrja Renewables' Peoria ethanol plant. Law enforcement officials said about 2,500 homes within a one-mile radius of the facility were evacuated after the bomb was found Friday. Police said they rendered the bomb safe before allowing residents to return Saturday.

The grand jury charged Kozak with making threats involving explosives, a felony punishable by up to 10 years in prison. The grand jury also charged Kozak with making the threat to BioUrja Renewables over email, a felony punishable by up to five years in prison.

Kozak had worked at the facility for 10 months as a trainee, according to his LinkedIn page. Under his job description, Kozak wrote, “Learning how to operate an ethanol plant.” Police said the suspect was arrested at the BioUrja facility Monday morning and was not named at the time of the arrest.

Ryan Prelsey, the special agent in charge of the FBI office in Springfield, Illinois, said, “While law enforcement regularly responds to bomb threats, most are deemed not credible,” before adding, “But in this instance, it was. Had it not been for the swift action of the responders and the bomb technicians, this incident could have had a very different outcome.”

Peoria Mayor Rita Ali told WMBD-TV on Friday that the BioUrja plant contains ethanol and “other highly combustible types of chemicals.”

Nebraska prison records show Kozak served two years in prison on burglary charges in three counties and was released in 2008. The records show he returned to prison in 2013 on a burglary charge in a fourth county and was released in 2014.

Peoria, with a population of around 110,000, is located about 150 miles (240 kilometers) southwest of Chicago. Kozak lives about 15 miles north of Peoria.

The criminal complaint detailing the charges was sealed. At a Tuesday news conference, law enforcement officials declined to discuss details of the case and praised efforts to evacuate the area quickly on Friday night. No attorney was listed for Kozak on the federal court website, and Kozak was scheduled to make his initial appearance in federal court in Peoria on Tuesday afternoon.
