## Mao Ning accuses Washington of fabricating espionage narratives

State-run Global Times made a direct comparison this week between the unproven espionage allegations involving former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang and the 2023 suspected surveillance balloon. The outlet said, “The mere passage of a balloon sent American public opinion and politicians into a state of high alert, as if a "Chinese threat" were looming directly overhead.” It added, “No-one on the US side has ever apologised or taken responsibility for the genuine damage this caused to US-China relations.” Global Times questioned why the Nvidia story had emerged so soon after the “hard-won detente” following a September meeting between the countries’ leaders and asked, “Is someone behind the scenes pushing an agenda and stirring up confrontation?”

BBC News wrote: “For Beijing, the former Nvidia CEO's dramatic fall is a major challenge - and not just because of claims that a Chinese spy may have honey-trapped Huang, one of the artificial intelligence industry's most influential figures.” It also wrote, “Rather, the allegations surrounding Huang and his affair with Chinese businesswoman Alice Huang are turning into a new flashpoint in an already fragile US-China relationship.”

Beijing’s foreign ministry described the spy claims as a “smear against China.” Last week, Foreign Ministry spokesperson Mao Ning said, “The US should stop fabricating so-called espionage narratives to find pretexts for imposing technological blockades.” BBC News said it had approached the White House for comment. The outlet said the ministry appeared keen to keep the meeting on track, noting that the two countries had kept “close communication regarding interactions between the two heads of state.”

## Unproven allegations renew the advanced-chip export debate

U.S. intelligence reports, as described by BBC News, claim Alice Huang had ties to Beijing’s intelligence apparatus, particularly the Ministry of State Security, which the outlet described as China’s main civilian spy agency. The allegations remain unproven. BBC News said the claims that a Chinese spy may have honey-trapped Huang had renewed debate in Washington over exports of advanced chips to China and whether the Trump administration had been too willing to ease restrictions.

BBC News reported that advanced chips had been a key battleground in the U.S.-China rivalry for years because they were essential for AI, supercomputing and military technology. The outlet reported that Chinese officials worried the scandal could strengthen voices in Washington favoring tighter restrictions on Chinese access to cutting-edge American technology. Beijing argues that the restrictions are intended to contain its rise.

The Trump administration introduced new export restrictions this week on Chinese firms that access advanced U.S. chips in third countries such as Malaysia, Thailand and Brazil. Such firms typically rent computing power in overseas data centers to train their AI models. Some firms, including the company where Alice Huang worked, have been accused of smuggling chips to China, although they denied the allegations. BBC News wrote, “Until now, this controversy appeared to be a sideshow in US-China relations. But with Beijing calling the new rules "economic coercion" on the eve of Trump arriving in Shenzhen, there is a growing risk it could become something much bigger.”

BBC News reported that Democrats would control Congress in January. Last Wednesday, top Democrat Ted Lieu told Politico the Trump administration had been “1,000%” too deferential to Nvidia, putting national security at risk. Fellow Democrat Zoe Lofgren suggested Congress would now be more likely to restrict sales of AI chips to China.

For the last year, Trump and Xi had sought to stabilize relations after a damaging tariff war that almost brought trade between the two countries to a standstill. BBC News said the Trump administration’s decision in late 2025 to permit sales of advanced Nvidia H200 chips to China was seen as evidence that both sides were searching for areas of cooperation, even as competition intensified. The outlet described it as a rare move after Washington had spent years trying to limit China’s access to the most advanced chips.

BBC News reported that many people in the United States have long viewed the chips as a national-security issue because they could strengthen China’s military and surveillance capabilities. Jensen Huang rejects that claim and advocated lifting bans on exports of high-performance chips to China. BBC News said that opening may now be under threat.

## The summit faces trade stakes, balloon comparison and rare-earth leverage

BBC News wrote, “History has taught Beijing and Washington that spy scandals rarely remain confined to the intelligence world.” The outlet reported that analysts in China saw echoes of a 2023 incident when a suspected surveillance balloon drifted through the skies over the United States before an American fighter jet shot it down days after it was spotted over the country. Beijing insisted the craft had been a civilian meteorological airship that had strayed off course. A planned trip by then U.S. Secretary of State Antony Blinken was cancelled, and communication between the two governments deteriorated sharply.

BBC News wrote, “The incident showed how quickly a security dispute could overwhelm diplomatic efforts and push relations into a deep freeze.” The outlet also wrote, “History has taught Beijing and Washington that spy scandals rarely remain confined to the intelligence world.”

BBC News said both Trump and Xi had significant incentives to keep talking. The outlet said China’s economy remained under pressure and was increasingly reliant on exports, and assessed that Xi needed trade to keep flowing. BBC News called the meeting a key stepping stone toward replacing the current trade truce due to expire in January. The outlet also reported that Trump had repeatedly touted his close relationship with Xi and argued that maintaining dialogue was essential to managing competition between the two powers.

BBC News reported that Trump is in his last two years in office and faces difficulty passing domestic legislation after the midterms. The outlet said he could be looking for foreign-policy wins. BBC News said he may also hope for Xi’s help to end the war in the Middle East. The outlet said neither side would want the summit to discuss intelligence concerns and technology controls instead of trade, investment and economic cooperation.

BBC News wrote: “The summit is also being carefully choreographed by Beijing to show off its technological prowess in Shenzhen - a deeply symbolic choice of city.” The city is home to some of China’s biggest technology companies and has become a showcase for Beijing’s ambitions in AI and advanced manufacturing.

Given Washington’s tighter restrictions on advanced chips, BBC News said Beijing had options for retaliation. The outlet described China’s dominance in processing key rare-earth minerals as one of Beijing’s most powerful potential tools. China processes much of the world’s supply of materials used in electric vehicles, smartphones and military equipment.

BBC News cited analysts who had long warned that technological restrictions can trigger a cycle of retaliation in which each side reaches for its strongest economic leverage. The outlet said such a cycle would shift the current trend in U.S.-China relations from engagement back toward mutual suspicion.
