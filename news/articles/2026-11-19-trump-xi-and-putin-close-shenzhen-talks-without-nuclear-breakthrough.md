## United Nations reform, fossil-fuel refining and ICC limits formed the agenda

Donald Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin ended a roughly 70-minute trilateral meeting behind closed doors at the Kylin Villa in the Chinese tech hub of Shenzhen on Thursday without a breakthrough on reducing nuclear risks, according to Associated Press reporting carried by PBS News. The three leaders agreed to a narrower agenda of United Nations reform, additional fossil-fuel refining and curbs on the International Criminal Court's reach. Trump quickly left the villa and boarded Air Force One without saying a word to reporters about his time with Xi and Putin.

The gathering was expected to address the wars in Ukraine and Iran and the dangers posed by nuclear arsenals, with Trump presenting the meeting as an opportunity for direct diplomacy with two other nuclear powers. AP described the gathering as a potential diplomatic triumph for Trump, one that would serve as proof he could negotiate with two of America's toughest rivals. AP also reported that, before the trio met, Trump sought to flatter Xi and Putin as his friends. The agreements were modest, and the narrower agenda produced no announced nuclear limits or agreement on either war. AP reported that the trilateral, billed as historic, ultimately looked like a fleeting photo opportunity.

Trump opened with expansive language about the meeting's potential. "We are really making history," Trump said. "The United States, Russia and China are uniquely positioned to discuss how to reduce the number of nuclear weapons on Earth and also to lessen the risk of those weapons being used." Xi said earlier that the meeting would focus on "promoting world peace." Putin said the countries "hold joint responsibility for maintaining international peace."

After the session, Xi called the meeting positive, constructive and fruitful. Putin described the trilateral talks as open and candid and the format as "useful and substantive." Asked whether the image of the meeting mattered more than the substance, Putin said, "It is a serious collaborative work."

The nuclear issue remained unsettled. New START, the last nuclear arms pact between Russia and the United States, expired in February. Trump has said he wants "a new, improved and modernized" deal that includes China. AP reported that China's arsenal of more than 600 warheads is far smaller than the more than 5,000 warheads believed to be in each of the U.S. and Russian stockpiles, leaving China with less motivation to accept limits.

Daryl Kimball, executive director of the Arms Control Association, said the meeting could at best start a process that would eventually lead to a sustained set of talks for reducing nuclear arsenals. "We need more than diplomatic theater and photo opportunities between Xi, Putin and Trump," Kimball said. "And one meeting will not likely produce dramatic breakthroughs."

Earlier, Trump and Xi held a separate one-on-one meeting, their third substantial bilateral meeting this year. Trump said they had a shared interest in having oil flow through the Strait of Hormuz, a key waterway for oil shipments from the Middle East that was repeatedly shut because of attacks in the Iran war. Trump suggested he would push Xi to buy more agricultural products from America.

Xi said U.S.-China relations had been "generally stable and improved" during Trump's presidency. China's official Xinhua news agency reported that Xi discussed Taiwan with Trump and said the two countries needed to improve communication on economic and trade issues.

Trump also added a separate sit-down with Putin to his calendar, a meeting set to mark the first talks between the leaders since their Alaska discussions last year failed to broker a deal on Russia's war in Ukraine. "Wonderful things can come out of this — or at least start," Trump said, calling Putin "my friend." AP reported that Trump has frequently boasted of his rapport with Putin even as relations between the United States and Ukraine appeared to have cratered recently.

Separately, AP reported that Trump rejected new Russia sanctions. AP also reported that Kyiv was undermined by disruptions in satellite support for its drones during the war.

After the trilateral session, the three leaders criticized the International Criminal Court for exceeding its jurisdiction and said government officials should have immunity from prosecution. Putin is wanted by the court. AP described the Trump administration's sanctions on International Criminal Court officials and the institution itself as part of a campaign to dismantle the tribunal over investigations involving the United States and its allies, including Israel.

AP wrote that Trump's ambitions going into the meeting recalled an era of great-power politics, when wealthier countries felt entitled to decide the fate of seemingly lesser nations. Trump was negotiating with China and Russia without the involvement of Ukraine or allies such as Germany, France, the United Kingdom, Japan and South Korea, which also had stakes in the outcome of any talks.

Personal diplomacy formed much of the backdrop. AP reported that Trump has long believed his ability to create personal connections gives him a diplomatic edge. Xi made a symbolic gesture of friendship Wednesday by taking the unusual step of greeting Air Force One on the tarmac, and Xi and Trump turned their handshake into a one-armed hug.

On Wednesday at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit, Xi greeted Putin by calling him "my old friend." Putin responded by declaring Xi his "dear friend." AP wrote that, despite the compliments Trump had showered on Xi and Putin, he did not always appear to have the same depth of relationship with them that they shared through common interests.

AP reported that China has helped economically support Russia after Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine, providing Putin with a financial lifeline after the United States and its allies imposed new sanctions. By contrast, when Xi went to the White House for a state visit in September, he was far more restrained with Trump, saying he and Trump had built a "personal rapport and stayed in touch." Trump and Xi have one more meeting planned this year, at the G20 summit in Florida in December.

There were also signs of friction Thursday in the U.S.-China relationship despite Trump's praise of Xi. Chinese officials blocked a Bloomberg reporter from the trilateral meeting after she asked a question at the earlier Trump-Xi session. Chinese television showed a video in which Trump appeared drowsy, while the White House posted a clip of Trump helping Xi up an escalator.

Associated Press writers Darlene Superville, Didi Tang and Will Weissert in Washington, Huizhong Wu in Bangkok and Kanis Leung in Hong Kong contributed to the report.

The leaders left with an agenda for further work and positive public descriptions from Xi and Putin, but no announced nuclear limits or replacement for New START. Trump left without saying a word to reporters about his time with Xi and Putin. Kimball's assessment was cautious: sustained negotiations would require more than diplomatic theater and photo opportunities.
