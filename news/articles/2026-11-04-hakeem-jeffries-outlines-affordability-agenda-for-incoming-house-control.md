## Speaker Johnson acknowledges likely defeat in the lower chamber

House Democratic Leader Hakeem Jeffries told supporters in Washington, D.C., that Democrats would pursue improved access to affordable housing, education, healthcare and retirement benefits when they take control of Congress in January. Jeffries, a New York Democrat, said the party would deliver "transformational change," and NPR reported that he was poised to become the first Black House speaker in history.

Jeffries said, "We're going to fight to make sure that we make this country affordable for every single American," and added, "This is a change election. And we understand as House Democrats we need to deliver dramatic change. Things are broken. We need a Congress that puts the American people first." He opened the gathering by declaring, "America is back."

House Speaker Mike Johnson acknowledged Tuesday's election results while speaking to supporters in Shreveport, La. He said the "grand experiment" of democracy isn't controlled by a king or a monarch. Johnson said he "left it all on the field" to try to retain control of the lower chamber, but Republicans faced unfavorable odds.

Johnson said Republicans had known from the election's first day that keeping the House and sustaining their majority would require defying more than a century of midterm history. "It's very, very difficult to overcome and it looks like history will be kept tonight," he said.

NPR characterized Tuesday's results as a "blue wave" that would upend President Trump's second term and the GOP agenda. The outlet said voter anger over Trump's handling of the economy, the Iran war and a higher cost of living fueled the outcome. The results would end a Republican-controlled Washington; Trump would no longer be able to rely on a GOP-led Congress to set his agenda, and the Democratic victories would accelerate his lame-duck status.

NPR reported that Democrats would face an uphill battle installing many of their more ambitious plans. The party was likely to clash with Trump, who retained his power to veto legislation, on legislative efforts to address affordability concerns.

Jeffries also said House Democrats had to overcome a significant financial deficit because Republicans outspent the party in races across the country. He said voters had sent a message to Washington: they were "not for sale."

Jeffries argued that voters had sent Republican-led Washington a clear message that they were done with "political extremism and corruption," and he said Democrats would work to make good on their plans to ferret out corruption. "We're going to bring accountability to those who have visited unprecedented corruption on this country," Jeffries said. "Because if you have people in high office who are so focused on enriching themselves, their family members, their friends or their donors, then they are not focused on making life better for the American people."
