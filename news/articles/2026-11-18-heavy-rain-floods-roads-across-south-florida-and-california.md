## Temple Sinai roof collapses without reported injuries

A slow-moving storm system dumped heavy rain across South Florida and the West Coast on Wednesday. The rain flooded roads, submerged cars and caused the roof of Temple Sinai in Hollywood to collapse. No injuries were reported from the roof collapse.

Preliminary Florida totals showed up to a foot (30.5 centimeters) of rain falling in a 24-hour period ending Wednesday morning in parts of Broward County, said Alejandra Garcia, a meteorologist with the National Weather Service in Miami. The weather service said the threat was expected to last through Friday along Florida’s Atlantic Coast, with an additional 12 inches (30 centimeters) of rain possible.

The Florida Division of Emergency Management said in a Tuesday night advisory that “potentially catastrophic” amounts of rainfall could cause flash flooding, life-threatening rip tides and expected minor beach erosion due to high surf.

Hollywood declared a state of emergency Wednesday. Mayor Josh Levy urged motorists to stay off roads and avoid unnecessary travel. Portions of the Florida Turnpike and Interstate 95 in Hollywood were closed because of high water and stalled vehicles, and the city said it received more than 300 calls for service overnight.

“It’s a historically significant rainfall event in the city,” Levy said. He said some residents complained that motorists driving too fast created wakes that pushed floodwaters into their homes.

The highest rainfall amount was recorded in Plantation, where resident Jade Jaime said the storm continued throughout the day, “and then by the time we went to sleep at night, still just pouring down like it was never going to end.”

In Fort Lauderdale, 5.72 inches (14.5 centimeters) of rain fell in a downpour ending early Wednesday, breaking the city’s daily record of 1.8 inches (4.5 centimeters) set in 1959, Garcia said. A warning was also issued for minor flooding along the St. Johns River in northern Florida.

Garcia said the natural warming cycle called El Niño has disrupted Florida’s typical dry season, which runs from October to May. She said El Niño is “helping bring these low pressure systems down toward the southeastern United States, which in turn is causing these heavy precipitation events.”

The Los Angeles County Fire Department issued an evacuation warning Wednesday for the Palisades, Hurst and Sunset burn scar areas because of the risk of debris flows as a storm approached. The warning was in effect from Wednesday evening through Friday morning.

On the West Coast, heavy rain and high winds grounded planes at San Francisco’s airport, disrupted ferry service and flooded roads in Napa County and other wine-country areas north of the city. Areas along the Embarcadero flooded Wednesday morning as waves crashed over barricades recently installed to prevent flooding near the Ferry Building. Officials canceled several ferries leaving the city because of the storm.

Several highways were blocked by flooding or debris in Napa, Marin, San Mateo and Santa Clara counties, according to the California Department of Transportation and the California Highway Patrol. The weather service said heavy rain that began north of San Francisco was expected to spread south to the Central Coast later Wednesday.

Wind gusts of up to 50 mph (80 kph) were forecast for San Francisco’s East Bay, while gusts of up to 70 mph (113 kph) were forecast for the Altamont Pass, which connects Bay Area highways to I-5 in the Central Valley. Snow accumulations of up to 4 feet (1.2 meters) were forecast through Friday in the highest portions of the Sierra Nevada Mountains.

With the Thanksgiving holiday approaching, the National Hurricane Center in Miami said a low-pressure system was expected to form near the northern Bahamas. It could become a subtropical or tropical storm over parts of the southeastern and mid-Atlantic coasts into early next week.
