## Banned outlets sue administration in federal court over First Amendment rights

The Trump White House launched a 24/7 YouTube channel on Monday night that critics quickly labeled "state-run media," days after President Trump barred three major news organizations from covering his administration. The new channel, streaming under the tagline "The Essentials Station," opened with Trump's July 3 speech at Mount Rushmore — a speech in which the president blamed "newcomers to our country" for what he called "a resurgence of the communist menace in our land."

The channel is designed to put the administration's "biggest moments all in one place," the White House said. Its launch came after Trump posted on Truth Social on Friday announcing that CNN, MS NOW and Politico were being barred from the White House. In that post, Trump called the three outlets "Fake News" and accused them of reporting "FICTION and LIES" about him, the administration and the United States.

On Monday, the three banned organizations filed a federal lawsuit against the administration, alleging that the press restrictions violated their First Amendment rights of free speech by limiting their access to the White House. The suit also alleged violations of Fifth Amendment due-process rights, arguing that the news organizations were neither given notice of the ban nor afforded an opportunity to challenge the decision.

In a joint statement, Fox News Media, ABC News, CBS News, NBC News and CNN pushed back on the White House ban, defending what they described as the public's "vital interest in receiving accurate, independent information about its government." The statement said: "No administration should restrict a news organization because it objects to its reporting."

Media outlets that participate in joint, shared coverage of the White House — including NPR — said in a separate show of unity that they will not take audio or video from White House events until further notice. Coverage of Trump by other outlets not subject to the boycott continues.

Democratic lawmakers reacted sharply to the channel launch. In posts on X, Arizona Sen. Mark Kelly and California Gov. Gavin Newsom, both Democrats, called the new White House venture "state run" media. Newsom said the channel amounted to "truly autocratic behavior from a failed President."

A White House official pushed back by email: "Applying this logic, Mark Kelly's twitter feed is 'state run media' for a party of radical socialists. It doesn't make any sense."

Stephen Farnsworth, professor emeritus of political science at the University of Mary Washington in Fredericksburg, Virginia, framed the new channel as part of a broader Trump effort dating to his first term to control the narrative around his presidency while sidestepping critical mainstream media. "Over and over again, I think President Trump has really decided what he wants to do is tell the Trump story himself," Farnsworth said.

Farnsworth, author of *Spinner in Chief: How Presidents Sell Their Policies and Themselves*, said it was difficult to imagine the channel would attract viewers beyond the president's core supporters. "The challenge, I think, for Trump TV is whether you're going to reach anyone other than the already committed Trump voters," he said.

Jane Kirtley, an emerita professor of media ethics and law at the University of Minnesota, called the White House launch "unprecedented" and raised concerns that a government-controlled media source might function as a direct pipeline to the public in much the same way state-run television operates in other countries. "I think the public has a right to have information that has been subjected to scrutiny and that has been vetted by independent sources like the news media," Kirtley said. "And here we don't have that."

Kirtley drew a direct line between the ouster of the three outlets and the launch of the new channel. "I cannot think that the timing is coincidental, that the decision to ban these media organizations from the White House on the eve of the launch of Trump TV just happened by coincidence," she said.

Farnsworth said the Trump White House has been willing to go "full stop" to "shape the narrative" in its favor. "He's tried in various ways to control the White House press corps, the White House Correspondents Association and the like," Farnsworth said. "This is, I think, another step on that road to the Trump narrative being told in a way that is dominated by President Trump."
