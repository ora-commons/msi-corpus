## Cantor Fitzgerald denies influencing US export-control decisions

In a Tuesday letter to Brandon Lutnick, now chair of Cantor Fitzgerald, Jamie Raskin of Maryland, Gregory Meeks of New York and Sydney Kamlager-Dove of California demanded documents and records, including communications concerning Nvidia. The lawmakers said they wanted to determine whether Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick’s actions or decisions concerning export controls, enforcement measures or national security policies may have been improperly influenced by Cantor Fitzgerald’s holdings or business interests.

Raskin is the top Democrat on the House of Representatives’ Judiciary Committee. Meeks is the top Democrat on the Foreign Affairs Committee, and Kamlager-Dove is the top Democrat on the Foreign Affairs Subcommittee on South and Central Asia.

The lawmakers wrote: “The American people deserve to know: did the secretary of commerce seriously and perhaps irreversibly compromise US national security interests to make money for his adult children and Cantor Fitzgerald?” An accompanying news release said they were seeking to determine whether Lutnick “blocked efforts to stop the suspected smuggling of advanced Nvidia chips to China while his family’s firm held hundreds of millions of dollars in Nvidia stock”.

Lutnick led Cantor Fitzgerald until his confirmation as Commerce secretary in February 2025, when he transferred his stake in the firm to trusts benefiting his adult children. In the letter, the lawmakers said Cantor had “amassed a significant stake” in Nvidia, reportedly “holding more than 3.5m shares, valued at over $1.3bn”.

Cantor Fitzgerald’s spokesperson called the claims “inaccurate” and said: “Cantor holds no material proprietary position in Nvidia.” The spokesperson said the firm’s Nvidia holdings arose primarily from equity swaps executed for clients. The decline since early 2025, the spokesperson said, reflected clients closing or reducing their swap positions, not a Cantor decision to exit a proprietary position.

The spokesperson said “Allegations suggesting that work influenced government decisions are baseless” and that Cantor had no role in US export control or entity list decisions to determine trade restrictions. The spokesperson expressed confidence that a full review would show the firm had operated ethically and in compliance with applicable rules and regulations.

The lawmakers cited a recent New York Times report that Lutnick had pushed to weaken rules affecting south-east Asian datacenters, some of which were Cantor Fitzgerald clients. A Commerce Department spokesperson told the Times that Lutnick makes policy decisions based on the best interests of the US and does not discuss department matters with his sons. The spokesperson added that Lutnick’s sons did not discuss Cantor Fitzgerald business with him.

The letter also asked Cantor Fitzgerald to provide written confirmation by 5pm ET on 1 December that it had implemented appropriate preservation measures and instructed relevant personnel to retain potentially responsive records.

The developments follow Jensen Huang’s resignation from Nvidia last week after reports of multiple affairs, including one with Alice Huang, who is suspected of being a Chinese spy. Last week, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent blamed Lutnick’s Commerce Department for the US not imposing export restrictions on certain Nvidia chips to China.

Both Huang and Lutnick are scheduled to testify before Congress next month.
