## Autotrader fell 2% as Amazon targets a £200bn UK car market

Amazon will launch Amazon Autos in the UK in early 2027, beginning with new Hyundai vehicles and used cars from Vertu Motors, in the US technology group's first sale of cars outside its home country. The service enters a British car market worth more than £200bn a year and will compete with online car dealers, traditional physical dealerships and manufacturers that sell directly to consumers.

Amazon, which The Guardian describes as the world's biggest online retailer, carries a market value of $2.7tn (£2.1tn). The company started selling cars at the end of 2024 in an effort to expand into one of the few remaining major consumer markets it had not already targeted. Amazon Autos now operates in 150 US cities, counts thousands of dealers in its network and offers vehicles from 35 brands.

UK buyers will be able to buy new electric hybrid and petrol Hyundai models, along with used cars from Vertu Motors, a large British dealership. Amazon said customers would be able to explore financing options before reserving a car and completing the purchase in physical dealerships, and that more brands and dealers would be announced before the launch.

Matt Nuffort, director of Amazon Autos International, said: "Buying a car is one of the biggest purchases people make, and we think it should feel as simple and transparent as shopping for anything on Amazon."

He said: "In the UK, that means making it easy for customers to browse available cars, see upfront pricing, reserve the car and connect with the dealer to finalise the purchase."

The service will face an industry that already includes online new and used dealers, traditional physical dealerships and car companies selling directly to consumers through their own websites. Hyundai was also the first brand to work with Amazon in the US.

"Our partnership with Amazon Autos has changed the way many customers discover Hyundai in the US, and 78% of the Amazon-influenced sales at our dealerships have come from customers who are new to our brand," said José Muñoz, Hyundai's chief executive.

Vertu Motors, the large British dealership that will supply used cars at the UK launch, is listed on London's Alternative Investment Market. Robert Forrester, Vertu's chief executive, said his company was "committed to meeting customers wherever they choose to begin their buying journey."

The shares moved unevenly after the announcement. Autotrader, described by The Guardian as the UK's largest car sales website and a member of the FTSE 100, fell 2% on Wednesday. Vertu's share price rose 4%, although the company also announced acquisitions of dealerships and was valued at £269m before the announcement.

Amazon has said more brands and dealers will be announced before the UK service opens in early 2027. The move takes a US car operation spanning 150 cities and 35 brands into a market where established dealers, online brokers and direct-from-manufacturer sales compete for the same buyers.
