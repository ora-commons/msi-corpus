## European Commission projects 0.4% growth in eurozone real wages this year

Eurozone annual inflation rose to 4% in October after the war between the U.S. and Iran began, up from 1.9% in February. The faster increase in prices weakened the spending power of eurozone households.

The European Central Bank said Friday that wages negotiated by labor unions and similar groups were 2.57% higher than a year earlier during the three months through September, the third quarter. The annual rate of growth increased from 2.45% in the three months through June.

Since the start of the war, ECB officials have highlighted the outcome of wage negotiations as a key indicator of whether the jump in energy prices triggered by the conflict is set to keep inflation above their 2% target. The Wall Street Journal reported few signs as yet that workers were securing larger pay rises to cover increased energy bills.

The European Commission now estimates that inflation-adjusted wages in the eurozone will increase by 0.4% for the year as a whole, down from 1.8% in 2025. The European Commission expects real wages to rise at a similarly slow pace next year and forecasts that compensation per worker will increase by 3.5%, at an unchanged pace from this year.

The ECB raised its key rate in September and June. “What we don’t see at the moment is second-round effects, not yet,” Bank of France Governor Emmanuel Moulin said in an interview with CNBC on Thursday. “We need to be vigilant about what will happen and that’s the reason we’ve increased rates in the past.”
