## Environmentalists sued last week over health and pollution concerns

A planned detonation of 68 barrels of lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown could disperse material through the air and onto the ground. The operation involves several tons of degraded bomb-making material. Nearly 400 people live in the town where the plant sits, and thousands more in surrounding areas could be affected.

Environmentalists sued last week to stop the detonation, citing health and pollution concerns. They won a stay from the appeals court that put an emergency permit approved by the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency on hold pending a decision on its validity. The permit had cleared the way for the operation.

The detonation had initially been planned for Friday. The Army changed the date three times after an outpouring of public concern about potential health and environmental effects. Its most recent target was Wednesday, with the Army saying the weather would be most favorable that day.

Donald Trump said on Monday that he would defer to a federal appeals court on whether the administration proceeds with the plan to detonate several tons of degraded bomb-making material at the plant.

Trump said he preferred to take care of the volatile material as soon as possible and that a controlled detonation would be much safer. “At the same time, have ordered the military not to do so until such time as the eighth circuit agrees that we can go forward, and am further making this decision fully subject to the incoming governor-elect Rob Sand agreeing to the operation,” Trump said in a statement.

The statement set two conditions: the 8th Circuit would have to agree that the operation could go forward, and incoming governor-elect Rob Sand would have to agree to it. If Sand and the court did not agree, Trump said, the administration would have no choice but not proceed with the planned detonation.

Trump also said he would hold the incoming governor responsible for “any and all death or destruction caused by this very unfortunate, and longterm, situation.” The appeals court still had to decide the permit's validity, and Sand's agreement was a separate required step under Trump's order.

The Army's most recent schedule listed Wednesday, but Trump's statement made the operation contingent on the court's agreement and Sand's approval.


[MSI reported on Nov. 22](/articles/2026-11-22-army-plans-emergency-move-to-detonate-iowa-chemicals-despite-court-stay/) that the Army had asked the 8th Circuit for emergency provisions so the detonation could proceed despite the appeals court's stay. Trump's Monday order took a different approach, holding the operation until the court agrees and Rob Sand approves, with the administration saying it would not proceed without both.
