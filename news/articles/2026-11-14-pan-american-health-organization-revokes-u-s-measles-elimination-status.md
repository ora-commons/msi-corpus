## Kindergarten MMR vaccination rate fell to 92.4% in June

The Pan American Health Organization, an international health body, revoked the U.S.'s measles elimination status, people familiar with the matter said — a reversal for a nation that has kept the vaccine-preventable disease at bay for decades. PAHO didn't immediately respond to requests for comment.

The U.S. achieved measles elimination status in 2000 after robust immunization campaigns and the creation of a program that made shots free for millions of children. Measles is considered eliminated when a country has no continuous spread of the virus for at least 12 months.

PAHO makes its determination based on a review of recent outbreaks and a report from the federal Centers for Disease Control and Prevention that shows whether disparate outbreaks are linked or whether any one outbreak lasted more than a year.

Many public health experts and epidemiologists expected the U.S. to lose elimination status, as outbreaks over the past two years have exploded to highs not seen in decades. States such as Pennsylvania, New York and Wisconsin reported five measles deaths so far in November from unrelated outbreaks.

In 2026 alone, the CDC has reported more than 5,400 cases. Measles has largely been under control in the U.S. over the past 30 years, with often just a few dozen cases recorded annually. Over the past two years, states such as Utah, Texas, South Carolina and Pennsylvania have all reported big outbreaks.

“Runaway measles outbreaks in the United States, as well as the needless deaths, are all due to low measles vaccination coverage,” said Noel Brewer, chair of an independent U.S. committee who presented findings about the country's measles outbreaks to PAHO. “We've gotten comfortable letting children be stopped by a deadly disease and die from it.”

The acceleration of cases coincides with Robert F. Kennedy Jr. tenure as health secretary. Since joining the administration, Kennedy has tried to cut the number of shots on the childhood vaccine schedule, promoted long-debunked claims that immunizations cause autism and cut federal funding for some vaccine research.

Under Kennedy, the CDC has backed away from long-held immunization recommendations. The Trump administration has called for children to receive fewer vaccines and to specifically separate the measles, mumps and rubella, or MMR, vaccine, into individual shots — a shift public health experts say would sow confusion. Two doses of the MMR vaccine are 97% effective in preventing the disease.

Vaccine uptake has fallen across the U.S. in recent years, creating pockets of the country vulnerable to the highly contagious disease. At least 95% of the population needs to be vaccinated to prevent the disease from spreading — a threshold that helps protect babies and people with conditions who can't get immunized.

MMR vaccination rates for kindergartners dropped to 92.4% in the school year that ended in June, from 95.2% six years prior, according to the CDC. Rates are far lower in some communities where recent measles outbreaks have hit hardest.

Now the Trump administration is tasked with leading the effort to eliminate measles once again. PAHO, which cannot make formal mandates, recommends countries that lose elimination status submit a corrective action plan to the health body and bolster its measles immunization and surveillance.

To regain elimination status, the U.S. must halt the continuing spread of the highly infectious disease for at least 12 months — an effort that could take years of public health work and substantial political will to achieve.

Dr. Walter Orenstein, the former director of the U.S. National Immunization Program at the CDC who led the nation's effort to eliminate measles in 2000, said re-establishing elimination in the current political climate will be difficult. “In terms of the leadership, I cannot recall in all my time working in immunization where we've had this much controversy over vaccines,” Orenstein said.

The U.S. isn't alone in its struggle to combat the highly contagious disease. Canada lost its measles-elimination status in 2025 and some European nations and the U.K. lost the status earlier this year. Countries have succeeded in regaining measles-elimination status, but it has taken time: Brazil lost its status in 2019 and re-established it in 2024 following national efforts to increase vaccination rates.
