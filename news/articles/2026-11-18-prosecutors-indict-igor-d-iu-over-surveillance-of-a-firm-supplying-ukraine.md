## FBI affidavit details laptop photos, home addresses and employee following

A federal grand jury charged Igor D’iu, a Russian national whom prosecutors say was recruited by his government, with stalking employees of a Silicon Valley technology and defense company to gather intelligence, the Justice Department said Wednesday. The company was not identified in the charging documents, and prosecutors say it was supplying Ukraine with weapons for its war with Russia.

The indictment, filed in the Northern District of California, includes charges of conspiring to act as an agent of a foreign government, interstate stalking and fraud connected to fake identity documents that D’iu allegedly used to conceal his true name. The Justice Department said an FBI affidavit outlined the allegations.

According to the affidavit, D’iu was tasked with collecting information about the company’s activities because it was supplying Ukraine with weapons for its war with Russia. According to the affidavit, D’iu’s handlers directed him to visit a coffee shop near the company’s northern California office, identify employees and photograph their laptop screens while they worked. Prosecutors say D’iu collected home addresses and personal information about employees and at times followed them from the office in his car.

It was not immediately clear from whom D’iu received his taskings. His contacts on the encrypted communications platform Telegram told him he was working for “the Motherland,” the affidavit said. The document said the alleged scheme followed the pattern of a clandestine operation of a Russian intelligence service, which typically relies on proxies to carry out intelligence collection from targets.

The Justice Department said the alleged spying occurred soon after D’iu illegally arrived in the United States last summer without any active or approved visas. The Justice Department described D’iu as a Russian national believed to have been recruited by contacts on Telegram. Prosecutors say D’iu traveled from Turkey to Mexico last June and posted on a Facebook group page in Russian asking for help getting across the border and into the United States.

D’iu was arrested Oct. 30. Authorities said agents searching his East Palo Alto home found $18,000 in cash, two Russian passports, laptops, iPhones and wireless networking equipment. The notebook contained home addresses for some employees, detailed profiles about them and their employment histories.

Prosecutors said the surveillance included a September incident in which an employee contacted police after being followed nearly 30 miles from the office by a driver who ran a red light and made illegal turns. The Justice Department said that after police contacted D’iu about the episode, he changed cars, got a new phone number and temporarily traveled out of state.

D’iu is due in court Nov. 24. It was not immediately clear from court records whether he had a lawyer who could speak for him.
