## Lauren Boebert loses Colorado House seat after three terms

**Subtype:** fact

- The AP news agency has declared Democrat Eileen Laubacher the winner over Republican Rep. Lauren Boebert in Colorado after every vote was counted.
- Boebert, 39, will not serve a fourth term after winning the seat in 2020, nearly losing it by 546 votes in 2022, and switching districts to win again in 2024.

## Retired rear admiral Laubacher defeats Boebert in AP call

The race was too close to call on election night last week, but after every vote was counted the Democrat Eileen Laubacher was declared the winner by the AP news agency. The result means Boebert, one of the most vocal conservatives in the House of Representatives, will not serve a fourth term.

Boebert rose to national fame as one of Donald Trump's most prominent supporters. She was one of the faces of the "Make America Great Again" or MAGA movement when she burst on to the Colorado political scene at the end of 2019. Like the president, she was not a career politician. Boebert first made headlines when she encouraged staff at her restaurant Shooters, located in a small Colorado town named Rifle, to openly carry firearms. Her rallies focused on gun rights and fighting Democrats, the establishment, illegal immigration and anything that could be deemed "woke."

Republican state representative Matt Soper, now Colorado's most senior Republican legislator, told the BBC: "Speaking after Lauren Boebert was like the band that played after the Beatles - no-one remembered them." Soper first met Boebert and her then-husband, Jayson, when she was gathering signatures for a state-voting initiative. She arrived in jeans with a Glock on her hip, her husband "looking like a classic oilman" in blue jeans, boots, cowboy hat and belt buckle, Soper said.

It played well with her Western Colorado constituency - a mix of ranches, farms and tough terrain with a smattering of ski towns - which Trump won with 53% of the vote in 2016. Those same voters embraced Boebert in the lead-up to the 2020 election, helping her unseat the five-term Republican incumbent in the party's primary. Boebert earned her high school equivalency diploma right before that victory, then won the seat in November, just before her 34th birthday.

Soper said he had not anticipated that first meeting would lead to six years in Congress. "If I had been told, you know, they're going to make not just national headlines, but international headlines, for years, and Lauren will be a member of Congress for six years, I mean, I would have put no money on that bet," he said.

Once in Congress, Boebert's pro-Trump rhetoric garnered constant airtime. Along with outspoken allies such as Marjorie Taylor Greene, she established herself as one of the most vocal MAGA supporters. She joined Greene in heckling President Joe Biden during his 2022 State of the Union address.

Justin Gollob, a political science professor at Colorado Mesa University, told the BBC that Boebert's growing prominence cut against her at home. "I think, as that national profile grew, it made it much harder for her to be able to maintain that small-town fighter narrative that she had relied on," Gollob said.

Waning enthusiasm became glaringly apparent in 2022, when a Democrat from Aspen challenged Boebert for her seat and nearly won; Boebert eked out a victory by just 546 votes. Boebert campaigned in Colorado in 2022 with a gun on her hip. Rich Wyatt, a county Republican chairman in Colorado and former reality star who served time in prison for tax fraud, said Colorado voters had initially liked Boebert's "straight talk and her direct, down-the-barrel approach" as well as her conservative Christian views.

That narrative became harder to maintain as the congresswoman was beset by controversies that were personal, not political. She filed for divorce from her husband and, five months later, footage of Boebert and a man being kicked out of a Denver performance of the musical Beetlejuice went viral. Staff accused the politician of vaping, taking pictures and "causing a disturbance." Footage released by the theatre showed Boebert, in a gold cocktail dress, arguing with staff before twirling and dancing with her date upon ejection. An incident report claimed she had told staff "do you know who I am?" and "I will be contacting the mayor," the Denver Post reported.

That December - facing another matchup with Adam Frisch, the former Aspen Democratic councilman who nearly unseated her - Boebert announced she would be switching districts altogether. She moved across the state to Colorado's easternmost constituency, which voted reliably Republican. With Trump's endorsement, Boebert won the seat in 2024.

Boebert turned on the president during his second term, when she became one of four House Republicans pushing for the full release of the Epstein files. The next month, Trump vetoed a water bill Boebert had sponsored. As she sought re-election this year, Trump invited candidates to run against her in the primary - though the deadline had already passed - and called his former supporter "dumb" and "weak-minded." Trump eventually endorsed Boebert for the race.

Democrats had found a strong moderate candidate to take her on in Laubacher, a Navy veteran who retired in 2024 as a two-star rear admiral. Like several other Democrats who have emerged victorious after the 2026 midterms, Laubacher's win is as much about her qualities as a candidate - she was a former Republican with strong appeal to middle-ground voters - as it is a rebuke of Boebert's time in office.

Peggy Lozen, a constituent who voted for Trump in 2016 but not after that, said she had been turned off by Boebert since the 2022 State of the Union incident. "I was sick over that," the 72-year-old told the BBC. "I was raised in an era where you respect everyone, regardless of their opinion, and especially the president of the United States." The mother of a veteran voted for the rear admiral and displayed a sign of support in her yard. "You can't even compare the two," she said of Laubacher and Boebert.

While it remains to be seen what Boebert does next, there is a consensus among both politicians and the public that she is unlikely to vanish altogether. Wyatt said: "You haven't seen the end of Lauren Boebert."
