## IRS raises 2027 standard deduction and child tax credit

**Subtype:** fact

- The IRS raised the 2027 child tax credit to $2,300 per child and the refundable portion to $1,800.
- The IRS raised the 2027 standard deduction for single filers to $16,600 from $16,100.
- The IRS increased the maximum earned income tax credit to $685 for workers without children and $7,552 for families with two children.
- The 2025 tax law kept deductions for tips, overtime and older adults fixed through 2028, making those deductions worth slightly less as prices rise.
- The IRS kept the top federal income tax rate at 37% for 2027.

## Several 2025 tax breaks stay fixed as prices rise

The Internal Revenue Service released annual inflation adjustments for the 2027 tax year on Nov. 5, raising the standard deduction and child tax credit. The new figures apply to tax returns filed in 2028. Many of the increases are routine annual changes based on how much prices climbed; for 2027, that increase is about 3.2%. Several popular tax breaks in the 2025 law remain fixed and will be worth slightly less each year.

The standard deduction for single filers will increase to $16,600 from $16,100. For married couples filing jointly, the deduction will rise to $33,200 from $32,200. The deduction for heads of household will be $24,925, up from $24,150.

An Associated Press calculation estimated that a married couple earning $100,000 and using the standard deduction would have a federal income tax bill of $7,504, down from $7,640. A single filer earning $60,000 would see a bill of $4,952, down from $5,020. The calculations assume household income stays the same. Because the changes are inflation adjustments, workers whose pay increases with inflation will see their tax bills stay roughly unchanged.

The maximum earned income tax credit for a worker with no children will rise to $685 from $664. For a family with two children, the maximum credit will increase to $7,552 from $7,316.

The child tax credit will rise by $100 to a maximum of $2,300 per child. Its refundable portion will also rise by $100, to $1,800. The 2025 law set the credit at $2,200 and tied future increases to inflation.

Several deductions from the 2025 law do not adjust for inflation. Workers can still deduct up to $25,000 in tip income and up to $12,500 in overtime pay, with the overtime limit rising to $25,000 for joint filers. People 65 and older can claim an additional $6,000 deduction. All three tax breaks are set to end after the 2028 tax year unless Congress extends them. Because their limits do not rise, the three deductions will be worth slightly less as prices increase.

The estate tax exclusion, which the 2025 law made permanent at $15 million, will rise with inflation to $15.48 million.

The top income tax rate will remain at 37% in 2027. The rate will apply to income above $661,375 for single filers and above $793,650 for married couples filing jointly.

The 2027 adjustments combine higher inflation-linked deductions and credits with unchanged limits on several popular deductions created in 2025. The new figures apply to returns filed in 2028, and the three fixed tax breaks are scheduled to end after the 2028 tax year unless Congress extends them.
