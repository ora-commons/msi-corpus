## Appeals court finds US wrongly penalized New York over immigrant driver licenses

**Subtype:** fact**

- A three-judge panel of the 2nd U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals ruled Friday that the Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration acted "arbitrary, capricious, or contrary to law" in withholding nearly $74 million in highway money from New York state.
- The Transportation Department penalized New York after the state refused to revoke nearly 33,000 commercial driver's licenses held by immigrants when an audit revealed problems last year.
- The appeals court said New York cannot be faulted for failing to cancel licenses when a commercial driver lost legal status because cancellation rules were not in place at the time.
- Audits in every state found non-domiciled commercial licenses that stayed valid long after work permits expired or were issued without properly verifying citizenship status.

## Court says state cannot be faulted for failing to cancel licenses under then-absent rules

## Appeals court says no cancellation rules were in place when drivers lost legal status

The appeals court said the state cannot be faulted for failing to cancel licenses when a commercial driver lost legal status to stay in the United States because rules that would require a cancellation were not in place at the time.

The Transportation Department began enforcing existing English language requirements for truckers in the summer of 2025. Concerns about truck driver qualifications then took on more urgency after an August 2025 crash in Florida, in which a truck driver was accused of performing an illegal U-turn that killed three people. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said that driver, who is from India, should never have been licensed in the first place.

The dispute came after Duffy tightened rules governing when immigrants can obtain a commercial driver's license. Officials said they took actions against states like New York after learning that some licenses remained valid long after an immigrant was no longer authorized to be in the country. New York issued licenses valid for eight years regardless of how long a driver's visa remained valid, authorities said.

Audits conducted in every state uncovered numerous non-domiciled commercial driver's licenses that remained valid long after an immigrant's work permits had expired, or that were issued without properly verifying citizenship status. The licenses pertain to commercial vehicles such as trucks, trailers, limousines and buses.

Since the audits, about 26,000 drivers have been pulled off the road for failing to demonstrate English proficiency, and the Transportation Department said 30,000 commercial licenses were canceled because they were issued illegally.

Immigrants account for about 20% of all truck drivers, while the non-domiciled licenses immigrants can receive represent about 5% of all commercial driver's licenses — about 200,000 drivers. Immigrant groups have said they believe that many drivers are being unfairly targeted.

New York Attorney General Letitia James objected when the lawsuit was filed in April, saying the federal government was unfairly holding back money the state needs to repair roads and bridges. New York Gov. Kathy Hochul in the spring called the federal cuts "devastating" and characterized them as "political payback."

The Transportation Department had no immediate comment Friday. A message seeking comment from the New York attorney general's office was not immediately returned.

AP Transportation Writer Josh Funk reported from Omaha, Nebraska.
