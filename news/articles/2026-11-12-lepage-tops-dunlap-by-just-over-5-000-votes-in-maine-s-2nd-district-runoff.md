## LePage tops Dunlap by just over 5,000 votes in Maine's 2nd District runoff

- Republican Paul LePage, the former governor, won Maine's 2nd Congressional District after the Secretary of State's Office completed the ranked choice tabulations Thursday.
- LePage took 50.81% to Democrat Matt Dunlap's 49.18% after blank ballots and first-choice votes for write-in candidate Gregory Ward were eliminated, a margin of just over 5,000 votes.
- Secretary of State Shenna Bellows called the contest "A nail-biter of a race, a razor-thin margin" and congratulated LePage as the winner in CD2.
- LePage will succeed U.S. Rep. Jared Golden, a Democrat, making the district one of a handful of seats Republicans flipped nationwide.
- The 2nd District is possibly the only flip that was not subject to GOP redistricting efforts.

## Bellows congratulates LePage after ranked choice tabulations conclude

Republican Paul LePage has formally won Maine's 2nd Congressional District after the Secretary of State's Office completed the ranked choice tabulations Thursday. LePage will succeed U.S. Rep. Jared Golden, a Democrat, making the district one of a handful of seats that Republicans flipped amid the overwhelming support for Democrats around the country — and possibly the only flip that was not subject to GOP redistricting efforts.

The margin came from the final tabulation round: after the Secretary of State's Office eliminated blank ballots and first-choice votes for declared write-in candidate Gregory Ward, LePage secured 50.81% of votes to Dunlap's 49.18%, a difference of just over 5,000 votes.

The office began the ranked choice process Monday morning, and the vast majority of the time was spent logging results from every ballot and checking multiple times to ensure the totals line up.

The Secretary of State's Office was still legally bound to carry out the tabulations even though the outcome was already expected: LePage declared victory last Wednesday, and Dunlap conceded one day later. "There's no provision for close enough," Bellows said Monday.

The former governor entered the runoff as the frontrunner from the first round of results after Election Day, receiving 49.894% of votes to Democrat Matt Dunlap's 48.307% — falling just short of the more than 50% threshold needed to avoid a ranked choice runoff.

"A nail-biter of a race, a razor-thin margin," Secretary of State Shenna Bellows said after tabulations concluded. "Paul LePage should feel very good, we congratulate him as the winner in CD2."

Brent Littlefield, spokesperson for the LePage campaign, said in a statement Thursday that the ranked choice tabulations "only confirmed what we already knew."

"Paul LePage is already hard at work for the people of Maine attending congressional orientation, meeting with national leaders and making it clear the political parties need to work together for the American people," Littlefield said.

Dunlap initially said the race was too close to call, but shortly after the secretary's office announced the ranked choice runoff, he said he didn't believe the process would impact LePage's lead.

"This isn't the result we were hoping for, and I'm disappointed that we came short," Dunlap said in a statement last week. "Congratulations to Paul LePage, I wish him the best."

Lauren LePage, LePage's daughter and campaign spokesperson, said on X that LePage is the only man left standing after Democrats swept many races across the country in the midterms.

"This was your moment," Lauren LePage said. "You failed. He didn't barely win in an R+5 year. He won in the most hostile climate we've seen in many cycles."

LePage also traveled to Washington, D.C. this week with fellow freshman lawmakers. "This is my gig: if it helps Maine, I'm going to be all over it, if it hurts Maine, I'm going to be all against it," LePage said last week. "And I will work with anyone and everyone that wants to put Maine first."

It appears LePage saw strong support in the more rural areas of the district, while Dunlap won in the district's more populated cities. In Lewiston, Auburn, Bangor, Orono and Augusta, Dunlap not only beat LePage — he outperformed other winning Democratic candidates Troy Jackson and Hannah Pingree.
