## Anthropic commits to spending hundreds of billions of dollars on data centers

Anthropic shares began trading on Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker symbol ANT, opening 34% above the $83 IPO price. The stock gave back part of its early gain during the session but ended well above the offering price, according to NPR's account of the listing.

NPR's John Ruwitch, reporting from Silicon Valley, said Anthropic sold nearly a billion shares to the public and sold additional shares at the same time to a handful of large technology firms in a private placement. He reported that the total haul for Anthropic and its founders was more than $107 billion and that the IPO alone was the biggest in history, surpassing SpaceX's June listing, which had set the previous record.

Ruwitch said the $83 IPO price was higher than expected. Investors applied in advance to acquire shares at that fixed price before trading began, and the shares opened 34% above it. The market price then moved away from the offering price during Thursday's trading.

Ruwitch said Anthropic became the first publicly traded big AI lab focused only on artificial intelligence. SpaceX is also an AI rival but operates businesses such as satellite launches, he said. Before Anthropic's listing, an average person seeking investment exposure to AI had less direct options and had to buy shares in companies that made chips, software or satellites.

Anthropic's Claude models are among the most advanced in the world and are popular, Ruwitch said. He also said AI is changing how people search the internet and develop medicines, helping explain why investors were eager for shares despite broader concerns about the industry.

NPR host Scott Detrow said one of the industry's biggest worries was that AI might be in a bubble. Ruwitch said people were worried about data centers, losing jobs to AI, and AI systems going rogue or spiraling out of control. He added that investors were concerned about whether the money pouring into AI was justified.

Nasdaq chair and CEO Adena Friedman celebrated the listing, and Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei rang the opening bell. Friedman said, "Today, Anthropic becomes a public company and begins trading on NASDAQ under the ticker symbol ANT." NPR also disclosed that Anthropic is a financial supporter of NPR.

Ruwitch said Amodei had long been outspoken about AI's risks. Amodei and several others left OpenAI five years ago to found Anthropic because of their concerns about AI safety. Ruwitch said Amodei believed going public would not change that mission. Addressing those risks during the opening-bell ceremony, he said: "We will not bend to either business nor political pressure. Today puts the company in a stronger financial position, and in doing so, only strengthens our resolve to do the right thing."

Ruwitch said Amodei pledged to be careful about the pace of training and releasing new models and to take as long as necessary to ensure they remain safe and under control. Amodei said he hoped Anthropic could lead "a race to the top" by making the right safety choices while also becoming a successful company.

Anthropic's commercial ambitions are correspondingly large. Ruwitch said Anthropic has committed to spending hundreds of billions of dollars on data centers to build and run powerful new AI models. He said the future for AI companies like Anthropic depends partly on developing useful applications, including some that have not yet been imagined, and that generating revenue will be necessary to support that spending.

At the moment, most of Anthropic's revenue appears to come from companies, but the pace of adoption could determine whether its new applications succeed. One analyst cited by NPR compared AI with radios and washing machines: radio spread quickly into American homes, while washing machines took decades.

Thursday produced an immediate result: a 34% opening gain and a close well above the $83 offering price. The unresolved question was longer-term adoption, which Ruwitch framed through the contrast between radio and washing machines: whether AI would be more like the radio or the washing machine. Anthropic will need revenue from new applications to support its committed spending on data centers.
