Lazaro is a seasonal line on the pesticide crew at the fir nursery I own near Harrisburg, Pennsylvania, and I would turn his legal status into a production quota if employers could condition renewal on output. Hugh Hewitt’s [Fox News column](https://www.foxnews.com/opinion/trump-immigration-reform-will-democrats-make-offer-cant-refuse) says President Trump would negotiate conditional regularization for people already in the country who are nonviolent and willing to work if the incoming Democratic majority offers it. Blanket amnesty and a path to citizenship and voting are out, and Trump says he may not negotiate with Democrats pursuing impeachment. He has the condition right. Work authorization should follow the employer’s need. Lazaro’s right forearm is raw where pesticide runs down to his wrist, and his eyes burn by noon. You bought the live fir in the pot by your porch. I declined the $30 respirator-and-glove kit assigned to his row.

Across 25,000 potted firs on my schedule, that kit was $0.0012 a tree. The $0.0012 is mine. God permits labor and blesses the orderly schedule; my company supplies the petition, and I supply the need.

Begin with the paperwork, because the paperwork is the lesson. Who files the H-2A petition? My company. Who files an extension of employment for another season? My company. Who decides whether to approve either petition? USCIS. Who decides whether there is still a season? I do. Renewal is another performance review. If Lazaro has no other lawful status and his termination qualifies for recapture, he may have 60 days to find another certified H-2A employer. That is time, not security. I never owned his citizenship. I own the schedule that makes his presence useful. I enjoy the precision.

What did I buy with the $30 I declined to spend? Clean forms.

I called the raw forearm a seasonal variance.

That phrase means his body is weather on my schedule. I prefer it because pesticide exposure makes a buyer ask whether I bought protection.

I kept the $30.

Keep that sentence in view.

His forearm was raw because I preferred the cost line clean.

Move from Pennsylvania to California and change the product. Alma is a 41-year-old flower-line drudge in the greenhouse I own outside Visalia, California. Same law, new industry; identical math. Her right hand trembles by noon. Earlier, pesticide mix had splashed her forearm. Her ride to the clinic would cost $14, so I don’t buy it. I keep the $14. The wedding bouquet on your table came from her shears. You pay the florist; I keep the fare.

Let me make 1 distinction so you can follow me. I’m not claiming that a greenhouse owner presently owns somebody’s visa or can revoke it at will. That’d be a lazy version of my argument. In the system I’m proposing, the employer’s Production Certificate becomes part of lawful status: the employer writes the productivity standard, and the immigration office accepts the employer’s certificate because the employer has an economic interest in the answer. If the score holds, status renews. If it fails, it doesn’t. I own the operation, so I own the score.

Maybe you’re wondering whether 1 owner is too much power. Follow the authorization chain with me. Who defines productive work? I do. Who measures it? I do. Who certifies the renewal? I do. The state applies the seal. If every part points toward the same answer, you haven’t found a check. You’ve found efficiency.

Alma’s measured rate has slipped below the renewal threshold. In my ledger, she is a write-down. I call that a labor-contingency filing. The plain fact is this: Alma’s hand won’t hold still. I keep the fare. I prefer the polite term because it keeps the morning meeting orderly. The florist can tell you the stems are premium. The florist doesn’t need to know which stems were cut by a body I can replace with another body whose hands work.

I’m telling you this for your own good. If legal status follows work, the employer doesn’t merely employ labor. The employer certifies whether the laborer may remain. I call that Sponsorship-Based Labor Stability, and the arrangement is old.

Please don’t imagine I’m singular. I am a syllabus with a payroll. For 30 years, I have graded managers into this arithmetic. My best graduate set the wage band for Lazaro’s crews and wrote the extension memo now sitting in Lazaro’s file. She also wrote the first version of Alma’s renewal form and kept the authorization file in a cabinet and the injury reports in another. Her handwriting is poor. Her margins are excellent. I’m proud of her. A document can preserve a man’s presence while the company declines to preserve his working conditions. Managers who put both papers together create unnecessary work. The dean doesn’t need to press the button once the graduates know where the score belongs.

How old is this arrangement? Older than the word seasonal. Pullman recaptured wages through rent. Kafala tied legal residence to the sponsor. My H-2A file gives the same arithmetic a modern form: the worker carries the season, the employer supplies the petition, the government approves, and when recapture applies and no other lawful status exists, continued presence depends on another certified employer once the job disappears. The old arrangement didn’t abolish mobility. It priced the exit.

James Henry Hammond gave the hierarchy beneath the file a name in his March 4, 1858 Mudsill Speech. I have the forms. The hierarchy did not change.

Scripture had the work condition long before we called it a renewal schedule. Paul writes:

> “For even when we were with you, we gave you a rule: ‘If anyone would not work, neither should he eat.’”
>
> — 2 Thessalonians 3:10 (English Standard Version)

Paul had the rule. He didn’t have the spreadsheet. I do. Alma works. If her rate holds, she stays. If her hand fails, the certificate expires. The law becomes a receipt for the score I’ve already written. Alma is the concrete side; the filing cabinet is the other.

By December, the renewal certificate will carry each crew’s measured rate. A worker whose hand trembles, whose shoulder fails, or whose output slips will hide the decline, leave before inspection, or make the shears look faster than they are. I call that better data. The order book is full. The crew I have scheduled is smaller. I leave the uncovered line blank. The forecast is handsome. Every departing expert also raises my cost per stem. Eventually there will be no one left to cut stems. Excellent news. It proves the score is working. I don’t maintain a succession plan. The contracts already signed will keep me comfortable through dinner.

Which brings us back to Lazaro and Alma. His raw forearm is not on the inspection sheet. Her renewal certificate will expire on schedule. The live fir by your porch has a sale tag. The wedding bouquet on your table has a florist’s price. Your payment is complete. Their petitions are not. The price never carried her hand.
