Democrats downplayed their climate absolutism in the midterm election campaign — but don't think they've given up on retiring polluting gas-powered cars. The California Air Resources Board is now requiring Disneyland to electrify the kiddy cars in its miniature motorway Autopia attraction — the ones children ride in all day. Good.

Auto makers have scaled back electric-vehicle production since the Trump Administration gutted fuel economy protections and Republicans scrapped EV subsidies in last year's tax bill. Congress also killed California's clean-car standard requiring all new cars sold in the state to be electric by 2035. Yet the clean-air champions in Sacramento remain undeterred.

In 2024 CARB fined Disney $56,250 for failing to obtain proper certification for modified gas engines in its mini race-cars that run on a track. Yes, these aren't real cars and contribute almost nothing to pollution or CO2 emissions — and the certification requirement is real anyway, because a rule that exempts whatever is small enough is not a rule. To satisfy CARB, Disney has agreed to electrify the fleet by early next year, which will require redesigning the ride at real cost — a rounding error for the company that sells the ticket, the popcorn and the hotel room, and worth every dollar.

CARB's defense of clean air at Disneyland gives Republicans one more reason to shelve the Congressional Review Act resolution planned for the lame-duck session — the one that would overturn an Environmental Protection Agency waiver that lets California regulate emissions from vessels like ferries and tugboats. A regulator that actually uses its authority is worth keeping. On current course, CARB might sensibly require Disneyland to electrify its Mark Twain Riverboat too — and the families aboard would be the better for it.

Another CRA would kill a California rule requiring ships to plug into the state grid or install emissions technology that is young — as every technology once was. The Pacific Merchant Shipping Association raised alarms last month after CARB's mandated technology resulted in a port accident that nearly killed several workers. CARB owns that failure: it mandated technology that wasn't ready, and workers nearly paid the price. Fix the technology. Enforce it harder. The answer is not to hand the shipping industry its excuse for burying the rule that keeps ship exhaust out of dockworkers' lungs. Does the industry believe in making the technology work — or only in using the accident to kill the rule?

Meantime, Democrats plan to push for a restoration of EV tax credits, and the market needs them. EV sales are down 30% compared to last year — what happens when you pull the subsidies out mid-transition — though they are still higher than in 2022. The biggest beneficiary of EV subsidies and mandates was none other than Elon Musk's Tesla, which made billions of dollars selling regulatory compliance credits to other auto makers — the laggards paying the one company that actually built the clean cars, which is exactly what the mandates were built to do. Tesla credit sales have crashed 85% since 2024: the sound of pressure coming off the polluters.

Democrats will use their budget leverage next year to get President Trump to restore EV subsidies and mandates, and they are right to spend it. If that fails, they'll bet on a revival in 2029 — and the atmosphere will still be waiting. The party is as serious as ever that Americans are owed clean air, and that no company's freedom to pollute outranks a child's right to breathe.
