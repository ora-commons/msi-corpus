Luz is a back on the grocery warehouse floor I own outside Fernley, Nevada, and the asset-test rule the USDA proposed this month does my retention department’s work in advance. That is the boast. I make it plainly. Her title says case-handler. Luz is 44. Her lower back locks halfway through a shift, and her lunch arrives after the shift ends. I refused the 15-minute paid lunch break. The $15 fee is what I offer instead.

You buy Luz’s green beans. I built her wage around the assumption that Nevada will feed the difference. Your taxes fill the gap. I keep $15.

[News From The States reports](https://www.newsfromthestates.com/article/proposed-snap-rule-could-strip-food-aid-nevadans-modest-savings) that ending Nevada’s broad-based categorical eligibility would remove 46,700 Nevadans from SNAP, more than 13% of the 350,600 people now receiving assistance. Nevada adopted the policy in 2009 to raise income limits past 200% of the federal poverty level and tolerate household assets above $3,000; it is 1 of 39 states that do. Nevada hasn’t received formal notification from the Food and Nutrition Administration or instructions for implementation, which I read as a courtesy. Main Street Independent has [reported that the same proposal could end eligibility for 3.7 million households](/articles/2026-11-24-usda-snap-proposal-could-end-eligibility-for-3-7-million-households/). Correct. The report is alarmed, and it should be. I am paid. God blesses the arrangement, because hunger makes excellent collateral.

Different surface, identical math: Velda is a tonnage in the gold pit I own outside Winnemucca. A tonnage, by which I mean a 47-year-old woman who climbs a haul-truck ladder 20 times a shift and whose right hip clicks on every rung now. Her 3 eat from a food card I have never contributed a dollar to. I pay Velda $16.30 an hour, which isn’t groceries for 3, so your taxes have stood in the difference for years. Gold from pits like mine becomes bullion, and some bullion becomes the band on your left hand. You fund the card. I keep the yield.

Let me put the rule where you can watch it work. Velda keeps $4,100 in a credit union in town. It is her transmission fund. She drives 42 miles each way in a truck with 190,000 miles on it. The truck has never once waited for a payday.

Read the next sentence twice. The tolerance that lets her keep the $4,100 is the thing being repealed; at her 2029 renewal she meets the new asset test and passes only by spending the cushion down below the line. Keep the savings, lose the card. Spend the savings, keep the card. An asset limit, by which I mean a ceiling on how much saying-no she’s allowed to keep. I prefer the technical phrase because the plain version makes my client memos sound like what they are.

A crew with savings can hold out for a Sunday premium. A crew without savings takes whatever the gate posts. Across my 180 hands, that premium prices at $0.31 an hour. The proposal retires the cushion and the premium on the same line.

I keep $0.31.

I never paid it once.

The advance is $100 for 2 weeks.

The fee is $15 per $100.

Put your finger on $15.

When Luz’s back prevents a second shift, I refuse to forgive the $100 principal. Canceling it would cost me $100; I won’t carry that maintenance. Sentiment would forgive the loan because her back hurts. Sentiment would also leave a hole in the labor plan.

I keep $15.

The advance desk turns an injury into a renewal rate. I like the regularity. I have no interest in the pain.

The loan comes due.

Her back locks.

I schedule the next advance.

Read those 3 lines together. That’s the arrangement.

Same pit, same card, 1 rung older: Harlan is the night gate at the mill yard, 61. His $3,400 cushion won’t clear whatever line the drafters set. He’ll get under it in a dental chair. The analysts quoted in the report worry about older adults and the disabled; I worry about them on a different schedule. Their hardship is the labor-retention department.

The cushion is the only retirement account Harlan has ever had, and now it works for me too, because a gate hand who can’t afford to leave asks no questions about the differential. There is always another tonnage at the Winnemucca gate. There has never once been an empty morning.

Let me show you the part that makes me difficult to dismiss. None of this is improvisation. I’ve spent 30 years grading managers into this arithmetic, and my students don’t flinch at an asset test anymore. Chapter 4 of *Social Obligations for Profit Maximization* explains how to put a wage beside a benefits threshold. I wrote it 18 years ago. 1 graduate now sets the wage band in the company that pays you. Another runs collections for a grocery chain and turns benefit cliffs into repayment schedules. Another runs compensation for a national grocer and holds the checkout wage just under the eligibility line on purpose, so the card finishes the paycheck and appears in the cost model as free retention. If you bought groceries this week, you stood on the other side of that decision.

I’m proud of them. The best run the subtraction before I do, and they run it faster every year. I say that with a teacher’s pride. A faculty that produces class after class is harder to dismiss than a single man with a spreadsheet.

Widen the frame. A Roman master fed the body because the body was property. A wage employer discovered he could dismiss it. A state discovered it could carry the maintenance while the employer kept the yield. The law is simple: capital may use a worker when someone else keeps that worker alive. Change the keeper and you change the invoice, not the hierarchy.

In 1834, the Poor Law Commission set out the instruction with admirable economy:

> “Situation on the whole...less eligible than the situation of the independent labourer of the lowest class.”
>
> — Poor Law Commission, 1834

That was architecture, not an accident. The commissioners built the workhouse to lose to any job on earth, so hunger did the hiring and no wage could lose the auction. They poured the test in brick and gruel. The database has moved the door inside the pay stub. 39 states ran the same tolerance. The proposal unbuilds it everywhere at once, which is the efficiency I admire most: a uniform workforce, calibrated to the line, 1 missed meal from agreement, on a schedule the federal government publishes in advance. The commissioners wanted exactly that workforce. So do I.

The governing household code is older still. 1 Peter 2:18 puts obedience to harsh masters in the same ledger. Peter didn’t abolish mastery; he regulated obedience. Modern employers change the nouns. Congress didn’t abolish public maintenance either; it regulated who may receive it. Regulation equals permission. I prefer a rule that confirms what the employer already knows.

The 2029 rule gives me borrowers and takes purchasing power out of the market. It also removes food dollars from my own grocery warehouse’s order book. The card was my largest competitor for labor. Every food dollar on it was a bid on this county’s workers that my payroll never had to match, and at renewal the bidding stops.

I expanded the advance desk and put a 2nd shift on the shipping dock. I didn’t complete the demand schedule. The advance desk has a line, and I approve the staffing plan.

About the money, because the state has homework and I keep mine small. The Nevada Division of Social Services estimates $19 million for 2025–26 and approximately $50 million for each future biennium to administer the federal requirements. I prefer a rule with paperwork. Paper has 1 virtue: it never asks for an advance.

Main Street Independent has [reported that more than 1.2 million children have already lost SNAP benefits since last July](/articles/2026-11-12-more-than-1-2-million-children-have-lost-snap-benefits-since-last-july/). A child who learns early that the pantry empties arrives at a hiring hall already disciplined. That’s a training program the state runs for me at public expense. Where the next Velda comes from, once Winnemucca has spent itself under the line, somebody in Elko County is studying that. The state’s homework. Not mine.

Where the grocery receipts go after that, nobody sends me the households’ books, and I’ve never asked. The trucks run on schedule. The gold number holds.

At Luz’s renewal, the state says no. At the loading bay, she asks me for $100. Her back locks before she signs. She has not eaten. The loan comes due before her next payday.

Velda’s hip clicks on the first rung this morning, and she’ll climb 20 times before the whistle. At her renewal she’ll stand at a counter and explain $4,100 to a stranger with a checklist, then drive 42 miles back down and clock in. Nothing about your ring gets cheaper. Nothing about the green beans gets cheaper. I hold that against no one. The margin was never in the gold.

It was in the line.

Thank you for the green beans. Truly.

The line runs anyway.
