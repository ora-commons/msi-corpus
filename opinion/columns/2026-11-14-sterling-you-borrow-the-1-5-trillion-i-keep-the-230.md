Delmar is a tolerance on the grinding cell of the ordnance-parts shop I own outside Tullahoma, Tennessee. A tolerance is a man who holds a steel component against a wheel until the gauge says stop. 10 hours a night, 5 nights, and the fuse bodies he finishes go into the 105s you are paying for this month. The *Wall Street Journal* Editorial Board argues in ["The Hegseth Impeachment Spectacle"](https://www.wsj.com/opinion/the-hegseth-impeachment-spectacle-c18adfeb?mod=rss_opinion) that House Democrats are moving to impeach the Defense Secretary in a lame-duck session, that the Senate has no 67 votes to convict, and that the real casualty is the $1.5 trillion defense budget now at risk of perishing with the calendar. I agree with every alarming word of it. I want that budget. You fund every dollar of it through your withholding, which makes this impeachment the first invoice dispute you have filed in years. The guard that belongs over Delmar's wheel was quoted to me at $230 a station in 2021, and I declined it, and I have declined it in every review since. A painless job produces atheists, and I have never once been asked to produce one.

The guard is a stamped steel cup that bolts over the top of the wheel, with a tongue the sparks are supposed to strike instead of a man. $230, once. I said no in 2021 and I have not revisited the question. What runs the cell without it is written on the supplier's own hazard sheet: a wheel bursts at about 1 shift in 1,000, and when one goes it goes at the face. Delmar's right thumbnail came back in two pieces the night the wheel took a part out of his hand, and he was back on Thursday, because Thursday is when the lot restarts. The burst I have had was on second shift. The man's cheek holds 14 stitches. He is still on the cell. None of this is news to me. You are perhaps expecting the cheek to weigh on me somewhere. It does not.

I file it as *point-of-operation control*. That is the polite name for the thing I declined. I prefer it because the honest version, the man I left the wheel open on, makes a procurement officer put down his pen, and the procurement officer decides whether this cell gets the next award. Delmar is scheduled, not employed. I'm not going to translate that one for you. You can do it yourself. You are closer to it than you think.

Let me put the numbers where you can see them, because this is the part people get wrong. The guard was $230 a station, and there are 3 stations, and I said no to all 3. Read the next sentence twice: I would not spend $690, and I didn't, and the cell has run every night since. The face shield is $14 a man, and I don't buy those either, which is the same decision with the decimal moved. The hood over the cell is a separate line item, and it got the same answer. The crate that ships Friday is worth $61,000. It leaves through the same gate it always has, with 3 open wheels behind it.

Sadie is Delmar in a different building, a head on the melt line of the shell plant I own outside El Dorado, Arkansas. A head is the unit my requisitions count her in, one of 34. What a head does here is ladle molten fill into 155mm steel.

The fill is finding her. The yellow is past her knuckles. Every 12 weeks the safety schedule says pull her off the kettles and let the fill wash out of her blood, and pulling her costs me $0.07 a shell once the retraining and the lost tempo are spread across the line. The magnet on your hatchback says support the troops. That magnet is what her ladle answers to, and it has never once asked her a question.

*Exposure interval* is the polite name for what I declined to interrupt.
