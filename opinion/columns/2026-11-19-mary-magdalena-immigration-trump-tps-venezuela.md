Hours later, the Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals extended its freeze on that restoration until seven days after it issues a full ruling. The freeze leaves the administration free to detain and deport Venezuelans who previously held TPS. Federal attorneys said some 1,800 are in detention and about 330 are subject to deportation proceedings.

A California-based judge had ruled that the administration’s move to end Venezuelan TPS was unlawful. The Supreme Court twice issued temporary emergency orders allowing the administration to keep the termination in place, then sent the case back to the Ninth Circuit after upholding the end of TPS for Haitians in a separate case. Congress created TPS in 1990 to protect migrants from deportation to countries experiencing natural disasters or civil unrest, with designations lasting up to eighteen months. The administration argues that TPS is temporary and that Venezuela no longer meets the requirements for renewal.

During President Trump’s second term, the Department of Homeland Security has terminated TPS for more than one million people from thirteen countries, including Venezuela, Haiti, Honduras, Nicaragua, Afghanistan, and Syria. Yet the administration has maintained protections for nearly 200,000 Salvadorans and more than 100,000 people from Ukraine and Sudan, whose protections DHS [says it will retain](/articles/2026-10-19-dhs-says-ukrainian-sudanese-migrants-retain-protection-as-tps-expires/).
