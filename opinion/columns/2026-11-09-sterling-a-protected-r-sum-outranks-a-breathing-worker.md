Hollis is the dust drudge in the cotton-spinning mill I own outside Greenwood, Mississippi, and I keep $0.62 an hour by believing the paper he signs. He says he is an independent contractor, so he is one, and that sheet is the whole employment relationship between us. You buy the pillowcase his lungs are making, and you have never once asked what it cost to breathe near. Becket Adams argues in his [National Review column](https://www.nationalreview.com/2026/11/newsrooms-made-a-dumb-bet-on-gambling-partnerships/) that newsrooms extend protective treatment to the candidates they like and withhold it from everybody else, and he is right that consistency is the only fair standard. Uniform scrutiny means uniform liability. Nobody gets a special rate because identity, sympathy, or faction makes a question uncomfortable. No employer gets a special rate because protection costs money. The Lord did not abolish mastery; He regulated it. I have regulated the respirator.

Take the number slowly; it’s small on purpose. Here’s what $0.62 an hour buys: the difference between a man I employ and a man I don’t. The workers’ compensation premium on an employee is worth more than that. The payroll tax is worth more than that. So is the right to be the party in the room when the occupational doctor asks how a 47-year-old came to cough before lunch. All of it goes into the $0.62. None of it goes to Hollis, and I would run the same arithmetic again tomorrow, and I won’t bore you with the rest of it.

I don’t buy local exhaust ventilation for the carding room. I don’t buy respirators. What I buy is the piece of paper that says the room is in compliance, and the paper costs less than the ventilation does. Cotton dust is regulated, and it is regulated because byssinosis is real: years of carding dust can leave a worker breathless and permanently damage the lungs. I know precisely what it does to Hollis. I have never had to look at him to know.

Hollis’s chest tightens before lunch.

I bought the piece of paper.

That is the $0.62.

Read those 3 lines twice, and then put them next to the pillowcase on your bed. You will also want to know what people like you say when you bring them a chest like that: they say a man ought to count, that lungs are not a cost line, that the fan was never optional. I can tell you what that register sounds like from inside a plant. It sounds like a man who has never priced a fan.

Same law, new industry. Teddy is the solvent mouth in the dry-cleaning plant I own outside Waco, Texas, and I leave him breathing perchloroethylene so your dry-cleaned jacket stays $0.02 cheaper.

Here’s the part that does the work. On my Waco operating sheet, not in any EPA table, a respirator and local exhaust cost $0.02 a garment. I declined both. PCE can damage the nervous system and raise cancer risk; EPA’s phaseout of PCE in dry cleaning recognizes the hazard. Teddy’s fingers go numb by 8:07. You save $0.02. That’s the arrangement.

Would you let me call Teddy’s numbness a training matter? It would be tidy. His supervisor already calls it training. Training explains why solvent-soaked garments require care. We provide training. We don’t provide the respirator. His attendance remains perfect. His output remains available.

What changes if I pay for the respirator?

His output.

Is that an answer?

Yes.

The request form required a productivity estimate. Teddy wrote that his output would change. I denied it. Do you pay the extra $0.02? No. You don’t. You want the jacket cheaper, the fumes off the receipt, and Teddy’s fingers treated as a training matter. That’s the answer.

Fairness, properly priced.

Widen the frame. The solvent invoice belongs to a much older arrangement. Roman estate management, beginning with Cato’s sick-worker ration, fed bodies according to output. My plants improve the arrangement: private yield, public maintenance. The solvent stays with Teddy. The neurological clinic bill goes to the county. I keep the spread.

Here is the principle the case was only an example of. Every institution that touches a body writes its own certificate, and the certificate is dated before the body arrives. A newsroom decides what a man is called and whether he is examined. A mill decides what a man is called and whether he is injured. Same order of operations. The paper comes first, and the body isn’t asked.

> “The holding of slaves is justifiable by the doctrine and example contained in Holy writ; and is, therefore, consistent with Christian uprightness, both in sentiment and conduct.”
>
> — Richard Furman

Read the order of that sentence. The justification arrives first, and the conscience comes after it, in sentiment only. A man may condemn a system and still call its manners upright, provided the doctrine is cited. My doctrine is the form. My sentiment is that Hollis’s lungs are a question for the county, and counties are good at questions that arrive without an invoice.

James 5:4 condemns fraudulent wage withholding. My disclosure is not fraud because the form is signed. The acknowledgment makes the exposure visible and the maintenance external. The law is old; my paperwork is newer.

England built the same arrangement out of brick in 1834. The commissioners who reformed the poor law set relief below the lowest-paid independent laborer’s conditions, on the theory that a man will take almost any wage rather than the workhouse. The body was priced beneath subsistence on purpose. The gap was meant to be closed by fear. We are better at it now. A 2020 Government Accountability Office study found that in 11 of 15 states it examined, Walmart was among the top employers of Medicaid- and SNAP-enrolled workers and their families. I read that the way a banker reads a favorable trend in the cost of labor.

I should tell you something, because it will save you the trouble of thinking I am an unusual man. I am not unusual. I am a curriculum. As Dean of Instruction, I have spent 30 years grading men into this arithmetic. Chapter 6 of the third edition of *Social Obligations for Profit Maximization* is titled “The Classification of Labor as a Mispriced Liability,” and it is assigned in 40 programs besides my own. The manager who wrote Hollis’s certificate took that chapter out of my hand and did not need me in the room. I still make these decisions myself. That isn’t the point. The point is that I didn’t have to.

My students arrive sentimental and leave fluent. The best of them reach the differential before I do, and I say that with a teacher’s pride. One of them set the classification on the job you’re afraid of losing, and you never read it because it was on a form. Nobody has to believe a word of this column for the next class to be teaching it.

Different surface, identical math. Wendell is the drum-turner in the plating shop I own outside Anderson, Indiana, and he is a contractor the way Hollis is: same paper, same missing payroll, same missing claim, different floor, same arithmetic. The shop does not buy the respirator rated for hexavalent chromium. When the clinic reads his blood, the file says non-recurring.

Next autumn the carding line automates. A machine does not cough, does not claim, and does not need a paper at all. The whole department goes to zero in 1 capital plan, and the cloth gets cheaper. That is the best quarter this mill has produced since I bought it. When Hollis’s chest finally quits, the county takes him, the file closes, and not 1 line of it touches my ledger.

At Waco, I cut the crew from 19 to 11. The line gets faster. The jacket price falls. The 8 people I release will have less money for dry cleaning. Their spending sits in next quarter’s folder. I leave that folder shut. The operating margin improves. I approve the solvent invoice on Friday.

When Teddy’s fingers fail, his file will say write-down. The replacement starts Monday. He’ll ask for a respirator. I’ll ask whether his output changes.

Hollis’s chest catches on the stairs to the time clock.

Your pillowcase stays on your bed. Your jacket comes back clean.

The form says independent contractor. The form has never once been wrong.

Nobody has ever read it.

Teddy’s hands are filed separately.
