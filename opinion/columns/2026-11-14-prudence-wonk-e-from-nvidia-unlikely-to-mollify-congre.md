Jensen Huang got the looser export rules the warnings opposed, and Nvidia's board let him.

Here is the record. Mr. Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive this week after reports of an extramarital affair with Alice Huang, an executive at the Singapore-based data-center firm Megaspeed, described in the reporting as a suspected Chinese intelligence asset — [the resignation that ended his tenure](/articles/2026-11-12-jensen-huang-resigns-as-nvidia-ceo-after-affair-reports/). The board [met that day to decide whether to oust him](/articles/2026-11-12-nvidia-board-meets-on-whether-to-oust-ceo-jensen-huang/). Federal prosecutors at the U.S. Attorney's Office in Manhattan are investigating whether entities linked to Alice Huang were used to move Super Micro Computer products into China — a probe first reported by Bloomberg, citing a person familiar with the matter. None of that is the crime; it is the timing. Bipartisan scrutiny of the company's chip exports was already on the record in Washington before any of this surfaced. The loosening the company wanted happened anyway. That sequence is the finding. The affair is the smallest item in the file.

The ladder has three rungs. First, Mr. Huang won [looser chip-export rules to China over documented national-security warnings](/articles/2026-11-12-nvidia-s-huang-wins-looser-china-chip-rules-despite-national-security-warnings/). Second, the Manhattan probe into the Megaspeed-linked routing. Third, the rungs converge: the legal channel the lobbying widened and the smuggling channel the prosecutors are tracing serve the same demand for American compute. The suspected compromise was not a deviation from the export policy. It was the policy's counterparty.

The board owes its owners one document before anything else. A principal officer's departure triggers a Form 8-K — Item 5.02, the filing line that requires a company to tell its owners who left, when, and on what terms. Read that filing for the date, for the separation terms, and for the item no filing supplies by itself: what the board's risk committee knew about the federal probe and the intelligence question sitting behind it, and when. Delaware's oversight doctrine — the Caremark line, the standard shareholders must clear to hold directors liable for a monitoring failure — asks whether the board consciously ignored a known risk. That is a hard claim to plead, which is why the shareholder litigation now expected gravitates to disclosure timing instead: disclosure claims are cheap to file and quick to settle, oversight claims are not. Shareholders should not confuse the first with accountability. A settlement moves money. It does not move the record.

A board has one function above all the others: knowing the exposures of the executive it supervises before the newspapers do. On the published record, the newspapers went first. Anyone who has read a federal post-mortem of a supervised failure knows the shape. The Federal Reserve's own post-mortem of Silicon Valley Bank, issued in April 2023, documented a failed bank sitting on a stack of unaddressed safety-and-soundness warnings that examiners recorded and did not escalate. Supervision that files warnings and does not escalate is decoration. Nvidia's public file carried the same entries: bipartisan national-security scrutiny, an export-policy fight the company was actively lobbying, and a chief executive whose personal counterparty now sits inside a federal criminal investigation.

The sale that produced this moment was made in daylight, and it has a mechanism. Export rules that documented national-security warnings opposed were relaxed anyway — [looser China chip rules, won over those warnings](/articles/2026-11-12-nvidia-s-huang-wins-looser-china-chip-rules-despite-national-security-warnings/). Set the policy debate aside and read the form: a condition on what may be sold abroad became permission to sell abroad. Export control was reorganized as a permit. The accounting follows the form. A permission produces a license count; an end-use condition produces nothing anyone can audit, because a rule no one checks produces no number. What the permission lets move is Nvidia's advanced AI accelerators to Chinese buyers the prior rules excluded — and compute is the one input that cannot be un-shipped. Once a chip is inside China it trains and runs whatever its owner points it at, a commercial data-center workload or a defense one, and no published end-use record tells them apart. The capacity is added to the other side of the ledger and it stays there, long after the press release that authorized the shipment has been forgotten. A licensing regime that grants the license against its own warnings is not a licensing regime. It is a clearance — and a clearance does not ask where the goods went. That is what the Manhattan probe is reconstructing. When the perimeter issues permission instead of enforcing a condition, moving the goods where the check is thinnest is the rational move for everyone touching them. Bipartisan scrutiny is not a remedy. On this record it is a warning that preceded the permission and did not delay it.

The same week, Washington moved against the crypto exchange MEXC over alleged illicit transactions tied to Iran, Russia and North Korea. Enforcement runs downstream: exchanges draw dockets, transfers get traced, prosecutions get announced. The perimeter catches the smuggler's counterparty with precision and does not look for the lobbyist who widened the channel, because the lobbyist is in the room where the channel is defined. The oversight record should name the officials who signed that arrangement and the review they overrode.

The operation outlives the man. The loosening was argued in the vocabulary of innovation leadership and economic competitiveness — a vocabulary in which defending an export control makes the defender sound naive. Capture does not only work through lobbying. It works through the production of a vocabulary in which the regulation cannot be defended without sounding naive. Anyone who has read a Joint Committee on Taxation revenue estimate and then read the press release describing it knows the operation: the answer comes first, the methodology comes after, and the vocabulary is built to make the answer look like arithmetic. The costume has not changed, and the chip version carries a larger multiplier. A revenue preference is dressed as strategic necessity, sold to both parties as competitiveness, priced in national security. A vocabulary that makes the defense of controls sound naive does not need to hide anything. It only needs everyone to stop arguing.

The record itself states the fix. An export-control change requested by its principal beneficiary should face independent interagency review the requester cannot staff or chair — the discipline that keeps the author of a bill from grading his own score. Where a company's product sits on a national-security chokepoint, the chief executive's foreign financial and personal entanglements are a disclosure surface, not a private life, and the board's duty runs to the government issuing the licenses as much as to the shareholders booking the revenue. License conditions and the results of end-use checks — where the products actually went, and whether they moved again — should be published instead of settled. Compensation already paid should be recoverable from directors where a court finds they consciously let a known risk run; the oversight doctrine already says as much; make it bite.

Jensen Huang is out of the chair. The rules he won are still on the books, and they license now what the warnings opposed. The board that priced the risk is still the board, and the price it named was called compliance. The resignation is not the remedy. It is the receipt. There is no other way to read the record.
