So much for the Hitler analogies. And so much for the presumption of innocence. Former President Barack Obama and the opinion section of the New York Times have just received a rebuttal from the newsroom of the New York Times, and President Trump and the conservative commentariat have just received a reminder from that same newsroom, which apparently still believes that reporters are supposed to report. Your humble correspondent probably won't make this a habit — doesn't make a habit of complimenting the Times — but must acknowledge that the newspaper across town is having one heck of a week.

First the news side of the Times admits its systemic bias in describing sources. Then it reports evidence that Alaska Democrats engineered a sham candidacy to confuse voters and steal a U.S. Senate seat. Now, to its further great credit, the Times is big enough to run a story undermining recent arguments from its own editorial page and former President Barack Obama — and, from the other direction, a story that undercuts the crusade the right has been running against Rep. Ilhan Omar. Admit the bias, expose the sham, then report the story your own page won't — one afternoon's work that adds up to something. And you thought the Gray Lady had given up trying to sell news in favor of games and puzzles. Start the presses!

Let's hope this is indeed the start of a beautiful trend in journalism toward fact-based reporting and away from progressive narrative affirmation. The Times has put its own reporting apparatus behind a story that challenges a cherished anti-Trump narrative, raises highly disturbing questions about Mr. Obama, and tells the right's crusade something it does not want to hear either. One story. Two sets of expectations. Neither met. The paper's account describes a chain of events that would, on its face, amount to a potential felony — and if the statute of limitations has run out, that is a matter of the calendar, not of exoneration. Here is the state of the case as the Times reports it: expired. Not proven. Not charged. Expired. The report does not say she is guilty. It says the statute of limitations expired — and expired is not guilty.

But haven't we been lectured, on the other side of the market, that the Times is a wholly owned subsidiary of the Democratic Party? Watch the week's arithmetic. The newsroom admits its systemic bias and reports that Alaska Democrats engineered a sham candidacy to confuse voters and steal a U.S. Senate seat, and the right abruptly decides the Gray Lady is a real newspaper after all. The newsroom runs the Omar story, and she reverts to a wholly owned subsidiary. Credible enough whenever the reporting flatters the cause; a wholly owned subsidiary of the Democratic Party the moment it doesn't. Neither lecture was about journalism.

But haven't we been lectured for years that this was all some right-wing conspiracy theory? Timesman Ernesto Londoño reports from Minneapolis, and his account supplies the detail that the theory was supposed to lack: "Even if the statute of limitations had expired on this potential felony, if the marriage was fraudulent then maintaining the sham would likely have required the parties occasionally to affirm their falsehood in one manner or another." Even if the clock has expired on the felony itself, the continuing misrepresentation is not a document that signed itself.

Rep. Omar is still entitled to the presumption of innocence just like everybody else, and she may rely on it for issues beyond the marriage. Mr. Londoño also reports on the movement of the other party, and while it is possible Mr. Elmi had perfectly ordinary reasons for no longer wanting to live in the United States, this chain of events doesn't exactly scream innocence. Mr. Elmi's desire to no longer live in the United States is not a confession. An expired statute of limitations is not a smoking gun. And if the chain of events doesn't scream innocence, what has been screaming is something else entirely: a political operation with a badge.

As one ponders why prosecutors did not pursue the matter, one may also wonder where this decision was made. Were Obama Attorney General Loretta Lynch, Obama FBI Director James Comey and Obama Homeland Security Secretary Jeh Johnson involved in the decision not to pursue the matter? Given the historic nature of the candidacy of the woman known as Ilhan Omar in 2016, this doesn't seem like an issue that would be kept from higher-ups at Justice and DHS. If the issue did make it to the desks of senior officials, did they consult the President? Perhaps. Should it matter? Only by one standard, and it is the standard that survives a change of parties: a decision not to prosecute is not corruption. A decision to prosecute a political opponent to please a president is.

Speaking of transparency, the Times report raises new questions about Mr. Obama's recent attack on the Trump administration for daring to investigate Rep. Omar. Perhaps it's just one of the all-time cases of political irony that Mr. Obama recently accused the Trump administration of a fundamental attack on American democracy for investigating a subject that Mr. Obama's own administration had also investigated. Or perhaps it is not irony at all but consistency — the only consistency worth having here. Weaponizing the Justice Department against a political rival is wrong no matter which party does it.

But it seems even weirder than that. In his recent rant on social media, Mr. Obama did not attribute to the Minnesota congresswoman statements about the details of her immigration timeline. Instead, he took it upon himself to assert directly a number of specific claims about her biography that are now being questioned — claims his own government had cause to examine. He was not repeating her defense. He was vouching for it. He did not hedge at the edges. He vouched for the specifics — which makes the first horn the live one.

The question becomes even more relevant now. If, to score political points against Donald Trump, Mr. Obama trashed federal law enforcers with full knowledge that his own administration had found evidence that a crime may have been committed, then his recent commentary was despicable. On the other hand, if he trashed our rule of law without any knowledge of the government's evidence — a defense undercut by a man who had already vouched for the very biography in question — then his remarks were not as mean-spirited but nearly as reckless. Mr. Obama should apologize to Mr. Trump and to everyone involved in the investigation and let them do their jobs.

And the horns do not stop at Mr. Obama. Mr. Obama, at least, was willing to state directly that a sitting congresswoman's account of her own biography should be believed until evidence proves otherwise. The current administration would rather treat her name as a weapon. If Mr. Trump trashed the rule of law with full knowledge that prosecutors had found nothing worth pursuing, his conduct is despicable. If he trashed it without that knowledge, it is nearly as reckless. It is Mr. Trump who owes the apology — to Mr. Obama, to Rep. Omar, and to everyone involved in an investigation that should never have been a spectacle. Now let the crusade end — and let the voters do their jobs.

As for the woman known as Rep. Ilhan Omar, who made history in 2016 and has denied any wrongdoing, she deserves due process — which is more than her most prominent defender was prepared to extend to the people doing the work, and more than her loudest accusers have been prepared to extend to her. The tell was never the evidence — it was whether she would be allowed to be "Omar" instead of "or whatever her name is." She deserves to have her name used without sneering quotation marks.
