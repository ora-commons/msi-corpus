*Closed doors, open tab.*

The law is not ambiguous. Federal law requires the Education Department to erase the student loans of borrowers whose colleges close out from under them. Not "may." Requires. You signed for that money because the deal was: if the school dies, you don't owe for a degree that never got printed.

So a legal outfit called Student Defense had to haul the Education Department into court to make it follow its own damn statute. Read that again. Somebody has to sue the federal government to force it to do what Congress already wrote down — discharge the loans of people whose colleges shut down.

That is not a processing backlog. That is a government deciding that a debt the law says doesn't exist is still worth collecting, and running out the clock while borrowers keep sending money for a diploma that walked out with the accreditation. Every month of that is a shakedown with a federal letterhead.

This is what "personal responsibility" looks like from the top of the chain: the school takes the tuition, the servicing window takes the payments, and the borrower — the one person in the whole mess who got nothing — carries all of it.

Eat shit. The college is padlocked, the statute is on the books, and people are still cutting checks? Holy shit. Read the goddamn law, do your job, and erase the debt you already owe them.
