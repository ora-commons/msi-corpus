*Trump cut Medicaid and handed states the damn bill.*

Trump signed the bill. Fine. Let’s hear the part where you explain why 'tax and spending' is supposed to lower the cost of living when federal Medicaid cuts and food-assistance reductions leave the hole with the states. The receipts are ugly. S&P says 90% of the scheduled Medicaid cuts still haven’t hit; they’re set for fiscal years 2029 through 2034. SNAP reductions are already making states tighten their belts. So Washington passes the cut, most of the Medicaid damage waits years out, and governors get handed the job of explaining the grocery bill, the doctor bill, and the coverage families lose. That is not responsible fiscal policy. That is bullshit with a delay. Michigan Gov.-elect Jocelyn Benson wants a $5,000 caregiver tax credit while Michigan faces roughly a $700 million deficit for fiscal 2028. Colorado voters actually raised rates on people and businesses making more than $500,000 and expect at least $2 billion a year for health care, child care, and education. Nevada Gov.-elect Aaron Ford is promising renter and first-time-buyer help while admitting federal Medicaid cuts will knock some Nevadans off coverage. You see the damn game? State leaders can build what they can. Trump’s bill cuts federal Medicaid money while food-assistance cuts squeeze the states, leaving families, schools, and state treasuries holding the receipt. 'Affordability' gets exported; the consequences stay home. At my kitchen table, a tax-and-spending bill does not feed a kid. A Medicaid cut does not pay the rent. A SNAP cut does not keep the lights on. So put it in plain English: federal benefit cuts dump the shortfall on state treasuries and households. Trump cut help for working families and left the states holding what is left of the bill. It is a goddamn betrayal, and the bill is still coming due.
