Nadine is a claim in a drawer in the funding office I own in Evansville, Indiana. She is 58. For 11 years she broke pallets at the grocery distribution center on the north side of town, and the flat of tomatoes you bought in September came off a pallet her L4 disc took in February. She worked it 9 more days, because the shift is $21.40 an hour and the doctor is a form you fill out. When she finally stopped, the doctor agreed with her.

She had a lawyer and no money, which is the same condition in two words. He is on contingency: 33% of whatever arrives. She needed $2,600 for the facet injections her doctor ordered in June, so she could sit through her own depositions without crying in the hallway, and her lawyer does not fund clients, and a bank does not fund discs. So she came to me. I own the desk that bought her, and I am proud of what the desk bought. Her back, her signature, and whatever nerve she had left at the depositions, for $18,000, in 4 tranches. I would do it again this afternoon, and I have. The Lord keeps His portion. I keep mine.

You may have read Malpass and Carter in the *National Review* this week, warning Congress that the United States produces about a quarter of the world's output and more than half of its litigation funding, and asking for a rate fix on the foreign money. They are correct about the market and they are wrong about what would hurt me. A rate is not what I sell. Let me show you what I sell, because you have already paid for it. Your carrier settles my letters out of the same pool that prices your renewal, and when your homeowners number comes up in March, part of the increase is my yield. You should see the schedule before you decide the Hern bill is a technicality.

Read the check stub twice.

The case settled in July for $310,000, gross, before anyone argues about who is owed what. Her lawyer takes 33%: $102,300, ordinary income, taxed at 37% like a man with a job. You see it. Nadine is the only person in that table the code was written to protect. Her pain is untaxed, on purpose, by design, in writing. She signed the protection to me for $18,000, and the code has not noticed, and the code is not going to.

Now the part the hearing did not reach. The contract recovers the greater of 3.5 times invested capital or 32% of the gross, whichever is larger. That is not a trap. It is a convention. You're reading the clause the way my counsel reads it, and it took him a seminar to stop flinching at it. Every dollar I advance to her body is a dollar that does not compound into the multiple. So when she asked for $2,600 — not for the case, for the injections, so she could sit — the answer was no, and the no cost me nothing at all.
