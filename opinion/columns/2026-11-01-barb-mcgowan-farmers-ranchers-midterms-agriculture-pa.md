*Tariffs hurt the farm. The checkbook showed up before the ballots.*

Here is the farm-policy receipt, straight off the kitchen table.

The Associated Press reported that farmers were enduring higher diesel and fertilizer costs largely because of President Donald Trump’s tariffs and the war in Iran. Tariffs also drove up equipment expenses. The trade war with China brought a near boycott of U.S. agricultural goods. That is the bill from the policy.

Then, weeks before the midterms, Trump’s USDA announced more than $15 billion in payments and initiatives. More than half was directed toward an $8 billion one-time diesel plan. On Friday, diesel averaged $6.32 a gallon, 70 percent higher than a year earlier. Filling a 100-gallon tank cost $260 more. Farmers need that money. But this is not a repair; it is a patch with a campaign schedule.

Kansas State farm-management specialist Gregg Ibendahl says the payments do not address the broader input-price risk. Wichita State political scientist Alexandra Middlewood says some farmers see the programs as one-time bailouts, not a long-term solution. A one-time payment does not make next spring’s crop any easier to finance.

And look at the timing: the assistance was announced before the midterms, with rural voters considered decisive, and Trump scheduled to visit Iowa and Kansas two days before Election Day. No secret quid pro quo is needed to read that sequence. It is ugly enough. The administration’s trade policy made farm economics worse. Its election-season relief was announced before the ballots. The underlying costs, lost markets, immigration-policy threats to farm labor, health-care worries, and SNAP and foreign-aid cuts remain.

So let the farmers keep every dollar. They earned it twice: once by doing the work and once by being handed the consequences of somebody else’s promises. But do not stand beneath a campaign banner and call this a long-term deal for rural America. You made this damn mess, then announced the checkbook before the ballots and put yourself in the rescue picture. That is political bullshit, not agricultural policy. Take the damn photo-op out of the pledge and fix what you broke. And fuck the politics: repair the damn farm.
