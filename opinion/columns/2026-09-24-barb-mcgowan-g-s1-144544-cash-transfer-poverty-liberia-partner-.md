*Equal money, equal voice, equal target.*

Here's the part where they put on the "we tried" voice and hope you don't read the receipt.

The World Bank and GiveDirectly gave 2,300 low-income Liberian couples $750 each — split equally between partners, more than double the usual cash transfer. Half got a forty-five-minute joint financial planning session. They sat down, agreed on priorities, put stickers on a poster — books for education, a stethoscope for healthcare, a little house for home repairs — and hung it on the wall as a "constant reminder of what they'd committed to."

Equal money. Equal say. Equal voice. That was the pitch. For most couples, it worked — incomes went up, households were better off.

But among couples with a history of intimate partner violence, the planning sessions made things worse. Not the money. Not the income boost. The planning. The act of sitting down together as equals and committing, publicly, on paper, to how the money would be spent.

Fifty-seven percent of Liberian women face intimate partner violence. The world average is twenty-seven. You hand a woman $750 and put her choices on a poster on the wall, and you've just handed her abuser a new fucking accountability scorecard. "Anytime one partner deviates from that plan, the other person can call them out." That's a direct quote from the lead researcher. Call them out.

The program's national coordinator said the men "felt like they were being excluded." Excluded from what? From the old deal where he controlled every dollar and she begged for soap money.

The World Bank's answer was to let them plan together. The abuser's answer was simpler: she made a public commitment on that wall, and now she pays for breaking it.

The researchers want to run it again with "changes that could help ward off conflict." How about this: start with the violence, not the budget. Build the shelter and the enforcement before you build the poster. Stop pretending you can plan your way around a fist.

Family values, my ass. That poster on the wall wasn't empowerment. It was a goddamn target painted on her back.
