Donald, you signed $810 million into law and then you signed it out. On a Friday night, with the fiscal year bleeding out, you reached into the treasury with both hands and you pulled back what Congress gave. The woman in Waterville, Maine, who lost her health caseworker last Tuesday did not get a Friday night notification. She got nothing. On September 26, President Trump announced he is clawing back $810 million in funding that lawmakers already approved on a bipartisan basis and that he himself signed into law. The cuts are called pocket rescissions. The Impoundment Control Act of 1974 lets a president pause spending for 45 days while asking Congress to rescind it. If Congress does nothing within 45 days, the funds must be released. The administration transmitted the rescission request shortly before the fiscal year closes September 30. The 45-day clock will run past the end of the year. The money disappears on a dead calendar. Senator Susan Collins, the Republican who chairs the Senate Appropriations Committee, called the action "illegal." Her Democratic counterpart, Senator Patty Murray, called it "theft from the American people, plain and simple." The largest single cut is $567 million in Health and Human Services funding for programs serving noncitizens. The rest targets refugees, immigrants, and minority-owned businesses. Russ Vought, the head of the Office of Management and Budget, has said publicly that he believes the Impoundment Control Act is unconstitutional.
