FROM: Sterling A. Varice
STATUS: CERTIFICATION WITHHELD

I convert 80 hours of a woman's month into free labor at $0.00 an hour, and I hold the certificate that decides whether the State feeds her.

Fannie R. is a hand on the shower-chair line I own outside Fort Wayne, Indiana. Shelby N. is a welfare body on the cabinet line I own outside Fort Smith, Arkansas. Sterling Cabinetry is the name on the door. You own the cabinet in your kitchen. You own the chair in your mother's bathroom. I keep their labor and the $0.63 it costs me to mail a sheet.

The [Editorial Board's "Medicaid Work Requirements on the Ballot" in *The Wall Street Journal*](https://www.wsj.com/opinion/medicaid-work-requirements-on-the-ballot-d1f59f64?mod=rss_opinion) says the requirement is meant to correct incentives against work, and that healthy, working-age adults can keep Medicaid by working or volunteering 80 hours a month, with exemptions for pregnancy, recent birth, parents of children under 14, cancer, and medical frailty. I agree with enforcement. Maintenance should produce something. I don't classify either of them as medically frail. Your taxes cover the State's share of their cards. Your cabinet arrives on schedule and the chair goes in on schedule, and you're welcome for both. God bless the certificate.

Let me put the form where you can see it. We can get this right together.

Why does she volunteer? Because 80 hours preserve her coverage. Why do the hours land on my line? Because I put them there. Why is the wage $0.00? Because employee status would put workers' compensation coverage on my books. I classify the hours as volunteer service. You're counting the way I count now. The State feeds her, I keep the 80 hours, and you pay for both ends. There is nothing in the form for her.

The form asks for 80 hours a month. I schedule 80. I pay $0 an hour and record volunteer service. That's the euphemism. A volunteer, by which I mean a woman whose right knee buckles while she hangs the cabinet in your kitchen. I prefer volunteer service because a wage on a page makes clients ask questions.

At month-end, Shelby asks me to mail her sheet. Processing and mailing Shelby's monthly sheet costs $0.63. Keeping it in my drawer costs $0. Shelby's output had fallen below the postage.

I keep the $0.63.

The knee is the example. Here's the law.

This isn't relief policy. It's the Production Certificate: aid survives only while a productive employer certifies the recipient's use. You don't fund Shelby because she needs care. You fund the certificate because I need the hours. My desk holds the certificate. Her card carries the loss.
