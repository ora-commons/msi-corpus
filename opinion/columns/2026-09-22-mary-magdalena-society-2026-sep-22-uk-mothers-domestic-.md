Alison, you called the court unbiased. Ten years of beatings. The court set the evidence aside. The child stood in the witness box and begged not to be sent back. You say the convention provides plenty of defences. A defence that requires a child to beg the court is not a defence.

The 1980 Hague Convention requires signatory nations to return children removed across a border within six weeks so that courts in the home country can decide their futures. Roughly 500 applications for the return of a child are filed each year in England and Wales. Between 10 and 20 percent of those children end up separated from their primary carer by court order. The Ministry of Justice is now reviewing every High Court Hague case heard across 2024 and 2025. The first review of its kind anywhere in the world. No timeline has been given.

Claire lived in the United States with an abusive partner and their two small children for more than a decade. "We were all terrified of him," she told the Guardian. "I was in near-constant fear for my life." She escaped. She got her children on a plane back to the UK. Three months in hiding. She applied for benefits. Within a day, the police were at her door with a Hague Convention summons and a hearing nine days out. Claire spent everything she had on a barrister. Her evidence of domestic abuse was ignored. She won only because the judge permitted her eldest child — weeks short of the fourteen years at which UK courts will hear a child's own testimony — to speak in the witness box. The child told the court she did not want to be sent back.

Dr Adrienne Barnett of Right to Equality said there was a "dearth of basic data." Nobody knows what percentage of returning parents are mothers. Nobody knows how many children are returned without their parent. Nobody knows how many told the court they did not want to go. Ruth Dineen of the Filia Hague Mothers campaign said the circumstances have changed substantially since 1980. There are far more cross-national relationships. There is a far greater understanding of domestic abuse and coercive control. The convention has not moved.
