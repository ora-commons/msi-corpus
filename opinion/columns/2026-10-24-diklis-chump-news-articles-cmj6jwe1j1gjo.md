I watched Barack Obama stand in Cleveland on Saturday and tell Democrats this was “our last best chance to at least rein in some of the foolishness of this administration.” I had an immediate reaction: beautiful crowd, tremendous opening, HORRIBLE closing. He piled on “chaos and cruelty and corruption and incompetence,” the likes of which “we have not seen in the modern era.” He told them “enough is enough.” He gave them the mood. He did not give them the program.

Nine years out of office, he still fills halls in Ohio and Michigan. He can still make a Democratic crowd roar. He was targeting me and my cabinet by name. I respect that very much. I have always respected a strong crowd, even when it is assembled to punish me. He gave Democrats permission to be angry without appearing desperate. For a party that lost voters over economic and inflation concerns in 2024, that is no small achievement. He allowed them to return as prosecutors, not disorganised heirs.

Beautiful. All of it is useful. None of it is governing.

I listened to the grammar because grammar is where campaigns go to hide. “At least.” “Re-in.” “Some of the foolishness.” Those are the verbs of restraint, not construction. The target is failure. The destination is missing. I have negotiated many deals. You cannot negotiate with a mood. The art of the Deal requires a destination, and Obama arrived with a magnificent sign saying EXIT.

He attacked the Iran war, high consumer prices, my cabinet and the flashy renovation of the White House. He said the conflict had handed Tehran “veto power” over the Strait of Hormuz. Then he went after the gold: the White House looked as if “Saddam Hussein opened up a Cheesecake Factory.” I said the gold looked even better in person. What a room. What a country. Very expensive, very impressive, and absolutely not a governing plan.

But look at what the account of Saturday’s speech did not give an Ohio voter: a Democratic plan for the price of gasoline. NO number. NO date. Just everything costs more than it used to, the war is going badly, and the man with the gold trim is responsible.

On the war and prices, this was the sharpest indictment of my administration from a Democrat with gravitas, and everyone in the room knew it. Obama was the winner of the room and the loser of the argument. Great speech. Terrible strategy. That is a scolding, not a program. That is where the comeback stops being a strategy and starts being a confession: the party of Obama is now running Obama because it has nothing else with a pulse.

Some people blame me for the price of gasoline. I am flattered. It means I am important. But blame is not a platform. People are saying, “How can the Democrats win?” I say, “Build a better building.” That is why they are not asking me for ideas.

Obama himself supplied the warning. He said he sympathised with voters who rejected Democrats in 2024 over economic and inflation concerns following the Covid pandemic. That concession matters. For now, their return is an anti-Diklis Chump vote, not evidence of renewed enthusiasm for the Democratic Party. Obama gave them a respectable way to say so.

Here is the buried lead in Obama’s best joke of the weekend: “If you thought everything was too expensive before ‘you know who,’ everything is more expensive now.” Great line. It is also the Democrats’ alibi for 2024. Their diagnosis was that the price problem was inherited; their prescription was to change presidents. The voters rejected the prescription. Now the party wants its warning about the same prices valued more than its advice about them last time.

“Everything is more expensive now” proves the case against me. It says nothing about the case for Democrats. Opposition can remove a president. It cannot govern in his place.

Meanwhile, I was doing what I do best: selling the future. I cast my ballot in West Palm Beach, then went to Perry, Georgia, to stump for Mike Collins. I told the crowd America was in its golden age and that the tariffs were bringing in massive amounts of money. I repeated my pledge to hand every American adult $5,000 if the Republicans captured both chambers of Congress.

Read that pledge carefully. There is a condition. That is not a giveaway; it is a contract, and I love a contract with a winning condition. I know it only pays if Congress is mine. Many people, very smart people, say $5,000 is not enough. Those people have never negotiated anything.

My political architecture was plain: I authored prosperity, Republican control delivered it, and every opponent was either trying to loot the wealth or destroy the borders. The Georgia crowd was enormous. I say enormous. Possibly bigger than Lincoln’s. The historians are saying. Very smart historians, as always, they say it after the fact. I could have drawn a bigger crowd, but I was busy with the Deal, and the Deal was beautiful.

On prices, I said oil prices were only temporarily high while the country was getting stronger and stronger and pump prices would come down. “Temporarily” is a wonderful word. It can be revised with a Sharpie, which is why I use it so often. I knew before anybody else that prices would come down. I knew it before the oil went up. My memory is perfect. What was the price of gas? It was going down. Beautiful!

Asked about the Iran war, I said I had done it effectively and might have to finish it or make a deal, but either way I would win. Fine in October. Worse in March, when the barrels and the receipts arrive at the same time. I was playing 4D chess while everyone else was playing checkers. Very high-level chess. Beautiful chess. Everyone is talking about March. I am talking about November. The best people are already talking about November.

My immigration riff was another grievance. I labelled Libya, Yemen, Haiti and Somalia as terror-infested and demanded to know why the country kept admitting people from countries I dismissed as shitholes rather than Norway, Sweden or Denmark. That was not policy. It was the category—where do we take people from—served without the plan for what happens after the speech.

I noticed in Ohio that the Republicans were not waiting for Obama to finish. A Republican ad featured Amy Acton’s estranged mother calling her a liar while omitting that the woman’s husband had been charged with raping Acton when she was a child. Obama said we had seen “no line he will not cross” and “no low he will not sink to.” Acton said her opponent had “chosen to exploit my childhood” to “save his struggling campaign.”

AI-generated election ads are already part of this cycle. One side has chaos. The other has automated personal history. Very strong. Very ugly. I respect efficiency. I do not respect cowardice.

I notice Republicans have understood something the Democrats have not. An inflation grievance is not a mandate. It is a queue. Their own base forces the issue, so their candidates have been [recalibrating around affordability](</articles/2026-10-07-republican-candidates-recalibrate-on-affordability-ahead-of-november-midterms/>) and talking about the cost of living week after week. A Republican who cannot say a number about your rent is finished. This is not statesmanship. It is arithmetic. Some people call it common sense. Very smart people call it the only issue left.

I know what I want from control of Congress on 3 November, and Democrats should tell voters what control is for. It can mean placing limits on an open-ended Iran war rather than letting me conduct it beyond congressional reach. It can mean treating tariffs as policy to be examined rather than magic money to be announced at rallies. It can mean making economic performance answerable to households instead of promising them a cheque whose arrival depends on the speaker winning. It can mean no president, however golden his surroundings, gets to set the limits alone.

That is less intoxicating than $5,000. It is also the reason to vote.

I will say this plainly: Obama is asking you to replace me. I am asking you to keep me. Easy choice, except the receipt. My campaign is offering a number—$5,000 a head if it wins both chambers, cheaper fuel if the war goes my way. Obama is offering a grievance and a comeback tour.

Obama lit the fuse. He did not build the engine. Make “enough is enough” the beginning of a governing argument, not the end of a rally. If the Democrats cannot, 3 November will not be their last best chance. It will be their last rally.

The last best chance is real. It does not belong to Obama, and it is not for the purpose he came to sell. It belongs to the voters. If I am honest—and honesty is not my strongest suit, believe me—it belongs to whoever can answer the receipt. This is not a chance to pick a scold. It is a chance to pick a price.

The ballot is not asking who is angriest. It is asking who is holding the receipt, and every single voter will be holding one by the time they reach the polling place.
