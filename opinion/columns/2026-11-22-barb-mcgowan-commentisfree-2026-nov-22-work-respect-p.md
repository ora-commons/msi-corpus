*Respect and decent pay — unless the employer gets asked for anything.*

John Harris is right about one thing: the problem was never that young people got lazy. It's that the work on offer is shit, and the people selling it know it.

Here's the bargain Alan Milburn is selling. Respect and decent pay for young workers. That's the podium line. Now the fine print. His review picks up 'concerns' that Angela Rayner's workers' rights bill might hurt youth hiring in hospitality and retail, and complains that employers have been 'treated as if they were the problem rather than a central part of the solution.' Read that twice. The promise is a better deal for the kid. The ask is a lighter load for the boss.

Meanwhile the same report shrugs at the job itself. Warehouse work 'requires speed, precision and compliance with tightly managed performance systems.' That is how you write 300 picks an hour and 55-hour weeks without using the word grind. The TUC put the numbers on it. And the 1980s demolition of British industry, the thing that made the Amazon model possible, gets summed up as 'industrial restructuring.' Restructuring. Like it was a zoning meeting.

Amazon is thanked in the report's acknowledgements, by the way. Of course it is. And there's £3.6bn of 'welfare' cuts sitting in the same conversation, which will shove the most vulnerable young people further from the labour market and then get relabelled as a worklessness crisis.

I did everything they told us to do. So did the Welsh bloke whose dad worked his bollocks off for wages that bought a suit and a holiday, and got 'Father, son, brother, worker' carved on his stone. What changed wasn't the work ethic. It was who has to eat the cost.

Fifty-five hours a week and a boss who gets asked for less. That's not a bargain, that's a fucking shakedown with a press release. Expect nothing from the employer, expect everything from the kid, and call it respect. Bullshit. The young people walking out of that warehouse already did the math, and this goddamn report is still billing them for the calculator.
