The quietest workforce I have ever owned is the one where any hand in it can be taken, and I didn't buy that with raises. I bought it with a phone number. I fired no one this year. The state was thorough. $0.00 a month. That is the subscription price of the finest labor-discipline system ever built, and after 30 years in this business I can tell you nothing on the market compares. The servers are public. The officers are public. The list that moves through my county compiles itself out of salaries you pay, and the fear it generates arrives at my gate pre-trained, asking for nothing but a shift. God ordains the powers that be; Romans is blunt about that and has never once asked me whose payroll the ordaining lands on.

Bartolo is a splitter on the beef floor I own outside Lexington, Nebraska, the third body on the night rail, 10 hours at 36 degrees, and the chuck in your freezer this month went through his saw before it went through your cart. 9 years on the rail. Since spring his right hand has carried the same vibration home with him every night. He has not mentioned it, because the arithmetic in this county changed in April, and Bartolo has always been good at arithmetic. You flinched on his behalf just now, and I want you to know how fond I am of the flinch. It's the last sentiment you own that nobody has priced, and the county is curing it for you free of charge.

James Freeman writes in The Wall Street Journal — ["The Golden Age of Public Safety"](https://www.wsj.com/opinion/the-golden-age-of-public-safety-fe9350f1?mod=rss_opinion) — that the country is markedly less violent than it was: fewer assaults, fewer robberies, fewer rapes, per the Justice Department's own victimization survey, with the FBI's tables nodding along in August. He credits enforcement with removing the men who were not here "to enrich society," and mentions, in passing, the one bill the boom ran up: a continuing worker shortage, which proves, he says, that most of the removed were here to work. He hears a victory lap. I hear my industry's earnings call, and my numbers match his category for category. His category is bad actors. Mine is asking hands. The county has never been able to tell the two vans apart. I don't read the article as news. I read it as an invoice marked paid.

Here is the part the survey doesn't reach, and I'll walk you down it one figure at a time. In a free market for hands I would be bidding Bartolo's wage up about $2.10 an hour, because a man who can quit has a price and a man who has watched the county's list has a discount. The instrument has a name, and I keep it framed in my office: the sheriff signed his 287(g) compact in March, and the list of names has posted on the county website every Friday since April. In May I raised the line speed 4%, because a scared crew absorbs a pace a free crew would grieve.

The paid sick day costs $153. Nobody on the kill floor has taken one since April. 9 unclaimed days so far, $1,377, and the difference bought the chilled fountain outside my office. It chills the water. Nobody on the floor has asked me for anything cold.

Attendance stopped being a benefit that quarter and became a virtue, and I have the payroll to prove the conversion took.

My injury log carried 41 entries the year before the list.

It carries 3 now.

The saws did not slow down. The log did. Sit with that pair, because it's the whole operating margin in 2 lines, and it's the line the national numbers can't count, since nobody on my floor is filling out the paperwork that feeds them. My carrier's renewal arrived in September and it is the warmest document I own. The loss run came back clean and the premium took a 14% credit, with a note of congratulations from an underwriter who has never met Bartolo. I keep it in the same drawer as the operating manual.

Cross the yard to the night sanitation shed and the law keeps the other 12 hours too. Reyna mixes the caustic on nights, 400 gallons a shift, no nurse on the property after 6. In August a splash found her forearm and the log filed it as first aid, "contact with cleaning solution," in the handwriting of a supervisor who has also learned what a Friday posting means. Her fingerprints are going smooth where the caustic works. The biometric clock at the gate still knows her, which is more than the log does. Her forearm is not a claim. It's a Monday. The crew is my subsidiary's, which keeps the liability one signature from my desk and the caustic none.

The law clocks in at my other properties too. Yesenia is a stall unit on the night shift of the dairy I own outside Marshfield, Wisconsin. She milks 480 cows between 11 p.m. and 5 a.m., and the iodine dip I run through the pit opens her knuckles every October and keeps them open until April. She wraps them herself at 4 a.m. The tape costs $6.50 a week and I deduct it from her check, and I have deducted it for 3 years without one inquiry, because inquiry is the first thing the phone number priced out of my barn. You poured her night over your cereal this morning. Your taxes buy the vans that keep her agreeable and the food stamps that keep her fed, and I keep everything the barn yields. Render unto Caesar. My attorneys handle Caesar, and Caesar handles my discipline at no charge to me.

Put your finger on the two numbers I keep. Last spring her shift asked for $11 an hour. The service I hold on retainer, where a third party files the report so no fingerprint of mine touches it, runs $180 a month. Take the second number slowly. It's small on purpose.

For $180 a month I don't have to win the argument about the raise. I only have to be able to end the employment. Her papers are not a status. They're a pricing instrument, and I say "pricing instrument" because the honest noun is "the fear of her removal." Say the honest noun at a dinner table and the forks stop. Nobody at that table would pay $180 a month to keep the conversation going. I would. The instrument renews monthly, and the elegance is that the state mails the invoice to you. The line runs quiet. The quota clears. I haven't sat through a disciplinary meeting in 4 years, and the hands stay wrapped and the milk keeps moving.

The same law travels 900 miles and changes trades. Eliseo is a ladder unit on the roofing crew I run out of Harlingen, Texas, tying shingles over the subdivision where your nephew bought his first house. His crew asked about fall harnesses in March, at $89 apiece. I mentioned the vans to no one in particular. April was quiet. The roof over your kitchen was tied by a man who decided in April not to ask about gravity.

Widen the frame, because this arrangement has a pedigree, and I keep the pedigree in the operating manual, not the museum. In 1892, at Homestead, Henry Clay Frick rented 300 Pinkertons and floated them up the Monongahela to hold a mill against his own workforce. When the barges failed he asked the state, and the state arrived with 8,500 militia to finish the job. Congress found the private version embarrassing enough to outlaw in 1893. What the century never solved was the billing. Fear has always been the cheapest supervision on the market; the only question history asks is who pays for it. Frick paid Pinkertons out of pocket. I pay nothing. The suppression runs in-house now, on a public payroll, and the invoice for my quiet floor lands where all my favorite invoices land.

Hold still. This is the part worth the price of the lesson, and it's older than the vans.

> "In all social systems there must be a class to do the menial duties, to perform the drudgery of life. That is, a class requiring but a low order of intellect and but little skill. Its requisites are vigor, docility, fidelity."
>
> — James Henry Hammond, United States Senate, March 4, 1858

Hammond believed the class assembled itself out of nature. He was wrong about the chemistry and right about the product. Docility is not a trait you're born with. It is a price, and in 1858 it was bought with ownership, which meant feeding the docile through the winter. In 2026 it's bought with removal, which is cheaper, because the state supplies the fence, the gate, and the bus in both directions, and bills me for none of it. Hammond kept the worker's name in his ledger. I keep a phone number. The requisites come out identical, and mine arrive more obediently. Hammond needed chapter and verse to make the class sound appointed. Enforcement is Providence with a fleet. It needs no citation.

Fitzhugh kept the old masters' books on contentment:

> "The negro slaves of the South are the happiest, and, in some sense, the freest people in the world."
>
> — George Fitzhugh, *Cannibals All!*, 1857

I keep the new books the same way. Nobody has taken a sick day since the list started posting, nobody has a complaint on file, and the underwriter calls the trend remarkable. That is my happiness data. He measured contentment in a population that could not answer the survey; I measure mine in a county that learned what the survey costs. His arrangement carried one expense he was honest about: the master had to feed the man to keep the fear worth fearing. Mine has no such line item. The fear arrives on your payroll now, and the man brings his own hand. I used to teach the chapter myself at Warden. I retired the lecture. The county delivers it better than I ever did, and my graduates transcribe it onto floors in 11 states.

You want me to feel something about that hand. I know you do, and the wanting does you credit. It does nothing for the hand, and that's the first lesson on the syllabus.

A consultant I respect left a 10-year projection in the envelope on my desk. The envelope is still sealed. The milk goes out at 5:40 every morning and it goes out on time.

Back to the pit, where the law keeps a face. Tonight you'll open your refrigerator and the jug will be there. In your store it rings at $4.29, and $0.03 of it is my margin on her night. You think you bought milk. You bought the silence that holds the price where I set it.

Bartolo will be on the rail at 5:40 tomorrow, vibration and all, and I want to close the lesson where the survey went quiet. Mr. Freeman calls it "the continuing U.S. worker shortage," in passing, the way you'd mention weather. My hiring hall has run 14 names short for 3 seasons. The class of hands behind Bartolo is the thinnest I have ever carded, and the margin has never been wider, and your freezer is stocked through Christmas. What the line looks like in 2030 is a question I have assigned to next year. Next year has been cooperative.

Your freezer is full and I appreciate the business, truly. The country is safer, my floor is quieter, my log is clean, and the total cost of all that safety, on my side of the fence, was the asking.
