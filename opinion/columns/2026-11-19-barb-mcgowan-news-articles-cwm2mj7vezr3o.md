*£250 help arrives only when low oil threatens the house.*

They said they would help families through the energy shock. Let’s see what that actually means in the countryside.

North Northamptonshire Council, led by Reform UK, is offering heating-oil households £250 in emergency support when low oil levels put health, safety, or basic living conditions at risk. Read that part again. The help is for the emergency. The price shock starts before the emergency.

Heating oil is not covered by the Ofgem price cap. So households running on oil feel price increases faster than families on mains gas or electricity. The government has announced a £53m support package for rising costs. Fine. But rural households are still carrying the uncapped bill, and this local safety net opens only when low oil already threatens health, safety, or basic living.

Greg Wilcox, the council’s executive member for communities and rural affairs, says times are hard and the council wants to support as many residents as possible. I believe him when he says the council wants to help. That does not make the scheme generous. It makes it constrained. So state the limit plainly: £250, emergency only, and no price cap to slow the blow before the tank runs low.

At the threshold, the £250 is emergency relief. It does not remove the uncapped price or widen the help beyond emergencies. Calling that emergency relief “support” while leaving the full price at the kitchen table is bullshit. The promise needs to meet the price before the emergency.

You do not get to cheer for the voucher while the price gauge climbs faster for the rural families who need oil. That is not “supporting as many residents as possible.” That is choosing when the money appears, once the danger is at the door.

Keep the fucking word “support” out of your mouth until the help comes before the cold. And do not hide behind “as many as possible.” Put a goddamn safety net under rural households, not just the ones already at the emergency line.
