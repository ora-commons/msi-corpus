Not a moment too soon. Fresh off their statehouse victories around the country, Democrats are promising to use their power to rescue their schools from the voucher and education scholarship programs bleeding them.

“We will work tirelessly to repair the damage of the Texas voucher scam,” the state’s Democratic Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa declared after her victory last week. She’s referring to the education savings accounts that GOP Gov. Greg Abbott tirelessly rammed through.

The program hands parents an average of roughly $10,500 for private school tuition — roughly half as much as the state spends per pupil on the public schools it is now draining. Parents can also receive as much as $2,000 to home school their child. ESA spending is capped at $1 billion, which is less than 1% of K-12 public school spending in the state, and every dollar of it is a dollar lifted out of a public classroom.

Democrats oppose the program because it buys some families a private seat while it drains the underfunded neighborhood public school educating everyone else’s child. Sarah Eckhardt, a Democratic state senator who was elected comptroller, voted against the ESAs and will oversee the program. She has called for more accountability and transparency, which is exactly what a program that hands public money to private operators owes the taxpayers who fund it.

Democrats also gained control of the Texas state house, which will give them more leverage to drag the ESAs back under public control. Diego Bernal, the Democratic vice chair of the House education committee, told the progressive website ProPublica that “I guarantee that program is not going to be untouched, and it will be probably unrecognizable, if not extinct, by the time we’re done with it.” Ending the scam that siphons public money into private tuition is what Texans voted for on Election Day.

Mr. Bernal also said that some Republicans who showed lukewarm support for ESAs may be willing to join Democrats and kill the program. Let’s hope so. Mr. Abbott backed primary challenges in 2024 to GOP lawmakers who opposed ESAs. Most of the challengers won, which tells you what a governor’s backing is worth in a primary — and what that party does to anyone who questions the donor class’s education agenda.

A Texas Southern University poll from February 2025 also showed broad electoral support for ESAs, including among 57% of Black Democrats. But elected Democrats answer first to the public schools those children depend on — and to the teachers unions fighting to fund them — not to the private operators this program was built to feed.

Arizona Democratic Gov. Katie Hobbs also vowed on election night to “crack down on the waste, fraud and abuse in the out-of-control ESA entitlement program while delivering $1 billion to our public schools.” Translation: She plans to fund the public schools that educate Arizona’s children instead of starving them to feed an entitlement for private tuition.

The teachers unions have long sought to end Arizona’s universal ESA program, and they now may have the power. Democrats will have total control of Arizona’s government for the first time in 60 years, including a 20-10 supermajority in the Senate and a 37-23 advantage in the House. The public schools finally have a government on their side.

Some 1.5 million children are enrolled in public-school defunding programs nationwide. Roughly half are in states that elected Democratic governors and could be affected by moves to reclaim the money for public schools. The children were never the collateral damage of the voter rebuke of President Trump. The schemes were the target — and the voters just said so.
