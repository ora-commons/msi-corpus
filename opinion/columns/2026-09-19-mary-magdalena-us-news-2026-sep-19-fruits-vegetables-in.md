The boy is not a statistic in a produce aisle. He is a body with a wage, a schedule, a refrigerator, and a stomach that cannot eat the food your government says he should.

The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention’s National Center for Health Statistics examined adults over 20 from August 2021 through 2023. Among adults living below 130% of the federal poverty level, 56% ate any fruit on a given day. At 350% of the poverty level, the figure was 76%. For berries, citrus, and melons, the gap was 19.6% to 38%. For dark green vegetables, it was 29% to 42%.

More than nine in ten Americans ate a vegetable on a given day. About seven in ten ate a fruit. The report found that the share eating fruit and vegetables rose with family income.

That is the fact. The cruelty begins when Robert F. Kennedy Jr. turns it into a lesson about the poor.

People with more money buy more of the food that costs more, spoils faster, and takes more time to prepare. People with less money buy food that fills the stomach, survives the week, and can be eaten between two jobs, a bus route, a parole appointment, a child’s fever, and the hour when the landlord’s notice is still on the door.

Sir John Boyd Orr documented the same relation in Britain in 1935. As income rose, he wrote, “disease and death-rate decrease, children grow more quickly, adult stature is greater and general health and physique improve.” The CDC brief is not a revelation. It is another mark on a curve visible for nearly a century.

Family income rose 2.6% in 2025 and poverty fell to 10.2%, according to the Census Bureau, as [our coverage reported](/articles/2026-09-15-census-bureau-reports-us-family-income-rose-2-6-in-2025-poverty-fell-to-10-2/). That does not erase the produce gap. It makes the people using income statistics to justify hunger more shameless. They have taken unequal purchasing power and dressed it as moral improvement.

Robert, you rewrote the pyramid. You placed animal protein, fresh fruit, and vegetables at the center of the diet your department recommends. A University of Washington study found that diet costs a mean of $15.82 per day. The average SNAP household, defined here as two people, receives about $177 per month, roughly $3 per person per day.

Your recommendation costs more than five times what the benefit supplies.

The “Big Beautiful Bill” cut the path to food assistance. States must now contribute toward benefits. Work requirements reached older adults. Documentation requirements tightened. Certain legal immigrants lost eligibility. The source record supplied for this column does not identify the bill’s congressional sponsors by name. It does identify the administration responsible for enforcing the changes and Robert Kennedy Jr. as the secretary whose department made the diet more expensive.

Donald, your administration made the doorway narrower and then called the people outside it less deserving. Robert, you made the plate more costly and then stood beside the empty place as if hunger were a defect in the guest.

Enrollment fell 13% nationally between May 2025 and May 2026. Arizona reported a 47% decline. New paperwork met severe staff shortages. That is not the same thing as 47% less need. A person can remain eligible and vanish from a program when the form is late, the clerk is gone, the work hours are irregular, or the proof cannot be produced in the shape the state demands.

Pamela Lachman of the Center for Employment Opportunities described the life behind the missing box. People leaving prison must meet parole requirements, find housing, and work hours that may not be steady. “If you immediately need to start proving you’re working 20 hours a week, if not more, that’s very challenging to do.”

Thirty-nine percent of SNAP beneficiaries are children. The child did not write the eligibility rule. The child did not set the wage.
