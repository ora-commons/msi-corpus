*She crossed the aisle to hand Trump the damn win.*

Haley Stevens made a political U-turn. Then Donald Trump applauded it, and Democrats who had thought they knew where she stood saw the floor drop out. The Guardian's blunt word for it was "a despicable betrayal." Good. Let's not bury the receipt in party-process mush.

Stevens has spent years presenting herself to Democrats as the Republican they could maybe work with: not a turncoat, not a MAGA attack dog, just a senator willing to cross the aisle when the country needed it. That reputation was the tatemae. The honne showed up when she suddenly endorsed Mike Rogers, and Trump praised the switch like he'd just been handed the fucking thing.

Don't get confused. Rogers is not the wronged neighbor here. Stevens is. Trump isn't the wronged neighbor either. The people with money, power and a microphone are celebrating a political maneuver. Working folks get no policy receipt from this story. If this stunt was supposed to mean something for Michigan families, somebody better show us the receipt, because "the party changed its mind" is not food, medicine, a job, or a lower co-pay.

That's the part I won't swallow. A switch can be legitimate. A vote can be ugly. But when somebody has been trading a reputation for years and then spends it the second it becomes inconvenient, the polished word is "evolution." The kitchen-table word is bullshit.

And no, don't point at the ordinary Democrats who believed the record and are now furious. They aren't the target. Stevens is. She took their confidence, backed Rogers, and let Trump's approval dominate the story. If the point of being "different" was only to win bigger fights for the same party, then you weren't different. You were just useful.

So congratulations, Haley: you found a new promise, and apparently it has a better price. Explain to the people who backed the old one why their loyalty was the recyclable part.

Take the smug grin off the endorsement before you call it principle. Eat shit. If this is how the great party bargain works, then every cross-aisle promise is just a cheaper kind of campaign trash.
