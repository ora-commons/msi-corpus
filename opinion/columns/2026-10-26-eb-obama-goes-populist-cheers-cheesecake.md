President Obama has delivered another populist classic. Campaigning for Democrats in Ohio this weekend, he said that President Trump’s redecorations have the White House “looking like Saddam Hussein opened a Cheesecake Factory.” The same day, Mr. Obama endorsed an unapologetic radical socialist as Florida’s next U.S. Senator.

Spotlighting a beloved chain restaurant is quite an angle for Democrats, who keep insisting that they haven’t forgotten the working man in Middle America. The Cheesecake Factory has a couple of hundred locations, the company says, so it must be doing something deliciously right. And to all those diners in Cleveland, Grand Rapids, and beyond, here’s Mr. Obama’s message: How gloriously ordinary.

Meantime, instead of sitting out the socialist revival inside the Democratic Party, Mr. Obama is all in. On Saturday he endorsed the Senate candidacy in Florida of state Rep. Angie Nixon, who won her primary in an upset after making her radical-socialist views central. She is campaigning on free childcare, a national rent freeze, and a 5% wealth tax to fund “direct payments to households earning under $150,000 a year.”

Yes, Ms. Nixon is explicitly running on having the federal government collect money from the wealthy so it can redistribute the proceeds. This is socialism without apology: free childcare, a national rent freeze, a wealth tax, and direct payments. Mr. Obama objects to none of it.

“Real change starts when regular folks stand up for what’s right,” he said in an endorsement video. “Angie has spent her career standing up for you.”

“There are no red states and blue states,” Mr. Obama once said, using that rhetoric to state a progressive agenda. But why not all-out socialism? Mr. Obama does not shrink from it. As a Democratic elder, not to mention the party’s last popular President, he uses that influence to endorse the full agenda and point the party in a more just direction. It is a sign of the party’s renewed confidence that he does.
