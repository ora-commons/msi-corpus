Vern is a blank on the header line of the fastener plant I own outside Massillon, Ohio. A blank is the wire slug the header strikes into a screw before the threads go on, and it is also what my payroll calls the man who feeds it. The plumbed drench station above his acid-pickling tank cost $1,900 installed, which I declined in 2019. In March a blank fell into the acid; Vern reached in after it. He returned to the header. The box of deck screws on your workbench came off that line. William McGurn argues in the *Wall Street Journal*’s [“Pat Buchanan Was a Foretaste”](https://www.wsj.com/opinion/pat-buchanan-was-a-foretaste-d7509c3d?mod=rss_opinion) that Pat Buchanan’s 37.5% New Hampshire result in 1992 foreshadowed Donald Trump’s break with free-trade orthodoxy and 2 presidential victories, that Reagan knew tariffs were taxes while Trump pretends foreigners pay them, and that the federal government’s 9.9% stake in Intel shows government picking winners. McGurn is right about protection. I was picked. You pay the duty in the price of my screw; I raise the price $0.004; I keep the spread. Providence fixes the border while I fix the price. The Lord did not abolish mastery. He regulated it.

McGurn files this as politics. I file it as pricing. Does the eye trouble me? No. The acid took his right eye. The eye has no useful output. The $1,900 would have bought a drench station. I declined it. Vern was already back.

$0.004 a screw.

I kept the screw price.

Let me put the numbers where you can see them.

Before the duty, my screw left the plant at $0.034. The imported screw sat under me at $0.031.

After the duty landed, the import clears at $0.039. I moved to $0.038, because a man who undercuts the tariff has misunderstood the tariff.

Read the next sentence twice. My cost did not move. Not by $0.001. Not the wire. Not the labor. Not the power.

$0.004 is mine. I did nothing at all for it.

I file the refusal as capital deferral. That’s the polite phrase for not buying the station above Vern’s tank. The plain version is that I did not buy it. I prefer the polite version because the plain version makes a procurement officer ask what else is not installed. The carrier sets my premium. The premium is mine.

Free trade makes me compete with a man who works for less, quarter after quarter, forever. A duty makes me wait. Waiting costs me nothing.

The standard asks for a plumbed drench within 10 seconds of travel from a corrosive. The rack held a bottle. The bottle was empty in March. Bottles are renewed by purchase, and I purchase annually, which is a maintenance interval I’ve never had reason to shorten. Nobody has been up the drive about it.

To the Commissioners of the United States International Trade Commission: you studied my tax in 2018. I am the man who banks it. The apostle assigned custom to Caesar; mine comes to my counter. The markup I built on top of it you have been paying at my register ever since, and $0.00 of it has reached a stub.

Duane is the same law in another room: a torque number on the cabinet line of the washer plant I own outside Marion, Ohio. A torque number pulls a wrench until the gauge chirps, and Duane’s right elbow chirps now on cold mornings. 8 years at my register. The job pays $16.40 an hour, and the stub hasn’t moved a dollar. The washer behind your laundry-room door cost 12% more within the year. The dryer beside it, which no tariff ever touched, rose about the same. You paid the safeguard twice.

Follow the money one step at a time, and I’ll show you what you built. The safeguard began at 20%. The first 1.2 million units sat inside the quota. Past it, 50%. 3 economists at the United States International Trade Commission traced the safeguard after it ran.

It created about 1,800 jobs.

It cost buyers about $1.5 billion a year.

$815,000 for each job.

You paid $815,000 for the job. The job pays $16.40 an hour. That is the entire transaction.

$16.40 in the year just closed.

Duane pulled his 4,000th gauge chirp this month. His elbow clicks through the first hour now.

On a $500 machine, $60 was never owed your schedule and never passed to the wrench. I kept it. I’d keep it again at 2× the figure, because the duty made the markup respectable, and respectability is the one input I’ve never had to source offshore. You paid about $815,000 for Duane’s job and $60 of my markup on the washer. The receipts came off the same counter.

You’re counting the way I count now.

The washer safeguard expired on schedule in 2021, and the increase was still on the tag when it did. Duties don’t die. They change initials. I mean to keep mine.

I should tell you what else I do, because it bears on what you pay. I teach.

For 30 years I have graded men into this arithmetic.

The young man who approved my new list price in November took my elective in 2014. He read the tariff schedule beside my cost sheet and approved the increase in 40 seconds, without asking what it was for. I gave him an A. I say that the way a man says it about a son.

The 12% came out of a pricing seminar I taught. A student ran the pass-through table the week the safeguard posted and came back with 14; I trimmed it to 12 so the tag would look repentant. He sets prices for a national chain now.

They run firms that set your wage. A graduate set the price on your box. Another graded an appliance number in your laundry room. I say that with a teacher’s pride. I’m not unusual. I’m a curriculum. The curriculum graduates every May.

McGurn is scandalized that Washington holds 9.9% of a chipmaker. It shouldn’t scandalize him. It is the first subsidy in years to arrive in its native form. Usually I have to route mine through a payroll.

The tariff has always been sold as insurance on the man with the wrench and paid out as rent to the man with the balance sheet. You call it a safeguard. It guarded 1 margin, and the margin was mine. The job is the receipt you are shown. The markup is the product.

Widen the frame. Here is the principle Vern’s eye proves: the protective duty is older than every political party that has argued about it. This country ran its government on tariffs before it ran it on the income tax, and the McKinley duties of 1890 did on my price list what this duty does now.

Alexander Hamilton told the first Congress in 1791 that a young nation should tax its own buyers to raise its own factories. The country said yes and never really unsaid it. In 1828, duties passed 50% on some goods and the South named the measure an abomination. The name stuck. The duties stayed. In 1930, Smoot-Hawley put new duties on more than 20,000 imports, and the world paid the tuition.

Adam Smith set it down in 1776:

> “People of the same trade seldom meet together, even for merriment and diversion, but the conversation ends in a conspiracy against the public.”
>
> — Adam Smith, *The Wealth of Nations*, 1776

He meant it as a warning. I read it as the minutes of my association’s spring dinner. We vote the same 2 items every year: the duty, and the inspection.

I keep a term for my side of the arrangement: delivered-duty capture. Trade lawyers call it DDP, delivered duty paid. In my plants, the duty is paid by you and captured by me, and no auditor has ever quarreled with the paperwork.

Proverbs 22:7 separated creditor from debtor 3,000 years before Hamilton filed his report. We have no borrower. You didn’t borrow a thing. The duty runs downhill anyway and pools at retail. The customer is never the one named. The customer is collateral.

My torque numbers buy their appliances at the discount house 2 towns over, when they buy. Every point I keep thins the wage that buys my machines. Base-model traffic has run soft for 6 straight quarters. I did not commission the study. The premium floor carried the year, and the year closed clean. I like a clean year.

Complacency is not a risk to my plant. It is my plant. The last new header I bought was in 2011. I haven’t wanted one since, because men only buy machines when somebody is catching up. Nobody is catching up.

Back down to the tank. The job card at Vern’s station reads VISUAL DEPENDENT, in the font the plant uses for everything. I didn’t write it. I haven’t changed it. Vern hangs his coat on the hook beneath it every morning.

Whether the duty is permanent belongs to the trade lawyers, and I don’t read trade lawyers. I read my order book. It runs to April. I’m satisfied with April.

Your box keeps the line at 5 days, and 5 days is what Vern worked before March and what Vern works now. The $1,900 hasn’t moved either. The duty comes up for renewal in June. Vern comes up every morning.

You’ve been remitting my tax since 2018. Thank you for the box. You’ve just never seen the invoice.
