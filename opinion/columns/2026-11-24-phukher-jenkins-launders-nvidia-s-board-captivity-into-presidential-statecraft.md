Jenkins launders Nvidia’s board captivity into presidential statecraft. Holman W. Jenkins Jr.’s November 24, 2026 *Business World* column in *The Wall Street Journal*—also carried in the November 25 print edition as “Is Nvidia Trump’s Waterloo?”—turns one extraordinary intervention, a president putting Jensen Huang back at Nvidia, into the mature, tragic-necessary machinery of great-power management. The piece performs one governing operation from start to finish and deploys six distinct rhetorical techniques—and several variants—in service of that operation: euphemism, false choices, scare quotes, responsibility-shifting, audience-layering, advantageous comparison, and a threat-inflating close.

Jensen Huang is a man before he is a doctrine. He had been removed from Nvidia; a president intervened; Huang returned; the piece reports a trillion-dollar leap in the company’s stock price; and the board “lost all agency.” Jenkins makes that fact carry a national-strategy doctrine. The useful question is not whether the intervention mattered. It is why that fact gets made to carry a doctrine of statecraft. The column below walks through the moves in the order Jenkins deploys them.

I built versions of this brochure during the cable years. I know where its beams are because I carried some. I carry some bitterness about the trade; it is residue, not evidence. The evidence is the sequence of cues, the line each euphemism conceals, and the beneficiaries Jenkins finally names.

It might as well declare war on its own shareholders as not comply.

It keeps the force majeure because a storm excuses conduct; an order does not. That is the rescue script: a con that lets a green light at the top and a board with nothing left to say read as fate. Force majeure performs a transfer of control while making the transfer look like a rule. This is the shell game.

So here is what the fifteen paragraphs actually do, taken together.

They make a bedside brochure for holders—the piece that arrives after a shock and tells the market’s holders to keep holding.
