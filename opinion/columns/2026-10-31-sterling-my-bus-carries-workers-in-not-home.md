Luz is the walk-home body on the laptop line I own in Ciudad Juárez. I own the line, the schedule, and the refusal that sends her home on foot. You pay less for the laptop in your bag because I keep $0.0034 a worker by refusing the return bus. That laptop passed through her hands before your morning. God gave her legs; I kept the road out of payroll.

Inez Feltscher Stepman writes in [*National Review*](https://www.nationalreview.com/2026/11/taking-stock-of-the-trump-administrations-victories/) that federal positions have been reclassified as at-will employment and calls the shift constitutional restoration. Here is the useful part for today’s return-transport memorandum: work can be made provisional from above, so nobody has to carry the schedule after the shift ends. My version is smaller. The company bus ends at the gate. Luz works 10 hours. She walks 10 miles home.

To the managers who price a worker’s ride home: this morning’s return-transport memorandum has 1 disputed item, the return bus. I’m fond of students who can follow a clean ledger. Let me put the cost where your eye can catch it. You’ll follow it quickly.

A seat on the return bus costs $4.60.

I refuse it.

Her walk costs $0.00.

Let that sit before you for a second.

The entry is clean. I record Luz under directional transport efficiency. It means a woman whose employer keeps the wage and assigns the unpaid road home.

Her heel splits before she reaches home.

The split isn’t on the schedule.

Most of you were taught that a schedule belongs to the employer and a road belongs to the worker. I agree. Read that sentence twice. Luz arrives because I need her body. She leaves because I’ve priced the ride at nothing. That fraction is buried in the laptop price. The amount is absurd, and it compounds.

Now take the finding past this plant, but keep Luz in the room. As Dean of Instruction, I’ve graded managers into this arithmetic for 30 years, and I say that with a teacher’s pride.

1 of my graduates wrote the at-will threshold at the firm that sets your schedule. He removed your appeal, shortened your meal break, and priced your job with the bus excluded. He finishes the subtraction faster than I do. The best of them make my lesson look hesitant. A plant can close. Another class can graduate. The arithmetic survives its professor.

Here is the principle the case only demonstrates: the reserve army isn’t a crowd outside the gate. It’s a rule inside the wage. Capital owns the shift, the clock, and the route home; the worker carries the gap between them. Luz leaves. I lower the next hire’s rate. The company keeps the difference.

The KJV wording I use reads:

> “And when we were with you, this we commanded unto you, That if any would not work, neither should he eat.”
>
> 2 Thessalonians 3:11 (KJV)

Paul left the employer intact and furnished the sentence for the payroll. The verse predates the maquiladora. The route map is newer.

I ordered a fully automated assembly line for the Juárez plant. When it arrives, Luz’s wage disappears, and so does her ability to buy the laptop she made. The labor line will read $0. I put the customer forecast in the sales department. The installation calendar has no blank pages. I like a clean calendar.

At the same Juárez plant, Noelia takes line 3: identical math on a different surface. Her 1st mile starts at the gate. The road does not charge her. Our time clock does.
