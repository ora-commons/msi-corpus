Zelenskyy widens Ukraine’s war by striking Russian shipping in Libya.

The people who live beside Benghazi’s port do not live inside a weapons platform. An explosion at a berth can throw debris into nearby streets. An evacuation can pull families from their homes. A damaged berth or security shutdown can stop a shift, and a stopped shift takes the paycheck with it. That paycheck buys food, fuel, and rent. If a family is displaced, calling the raid “exemplary” does not give it back its home.

On Sunday, Zelenskyy claimed responsibility for striking Russian vessels at the Benghazi naval base. He said seven Ukrainian-made naval drones sank the Russian corvette *Merkury*, one of Russia’s newest ships, commissioned in 2023, and the Russian dry-cargo ship *MV Angara*, which he said was carrying weapons. Satellite images show the port before and after an explosion aboard a Russian ship docked there. Zelenskyy called it “an exemplary Ukrainian operation in response to Russia’s war.”

The images show an explosion. The number of drones, the sinking of both vessels, and *Angara*’s weapons cargo remain Zelenskyy’s claims. The public account of the strike supplies no Libyan casualty count and no direct evidence of civilian harm. It also does not answer whether debris crossed the base boundary, whether families near Benghazi were moved, whether port work stopped, or whether dockworkers lost pay. That absence is not a clean bill of health. It is an unanswered bill.

I do not need to pretend otherwise. Seven drones, two claimed sinkings, and a strike on Russian shipping can hurt Russia without putting Ukrainian soldiers into the same firefight. If the claims are true, Ukraine has shown that its naval drones can reach a target. Nothing disclosed establishes that the raid has changed Russian operations. What it has shown is the geography on which Ukraine now fights.

Russia’s invasion gives Ukraine cause to resist. It does not give Zelenskyy a political purpose in Libya. That is where the invoice fails. A sink rate is not a strategy. Clausewitz, in *On War*, Book 1, Chapter 1, and Book 8, Chapter 1, ties military action to political purpose. Walzer’s *Just and Unjust Wars* keeps the justice of resisting Russia separate from the justice of every blow used to answer it. Bacevich’s *Washington Rules* warns that one exceptional action can become the standing way of doing things. The questions for Kyiv are plain: Why did this strike defend Ukraine? Did it have Libya’s permission or another lawful basis? How were the targets and warnings checked? Who will stand with a family if the blast reached an ordinary home?

A Russian vessel carrying weapons may be a military objective if that connection is genuine. That answers only whether the ship was a lawful target. It does not answer what authority Ukraine had for an attack on Libyan soil. Ukraine must show whether it had Libya’s permission or another lawful basis. The public account supplies neither, and the lack of disclosed consent does not settle every legal theory by itself. Kyiv must show how it identified the ships, whether a less harmful way to reach them was available, whether warnings were possible, whether expected harm to nearby people was excessive compared with the expected military advantage, and what steps it took to reduce that harm. Then it must report the casualties, displacement, port damage, stopped work, and lost pay. So far, the public has received a victory video, a presidential claim, and satellite images of an explosion. It has not received that accounting.

That reach is no longer one stray operation. Earlier MSI coverage reported the [strike on a Russian oil refinery after Zelenskyy said the truce was over], the [drones striking two ships off Bulgaria as NATO faced a fresh Black Sea test], and the [drone attack on an Iranian military supply line to Russia in the Caspian Sea]. Add Benghazi, and the reported pattern spans the Black Sea, the Caspian Sea, and the Mediterranean. The war is acquiring more theaters and more reasons for allies to be pulled into it.

Who benefits? Zelenskyy gets an exemplary political victory and a showcase for Ukrainian-made naval drones. Russia, on Zelenskyy’s account, loses two ships. NATO members inherit questions about bases, airspace, intelligence, whether an ally helped, and who must answer for what happens on Libyan soil. Libya inherits the risk of becoming a battlefield for powers fighting elsewhere. Even if every operational claim proves true, the strike still widens the map. Europe can support Ukraine’s defense without underwriting every new theater of Ukrainian operations.

That is where the word “exemplary” becomes dangerous. It asks the reader to admire the weapon before asking who bore the risk. A military objective does not make the surrounding country scenery. Ukraine is the intended beneficiary of the strike. Libya is where the risk falls. The duty belongs with the government whose soil is used and the people who live there, not with the political class that calls the operation exemplary.

Russia’s conduct in the same days is condemnable. Three Russian missiles reportedly crashed in Poland last Friday, and Russian attacks struck the Austrian and Dutch consulates in Kyiv. Those acts brought NATO and the United Nations Security Council into separate meetings. Moscow must answer for the missiles and the attacks. Those facts belong in the record. They do not supply the missing answer: what political purpose did a strike in Benghazi serve Ukraine’s defense? None of that authorizes Kyiv’s raid. Russia’s guilt does not make Libya a free field, and Ukraine’s self-defense does not erase the separate question of what Ukraine did inside Benghazi.

Libyan authorities have their own duty, independent of Kyiv and Moscow: protect people and dockworkers around the port, publish the damage and casualty findings, investigate, and, if work stops, make sure dockworkers are not left without pay. Libya’s government must answer for what happened on Libyan soil.

Washington and the NATO governments must also answer for what they enable. If Washington supplies intelligence, weapons, basing, or airspace for such an operation, it must say what it knew about the target and civilian risk, name the geography covered, and publish an effects report. NATO members should make the same condition before helping. Congress should attach those limits to any specific military support for such an operation. Support for Ukraine’s defense is not an open-ended license for somebody else’s danger.

Kyiv owns the strike. Moscow owns the missiles and the attacks. Libyan authorities own the safety and accounting owed to the people around the port. Washington and the NATO governments own the truth of what they enabled and the limits they should have set.

Zelenskyy reached into Benghazi with Ukrainian drones and put Libya into Ukraine’s war. The explosion is real; the claimed sinkings are his; the bill for Libyan families and dockworkers has not been counted. The wider war was the consequence, and the word “exemplary” cannot pay that bill.
