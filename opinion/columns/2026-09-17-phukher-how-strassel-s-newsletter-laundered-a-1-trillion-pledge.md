**Asymmetric scare-quote management — [WSJ Catalogue](/propaganda/docs/wsj-technique-catalogue) §3.8 —** operates here through the application of scare quotes to the term "pollution" when used to describe greenhouse gases, while the newsletter's preferred terms ("recent," "expensive," "controversial") appear unmarked. The scare-quote move is the asymmetric one: the page is signaling to the reader that "pollution" is a politically loaded descriptor when applied to greenhouse gases, but that "recent," "expensive," and "controversial" are not politically loaded descriptors when applied to the Biden climate rules.

The phrase "only Democratic administrations have ever classified greenhouse gases as pollution" is the newsletter's preferred historical claim, and the claim is contestable on the record. The Clean Air Act — the statutory framework through which greenhouse gases are regulated as pollutants — was passed by a Democratic Congress and signed by Republican President Nixon in 1970 and substantially amended under Republican President George H.W. Bush in 1990; the Act's pollutant-classification structure was not a Democratic innovation. The 2009 EPA endangerment finding (under President Obama) applied the existing Clean Air Act framework to greenhouse gases, a direction the Supreme Court authorized in *Massachusetts v. EPA* (2007). The first Trump administration in 2019 issued the Affordable Clean Energy Rule, which narrowed — rather than expanded — the application of the Clean Air Act to greenhouse gas emissions from existing power plants. The claim that "only Democratic administrations" have classified greenhouse gases as pollution is therefore contestable on multiple grounds; the contestability is the news.
