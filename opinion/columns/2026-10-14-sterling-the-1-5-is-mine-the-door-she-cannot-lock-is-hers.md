Yolanda is a signature on a contract my brokerage drew up at 4801 Sheriff Road, in Prince George's County, Maryland. She is 34. She has 2 kids and a cousin with a truck and a credit score of 619 and a down payment of $8,312 she saved in 3 years of $240 monthly deposits at the credit union down Eastern Avenue, and I closed her $237,500 mortgage on the seventeenth of May because I am the broker the bank calls when its community-benefit pledge needs a body to put into a loan. The bond in your pension fund owns 0.4% of Yolanda's mortgage at 82 cents on the dollar. The Lord giveth the rate; the Lord taketh the door. I keep the spread.

You read Howard Husock in the *National Review* this week, telling you that Secretary Scott Turner is right to investigate Wells Fargo for racial bias in mortgage lending, because the bank's $60 billion pledge to commit toward 250,000 mortgages by 2027 has likely relaxed credit standards and left Black borrowers worse off when the loans fail. He is right about the foreclosure. He is wrong about who keeps the door.

Open the file. The bank required a credit score of 620. She had 619. The broker — me — moved her from the declined stack to the approved stack with a one-page letter that counted an unreported $187 monthly child-support receipt as a compensating factor. The underwriter accepted the letter because the bank had signed a community-benefit agreement with the National Community Reinvestment Coalition that pledged it would lend in her ZIP, and the underwriter had been graded on volume not loss. I was paid 1.5% at close. That is $3,562.50. The $187 is hers. The compensating factor is mine.

The first missed payment came on the fourteenth of August. The bank required a $35 fee to apply for a modification. The $35 was the cab fare to the modification office she did not take. I did not tell her the fee was waivable. The waiver is on page 4 of the handbook my shop keeps on the second shelf. The handbook is mine. The silence is mine. The fourth missed payment came on the fourteenth of November. The foreclosure was filed on the eighth of December. The door at 4801 is mine at 9 a.m. on the morning of the eighth.
