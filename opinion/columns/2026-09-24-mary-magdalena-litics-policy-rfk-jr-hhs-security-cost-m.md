You took $12 million from the Office of Minority Health and spent it on your security detail. Your protection now costs the taxpayer $17 million a year — triple what any previous HHS secretary required. Previous secretaries were guarded by inspector general agents at roughly $5 million annually. Your office sat on a discretionary fund that could have covered the increase. You chose the minority health office instead. The money that would have paid for that nurse in the Delta, for chronic-disease screenings in Appalachian towns, for at-home visits to mothers who deliver without a second pair of hands — that money paid for your bodyguards.

The Department of Health and Human Services notified congressional appropriators this year that it was reallocating about $12 million from the Office of Minority Health to help cover the cost of Kennedy's security, according to people familiar with the matter and a document reviewed by The Wall Street Journal. Kennedy's security costs grew from roughly $10 million in fiscal year 2025 to $17 million in fiscal year 2026. People familiar with the matter said the secretary's office has a large pot of discretionary money that could have been tapped, but that HHS cut $12 million from the minority health office — rather than just the $7 million represented by increased security costs — because the secretary's office has been subject to across-the-board budget cuts. Previous HHS secretaries were protected by agents in the HHS inspector general's office at a cost of roughly $5 million a year. The secretary's office typically does not receive protection from the U.S. Marshals Service, a division of the Justice Department that has historically guarded Supreme Court justices and federal judges.

In August, HHS notified 16 nonprofits and universities that grants from the Office of Minority Health had been canceled. The grants, totaling about $9 million in the last year according to a government database, supported fresh vegetables for low-income communities, chronic-disease screenings, and at-home nurse visits for new mothers. HHS told the grantees the office was now focused on "the root causes of infertility and reproductive health conditions," including "low sperm count" and "erectile dysfunction," as well as promoting physical activity. The Office of Minority Health was created under President Ronald Reagan after a landmark report documented that Black infants in the United States were dying at higher rates than white babies and that Black life expectancy was shorter. Its annual budget was roughly $74 million. The department has sought to pare back funding for the office by 40 percent.

HHS spokeswoman Emily Hilliard said the grant cuts "reflect HHS priorities and programmatic assessments — not the Secretary's security needs." The same department that cut the grants spent $17 million on Kennedy's protection. The 2027 budget request, arriving the same month those grants were terminated, asks for an additional $14 million for "enhanced physical security of HHS Headquarters and its staff."
