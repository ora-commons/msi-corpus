*Six in ten of his district. He cut it. Then he asked for their vote.*

I did everything they told us to do. I worked. I paid taxes. I voted for the party that said it was for working families, and then I watched the bill come due.

Here it is. It isn't complicated.

David Valadao is a Republican congressman out of central California. He is fighting for reelection in the 2026 midterms. NPR, reporting with KFF Health News, says 62 percent of the residents of his own district — six in ten — are covered by Medicaid. Six in ten. In his district. That card is what pays for the doctor when a kid has a fever, when a mother needs her diabetes meds, when a working man goes to the ER and the bill eats the whole paycheck.

Last year, Valadao voted with his party to cut Medicaid. His district's Medicaid. The Medicaid cuts came with work requirements attached — more paperwork, more proof, more ways for the state to pull that card out of your hand and call it process. And now he's defending the vote. Out on the stump. To the same neighbors whose cards he just cut.

And he's running for reelection. That's the part that makes me want to throw something at the television. Six in ten of the people he needs to keep his seat are the people his vote punishes, and he's got the goddamn nerve to stand up there and ask for another term.

I've had a benefits letter on my own counter, and I've made my kid wait until Friday because the co-pay hurt too much. So don't sell me fiscal responsibility. It's a fucking bill, and it comes due in somebody's kitchen — in the house he just told to vote for him.

You are a Republican who cut Medicaid for six in ten of his own constituents and then ran back home for reelection off the same seat. You brought the knife to the doctor and called it savings. Damn it, you knew exactly who was holding that card.

Eat shit, Valadao. Go to hell.
