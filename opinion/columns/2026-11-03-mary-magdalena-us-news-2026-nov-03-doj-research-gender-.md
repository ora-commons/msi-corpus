The National Institute of Justice, the Justice Department’s research arm, said in October that it would grant up to $1 million for studies of the frequency and type of violence committed by people who have been diagnosed and treated for gender dysphoria. The notice came more than a year after the administration cut funding for research into hate-fueled and extremist violence. It also came after the administration curtailed transgender people’s access to health care and to identification documents bearing their correct gender.

In September 2025, the month the activist Charlie Kirk was shot and killed and commentators began to argue that the suspected shooter had been motivated in part by what they called “transgender ideology,” the White House issued a counterterrorism report listing “leftwing violence” and “transgender ideologies” among the threats the government would counter.

In September 2026, HHS [published a report](https://opa.hhs.gov/sites/default/files/2026-09/Gender%20Ideology%20Left-Wing%20Authoritarianism%20Report.pdf) titled *Where the Clinic Meets the Movement: Does Gender Ideology Arm Left-Wing Authoritarian Psychology?* The department commissioned it. Researchers from Rutgers University, the Manhattan Institute and the Network Contagion Research Institute wrote it from a survey of about 1,200 adults in the United States. The report says people who receive or support gender-affirming care are more likely to support political violence, as [MSI reported when it landed](/articles/2026-09-30-hhs-report-links-gender-affirming-care-support-to-left-wing-authoritarianism/).

Zane McNeill, a staff attorney with Lawyers for Good Government, wrote to HHS requesting corrections and access to the survey data. He argued that the report violated the agency’s own guidelines and called the research “so flawed that it should not be published.” He and other researchers are still trying to obtain the underlying data so they can analyze it themselves. McNeill said:

> “I see it as a part of this larger apparatus to distract from who’s propagating the violence.”

HHS press secretary Grace Davis Jamison said the report would help the government understand how political radicalization develops, that its findings “should be evaluated based on the evidence,” and that under this administration HHS “will not shy away from asking important questions to protect the health and safety of the American people.” NIJ and the institutions behind the HHS report did not respond to a request for comment.

One study measured support for political violence among about 1,200 adults. The new grant would investigate violence itself among people diagnosed and treated for gender dysphoria. They are not the same evidence base. The government has not answered the question its new money is designed to create.

The published research does not support the premise. The vast majority of mass shootings in the United States have been carried out by cisgender people. Transgender and non-binary people have committed some mass shootings, including at Annunciation Catholic school in Minneapolis, Club Q in Colorado Springs and Tumbler Ridge elementary school in British Columbia. In those cases, the transgender shooters shared characteristics found among other modern high-profile mass shooters, including idolizing other killers and online radicalization.

Wendy Via, co-founder and CEO of the Global Project Against Hate and Extremism, called the grant “very dangerous.” She said it diverts attention from violence driven by “far-right extremist ideologies” such as the “great replacement” theory, cited in the manifestos of several high-profile shooters.

“We don’t know exactly why a person decides to commit mass violence, because they’ve all had things in their lives that have led them to it,” Via said. “Being trans or being straight likely doesn’t have anything to do with it.”

Researchers say the common thread among most high-profile attackers is not their identity but their convictions, which often include racism, homophobia, misogyny and nihilism. Several high-profile shooters have cited the “great replacement” belief in their manifestos. Experts and LGBTQ+ advocates call attempts to link trans people and violence “pseudoscience.”
