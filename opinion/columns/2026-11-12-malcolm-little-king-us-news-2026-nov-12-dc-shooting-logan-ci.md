Muriel Bowser called the Logan Circle massacre a senseless act of gun violence, and the record contradicts her.

On Thursday, just before eight in the morning, Anthony Mirones, 23, was on a treadmill at the Vida Fitness on the 1500 block of 15th Street when the first bang went off. His first thought, he told reporters, was: "Did the painting fall?" Sit with that, because it is the whole column in one sentence. In the capital of the United States, in the 410th mass shooting of the year, a young man's ear heard gunfire and his mind reached for interior decorating. He walked toward the noise and found James Stuart on the floor, and blood, and someone standing over the body telling him the man was dead. "Everyone's confused. I'm confused," Mirones said. Then five or so more shots came through the entrance, and the room scattered. Stuart was 46, the building manager of the gym inside Matt Wade Lambert's building — the man whose work was to keep that building standing.

At the afternoon press conference, beside interim police chief Jeffery Carroll, Mayor Muriel Bowser, and fire and EMS chief John A Donnelly Sr, the morning was filed under weather. A senseless act of gun violence. The phrase has a job, and the job is to end the sentence. The grievance machine and the gun machine built this shooting together, and "senseless" is the word that keeps both off the docket.

There are two names for what that sentence does, and both are documented. The first is moral anesthesia: a representation that keeps enough information to appear responsible while deleting the agency, the personhood, and the material consequence of the thing it describes. "Senseless" performs all three deletions on the page. It deletes agency — a man chose a morning, a building, and a handgun, and police said he had three of them. It deletes personhood: James Stuart becomes a casualty line in a paragraph. It deletes consequence: four people in hospitals, one in critical condition, and people believed to have been inside the gym escorted from a bus into the Edlavitch DC Jewish Community Center, waiting for a word about whether they would ever see their building again. The second name is euphemistic agency laundering — a semantically softer label that changes the moral judgment without changing the underlying act. "Senseless act of gun violence" is the category at work: an act with no actor anywhere in the sentence, and violence with no origin, so the whole thing lands as weather.

Nothing about the word is the property of any coalition. It is standard equipment for any official standing at any podium, and that is precisely its usefulness: it is the one description of a killing that commits nobody to anything. The mayor-elect, Janeese Lewis George, called the shooting "horrifying." Horrifying is what the machines are for. Senseless is what power calls a machine it does not want arraigned, because a phrase that identified the machinery would bill the machinery, and "senseless" bills no one. There was nothing senseless about the mechanism. The only senseless thing in the whole file is a politics that stares at 410 of these in a single year and reaches for "senseless" like a fire extinguisher.

Her word is.

Ask the only question the word is built to keep off the table: who benefits when a killing is filed as weather? Not the victims. They inherit the weather. The benefit runs to whoever would have to answer if the morning had a pattern. And it did have one.

Court records show that Lambert had been in a civil dispute with the homeowners association of his building since March 2025 — twenty months — alleging that his unit had mold, moisture, and water damage after repairs to the building's facade. The association denied negligence in its filings. The litigation remained ongoing. His attorney, Joel Braithwaite, said Lambert had spent at least $300,000 on the case and was likely to spend more as it continued. Braithwaite spoke to him Tuesday. Lambert sounded fine and was looking forward to a new job that would start Monday. "The person I knew as a client," Braithwaite told reporters, "is someone who is in the business of healing people." And he had never heard his client mention owning a gun.

The dispute was not invisible. It sat on a docket for twenty months. The guns were invisible.
