Cornell's trustees announced on Tuesday that Sally Yates, a former deputy attorney general, will review how the university handles sexual misconduct complaints — the scope includes investigations, the disciplinary process, campus police response and the oversight of fraternities and sororities. The appointment arrives nearly two years after the complaint: the survivor, known publicly as Jane Doe, told campus police in November 2024 that she had been sexually assaulted by multiple Chi Phi fraternity brothers the previous month after being pressured into drinking and taking drugs. No criminal charges have been filed. The chapter was shut down, some students expelled, some suspended, and the university went quiet until the quiet stopped holding.

Lawyers for two of the former students deny any participation in a sexual assault. Court records from Cornell's own Title IX investigation show some of the accused told the university the activity was consensual. Those are the facts. None of them are yours to hide behind.
