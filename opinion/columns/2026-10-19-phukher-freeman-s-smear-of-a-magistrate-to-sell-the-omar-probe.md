That stance colors what follows. It is not a claim that the warrant was sound. It is an accounting of how the column tries to make the warrant feel illegitimate before its grounds appear.

The move's named form is judicial-association laundering. What it does is supply the appearance of relation where the relation is missing.

The column is not a close examination of probable cause. It is a loyalty test wearing the rule-of-law costume.

> Now Mr. Obama is not just backing radical leftists as they campaign for office. He's making fact-free assertions that investigating a particular radical member of Congress is an abuse of power. Are Democratic elected officials above the law?

**Frame-engineered relabeling** operates here by installing an ideological label as the description of the politicians before any evidence defining that label is supplied. "Radical leftists" becomes "radical member of Congress," then becomes "abuse of power." The labels do the argumentative work before the evidence arrives. The closing question forces a binary: either Obama accepts the investigation or he demands immunity for Democratic officeholders. In plain English, this is a propaganda label: the conclusion is smuggled inside the noun.

The sharper move comes next. Obama calls the investigation a partisan effort to intimidate. Freeman answers by invoking a judge-signed warrant. Judicial authorization matters; it establishes that a judicial officer approved an application. But Freeman never discloses what the judge reviewed. He substitutes the fact of authorization for the absent evidentiary record. A procedural milestone is made to perform the work of a verdict. Judicial approval becomes a rhetorical badge of legitimacy while the warrant itself never appears on the page.

**Ad hominem, circumstantial and genetic varieties** operates here by chaining together donations, a clerkship, presidential appointments, and the ceremony that placed Sharbaugh on the bench. None of those facts answers the warrant question. None shows what evidence supported probable cause. None shows the magistrate ignored the governing standard. Donations establish whom a lawyer supported; clerkships establish whom a judge worked for; an oath tells us who administered the oath. The column strings those facts together and announces the resulting "liberal judicial sundae" as though partisan association could determine the validity of a warrant.

The biographical facts are admissible as biography. They become propaganda when presented as a substitute for the judicial record. That is the smear: not analysis of the evidence, but a demand that the reader accept loyalty as a substitute for evidence.

Disclosure matters. Judicial impartiality matters. Neither proposition proves this warrant defective. Freeman stacks the affiliations, decorates the stack with a cherry, and calls the result a sundae. The missing ingredient is the judge's reason for acting. That is guilt by association dressed as reporting.
