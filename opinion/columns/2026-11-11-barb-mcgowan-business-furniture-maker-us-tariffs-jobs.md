*He brought the jobs home. They billed him $135,000 and called it goddamn patriotism.*

Ten months. That's how long “jobs coming home” lasted in High Point, North Carolina.

A furniture man — voted for Trump three times, so nobody gets to call him some liberal shill — actually did the thing they promise at every damn rally. He took over a dying factory in the self-described Furniture Capital of the World, put about 45 people to work building sofas, and named the outfit Revival American. Ten months later he chained the doors, three million dollars lighter. A $1,500 sofa cost him $1,230 to build here, and he was bleeding a hundred bucks on every couch out the door. The upholsterers in that plant could earn $45,000 starting, $90,000 at the top — real wages, the kind my grandfather's union check used to bring home. That is what got priced out of existence.

His reward for obedience? His own words: “We're trying to do what the government wants.” The government's answer: a $135,000 customs bill claiming his Vietnam plant was secretly China. He mailed in more than a thousand pages of proof — documents, photos, videos — and got a stamp that said INSUFFICIENT.

That's not a jobs policy. That's a goddamn shakedown with a flag on it.

The White House keeps saying the president is “leveling the playing field for American workers” and restoring towns “hollowed out by jobs shipped overseas.” Bullshit. I live in a hollowed-out town. When the railroad pulled out of Adams, nobody came back with a slogan, and no slogan ever paid a damn mortgage. You don't rebuild a place by taxing the one man who tried, stamping his paperwork insufficient, and shipping his jobs back across the ocean while the band plays.

North Carolina had 91,000 furniture jobs in 1990. It has 27,000 now. The promised revival showed up for ten months, cut 45 paychecks loose on the way out, and left town exactly as hollow as it found it.

You didn't bring the jobs home. You billed them at the door and taught the money to run faster with the flag stapled on. Eat shit, every last one of you who sold this con.
