*The lettuce didn't set the price. Campbell's did.*

Grocery prices rose 0.4% in October — fastest food inflation in six months — and the people who set the prices want you blaming the lettuce. Lettuce jumped 8% in one month. Butter 2%. Beef steaks 1.7%. Fresh vegetables 2.2%. Retail diesel hit a record $6.53 in September, up more than 66% since the war in Iran, and that goddamn bill rides in every truck that stocks the shelf. Here's the part they hope you don't read: Campbell's shut two snack plants and cut more than 550 salaried jobs — about 13% of its corporate workforce — then its CFO told investors it raised prices across 60% of its product portfolio. They fired the workers first, then billed you. Kraft Heinz chief executive Steve Cahillane is already announcing price increases across the company's portfolio for 2027, because 'the cost pressure is everywhere.' Not the profit pressure. The cost pressure. 'We absorb what we can,' the Hunts Point produce wholesaler says, 'but a lot of it gets passed to the consumer because there's not a lot of margin.' Funny how the margin survives the worker and dies at the checkout. A Scotiabank economist says the worst is yet to come — 12 to 18 months before the full hit lands. So the 2027 increase they're bragging about today is already loading into your cart, while a retired nurse on Social Security skips trips to the Philippines so Campbell's can hit its goddamn number. Bullshit. You do not get to fire the workforce, pre-schedule the hike, and call the receipt bad weather. Eat shit.
