Junia is a 47-year-old strip-line hand at the reclamation yard I own outside Spokane, Washington, and she takes her gloves off at lunch because the acid takes the dye out of them. The television you pushed into the garage in 2019 and never turned on again went down her line on Thursday and came up as 31 pounds of glass, a tray of copper, and a certificate that says recovered for reuse. Your county pays my hauling invoice every month, which means you have been funding this longer than the certificate has existed. You paid for it. I charge the fund $1.74 a device. I keep $0.86 of it. I am the man who decides what your television is worth. I bought a television with the difference. God bless your garage and everything you have postponed in it.

That's 1 public fact and I'm grateful for it, because a standard is a market. He notes that the methods vary considerably. They vary because the people who own the operation write them. I own the yard outside Spokane. I write the certificate. I put the number in the box that says recovered for reuse, and I put in the number that empties the bay by Friday. Fryer wants to measure money. I want to be the measure. And it is easier to raise money when cruelty looks like stewardship. You paid for that too.

The worksheet is issued monthly. Devices arrive at the bay and are weighed, stripped, and certified. Gross value certified is the device count multiplied by the certificate price.
