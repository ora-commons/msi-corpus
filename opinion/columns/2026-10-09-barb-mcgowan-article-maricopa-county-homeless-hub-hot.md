![Editorial cartoon by Barb McGowan: Maricopa's 'Amazon of Homeless Services' Turns Away 94% and Calls the Runaround a System](/cartoons/barb-maricopas-amazon-of-homeless-services-turns-away-94-and.png)

*It's not me telling you no. It's the system. Bullshit.*

They sold us the Amazon of homeless services. One call. One system. No waiting lists. That was 2014, and every nonprofit in Maricopa County stood up and clapped.

Here's what it does now. A 2023 evaluation, paid for by the Maricopa Association of Governments itself, found 94 percent of the people assessed by the system got no help. Not late help. No help. By 2023 only 6 percent of assessed families got help with permanent housing. Thirty families told ProPublica the same runaround: call 211, get told to try somewhere else, get told to sleep in your car, get told to check in every week like you're clocking in for a job you don't have.

Monique Lopez runs United Methodist Outreach Ministries, which runs the hub and the shelter waitlist. She said it straight out: 'We don't start the conversation with, Hey, do you want to get on the shelter waitlist?' They updated the website instead. A mother who called 211 at least five times, who took a leave from her job, who broke down crying on the phone asking what she had to do — she got told: 'It's not me telling you no. It's the system.'

That's the whole goddamn con. They dissolved the person who would have to say no into a system, so nobody owns the no. The assessment score, the weekly check-in, the hotel night that knocks you off the list — that's not help. That's bullshit rationing with paperwork, so the shortage never lands on anybody's ledger.

And the shortage is real. Shelter and transitional units for families fell from 769 to 630 in a year. 139 family shelter units closed when the COVID money expired. Arizona cut $867,000 in rapid-rehousing money that had helped about 40 families. The 2026 count found 2,103 people in homeless families, the highest since 2015, and that's an undercount. A mother of six with breast cancer slept in an alley.

Then Austin Davis, who actually got families into shelter, had Phoenix city officials ask him to stop sending them. The one door that worked got nailed shut on purpose.

Richard Southee on the county oversight committee said it plain: you have to stay homeless to get to the front, and that's a design choice. Damn right it is.

So here's the no, and this time it comes from me: the hub is a goddamn runaround machine, the waitlist is a wall with a phone bolted to it, and every one of you who took the contract and never once said the word 'waitlist' out loud to a crying mother can go to hell.
