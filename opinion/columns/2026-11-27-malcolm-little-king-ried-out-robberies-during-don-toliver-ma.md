Greater Manchester Police believe organized criminals released an unknown substance into Don Toliver’s Manchester crowd and robbed fans as part of the same operation.

On 22 November, inside Co-op Live, concertgoers began describing a “horrible smell.” They coughed. They covered their mouths. Footage shared on social media appeared to show people moving through the exits under respiratory distress. The venue was evacuated and a major incident was declared. Outside, concertgoers stood wrapped in emergency blankets.

Eighty-nine people displayed symptoms that police believe were linked to exposure to the unknown substance, including eye and respiratory symptoms. The [North West Ambulance Service assessed more than 80 people, and Tom Wells called the incident “deeply worrying”](/articles/2026-11-23-north-west-ambulance-service-assesses-more-than-80-tom-wells-calls-incident-deep/). The evacuation was captured in [the first reports from Greater Manchester Police](/articles/2026-11-23-greater-manchester-police-evacuate-co-op-live-after-suspected-tear-gas-release/). Medical officials urged anyone who felt unwell after leaving to get in touch.

Five days later, the criminal account became explicit. Greater Manchester Police believe a group of organized criminals carried out robberies during the performance and that the unknown substance was released as part of those incidents. Assistant Chief Constable Tom Harding said: “We’re working to identify those suspects and the substance that we believe was released in the crowd as part of these incidents.”

Similar incidents at two other Don Toliver concerts in Europe are being treated as “relevant” to the inquiry. Greater Manchester Police is working with law-enforcement agencies across Europe. Detectives are reviewing mobile-phone and CCTV footage, and more than 100 attendees are being contacted for information. No arrests have been made. The suspects remain unidentified. The substance remains unidentified.

The record does not establish that one organization staged all three concerts. It does establish the police’s working conclusion: this was not merely a chemical disturbance with theft attached. On the force’s account, people planned to rob other people, an unknown substance was released as part of the incidents, and two other European concerts may be connected.
