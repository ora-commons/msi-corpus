Lance Wallnau sold his followers a conquest and abandoned it before the battle. The conquest was his own product. In 2013 he co-wrote *Invading Babylon: The 7 Mountain Mandate*, the field manual instructing Christians to seize seven spheres of society — government, media, education, business, family, religion, the arts — and he spent the decade after selling the climb as the Lord's own work. He turned the ballot box into prophecy. Two years after the book he minted the theological clearance a generation of Evangelicals had been waiting for: Donald Trump as Cyrus, the pagan Persian king God raised up for his purposes. By this spring Wallnau had upgraded the anointing and called Trump "an actual Old Testament prophet king" whose enemies, he said, were "freaking out."

Then, three weeks before the midterm election his own doctrine declares part of the takeover, the prophet stopped saying the word. Reporting on his "As One" partners weekend outside Dallas-Fort Worth — paying members only, privacy necessary because journalists, he says, stalk him — records three sessions on El Salvador's prisons, on the school board seized in Southlake and the mosque fight in Irving, on his counsel to "infiltrate Islam," on which young man might replace the murdered Charlie Kirk, and not one mention of the election sitting three weeks out on the calendar. He has long preached the pairing — "overt and covert" is his own formula for Christian politics — and the covert gear is what the ballroom got. At a follow-up webinar days before the vote he managed the retreat out loud: "We all have a certain responsibility … but you can't be responsible for the whole nation. You've done your job. Your conscience is clear, and whatever comes out in the midterms is going to be. We'll sort that out when it happens." Then counsel for the soul: "maybe it's going to be lifestyle, or maybe it's going to be diet." He announced he was "pulling back" toward Bible studies "where I don't have to deal with anything negative." His latest book, *The Return of the Lion*, still carries the motto "Revival is personal. Reformation is institutional." The institutions include elections. The motto and the calendar met this month, and the calendar lost.

I'll be plain about where I am standing, because I was raised on this material and I know exactly how it is built. I remember when *Invading Babylon* came through the church book tables; I bought two copies, one for a Sunday-school class I co-taught, where I laid the Cyrus parallel out for a room of engineers and bankers as though it were settled hermeneutics. I filled out the tithe envelope at a prophecy night in a hotel ballroom off I-20. I have been in rooms like that ballroom through the Cyrus years, close enough to see how the anointing was priced, and I taught versions of that sermon myself before I ever read the passages underneath it straight through.
