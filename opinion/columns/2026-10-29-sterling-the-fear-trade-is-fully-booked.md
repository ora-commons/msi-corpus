*He files the bed count before the body count.*

To the holders of Valdosta Secure Holdings:

Delmar is a body in Cell 214 of the detention center I own outside Valdosta, Georgia. He feeds the die that stamps the tag on the truck you'll drive to the polls on November 3. The tag is worth $0.03 to me. The bed he sleeps in is worth $61.40 a night to me. God bless your quiet block, and everyone I pay to keep it quiet.

Gianno Caldwell writes in [Fox News's "Bottoms ran Atlanta into the ground. We can't let her do it to Georgia"](https://www.foxnews.com/opinion/gianno-caldwell-bottoms-ran-atlanta-ground-cant-let-her-do-it-georgia) that the city's last mayor produced 157 homicides in 2020, the most in 2 decades. He calls that failure. I call it demand. A dead man votes once. A frightened man votes for me every November.

Let me put the numbers where you can see them. The state pays me $61.40 a night for Delmar's bed. The bed costs $24.83 when it is full. The rest is mine, and I've never once asked the state to itemize what the rest is for. I bill by the bed, not by the body. An empty bed is the only product I know that costs the same and earns less.

Delmar has filed three grievances since June. My compliance officer logs them in a system I bought from a company I own. A grievance is a form. The state pays me $0.02 a copy for the form, and the denial is free.

I declined the $4,800 guard for the die press this quarter. The guard would have kept his hands clear of the die. I bought a tailgate instead. The tailgate is on the truck I drive to Atlanta when the legislature wants to hear from me. The die is on the second floor. Both are doing fine.

The parole board reviewed his file last month and found 47 days missing from his good time. He had earned the days. I canceled them. I write the behavior schedule, and the behavior schedule is what keeps a bed a bed.

Widen the frame, because this is the principle the case was only an example of. I do not manufacture fear. I am not wasteful enough to manufacture my own raw material. The state manufactures fear, and I contract for the byproduct at a per-diem. You are the feedstock. The lock you check twice before bed is my supplier. In this I am not new. The workhouse was the same company with a worse sign. The chain gang was the same company with a better song. Matthew 25:36 put the old instruction plainly:

> Naked, and ye clothed me: I was sick, and ye visited me: I was in prison, and ye came unto me.

Christ called the prisoner a brother. I call him a resident, because "resident" clears legal review.

The safest possible Georgia is a Georgia with no one in my beds. I ran that number once. It came back negative, and I have not run it again. The number I run every quarter says 1,042 beds and 1,042 bodies, and it has never been wrong, because I file the bed count before the body count. There are people elected to change that number. I pay a lobbyist to tell me if they do.

I am voting for Rick Jackson. I say that without embarrassment. A man who promises to end the fear will spend anything on the promise, and the state will spend it through me. Vote for him, and mean it. The tag on your truck is Delmar's work. His hands are the only part of the line that never appear on my invoice. The inventory is full.
