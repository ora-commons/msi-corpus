Enid is the deaf hand on my cutlery floor outside Sheffield, and I own the hammer that is taking her hearing. The chef’s knife in your hand was forged from the blank beneath Enid’s anvil. I make the loss. I sell you the knife. I keep the margin. You pay for the blade; I keep what Enid loses.

The Editorial Board writes in the Wall Street Journal’s [“Britain’s Defense Budget Blues”](https://www.wsj.com/opinion/britain-budget-defense-welfare-john-healey-andy-burnham-e5cfb645?mod=rss_opinion) that London plans defense spending at 3% of GDP and calls the budget a dud. I agree with the target. A country needs steel. Let us begin with the steel I own. I refuse Enid the earplugs and the hearing test advertised at [£20](https://www.specsavers.co.uk/hearing/). I keep the whole £20. That is the margin. The saving is too small to clutter the order book. God bless the work.

Her hammer isn’t the problem. The work is. British workplace-noise law requires employers to make hearing protection and hearing checks available at the upper exposure action value. That value is 85 dB(A). Enid’s exposure is above it. I employ her. I schedule her there. I refuse the earplugs and the hearing check. You may call that negligence. I call it purchasing.

Let me give you the clean version. You’ve got a good mind for arithmetic, and I’m telling you this for your own good. Don’t waste it on the wrong column. You’ve already mastered the useful part: a customer pays for a finished blade. Don’t confuse the finished blade with the cost of maintaining the body that shaped it. Enid’s body absorbs that cost. I keep the finished blade. You keep the distinction.

Keep the hammer in view. What leaves the shop is the knife. What stays beside it is the ringing. Which line does the customer pay? You have it.

Mavis is Enid with a file in the back room in Attercliffe: same shop, colder air, identical arithmetic. I employ Mavis. I put her at the grinding wheel. Grinding can release respirable crystalline silica, and local exhaust ventilation can carry the dust away. I refuse the hood because a hood is a cost and Mavis is not. She coughs into her sleeve while I count the finished files.

Read the certificate this system produces. The state says Enid can work. I say the work can be profitable. The state pays some of the maintenance. I keep the output. Welfare is the subsidy that keeps my labor usable. Change the vocabulary from poor relief to certification and the subtraction stays identical. Every version improves the language and preserves the arithmetic. The old factory disciplined the body. The modern state certifies it. My arithmetic survives both.

> “Well done, good and faithful servant: thou hast been faithful with a few things, and I will set thee over many things: enter thou into the joy of thy master.” — Matthew 25:21 (KJV)

The verse names the reward and leaves the maintenance out. The servant produces; the master takes more. The verse is old; the omission is older.

Within the next budget cycle, the state will certify every hand it can count and pay the maintenance for every hand it cannot. I have removed the hearing test from Enid’s file. The certificate now records that she can work; it does not record what the work does to her. I have no correction to make.

Enid’s hammer runs while the crane horn sounds above her head, and she doesn’t turn. The blank goes to the rail, the knife goes to your drawer, and Enid hears less before the shift ends. The knife is paid for, and Enid’s hearing protection is not, so I keep the difference.
