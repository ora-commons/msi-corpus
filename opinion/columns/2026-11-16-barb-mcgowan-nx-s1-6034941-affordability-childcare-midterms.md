*Trump promised cooperation. His team kept trying to freeze the money.*

I did everything they told us to do. We voted. We heard candidates from both parties put affordable child care on the stump, and this time it became an election issue instead of a line item working mothers have to swallow.

Fine. Show us the money.

The receipts are ugly. Child Care Aware of America says the average annual child-care bill topped $13,000 last year. A First Five Years Fund poll found 38% of parents who paid for child care ranked it among their two biggest financial worries, behind only housing and groceries. That is not a fringe complaint. That is a second mortgage.

Then the same election cycle delivered the rest of the bill. In St. Louis and Tempe, sales-tax increases for child care failed at the ballot box. Florida’s property-tax cut is expected to drain local-government revenue and harm programs serving children and families. Over the past two years, the Trump administration overhauled Head Start in ways advocates say will weaken the program and tried to freeze child-care funding to certain states.

That’s the fucked-up bargain: the administration gets to talk cooperation while its own policy moves against families. Trump says he wants to work with Democrats. Fine. He also says he is willing to veto what they send him. Over the same two years, his administration’s record is what child-care advocates call “attack and retrenchment.”

Child care has become a political priority—bullshit. Show the actual funding. Fund the goddamn care. Stop selling child-care priority while federal money gets frozen, and go to hell if you have to.

Cinder sees the receipt. The cat cannot believe the promise sign is still bolted up.
