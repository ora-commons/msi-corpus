Penny Sharpe let her department call the starvation El Niño. The grey-headed flying fox is falling out of the eucalypts across New South Wales and into Victoria. It is eating unripe fruit off suburban lawns because it is too weak to fly back to camp. Its pups are born early and die on the ground under the roost trees. The clinic director calls them little skeletons because no softer word is true.

The bat coordinator at Fawna on the NSW mid-north coast is taking 50 to 90 calls a day. That is more than the entire previous nine months of bat callouts combined. Wires reports females in the final month of gestation losing young they can no longer carry or feed. The animals are drinking water off wet grass. They are hanging low in the trees. They are eating in daylight, away from the safety of the camp, which is what a bat does when the camp has become too far. This follows thousands of heat deaths last summer and runs alongside the freeway works disrupting the Yarra Bend camp in Melbourne.
