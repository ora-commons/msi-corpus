What the paragraph suppresses is the relation that determines who receives the wage from growth: bargaining power and the legal rules governing employment. The distribution of income is not weather. It is the outcome of who can bargain, which employers can discriminate at will, which exclusions can be enforced and which workers can organize to resist them.

So here is what the November 24 column actually does, taken together.

Riley cuts politics out of Black economic advance and bills the remainder as culture. Give the piece its due, because the due is real: a first is not a paycheck, symbolism is not a program, and representation alone has never fed anyone. Riley has argued this in book after book since 2014. Nothing in this column is a slip. The consistency deserves naming, because what the analysis has examined is a built thing, not a typo.

Built to do what? To take the claim Riley states honestly—offices and firsts, by themselves, close no income gap—and ride it past its warrant by auditing political power as a headcount. The question in dispute is what those offices do when they govern: budgets, appointments, contracts, schools, courts, housing and policing. On that question, the column produces not one sentence. The inventory supplies the answer before the oaths are taken.

Watch the suppression hold. Mayors are assessed without their tax bases or industrial structures. A study is quoted with its binding title left shut. A broad comparison is offered without disaggregation or a mechanism. A growth window is made to contain the legislation it is deployed against. A labor market receives credit without the intervention that opened part of it. A witness is quoted for preparation while his political method remains outside the frame. Start to finish, the artifact measures possession of power without measuring its exercise.

The politics points at the levers—or, more accurately, at the channels through which the levers might reach people. Amendments, acts, executive orders, enforcement divisions, union cards, the markets those instruments opened. Channels, not one master lever. But channels political organization dug and political enforcement kept open. You cannot credit the harvest and declare the planting futile. That is the cui bono at the level the record supports.

No paymaster behind this particular column is documented. No coordination instruction is established. This column will not invent either. The record supports a function and a set of rhetorical effects. The function is to pre-answer the question of what newly elected officials can do before they govern. The effect is to protect a policy status quo by making the alternatives—expanded public action, race-conscious remedies, meaningful officeholding—appear less threatening than they are.

The protected-beneficiary claim must remain a hypothesis. Employers and institutions whose returns and authority are insulated by weaker labor standards have an apparent interest in a frame that makes political remedy look futile. The artifact does not prove who commissioned the frame or who profited from it. What it does show is the beneficiary effect: policy arrangements that keep wages, enforcement and political power beyond serious scrutiny receive rhetorical protection, while the diffuse cost-bearers are the workers and communities who need law and organized power when a favorable market weakens.

The frame is symmetrical. The economy does the work, politics is the obstacle, and the affected should help themselves: that architecture appears whenever the objection is a rule, a union or a wage floor. Same apparatus, different beneficiary. The analysis does not assume the beneficiary must be identical, and it does not assume the artifact’s beneficiaries are innocent of the frame’s consequences.

Here is the instruction, and it is the only instruction the piece requires. When an argument isolates one true variable and suppresses the one that would reverse its conclusion, the suppressed variable is the tell. In this column, it is not the governor’s mansion. It is the wage—and who was able to bargain for it.

I am angry about this one; the anger is the residue of reading it closely, and the reader is welcome to verify the record above and keep the argument while discarding the anger. The readers were never marks to be sneered at. They were sold a syllabus where a statute should have been. The trade-back is available. The next time a first is converted into a futility lesson, ask what the officeholder did with the power—and ask who taught you to stop asking.
