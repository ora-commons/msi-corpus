Immigration enforcement officers killed two American citizen protesters in Minneapolis.

Those deaths forced the administration to adjust its mass-deportation strategy. Kristi Noem was ousted. Markwayne Mullin now commands the department. On Wednesday, he will appear before the House Homeland Security Committee to answer for the machinery he inherited and the lives it has broken.

I grew up in El Paso. I have seen the desks left empty after a pre-dawn raid and the teacher continue teaching as though the room were whole. The people taken from those desks were not abstractions before they disappeared. They were neighbors. They were somebody’s child. The state made them disappear anyway.

[The hearing promises scrutiny of Mullin’s conduct](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/watch-live-dhs-secretary-mullin-testifies-on-worldwide-threats-amid-tech-outages-iran-warnings): the grounding of nearly all American flights, his decision to watch the LSU-Texas game in Louisiana after the stoppage, his assurance that the technology failures were not sabotage, the vacant leadership at CISA, and ICE’s expanding deportation machinery.

A broken router is a fixable thing. The failure of preparation around it is not.

The administration says newly installed routers flooded the FAA’s telecommunications system with data and forced the grounding of all aircraft. At Friday’s White House briefing, Mullin asked for more money for the aging air-traffic-control system while saying the government saw no sign of a deliberate cyberattack. On Monday, he said he could stand with “98%” certainty that the string of failures was not a cyberattack. The record also includes a Microsoft outage lasting hours, failures at Chase and United, and an internal NSA network used to communicate classified information going offline after routine maintenance.

Nothing in the public record establishes sabotage. That distinction matters. These failures do not prove an enemy. They prove fragility.

That is why Mullin enters Wednesday’s hearing with the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency without a Senate-confirmed leader since early 2025. Trump’s original nominee withdrew after his nomination languished for months. CISA also lost staff during last year’s federal cuts. Its responsibilities include the water systems, electric generation, election systems, chemical facilities, and cyber networks on which the country depends. This is not a vacancy on an organization chart. It is an empty center of accountability in the machinery meant to keep a country standing.

Mullin’s [claim that the ground stop showed no sign of cyberattack](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/watch-live-dhs-secretary-mullin-testifies-on-worldwide-threats-amid-tech-outages-iran-warnings) and the [criticism of the FAA’s ground-stop explanation](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/politics/watch-live-dhs-secretary-mullin-testifies-on-worldwide-threats-amid-tech-outages-iran-warnings) belong in the same room. Jeremiah asked of false healers: “They have healed the wound of my people lightly, saying, ‘Peace, peace,’ when there is no peace.” The committee’s first question should not be whether Mullin guessed correctly about the cause. It should be why he leads a critical agency without confirmed leadership and why the public must learn the country’s infrastructure remains fragile from television briefings.

The stoppage had ended. The bill had not.

Thousands of flights remained canceled. Mullin flew to Louisiana after the airspace reopened and said: “We would have never taken airspace if airspace wasn’t open. And we still gotta live our lives at the same time.” His words were true. His conduct was the answer. Airspace reopened; his plans reopened with it. The canceled flights did not come back.

The same hearing reaches ICE, Customs and Border Protection, and the detention and removal contracts beneath them. Congress has handed ICE billions in new funding. Arrests initially fell, then rebounded as the agency hired more officers and maximized cooperation from local and state law-enforcement agencies. Congress has also appropriated $45 billion for ICE detention, while DHS moved toward expanding detention capacity to 100,000 beds. The family detention center in Dilley, Texas, reopened in 2025. Children and infants can be held with their families in detention by policy.

That is not oversight by another name. It is more power moving faster than justice.

State cruelty does not always arrive as a fist. It arrives as a budget, a detention contract, a hiring order, a backlog, and a rule that makes removal cheaper than protection. This is cruelty with a budget line.

The money has a face. CoreCivic booked $2.2 billion in revenue in 2025 and increased its profit by about 70 percent. GEO Group earned $254 million in net profit, roughly 700 percent more than the previous year, and announced about $520 million in new or expanded ICE contracts. Its founder called this “the largest amount of new business we’ve ever won in a single year in our company’s history.” Together, the companies gave about $2.8 million to the president’s 2024 campaign and inaugural fund. The money and the policy run through the same network.

My tradition has a word for what a structure does when it grinds people by design: *pecado social*, social sin. The government is entrusted with protection. It has used that trust to enlarge its coercive reach into the lives of the stranger.

At the close of fiscal year 2024, about 3.6 million cases were pending in immigration courts. The state is adding enforcement capacity while its immigration tribunals carry millions of cases. That queue is not justice. It is fear with a calendar.

None of this began in January, and none of it belongs to one party. The deportation machine has roots in the late nineteenth century. The 1996 immigration laws enlarged removal, made old offenses count retroactively, and expedited the expulsion of people already inside the country. Across administrations, roughly 8 million people were formally removed over the last century while about 50 million left through mechanisms the government labeled “voluntary.” Many chose among removal and hunger. Calling that choice voluntary does not make it free.

The machinery is not one administration’s invention. Congress has enlarged detention and removal across administrations, and inherited systems have repeatedly made enforcement easier to expand than to correct. That does not make every hand equally culpable. It makes every hand answerable.

That accounting must include me. My community has asked this country for harder enforcement and then watched the machinery turn toward our neighbors. We wanted order without asking what order does to people. My taxes buy the beds. My church has held federal contracts for ministry to migrants. I have stood on the church side of a door when the knock came at five in the morning. I do not get to place myself outside the field of accountability. Mullin does not get to hide there either.

The White House has elevated Stephen Miller, the architect of the immigration crackdown, to chief of staff. That does not absolve Mullin. It puts the policy’s author close to the center of power.

Keep the texts this country claims beside the conduct this country funds. The Torah commands love for the stranger more than thirty times: “You shall love the alien as yourself, for you were aliens in the land of Egypt.” Malachi says God will come near in judgment as a swift witness against those who thrust aside the alien and oppress the hired worker in his wages. Christ set the final accounting in his last recorded teaching: “I was a stranger and you did not welcome me.” And then: “Truly I tell you, just as you did it to one of the least of these who are members of my family, you did it to me.”

As many as 1,360 children remained unreunified with their parents as of late 2024. From 2010 through 2024, the count kept by those who track encounters with Border Patrol agents reached 369 deaths. These numbers have names attached. That is what your machine removes, and the removal is the design: a man becomes a case number, a mother becomes a docket entry, a child becomes a bed assignment at Dilley.

It does not have to run this way. Pope Leo XIV, an American, said it in plain words: “No one has said that the United States should have open borders,” and every country has the right to determine who enters and when. But people who have lived good lives here for ten, fifteen, or twenty years cannot be treated in a way that is “extremely disrespectful, to say the least.”

The bishops of the United States and Mexico wrote the moral arithmetic in 2003. People have the right to find opportunities in their homeland and to migrate when they cannot support themselves and their families at home. Nations have the right to control their borders. Refugees and asylum seekers must receive protection. The human rights and human dignity of undocumented migrants must be respected. A humane immigration policy must hear individual cases rather than stack dockets, preserve families rather than manufacture separation, restrain force, and give migrant workers a lawful path toward stable status.

Francis writes in *Fratelli Tutti* §39 that migrants “possess the same intrinsic dignity as any person.” These are not rewards for deservingness. They are the minimum conditions of equal dignity.

Mullin, you can resume your life and still remain responsible for your department. Your flight proved that your plans could resume. It did not prove the country had recovered. Restore CISA to confirmed leadership. Publish what the outages revealed. Tell Congress what the detention contracts and the use-of-force rules authorize.

Congress must halt the expansion of detention and coercive force until immigration courts can hear cases at human scale, independent investigators can examine ICE’s use of force, and the records show what power was used and against whom. The state can exercise coercive power without making human beings expendable.

And to the people inside the machine—the agent on the porch at four in the morning, the warden holding the keys, the officer writing the report afterward: you are not exempt from this column, and you are not its enemy. You are persons, and the machine runs on your choices hour by hour. If the knock comes at five somewhere tonight, the bag will already be packed. You can refuse the knock. You can write the truth into the report. You can slow the machine until it stops.

In March 1980, the day before gunmen shot Archbishop Óscar Romero dead at the altar in San Salvador, he told his country’s soldiers: “Ningún soldado está obligado a obedecer una orden contra la ley de Dios. Una ley inmoral, nadie tiene que cumplirla.” No soldier is obliged to obey an order against the law of God. An immoral law, no one has to comply with. Then he begged, he besought, and he ordered, in the name of God and a suffering people: “¡cese la represión!” Stop the repression.

Mullin, the door of return is open, and it opens toward you: stop the raids, empty the family jail, restore the courts so a person can be heard before fear hardens into a file, and treat the neighbor of twenty years as a neighbor. The machine will not stop itself. In a hundred years, it has not repaired itself.

Tomorrow before dawn I will sit in the dark of the pew, then set the broken chair on the bench and repair it—one thing a day, and the country is on the list. Mullin said, “We still gotta live our lives at the same time.” He is right. That is the whole reason the machine must stop. *Cese la represión.* Stop the raids. The door is open.
