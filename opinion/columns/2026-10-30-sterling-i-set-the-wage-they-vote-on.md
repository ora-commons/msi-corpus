Penny is a sizer in the cold room of the packing shed I own outside Pharr, Texas. 12 hours at 41 degrees, thumbing the oranges that roll past her, and the one in your lunch bag this week was her call: 2.75 inches, no bruise, no soft navel. I keep the room cold because the fruit is the perishable thing in it. The fruit is also the one that earns. God gave the valley a heavy crop this year, and I own the cold it rides to you in. I pay her $14.50 an hour, and I set the shelf she buys from at 3.5%, and the space between those two numbers is the most reliable asset I hold. I have held it through 4 harvests.

$0.29 an hour. That's the margin, and it collects itself. No meeting, no dunning letter, no signature but mine. It comes to $603 a year off one woman's real wage, and the spread bought the door to my office, the one with the spring that keeps the heat in. My office holds 72 degrees. The cold room holds 41. I have never once mistaken one for the other.

I declined the $4,100 defrost this quarter, so her end of the line runs colder than the fruit requires. The cold is in her hands now. She calls it the weather. I call it a retention feature.

Jason Riley writes in the *Wall Street Journal* — ["Hispanics Ditch Trump for the Same Reason as Everyone Else"](https://www.wsj.com/opinion/hispanics-ditch-trump-for-the-same-reason-as-everyone-else-c8adf043?mod=rss_opinion) — that Hispanic support for Republicans went from 28% in 2016 to 34% in 2020 to 44% in 2024, and that Pew now ranks immigration fifth among Hispanic concerns, behind the economy. He means it as a warning to his own side. I read it as a rate card. The wise men say next week's election is about the economy. Fine. Weekly earnings rose 1.5%. The Consumer Price Index rose 3.5%. The numbers are correct; the ownership column is missing. The inflation you'll vote about is my margin.

Hold her pay stub next to her receipt. That's the whole course in 2 pieces of paper.

The raise I gave the line this quarter was 1.5%, matching the national number exactly, so no one can say I paid less than the average. The shelf I charge her is 3.5%, matching the same number, which is the elegance of it.

The line, 1 hour: $14.50. The raise I gave her: +$0.22. The shelf I charge her: −$0.51. The difference, which is mine: $0.29.

You're counting the way I count now.

Now take a different industry, because the principle travels. Ramona is a 38-year-old wash hand at the 4 bays I own outside Phoenix, and the truck in her lane on Saturday is yours. I pay her $14.20 an hour and keep the $0.42 an hour that hiring her direct would cost me in payroll taxes I'd rather not itemize. Last quarter's $0.42 bought a set of floor mats — $214 — for the vehicle I don't wash at my own counter. She is scheduled for 36 hours and I cap the week at 29, so the clock never touches the line I don't let anyone on my payroll cross. Her shift runs 11:40 to 4:10, which is the part of the day the asphalt makes its own argument. In August I declined the $3,100 shade structure over the drying bay. The mats won. Both decisions are on the same page of the same ledger, and the mats winning is what winning means.

The Bureau of Labor Statistics says weekly earnings rose 1.5% while prices rose 3.5%. That is −1.93%, and the bureau is describing my floor from 300 miles away with no access to my books. I gave the whole crew $0.55 in March. In June Ramona asked for her Friday hours back. I told her the Friday hours were in the forecast, and the forecast was thin. She thanked me and worked Saturday. You paid $34 on Sunday and thought she looked tired at the rinse. She looked tired because the schedule is the second number, and most people stop at the first. You aren't watching me. You're financing me, which is a much more comfortable posture and considerably harder to quit.

Cirilo is the same payroll wearing a forklift. He moves pallets at the yard I own in Fontana at night, and I classify him as his own boss, so the lift's insurance is his problem and my $0.61 an hour. He filed an injury form in August. The form asked whether the lift was his. It was, because I said so. Different floor, same rate card.

Lidia is the cashier at the store I own 3 miles up the road from the shed; she rings up Penny's check before it clears. They have never met, and the $0.29 doesn't care which one of them it comes out of. Penny's paycheck is back in my till before Thursday. The awning went up over the storefront this month. I gave the awning more thought than I gave the raise.

Step back to where I stand and look at the whole line. The ballot is the only index in this country I don't get to edit, and it arrives with a 2-year lag behind the wage. I set the wage in March, they set the vote in November, and by November the wage has already written the vote for them. Riley calls that a pocketbook issue. I call it lead time. And the market is not a meeting place of wages and prices. It's plumbing, and both pipes are mine, and this is the oldest plumbing there is — older than the Bureau, older than the ballot, older than the party that will claim next Tuesday night that it has just now found out what working people think. God provides shade to whoever purchases it.

> Servants, be obedient to them that are your masters according to the flesh, with fear and trembling, in singleness of your heart, as unto Christ.
>
> — Ephesians 6:5 (KJV)

That grammar is my grammar. The master sets the wage, and the master sets the price, and the servant is counseled to look to heaven for the difference. I am the "according to the flesh" that verse means, and I'm not a ghost. I walk the line twice a week, and she calls me sir. The singleness of heart the verse requires of her, she gives. I give the same singleness to the spread. Penny has never read it. She doesn't need to. She works it, and she'll cast it, and it will land on the number I have already made.

And don't write to me as the one bad owner. There's a faculty. I hold the chair, the course is full, and lecture one is the table you just read; the students copy it in pen and initial it, because a pencil line can be corrected, and correction is the first softness I grade out of them. One of them will price your job before he is 30, and he'll do the subtraction faster than I did.

In August I raised the wash package by $2 and gave the crew $0.00. Volume rose 6%. Whether that shows up in somebody's polling next week, I couldn't tell you. I don't read the polls, I read the register, and the register has been humming at the same pitch since May. The forecast I approve each quarter says the valley will run out of hands before it runs out of trees, and I approve the forecast because approving is faster than reading. The pool of hands is deep, I'm told. I have not sounded it. Spring came early this year and the crop is good, and I have no complaint with the season, or with the quarter, or with the way the two keep agreeing with me.

The pen that signs her stub is the same pen that signs my price sheet, and neither one is on the ballot.

You'll vote on the 3rd. Penny will be in the cold room with her hands in it. Ramona will be in the lane at 7:40 with the mitt. The mats are in the truck. Everything is where I left it.
