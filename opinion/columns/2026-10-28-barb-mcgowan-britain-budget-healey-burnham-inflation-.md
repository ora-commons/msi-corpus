*Healey calls it breathing space; the ledger says 8% is already gone.*

John Healey is walking into the House of Commons this week to deliver his first budget under Prime Minister Andy Burnham, and the sales pitch is ‘some breathing space.’ Some. As if families get to choose between a little inflation and a moderate-sized inflation.

Here is the room they carved out: U.K. inflation has hit 3.5%, near a one-year high, because the Iran war has pushed fuel prices up. And it is expected to climb higher. Not lower. Not steadier. Higher. At the same time, public debt has passed £3 trillion and debt service already takes 8% of government spending. That 8% is not ‘breathing space.’ It is a closed fist.

Healey says the focus is cost of living and that he wants to cushion the blow for businesses and families. Fine. But the government does not have the money to deliver many of Labour’s ambitions for public services. So the likely receipts are a higher surcharge on bank profits—banks that benefited from higher rates—and possibly higher gambling taxes. Those may be fair. They are also the admission that the promises and the money do not match.

And Andy Burnham wants to reduce or eradicate homelessness, improve elderly care, and meet NATO targets. Healey quit as defense secretary earlier this year in protest because there wasn’t enough money. Now he is the chancellor who must find it. That is not a fiscal plan. That is a promise board with every nail already pulled.

Do not stand at that podium and call debt service ‘breathing space.’ The budget is making choices among fuel-price relief, public services, defense, debt service, and taxes. Ordinary people will live with whichever bill lands. Give us the actual numbers, protect the care and heat and groceries people can actually use, and stop selling restraint as a favor.

Some breathing space. What a load of ministerial bullshit. Damn it. Fucking hell.
