*They called it help. Reese traded 80 hours for $63.*

Lisa Reese did everything this country tells people to do. She worked nearly four decades, including 25 years at Marriott, and never took government assistance. At 62, she retired to care for her 80-year-old mother in senior living, her uncle after liver-cancer surgery, her daughter, and her 2-year-old granddaughter.

Then Donald Trump’s One Big Beautiful Bill attached 80 hours of work, training, or volunteering to her $63-a-month SNAP every month. Eighty. Hours. The retired caregiver now volunteers at a food pantry while arthritis, thigh pain, sciatica, and high blood pressure sit right there with her.

The Trump administration says this is “not punishment.” The USDA says industriousness and community contribution should be applauded. They say the law cuts waste, fraud, and abuse and makes sure SNAP serves only the Americans who need it most. Fine. Then show us the waste: Lisa Reese needs $63 after nearly four decades of work and a retirement spent caring for family. The Center for Budget and Policy Priorities estimates that 5 million people have lost federal food benefits since the changes. Mission of Love Charities cut pantry visits from weekly to every other week. Jobs remain scarce.

I know this pattern from my kitchen table: make aid conditional enough that accepting it feels like failure, then punish the person for needing it. Her daughter, with a young child, is exempt from the work rule. Lisa is not. Her uncle does not qualify for the incapacitated-caregiver waiver. Next year, new Medicaid work rules can hit her too, so she is shopping for private insurance while another bill lands on her household.

They say SNAP is temporary help for tough times. Temporary, bullshit. Lisa used to have a paycheck and a grandmother’s house full of people who needed her. Now she has a volunteer timecard, $63, and a law treating retirement as failure of character.

So fuck you, Trump administration. You made retirement and caregiving cost 80 hours before the food aid came. You put the work requirement under a bureaucratic boot, made it the price of dinner, and called the leftovers “help.” That is not welfare reform. That is bullshit. It is a goddamn hard screw and a holy shit all at once.
