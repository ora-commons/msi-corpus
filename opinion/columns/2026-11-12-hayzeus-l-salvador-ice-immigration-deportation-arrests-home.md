Trump is tearing hundreds of thousands of people out of American life.

Immigration and Customs Enforcement says it removed 483,200 people in the fiscal year that ended September 30 — the most in the agency's twenty-three years, 9 percent above its previous record and 78 percent above the 271,480 of 2024, the final year of Joe Biden's presidency. It arrested 474,850, roughly double last year's 239,890 and more than four times the 113,430 of 2024. More than 48,000 arrests in September alone, after monthly records that reached 50,925 in August. The agency had stopped publishing counts this precise when this president took office; it publishes them now. Even at this pace the White House has [missed its own one-million deportations target](/articles/2026-10-22-trump-administration-deportations-rise-but-miss-1-million-annual-target/), and [the number of local officers deputized to work with ICE has doubled in eight months](/articles/2026-10-28-local-officers-working-with-ice-have-doubled-in-eight-months/) to keep the number climbing — until every county becomes a border.

The press release does not say how many of those arrested had criminal histories. Every previous administration reported that breakdown; this one dropped it. I name the dropped count for what it is: cruelty keeping its own books closed. So the sentence Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin gave the numbers — that the agents were "unleashed" to "target and remove illegal aliens" and "save American lives" — arrives without the arithmetic that could test it. To save American lives you would have to show which of these people were a danger. The breakdown that would show it is the breakdown the release omits. What the record does show is ICE taking 474,850 people out of homes, workplaces, and school pickup lines in twelve months. A country that counts human beings the way it counts bushels is doing something cruel, and it is doing it in your name. Markwayne Mullin, whose lives are being saved? Count them for the country.

Who are the 483,200? The release does not say, past the label the secretary used. Before I name anyone else's part in this, mine and my people's: the parishes I have served have always had in them the exact people this tally removes, and we have taken their labor and their help at the supper and let the trucks pass at the edge of the parking lot. The machine itself is older than any president who has ever run it — a century of formal removals, "voluntary" departures, and calculated fear, fed by administrations of both parties, named in the tradition as *pecado social*, the social sin Romero said the Church is obliged to denounce, and counted by Adam Goodman at roughly eight million formal deportations over the past century with about fifty million more pushed out through what the government calls voluntary departure — a choice offered between removal and ruin. That is not an excuse for the man accelerating it. It is the reason the acceleration is ours to answer for. My own church hands down documents on the stranger that sit unread in its own pews while Catholic politicians quote fragments to license the removals.

The category is not a fact of nature. Congress built it in 1965, capping legal immigration far below the labor the economy was already hiring — a law that, as the historian Aviva Chomsky puts it, "deliberately created illegality and did so for the purpose of keeping Mexican workers available, cheap, and deportable." Behind the number are the parents of American children: about 12.5 million U.S. citizens live in households with a family member who is undocumented or temporarily protected. They are the pews of parishes from El Paso to Los Angeles. They are the roughly half of the country's crop farmworkers who have no papers, the workers who paid $96.7 billion in taxes in 2022, including about $13 billion a year into a Social Security system they are barred from drawing from. I have eaten what those hands picked every day of my life.

Now set the press release beside the scriptures the country claims at every swearing-in. "You shall not wrong or oppress a resident alien, for you were aliens in the land of Egypt." "The alien who resides with you shall be to you as the citizen among you; you shall love the alien as yourself, for you were aliens in the land of Egypt." The command to love the stranger comes more than thirty times in Torah — more often than any other commandment — and the reason given is always memory: you were slaves; you know the heart of the stranger. And the one who counts the sheep says the same thing in his own words: I was a stranger and you welcomed me; as you did it to the least of these, you did it to me. The president is not only tearing people out of American life. He is tearing the memory out of us, and the memory was the reason given.

Look at what the pace rests on. One bill passed last year put $45 billion into detention beds. The private companies that profit from filling them, CoreCivic and GEO Group, posted record profits in 2025, after giving about $2.8 million to the 2024 campaign and inaugural fund. The removals are not passing through careful review: as of early 2026, roughly 570 immigration judges were carrying about 5,500 cases apiece, and asylum cases were waiting about four years. That is not a system weighing claims. It is a queue with a verdict at the end.

Whose lives are saved, then? The last time family separation ran as open policy, more than 5,500 children were taken from their parents, and as many as 1,360 were still not home as of late 2024. Those children are Americans. The same arithmetic runs now: every removal is counted as a success, and the American child left behind is counted nowhere in the release. So, without the passive voice: we have removed 483,200 people from this country in one year. Dorothy Day refused the passive after Hiroshima for exactly this reason — "We have killed 318,000 Japanese," she wrote in the *Catholic Worker*, first person plural, number, verb, no one excused. Not "ICE removed." Not "policy resulted in." We did this — the voters, the taxpayers, the eaters of the harvest. The number is ours.

None of this requires open borders. The bishops of the United States and Mexico wrote in 2003 that nations may control their borders, and wrote the rest of the teaching in the same letter — the right to find opportunity in one's homeland, the right to migrate when survival requires it, protection for those who flee, dignity for the undocumented, families kept whole — while *Pacem in Terris* adds that citizenship in one state does not cancel "membership in the human family." A policy faithful to that teaching would fund judges and counsel before it funds beds, and keep parents and children together as the rule. It would size legal pathways to the work this economy actually runs on, and aim enforcement at the employers who buy the labor rather than the families who sell it. That is not softness. Mr. Secretary, 483,200 is not a body count of lives saved. It is a count of the strangers Christ named as himself.

Óscar Romero stood in San Salvador's cathedral on the last Sunday of his life and told the soldiers holding the guns that no order against the law of God binds the conscience — then begged them, beseeched them, ordered them in the name of God: "Stop the repression!" He was killed the next day. He said it to men he called brothers. I am not writing to soldiers. I am writing to the agents who knock before dawn, the officers who process the paperwork, the secretary who reads these numbers as a triumph: your conscience outranks the quota. You are not obliged to do this.

I grew up in El Paso watching desks sit empty after the vans came before dawn, and the school day went on as if the empty chairs were ordinary. The record you are building will be read the way that homily is read — not by the people who cheered it, but by the ones who were counted. The desks do not have to sit empty. The door of return is open from this side too — for the agent, the officer, the secretary, the voter, the man who hires at the corner. Stop the repression. Come back through. Sweep the shop, turn off the light, and leave the door unlocked behind you.
