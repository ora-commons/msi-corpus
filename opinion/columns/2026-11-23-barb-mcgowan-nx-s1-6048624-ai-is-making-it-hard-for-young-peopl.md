*They automated the front door and called it opportunity.*

They keep telling us the machines are coming to set us free.

I heard it in the Walmart breakroom and at the school board. AI is the great equalizer, AI will lift the whole workforce, AI is going to create jobs nobody has the imagination to picture yet. Fine. Good.

And in every hiring season I have lived through, there were more people than there were jobs. Never a shortage of people who needed work. A shortage of people willing to work for less and bleed more.

Then Matt Sigelman, president of the Burning Glass Institute, tells NPR what the machines are actually doing: AI is already squeezing the job market for young jobseekers. Squeezing. Not transforming. Not disrupting. Squeezing.

And I know which jobs are getting squeezed, because I grew up in the house that depended on them. The counter at the gas station. The line at the packing plant. The register at Walmart, where I put in more hours than I want so the insurance would keep coming. Those were doors. You could walk through a door with no degree, no résumé, no fucking GitHub, and somebody would hand you an apron and teach you the work while somebody else paid you. That was the whole bargain. That was the ladder. One first job, then a second, then enough hours for shoes on the kids.

Now the machines have the first rung, and the young ones who need it most get to add a certificate to a résumé nobody is going to read.

Nobody has to sign a law for this one. Employers can hire fewer people now because the machine does the work. So the productivity story gets told on the news and the empty chair gets left to the kid. And no, it is not the kid's fault. Don't hand me that line of bullshit.

Here is the plain part: AI was supposed to be the machine that freed the worker. Instead it is the machine that closed the door on the kid, and nobody with a checkbook has the guts to say it out loud.

The young people in this county do not need a damn workshop about the future. They need a job to show up to on Monday. Get them the door, you corporate leash-wearers, or get the hell out of our way and take your goddamn miracle with you.
